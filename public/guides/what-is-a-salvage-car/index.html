What is a Salvage Car?

A practical beginner guide to salvage titles, risk factors, and how to evaluate auction vehicles with confidence.

Salvage Title Definition

A salvage car is a vehicle that an insurance company has declared a total loss. This usually means the estimated repair cost, plus related costs, meets or exceeds a large percentage of the car’s pre-loss value under state and insurer rules.

Why Insurance Companies Total Vehicles

Insurers total vehicles when repairs are not economically practical. The decision is financial and safety-driven, not always a sign that a car is unusable forever. Some salvage vehicles are rebuilt successfully, while others are better used for parts.

Common Causes of Salvage Titles

  • Collision damage: front-end, rear-end, or structural impact damage.
  • Theft recovery: stolen vehicles recovered after claim payout, sometimes with missing or damaged components.
  • Flood damage: water intrusion affecting electronics, interior materials, and drivetrain systems.
  • Hail: severe body and glass damage that can be costly to repair at scale.
  • Fire: engine bay, cabin, or wiring damage that may create major restoration complexity.

Recommendations Before You Buy

  • Check title status and rebuild rules for your state before bidding.
  • Review auction photos closely and budget for hidden damage risk.
  • Get a VIN history report and compare it with auction condition notes.
  • Estimate total cost: winning bid + fees + transport + repairs + inspections.
  • Start with lower-risk damage types while you build experience.
