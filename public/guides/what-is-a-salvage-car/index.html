What Is a Salvage Car?

A beginner’s guide to salvage titles, common causes of vehicle damage, and the difference between salvage and rebuilt vehicles.

What is a salvage car?

A salvage car is a vehicle that meets a state’s requirements for a salvage classification, generally because it has sustained significant damage or has been declared a total loss.

Insurance companies often declare vehicles a total loss when the cost of repairing them is too high relative to their value. However, an insurance total-loss determination and a state’s salvage title classification are not always the same thing.

Each state establishes its own rules for when a vehicle must receive a salvage title or other salvage ownership document. These rules may depend on the extent of damage, repair costs, vehicle value, age, and circumstances of the loss.

Why do insurance companies declare vehicles a total loss?

An insurance company may declare a vehicle a total loss when repairing it is not economically practical under the applicable insurance and state requirements.

A total-loss decision does not necessarily mean the vehicle is beyond repair. Some vehicles can be repaired and returned to the road, while others may be suitable only for parts or scrap.

The important distinction is whether the vehicle is legally eligible to be rebuilt and whether repairing it makes practical and financial sense.

What causes a vehicle to receive a salvage title?

Vehicles may receive salvage classifications for several reasons, including:

  • Collision damage: Damage to the body, frame, structural components, or mechanical systems.
  • Theft recovery: A stolen vehicle recovered after an insurance settlement, sometimes with missing or damaged components.
  • Flood damage: Water intrusion that may affect electrical systems, interior materials, and mechanical components.
  • Hail damage: Extensive exterior or glass damage that may be expensive to repair.
  • Fire damage: Damage to wiring, mechanical systems, structural components, or interior materials.

Not every vehicle with these types of damage receives a salvage title. Classification depends on the applicable state requirements and the circumstances of the loss.

What is the difference between salvage and rebuilt?

A salvage vehicle has received a salvage classification under applicable state requirements. A salvage title generally does not authorize the vehicle to be driven on public roads in ordinary use.

A rebuilt vehicle is a previously salvaged vehicle that has been repaired and completed the applicable state process for returning it to road use. That process may involve inspections, documentation of repairs, and a change in title classification.

The terminology and requirements vary by state. Some states use terms such as reconstructed, prior salvage, or revived salvage.

A rebuilt classification does not erase the vehicle’s damage history or guarantee its mechanical condition or safety.

Some vehicles receive nonrepairable, junk, or similar classifications that prevent them from returning to ordinary road use. These should not be confused with salvage vehicles that are eligible for rebuilding.

Why does the title classification matter?

A vehicle’s title classification can affect whether it may be repaired, titled, registered, insured, and legally driven.

It can also affect resale value and a future buyer’s willingness to purchase the vehicle.

A vehicle’s classification in one state does not necessarily determine how another state will treat it. If you buy a salvage or rebuilt vehicle from another state, your home state may have different requirements for accepting the title, inspecting the vehicle, or allowing it to be registered. Check your state’s requirements before purchasing or transporting the vehicle.

Before buying or rebuilding a salvage vehicle, confirm its title classification and the requirements that apply in your state.
