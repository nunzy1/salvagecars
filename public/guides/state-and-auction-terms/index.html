Common State and Auction Terms for Salvage Cars

A reference to terms you may encounter on vehicle titles, ownership documents, and auction listings. State-issued classifications and auction descriptions serve different purposes.

Common State Designations

States use different terms to describe vehicle damage, title status, and eligibility for rebuilding. The same term may have different meanings or requirements depending on the state. This list provides general explanations, not legal definitions for every state.

Before buying, repairing, or registering a vehicle, consult our State Salvage Vehicle Guidance directory and verify the requirements with the appropriate state agency.

General explanations of state vehicle title and ownership classifications
TermGeneral meaning
Salvage TitleA state-issued title identifying a vehicle as salvage under that state’s requirements. An insurance total-loss determination does not always result in the same title classification in every state.
Rebuilt TitleA title or classification for a previously salvaged vehicle that has completed the applicable state process for returning it to road use. Requirements may include inspections and supporting documents.
Reconstructed VehicleA state-specific classification that may involve substantial rebuilding or changes to vehicle components. It is not always equivalent to a rebuilt salvage vehicle.
Prior SalvageIndicates previous salvage history. The way this term appears on a title and what it signifies depend on the state.
Revived SalvageA term used in California for a previously salvaged vehicle restored to operational condition and processed under California’s requirements.
Restored SalvageA term that may describe a previously salvaged vehicle that has been repaired. Its legal meaning, if used as a designation, depends on the state.
Repaired SalvageA description of a salvage vehicle that has undergone repairs; it does not by itself establish that the vehicle is eligible for registration.
Salvage CertificateAn ownership document used in certain states for vehicles classified as salvage. It may differ from a standard vehicle title.
Salvage Certificate of TitleA title document identifying a vehicle’s salvage status, as defined by the issuing state.
Certificate of DestructionA document generally associated with vehicles restricted from returning to road use. The legal consequences depend on the issuing state.
Non-Repairable VehicleA state classification for a vehicle that cannot legally be rebuilt for ordinary road use under the applicable rules.
Junk TitleA state-specific title or classification generally associated with dismantling, parts, or scrap. Restrictions vary by jurisdiction.
Parts Only VehicleA description indicating that a vehicle is intended for parts rather than ordinary road use. Confirm the actual ownership document and state restrictions.
Dismantler VehicleA term associated with vehicles intended for dismantling. Its use and legal effect vary by state.
Total Loss VehicleA vehicle determined to be a total loss under insurance or applicable state rules. Total-loss status and title branding are related but not always identical.
Flood VehicleA vehicle affected by water damage. A state may apply a flood brand or another classification depending on its rules.
Hail Damage VehicleA vehicle damaged by hail. Whether it receives a title brand depends on the circumstances and state requirements.
Theft Recovery VehicleA vehicle recovered after being stolen. Title treatment may depend on the insurance settlement, damage, and state requirements.

Terms Often Used by Auctions

Auction listings may display the state-issued title or ownership document alongside auction descriptions of condition or damage. An auction description does not determine the vehicle’s legal title status or guarantee its roadworthiness. Terms and criteria may vary between auction operators.

Clean Title
A commonly used term for a vehicle title without a salvage, rebuilt, or similar damage-related brand. It does not guarantee that the vehicle has never been damaged or that it is free of liens. Verify both the vehicle’s title history and any outstanding liens.
Clear Title
A term generally used to describe a vehicle title with no outstanding liens or other ownership claims that would prevent an unrestricted transfer.
Repairable
An auction or seller description suggesting that repairs may be possible. It does not establish that the vehicle can legally be rebuilt, titled, or registered in your state.
Run and Drive
An auction condition designation generally indicating that the vehicle started, engaged a gear, and moved under its own power when checked. It does not guarantee operation at pickup or that the vehicle is safe or legal to drive on public roads.
Flood
An indication of reported water exposure or damage. It does not necessarily identify the vehicle’s official title brand; water damage can affect electrical, mechanical, and interior components.
Biohazard
A condition or damage description indicating possible biological contamination. The nature and severity of the hazard may not be fully established by the listing.
Theft Recovery
Indicates that a vehicle was stolen and subsequently recovered. It does not, by itself, establish the title classification or the extent of any damage.

Before you buy

An auction description does not determine whether a vehicle can legally be repaired, titled, or registered. Check the actual ownership document, confirm the vehicle’s history and condition, and review the requirements of the state where you intend to register it.
