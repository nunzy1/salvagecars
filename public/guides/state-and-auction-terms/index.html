Common State and Auction Terms for Salvage Cars

The most common umbrella term states use is "salvage vehicle", but states and their Departments of Motor Vehicles (DMVs) use many other designations depending on the vehicle's history and whether it has been repaired.

Common State Designations

This list is not meant to be all-inclusive, but covers terms that are commonly used across states.

Term Meaning
Salvage Title Vehicle declared a total loss by an insurer or owner
Rebuilt Title Previously salvage vehicle that has been repaired and passed inspection
Reconstructed Vehicle Similar to rebuilt. Often used when major components are replaced
Prior Salvage Indicates the vehicle was previously issued a salvage title
Revived Salvage Used in some states after a salvage vehicle returns to the road
Restored Salvage Similar to rebuilt. Repaired and re-registered
Repaired Salvage Salvage vehicle that has undergone repairs
Salvage Certificate Ownership document before the vehicle can be retitled
Salvage Certificate of Title Formal title document indicating salvage status
Certificate of Destruction Vehicle intended only for parts or scrap, not road use
Non-Repairable Vehicle Vehicle that cannot legally be rebuilt for highway use
Junk Title Vehicle designated only for dismantling or scrap
Parts Only Vehicle Can be used for parts but not retitled for road use
Dismantler Vehicle Vehicle designated for dismantling operations
Total Loss Vehicle Insurance designation that often leads to a salvage title
Flood Vehicle Vehicle damaged by flooding. May receive salvage branding
Hail Damage Vehicle May receive salvage branding in some states
Theft Recovery Vehicle Recovered after theft and sometimes branded depending on condition

Terms Often Used by Auctions

These are the most common terms you will encounter at salvage auctions, but others may appear depending on the platform or state of origin.

  • Clean Title: Vehicle has no branding and a standard, unencumbered title.
  • Salvage Title: Vehicle was declared a total loss and has not been repaired or reinspected.
  • Rebuilt Title: Previously salvaged vehicle that passed a state inspection after repairs.
  • Certificate of Destruction: Vehicle is intended for parts or scrap only and cannot be registered for road use.
  • Non-Repairable: Cannot be legally rebuilt or returned to road use in the issuing state.
  • Parts Only: Vehicle is sold for parts and cannot be retitled for driving.
  • Repairable: Auction platform's indication that the vehicle is restorable, though title type still governs what is legally allowed.
  • Run and Drive: Vehicle starts and moves under its own power at time of sale.
  • Enhanced Vehicle: Auction designation indicating a higher-value or specialty vehicle with additional condition detail available.
  • Flood: Vehicle sustained water damage, which may affect electronics, interior, and structural components.
  • Biohazard: Vehicle requires decontamination before it can be safely worked on.
  • Theft Recovery: Vehicle was stolen and later recovered. May carry salvage branding depending on condition and state rules.
