Mississippi Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Mississippi Salvage and Rebuilt titles, owner-retained total losses, older-vehicle exceptions, rebuilt inspections, parts documentation, restrictive title brands, and what must happen before a salvage vehicle can return to ordinary road use.

What you should know

Mississippi uses a Salvage title for certain vehicles acquired by an insurance company after it pays a total-loss claim.

Once a Salvage title has been issued, the vehicle cannot be operated on Mississippi roads or highways, cannot receive a license tag, and cannot be sold at retail by a licensed motor vehicle dealer until it has been properly rebuilt and retitled.

An eligible vehicle can return to road use after reconstruction in Mississippi, inspection by the Mississippi Department of Public Safety, and issuance of a Rebuilt title.

Mississippi also has an important owner-retained rule. If an insurance company declares a vehicle a total loss but the owner keeps the vehicle and the insurer does not obtain a Salvage title, the owner can rebuild the vehicle, have it inspected in the owner's name, and apply for a Rebuilt title.

Mississippi will not issue a title or registration for a vehicle branded Junk Vehicle, Non-Rebuildable Vehicle, Certificate of Destruction Title, or Parts Only Title.

Understand Mississippi terminology

Salvage vehicle

Mississippi defines a Salvage vehicle as a motor vehicle that an insurance company obtains from the owner after paying a total-loss claim resulting from collision, fire, flood, accident, hail damage, trespass, unrecovered theft, or another occurrence.

That definition is important because it expressly involves the insurance company obtaining the vehicle after paying the claim.

Mississippi excludes certain vehicles from this definition, including a vehicle that is 10 years old or older and has a value of $1,500 or less, and a vehicle whose damage requires the repair or replacement of five or fewer minor component parts.

Rebuilt vehicle

Mississippi defines a Rebuilt vehicle according to the extent and type of reconstruction performed in Mississippi after collision damage.

The Rebuilt definition can apply when the reconstruction involves one major component and five minor component parts, six or more minor component parts, two or more major component parts, the cowl or firewall, or both the front-end assembly and rear clip.

The exact components repaired or replaced therefore matter to Mississippi's title process.

Junk and other restrictive brands

Mississippi will not issue a title or registration for vehicles branded Junk Vehicle, Non-Rebuildable Vehicle, Certificate of Destruction Title, or Parts Only Title. These documents are materially different from a repairable Salvage title.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, if the vehicle is eligible for Mississippi's rebuilding process.

A vehicle with a Mississippi Salvage title cannot be operated on Mississippi roads or highways and cannot receive a license tag while it remains Salvage. After reconstruction in Mississippi and the required Department of Public Safety inspection, an eligible vehicle can proceed to a Rebuilt Certificate of Title.

A vehicle carrying a Junk Vehicle, Non-Rebuildable Vehicle, Certificate of Destruction, or Parts Only brand does not have that path to Mississippi title and registration.

Before buying, ask:

What exact ownership document does this vehicle have?

Then determine:

Does Mississippi allow a vehicle with that document to complete the Rebuilt-title process?

How do I get a Mississippi salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible Salvage vehicle, the general path is:

  1. Establish ownership with the appropriate Salvage title or other acceptable ownership documentation.
  2. Reconstruct the vehicle in Mississippi.
  3. Keep bills of sale and invoices for component parts used during reconstruction.
  4. Completely restore the vehicle and prepare it for inspection.
  5. Complete Form 78-021, Application for Inspection of a Salvage/Rebuilt Vehicle.
  6. Schedule the required inspection with the Mississippi Department of Public Safety.
  7. Present the vehicle, ownership documents, Form 78-021, and required parts documentation for inspection.
  8. After a successful inspection, receive the inspection-completion documentation.
  9. Apply for a Mississippi Rebuilt Certificate of Title.
  10. Complete the applicable registration requirements before returning the vehicle to ordinary road use.

Mississippi specifically states that a vehicle repaired in another state cannot be inspected in Mississippi under this process.

What inspection does Mississippi require?

The Mississippi Department of Public Safety conducts the rebuilt-vehicle inspection.

The inspection includes examination of the vehicle and the parts identified by the applicant as repaired or replaced. The inspector checks vehicle and component identification numbers, the accuracy of information supplied by the applicant, and indications that the vehicle or any parts are stolen.

If the vehicle successfully passes inspection, the inspector issues documentation needed for the Rebuilt-title application.

Does passing the inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

Mississippi's regulation specifically states that the inspection certification does not attest to the roadworthiness or safety condition of the vehicle.

The inspection is primarily concerned with vehicle and component identity, documentation, and indications of stolen parts.

Before buying a Rebuilt vehicle, independently evaluate structural repairs, frame or unibody alignment, airbags and restraint systems, brakes, steering and suspension, electrical repairs, water or flood damage, mechanical condition, and the quality of replacement components and repairs.

What documentation should I keep for replacement parts?

Mississippi requires detailed documentation for parts used during reconstruction.

For major component parts, the applicant must provide notarized bills of sale. The records identify the source of the components and include information about the seller and donor vehicle.

For minor component parts, invoices are required, although the invoices do not need to be notarized.

Form 78-021 also requires the applicant to identify major components that were repaired or replaced and provide identifying information for applicable parts.

Keep these records while rebuilding the vehicle. Missing or inadequate parts documentation can interfere with the inspection and title process.

What documents and forms should I expect?

Depending on the vehicle and transaction, Mississippi's process can involve:

  • the Salvage Certificate of Title or other acceptable ownership document,
  • Form 78-021, Application for Inspection of a Salvage/Rebuilt Vehicle,
  • notarized bills of sale for major component parts,
  • invoices for minor component parts,
  • donor-vehicle VIN and identifying information,
  • inspection-completion documentation,
  • the application for a Rebuilt Certificate of Title,
  • an insurance-company letter for an owner-retained total loss,
  • registration documentation, and
  • applicable taxes and fees.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company totals it?

Mississippi has a distinct owner-retained process.

If the owner keeps possession of the vehicle and the insurance company does not obtain a Salvage Certificate of Title, Mississippi allows the owner to have the vehicle rebuilt and inspected in the owner's name.

After inspection, the owner takes the insurance-company letter and required salvage-inspection forms to the County Tax Collector's office and applies for a Rebuilt Certificate of Title and new registration.

Mississippi's formal Salvage vehicle definition focuses on a vehicle the insurance company obtains from the owner after paying a total-loss claim.

For a buyer, ask not only “Was this vehicle declared a total loss?” but also “Who retained or acquired the vehicle after the insurance settlement, and what title process applied?”

What happens to an older vehicle that an insurance company totals?

Mississippi has a specific age-and-value exception within its Salvage vehicle definition.

A vehicle does not fall within that definition when it is 10 years old or older and valued at $1,500 or less. Both conditions matter.

Mississippi also separately excludes a vehicle when the damage requires repair or replacement of five or fewer minor component parts.

For an older total-loss vehicle, determine its age, value, nature and extent of damage, whether an insurer paid a total-loss claim, whether the insurer acquired the vehicle or the owner retained it, and what ownership document was issued afterward.

Do not assume that every Mississippi vehicle more than 10 years old is exempt from the state's salvage rules.

What if the vehicle was rebuilt outside Mississippi?

This is an unusually important question in Mississippi.

Mississippi DOR states that a vehicle repaired in another state cannot be inspected in Mississippi.

Someone considering an out-of-state salvage vehicle therefore needs to distinguish between an unrepaired salvage vehicle that might be brought into Mississippi for reconstruction and a vehicle already repaired or reconstructed outside Mississippi.

Verify the exact vehicle status and ownership document with Mississippi DOR before buying or transporting the vehicle.

What if the vehicle has an out-of-state restrictive title?

Mississippi states that it will not issue a title or registration for vehicles carrying a Junk Vehicle, Non-Rebuildable Vehicle, Certificate of Destruction Title, or Parts Only Title.

Do not assume that a restrictive document from another state can be converted into a Mississippi Rebuilt title merely because the vehicle is physically repairable.

Before buying, verify the exact ownership document, whether the vehicle has already been repaired, whether Mississippi accepts the document for the intended process, and whether the vehicle can legally complete Mississippi's inspection and Rebuilt-title requirements.

What should I check before buying a Mississippi salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Has a Mississippi Salvage title already been issued?
  • Was the vehicle declared a total loss by an insurance company?
  • Did the insurer acquire the vehicle, or did the owner retain it?
  • If owner-retained, what documentation exists from the insurance company?
  • Is the vehicle 10 years old or older and, if so, was its value $1,500 or less?
  • Does the damage involve five or fewer minor component parts?
  • Has the vehicle already been repaired or rebuilt, and where?
  • Can it qualify for Mississippi's rebuilt inspection?
  • Does it carry a restrictive brand?
  • Are the required parts records available?
  • Has repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, transportation, inspection, title, tax, registration, and other costs remain?

The central Mississippi question is:

What happened after this vehicle was declared a total loss, what ownership document does it have now, and does that status allow it to complete Mississippi's Rebuilt-title process?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

A vehicle that completes Mississippi's salvage-reconstruction process receives a Rebuilt Certificate of Title, preserving the fact that it went through the state's rebuilt process.

A licensed motor vehicle dealer cannot sell a Salvage vehicle until it has been retitled as Rebuilt.

For a future private transfer, make sure the title assignment is properly completed. Mississippi DOR warns buyers and sellers not to accept or deliver an incomplete title assignment and specifically warns against leaving the buyer's name blank.

Retain the vehicle's reconstruction and parts documentation even after the Rebuilt title is issued.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Mississippi Department of Revenue, Mississippi Department of Public Safety, your County Tax Collector, or another government agency.
