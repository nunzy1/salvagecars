Minnesota Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Minnesota Salvage and Prior Salvage title brands, which damaged vehicles require inspection, how vehicle age and value affect the process, and what to check before buying or rebuilding a damaged vehicle.

What you should know

Minnesota uses two closely related title brands that are important to distinguish: Salvage and Prior Salvage.

A Minnesota title carrying a Salvage brand allows the vehicle to be possessed, transported, and transferred, but it does not authorize registration.

For a late-model or high-value vehicle carrying a Salvage brand, Minnesota generally requires a vehicle inspection before the state will issue a title carrying the Prior Salvage brand.

Prior Salvage remains part of the vehicle's title history.

Minnesota also distinguishes repairable vehicles from an unrepairable total loss vehicle. An unrepairable total loss requires a junking certificate rather than a salvage-title path back to ordinary road use.

Vehicle age and pre-damage value matter. Minnesota can require different title brands for older or lower-value damaged vehicles than it does for late-model or high-value vehicles.

Understand Minnesota terminology

Salvage

Minnesota requires a Salvage brand in several circumstances involving damaged late-model or high-value vehicles.

For example, when a Minnesota insurer acquires a late-model or high-value vehicle through payment of damages, other than a recovered intact vehicle, the insurer must apply for a Salvage title or stamp the existing title Salvage.

A Salvage title allows possession, transportation, and transfer of the vehicle, but it does not authorize registration.

Prior Salvage

Prior Salvage generally identifies a vehicle with qualifying previous salvage history that is no longer being titled simply as Salvage.

For a late-model or high-value vehicle carrying a Salvage title, Minnesota requires the applicable inspection certification before issuing a title branded Prior Salvage.

Minnesota also uses Prior Salvage directly in some situations involving older or lower-value damaged vehicles.

Late-model vehicle

Minnesota defines a late-model vehicle as a vehicle with a manufacturer's designated model year equal to or greater than the fifth calendar year immediately preceding the current calendar year.

Because this definition moves with the calendar, buyers should verify whether a particular model year currently qualifies rather than relying on a permanently stated list of model years.

High-value vehicle

Minnesota defines a high-value vehicle as a vehicle that had an actual cash value of more than $9,000 before being damaged, or certain vehicles rated over 26,000 pounds gross vehicle weight that are not late-model vehicles.

This means an older passenger vehicle does not necessarily escape Minnesota's Salvage process simply because of age. If its pre-damage value exceeds the statutory threshold, it can still be a high-value vehicle.

Junking certificate

A junking certificate applies when a vehicle is an unrepairable total loss.

Minnesota requires a person acquiring an unrepairable total loss vehicle to surrender the assigned certificate of title and apply for a junking certificate.

A junking certificate does not provide a normal pathway for rebuilding the vehicle and returning it to ordinary titled road use.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, if the vehicle is repairable and its ownership document permits the applicable Minnesota salvage process.

For a late-model or high-value vehicle carrying a Salvage brand, the Salvage title itself does not authorize registration. The vehicle must complete the applicable inspection process before Minnesota can issue the required Prior Salvage title.

The first questions should therefore be:

What exact title or ownership document does the vehicle have?

Does Minnesota classify the vehicle as late-model or high-value?

Is the vehicle repairable, or has it been classified as an unrepairable total loss?

Do not assume that an auction description such as “salvage,” “repairable,” “rebuildable,” or “parts” answers those questions.

How do I get a Minnesota salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible late-model or high-value vehicle carrying a Salvage title, the general path is:

  1. Establish ownership with the appropriate Salvage title.
  2. Repair the vehicle.
  3. Keep proof of ownership for replacement parts and components.
  4. Complete the title and inspection documentation required by Minnesota Driver and Vehicle Services.
  5. Present the vehicle for the required inspection.
  6. Obtain the required certification of inspection.
  7. Apply for the appropriate Minnesota title.
  8. Receive a title carrying the Prior Salvage brand.
  9. Complete the ordinary registration requirements that apply to the vehicle.

The exact documentation and inspection requirements depend on the vehicle and how it entered Minnesota's salvage system.

What inspection does Minnesota require?

Minnesota requires an inspection before a late-model or high-value vehicle with a Salvage title can receive a Prior Salvage title.

The inspection process is intended in part to verify the vehicle and the ownership of components used in its reconstruction.

Minnesota's reconstruction guidance emphasizes that vehicle identification numbers must remain on parts and that proof of ownership is required for major parts used in reconstruction. If identifying numbers appear to have been tampered with or removed, title issuance can be affected.

The inspection should not be treated as proof that the vehicle is a good purchase or that every aspect of the repair has been independently evaluated.

What documentation should I keep for replacement parts?

Keep clear documentation showing where major replacement components came from.

Minnesota's reconstruction guidance requires proof of ownership for major parts used in a reconstructed vehicle and warns that insufficient ownership documentation for major components can create additional title requirements.

For a salvage repair, retain receipts, bills of sale, donor-vehicle information, VIN or identification information when available, repair records, and other documentation showing lawful ownership of replacement components.

Do this as repairs occur rather than trying to reconstruct the documentation immediately before inspection.

What documents and forms should I expect?

Depending on the vehicle and transaction, the Minnesota process can involve:

  • the current certificate of title,
  • an application to title and/or register the vehicle,
  • inspection documentation,
  • proof of ownership for major replacement components,
  • bills of sale and receipts,
  • reconstruction documentation when applicable, and
  • applicable title and registration fees.

Minnesota DVS may request additional documentation depending on the vehicle's history and the type of work performed.

What happens to an older vehicle that an insurance company totals?

Minnesota handles this differently from states where older vehicles simply fall outside the salvage-branding system.

When a Minnesota insurer acquires ownership of a vehicle through payment of damages, the required brand depends partly on the vehicle's age and value.

For a late-model or high-value vehicle, the insurer generally must obtain a Salvage brand.

For a vehicle that is not late-model or high-value, Minnesota generally requires the insurer to obtain a Prior Salvage brand instead.

That distinction is important for older cars.

An older vehicle can still qualify as high-value if its pre-damage actual cash value exceeded $9,000. If it does, the Salvage rules for high-value vehicles can apply despite the vehicle's age.

If the older vehicle is neither late-model nor high-value, an insurer-acquired total-loss vehicle generally moves directly into Prior Salvage rather than first receiving the Salvage brand and going through the same Salvage-to-Prior-Salvage inspection sequence required for late-model or high-value vehicles.

An unrepairable total loss is different. That vehicle requires a junking certificate and should not be treated as a repairable salvage vehicle.

What about owner-retained total-loss vehicles?

Do not assume that every insurance total loss follows exactly the same title path.

Minnesota's salvage statute specifically addresses situations in which an insurer acquires ownership of a vehicle through payment of damages, while other provisions address damaged vehicles acquired by other persons and self-insured owners.

If an owner retains a vehicle after an insurance settlement, verify the title and branding requirements that apply to that particular transaction with Minnesota Driver and Vehicle Services rather than assuming the insurer-acquisition rule applies unchanged.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

Minnesota carries forward qualifying salvage history from other jurisdictions.

For late-model or high-value vehicles, an out-of-state salvage certificate or a title carrying terms such as damaged, repairable, salvage, or a similar designation can trigger Minnesota's Salvage requirements.

For other damaged vehicles, prior out-of-state salvage, rebuilt, reconditioned, or similar history can result in a Minnesota Prior Salvage brand.

A title or ownership document stating that the vehicle is junked, non-repairable, scrapped, dismantled, destroyed, or similarly restricted should not be assumed to provide a path to an ordinary Minnesota title.

What does Minnesota's 80% damage threshold mean?

Minnesota uses an 80% damage threshold in specific statutory situations, including self-insured owners, and separately requires written disclosure in certain sales when a seller has actual knowledge of collision or other damage exceeding 80% of the vehicle's pre-damage actual cash value.

Buyers should not assume that the 80% figure is the only way a vehicle can acquire a Salvage or Prior Salvage brand.

Insurer acquisition, vehicle age and value, existing title history, out-of-state brands, and other statutory conditions can also determine the required title treatment.

What should I check before buying a Minnesota salvage vehicle?

  • What exact title brand appears on the vehicle now?
  • Is it Salvage, Prior Salvage, Flood Damaged, Reconstructed, or something else?
  • Is the vehicle late-model under Minnesota's current definition?
  • What was its pre-damage actual cash value?
  • Does it qualify as a high-value vehicle even if it is older?
  • Why did the vehicle enter the salvage system?
  • Was it acquired by an insurer after payment of damages?
  • Is it repairable, or has it been classified as an unrepairable total loss?
  • If it is currently Salvage, does it require inspection before receiving a Prior Salvage title?
  • Can the ownership of major replacement components be documented?
  • If it came from another state, what exact title brand or ownership document does it carry?
  • Has repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, inspection, transportation, title, tax, registration, and other costs remain?

The central Minnesota question is:

What title brand does this vehicle require based on its age, value, damage history, and current ownership document, and what must happen before Minnesota will allow it to be titled and registered for ordinary road use?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Minnesota title brands provide future buyers with information about a vehicle's history. Prior Salvage generally remains on subsequent Minnesota titles once required.

Minnesota also has damage-disclosure requirements that can apply in certain transactions. When selling a vehicle with salvage history, accurately represent the existing title brand and comply with the title-transfer and damage-disclosure requirements that apply to the transaction.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from Minnesota Driver and Vehicle Services, the Minnesota Department of Public Safety, or another government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, inspections, and procedures can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate Minnesota agency.
