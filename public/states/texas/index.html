Texas Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Texas salvage, rebuilt salvage, and nonrepairable vehicle rules, the rebuilt-title path, documentation, inspections, and official state requirements.

What you should know

A Texas salvage vehicle may potentially be repaired, rebuilt, titled as Rebuilt Salvage, and registered for road use.

A nonrepairable vehicle is different. Certain nonrepairable or restrictive ownership documents prevent a vehicle from returning to normal road use.

Determine the exact title or ownership document before buying or spending money on repairs.

Understand Texas terminology

Salvage motor vehicle. Texas generally treats a vehicle as salvage when damage or missing major components make repair costs exceed its pre-damage actual cash value. A salvage vehicle cannot be operated on public roads while it remains in salvage status.

Rebuilt salvage vehicle. A vehicle that previously had salvage status can potentially be rebuilt to roadworthiness and titled with the brand Rebuilt Salvage. The brand remains part of its title history.

Nonrepairable vehicle. A Texas Nonrepairable Vehicle Title issued on or after September 1, 2003 cannot be used to rebuild, reconstruct, retitle, or register the vehicle.

Salvage does not automatically mean nonrepairable.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

It depends on the vehicle's title and ownership history. A qualifying salvage vehicle can potentially return to the road after repair, applicable inspection requirements, and issuance of a Texas title branded Rebuilt Salvage.

TxDMV identifies documents that prevent rebuilding, retitling, or registration, including certain Texas Nonrepairable Vehicle Titles, restrictive out-of-state documents, demolition-only authority documents, and certain titles marked FOR EXPORT ONLY.

  • Check the exact ownership document.
  • Determine whether it permits rebuilding and retitling.
  • Do not assume physical repairability means legal eligibility for a road title.

How do I get a salvage vehicle back on the road?

For a qualifying salvage vehicle, the general Texas path is:

  1. Establish ownership.
  2. Repair or rebuild the vehicle.
  3. Document work and applicable component parts.
  4. Complete any Texas vehicle inspection that applies.
  5. Complete Rebuilt Vehicle Statement (Form VTR-61).
  6. Complete Application for Texas Title and/or Registration (Form 130-U).
  7. Provide required ownership, identification, insurance, inspection, and supporting documents.
  8. Submit the application to the appropriate county tax office.
  9. If approved, receive a Texas title branded Rebuilt Salvage.

What inspections are required?

Texas's vehicle inspection system has changed, so current requirements should be used rather than older salvage instructions. For many non-commercial vehicles, the former annual safety inspection is no longer required as a condition of registration. Vehicles in designated emissions counties may still require emissions inspection, and commercial vehicles continue to have applicable requirements.

The rebuilt-title process can therefore depend on the vehicle type and where it will be registered.

What is the Rebuilt Vehicle Statement?

The Rebuilt Vehicle Statement (Form VTR-61) documents the work performed by the person who repaired, rebuilt, reconstructed, or assembled the vehicle. Applicable component-part information must also be documented.

Keep records while rebuilding rather than trying to reconstruct the repair history at title-application time.

What documents and forms might I need?

Important documents can include the salvage title or other acceptable ownership evidence, Form 130-U, Form VTR-61, applicable component-part documentation, government-issued identification, proof of insurance, and inspection documentation when applicable.

What about component parts used in the rebuild?

Texas places importance on documenting applicable parts used to rebuild a salvage vehicle. Keep bills of sale and other records showing where major replacement components came from.

If you are buying a vehicle someone else rebuilt, ask whether documentation for major replacement parts is available.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

Texas looks at the actual ownership document and title history. Some out-of-state documents identifying a vehicle as nonrepairable, junked, for parts, dismantling only, or otherwise prohibited from rebuilding can prevent it from being rebuilt or titled in Texas.

Do not rely only on a seller or auction description. Review the actual document before buying or beginning repairs.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in Texas?

  • What is the exact title or ownership document?
  • Does it allow the vehicle to be rebuilt and retitled?
  • Is it marked FOR EXPORT ONLY?
  • How will Texas treat an out-of-state document?
  • What repairs remain and what records are available?
  • Are major replacement components documented?
  • What inspection requirements apply where it will be registered?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What title, registration, inspection, repair, transportation, and other costs remain?

Does this particular vehicle have a legal path to a Texas road title? A vehicle can be physically repairable while still being legally ineligible for normal road use.

What should I know if I later sell the vehicle?

A rebuilt vehicle's prior salvage history does not disappear when it is sold. The Texas title identifies the vehicle with the Rebuilt Salvage brand.

Texas also has disclosure requirements associated with rebuilt motor vehicles. Verify the current requirements before a sale and make sure the buyer understands the branded-title history.

What if I rebuild multiple salvage vehicles?

Texas distinguishes occasional individual rebuilding from operating a salvage-vehicle business. TxDMV states that a person who repairs, rebuilds, or reconstructs more than five salvage vehicles in the same calendar year must obtain the applicable salvage vehicle dealer license.

Other licensing requirements can apply to regulated buying or selling activities.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the appropriate Texas government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, inspection procedures, and other rules can change, and the requirements that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate state or local agency.
