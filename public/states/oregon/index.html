Oregon Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand how Oregon classifies totaled and salvage vehicles, which title and VIN inspection requirements may apply, and where to find official state guidance.

What you should know

Oregon uses the term totaled for vehicles meeting its total-loss definition. A vehicle may be totaled because an insurer declares it a total loss or because it meets other statutory conditions. For certain uninsured losses, estimated repair costs of at least 80% of the vehicle’s pre-damage retail market value may result in a totaled classification.

A vehicle may also be considered totaled because of theft, even if it is later recovered with little or no damage. Oregon provides a separate title process for qualifying recovered stolen vehicles, and a reconstructed designation may not be required when the necessary documentation is provided.

When a vehicle is declared totaled, its title generally must be surrendered within 30 days. An eligible vehicle may receive a salvage title, but a salvage title does not authorize ordinary road use. After qualifying repairs and title procedures, a vehicle may receive a regular Oregon title carrying applicable brands such as Totaled and Reconstructed.

Oregon does not issue titles for vehicles with disqualifying junk or non-repairable classifications. Verify title history and eligibility with Oregon Driver & Motor Vehicle Services (DMV) before purchasing or rebuilding.

Understand Oregon’s title terminology

Oregon uses several classifications and title brands to describe a vehicle’s history and eligibility for title and registration.

Totaled

A vehicle meeting Oregon’s statutory total-loss definition, which can include insurer-declared losses, theft-related circumstances, and qualifying uninsured damage.

Salvage title

An ownership document for an eligible totaled or damaged vehicle that cannot be registered for ordinary road use.

Reconstructed

A designation for a vehicle meeting Oregon’s reconstructed-vehicle definition, including certain rebuilt vehicles.

Assembled

A designation for a vehicle constructed or substantially altered in a way that meets Oregon’s assembled-vehicle definition.

Junk / non-repairable

A disqualifying classification, such as junk, scrap, or destroyed, that generally prevents Oregon from issuing a title.

Flood

An out-of-state flood brand may carry forward. Oregon does not issue its own separate flood brand.

A vehicle may carry multiple brands. A previously totaled vehicle that has been reconstructed may display both designations.

Can a salvage vehicle return to the road?

An Oregon salvage title does not authorize ordinary road use. When a salvage title is issued, existing registration is canceled. An eligible vehicle must satisfy the applicable title and registration requirements before returning to ordinary road use.

A valid trip permit may authorize limited operation under its conditions, but it does not replace regular title and registration requirements. Vehicles with disqualifying junk or non-repairable classifications generally cannot be titled in Oregon unless that classification is removed by the issuing jurisdiction.

How do I get an eligible salvage vehicle back on the road?

For a vehicle that requires reconstruction, the general process is:

  1. Confirm the vehicle’s title status and eligibility for an Oregon title.
  2. Complete necessary repairs and retain ownership records and replacement-part documentation.
  3. Gather the existing title or salvage title, ownership releases, and required evidence for major parts and the frame.
  4. Complete the Assembled, Reconstructed or Replica Certification when applicable.
  5. Obtain the required vehicle identification number (VIN) inspection and any additional verification requested by DMV.
  6. Apply for the appropriate Oregon title with supporting documents and fees.
  7. Complete registration and any applicable emissions or other road-use requirements.

Not every totaled vehicle requires reconstruction. For example, a qualifying stolen vehicle recovered with little or no damage may follow a different title process. Confirm the procedure that applies to the specific vehicle with DMV.

Vehicle identification number (VIN) inspections

Oregon generally requires a VIN inspection when an eligible salvage-titled vehicle is being titled for regular use.

A VIN inspection verifies the vehicle’s identity and helps confirm that its identification information matches the ownership documentation. It is not a comprehensive mechanical or safety inspection and does not certify that the vehicle is roadworthy.

Depending on the vehicle’s history and documentation, Oregon DMV may require additional verification or refer the vehicle to Oregon State Police.

Keep original receipts and ownership documents for replacement parts and repairs. Oregon may require original releases or bills of sale for major components used in a reconstructed vehicle. These documents help establish the source and ownership of replacement parts.

The Assembled, Reconstructed or Replica Certification is an application-related certification, not proof that a comprehensive roadworthiness inspection has been passed.

What documents and forms might I need?

The exact requirements depend on the vehicle and transaction. Common items include:

  • Application for Title and Registration.
  • Existing title, salvage title, or other acceptable proof of ownership.
  • Assembled, Reconstructed or Replica Certification, when applicable.
  • Original releases or bills of sale for major replacement parts.
  • Title, Manufacturer’s Certificate of Origin, or other acceptable evidence for the frame.
  • VIN inspection documentation and any additional verification requested.
  • Prior-owner and lienholder releases, when applicable.
  • Odometer disclosure, when required.
  • Applicable title, registration, and inspection fees.

Oregon also provides a separate Application for Salvage Title when that transaction is required. Check the current DMV instructions and fees rather than assuming every totaled vehicle needs a separate salvage-title application.

Where can I get a VIN inspection?

Oregon DMV performs VIN inspections at offices offering that service. Some inspections may require an appointment. If DMV refers the vehicle to Oregon State Police for additional verification, follow the referral instructions.

Before arranging an inspection, confirm the location, appointment requirements, required ownership documents and certifications, applicable fees, and whether the vehicle must be transported or can be operated under an authorized permit.

What if the salvage vehicle came from another state?

Oregon generally requires a VIN inspection when an out-of-state vehicle is first titled in Oregon. Salvage and rebuilt vehicles may require additional ownership, title-history, and component documentation.

Oregon recognizes applicable title brands from other states, but a vehicle carrying a disqualifying junk, non-repairable, destroyed, or similar classification may not qualify for an Oregon title.

Before buying, confirm that Oregon will accept the existing title, which brands will be carried forward, and whether reconstructed-vehicle certification or other verification is required.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in Oregon?

  • Verify the title and any Totaled, Reconstructed, Flood, or other brands.
  • Confirm the vehicle is eligible for an Oregon title and does not have a disqualifying non-repairable classification.
  • Investigate the damage or theft history and consider an independent mechanical and structural inspection.
  • Review repair records, photographs, original parts receipts, and component ownership documentation.
  • Determine which title, VIN inspection, and registration requirements apply.
  • Confirm that the vehicle can be insured and registered for the intended use.
  • Estimate repair, transportation, inspection, title, registration, and unexpected costs.

Oregon does not issue its own separate flood brand. The absence of that brand does not guarantee that a vehicle has never sustained water damage. A branded title also does not certify the quality of repairs or roadworthiness.

What should I know if I later sell the vehicle?

Complete the applicable Oregon ownership-transfer procedures and accurately represent the vehicle’s title classification and history. A reconstructed or totaled designation may remain part of the title record.

If a vehicle already has an Oregon reconstructed title and is being transferred without another qualifying change, the buyer generally does not need to repeat the original reconstruction certification solely because ownership changes.

Oregon generally requires buyers to apply for a title within 30 days of the sale.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from Oregon Driver & Motor Vehicle Services or another appropriate government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, inspection procedures, and other rules can change, and the requirements that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate state agency.
