Illinois Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Illinois salvage and rebuilt titles, licensed rebuilder requirements, IDOT safety testing, Secretary of State Police inspections, and whether a vehicle can legally return to the road.

What you should know

A vehicle with an Illinois salvage certificate cannot simply be repaired by its owner and then converted to a normal road title.

Illinois requires a licensed rebuilder to bring a vehicle out of salvage. If you are not a licensed rebuilder, you must contract with one to obtain a rebuilt title.

Salvage vehicles generally cannot legally be driven on Illinois roads and do not qualify for ordinary license plates while they remain in salvage status. Limited permits or qualifying special plates can be used for inspection-related travel.

Before the rebuilt-title application is completed, the vehicle must pass the applicable IDOT safety test. Vehicles eight model years old or newer generally also require a separate inspection by the Illinois Secretary of State Police before a rebuilt title can be issued. Flood history can affect that inspection requirement.

A vehicle that has been declared junked cannot be rebuilt for road use.

Understand Illinois terminology

Salvage vehicle and salvage certificate

A salvage certificate documents a vehicle's salvage status. While a vehicle remains salvage, it generally cannot be legally driven or operated on Illinois roads and highways.

Illinois also restricts who can receive an assigned salvage certificate. For someone who wants the vehicle returned to road use, the licensed-rebuilder requirement is particularly important.

Rebuilt vehicle

A rebuilt vehicle is a vehicle for which a salvage certificate was issued and that subsequently has been returned to operating condition through the legally required rebuilding process. When Illinois issues a new certificate of title for a vehicle that previously had a salvage certificate, the title generally carries the notation REBUILT, subject to limited statutory exceptions.

Junking certificate

A junking certificate is fundamentally different from a salvage certificate. A junked vehicle cannot later be rebuilt for road use. If you intend to return a damaged vehicle to the road, determine whether the ownership document is a salvage certificate or a junking certificate before you buy.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Possibly. A qualifying salvage vehicle can potentially be rebuilt and issued an Illinois REBUILT title.

But two questions should be answered before investing in the vehicle:

Does this vehicle's ownership document allow it to return to road use?

Who will perform or take responsibility for the rebuild under Illinois law?

If the vehicle has been junked, the road-title path is closed. If it is salvage, Illinois requires a licensed rebuilder to bring it out of salvage.

Do I need to be a licensed rebuilder?

You do not necessarily need to personally hold a rebuilder license to own a vehicle that will ultimately receive a rebuilt title. However, Illinois requires a licensed rebuilder to bring the vehicle out of salvage.

If the applicant is not a licensed rebuilder, the Illinois Secretary of State's rebuilt-title guidance requires the applicant to contract with a licensed rebuilder. That makes the rebuilder relationship something to establish before buying a salvage vehicle as a project.

How do I get a salvage vehicle back on the road?

  1. Confirm that the vehicle has a salvage ownership document rather than a junking document.
  2. Establish who the licensed rebuilder will be.
  3. If you are not the licensed rebuilder, complete the required salvage/rebuilding contract.
  4. Rebuild the vehicle.
  5. Keep documentation for essential parts used during the rebuild.
  6. Complete the required salvage affirmation identifying replaced essential parts.
  7. Take the rebuilt vehicle to an Illinois Department of Transportation Safety Testing Lane and pass the applicable safety test.
  8. If the vehicle is subject to the Secretary of State Police inspection requirement, schedule and complete that inspection.
  9. Provide the required salvage certificate, rebuilt-title application, parts documentation, safety-test documentation, and other supporting documents.
  10. Submit the rebuilt-title application and applicable tax documentation and fees.
  11. If approved, receive an Illinois certificate of title carrying the REBUILT notation.

Why are there two inspections?

Illinois separates two different questions.

IDOT safety test

Before being brought to a Secretary of State Police salvage inspection when that inspection applies, the rebuilt vehicle must first pass the applicable Illinois Department of Transportation safety test. The IDOT vehicle inspection report is part of the rebuilt-title documentation.

Secretary of State Police salvage inspection

For vehicles subject to this requirement, the Illinois Secretary of State Police performs a separate salvage inspection. It examines the vehicle, its parts, and its documentation to determine that identification numbers have not been altered or tampered with, information in the title application is correct, and there are no indications that the vehicle or its parts have been stolen.

The Secretary of State's salvage-inspection guidance expressly states that these inspections are not intended to certify the safety or roadworthiness of the vehicle or the quality of the rebuilding work.

Which vehicles need the Secretary of State Police inspection?

Vehicle age matters. Illinois law generally requires the Secretary of State inspection for a rebuilt vehicle that is eight model years old or newer. Vehicles more than eight model years old generally are not required to complete that inspection before a rebuilt title is issued.

Flood history makes the rule more nuanced. Illinois law separately requires the Section 3-308 inspection for flood vehicles that sustained damage greater than 50% of their fair market value, while the Secretary of State's current rebuilt-title fact sheet identifies an exception to its general eight-model-year inspection instruction when the vehicle was salvaged due to flood damage only.

Because vehicle age, flood history, and the vehicle's title record can affect the inspection path, verify the current requirement for the particular vehicle rather than assuming that every Illinois rebuilt vehicle follows exactly the same sequence.

What does the Secretary of State Police salvage inspection establish?

The inspection focuses heavily on vehicle identity, parts identity, ownership documentation, and theft prevention.

Among other things, the inspector checks whether identifying numbers have been altered or tampered with, whether information in the title application is correct, and whether there are indications that the vehicle or any of its parts were stolen.

Passing this inspection does not mean that the Secretary of State Police is certifying the vehicle's roadworthiness or guaranteeing the quality of the repairs.

Why are parts receipts important?

Illinois requires documentation identifying essential parts replaced during the rebuild.

The rebuilt-title documentation includes an Affirmation Supporting Salvage Certificate (RT 11) identifying essential parts replaced on the rebuilt vehicle. If essential parts were replaced by the Illinois rebuilder, receipts or invoices are required.

For vehicles subject to the Secretary of State Police salvage inspection, the inspection documentation includes original uniform invoices for essential parts that were changed. Keep this paper trail during the rebuild rather than trying to reconstruct it at the end.

Can I drive a salvage vehicle to an inspection?

Not as a general rule. Illinois law provides that a salvage or junk vehicle may not be driven or operated on Illinois roads and highways.

There are limited exceptions for inspection-related travel. A qualifying salvage vehicle displaying valid special plates may be driven to or from the required inspection, and Illinois law authorizes a short-term permit for qualifying inspection-related travel.

Do not treat those exceptions as permission for ordinary road use while the vehicle remains salvage. Verify the current permit, plate, registration, insurance, and inspection requirements before driving the vehicle.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

The actual ownership document matters. Illinois's rebuilt-title law expressly addresses vehicles that were previously titled as salvage in Illinois or another jurisdiction.

If the vehicle comes from a state that does not issue salvage titles, Illinois's current rebuilt-title guidance calls for a bill of sale. If the originating state requires a salvage title but the applicant did not receive one from the seller, the Secretary of State directs the applicant to the state's bonded-title procedures.

  • Inspect the actual ownership document.
  • Determine whether the vehicle is salvage, rebuilt, junk, or subject to another restriction.
  • Confirm that Illinois will accept the ownership document.
  • Determine which inspection requirements apply.
  • Identify whether a licensed Illinois rebuilder must become involved.

Do not rely solely on the auction description.

What about flood vehicles?

Flood history can change the Illinois inspection requirements.

Illinois law specifically addresses flood vehicles that sustained damage greater than 50% of fair market value and requires the Secretary of State inspection in circumstances defined by the Vehicle Code.

At the same time, the Secretary of State's rebuilt-title instructions identify a flood-damage-only exception from the general inspection statement for vehicles eight model years old or newer. The interaction between the vehicle's age, title brand, and extent of flood damage therefore matters.

If a vehicle has flood history, do not rely solely on its age to determine the inspection path. Verify the current requirements for that particular title history.

Completing Illinois's title process also does not establish that a flood-damaged vehicle is free from corrosion, electrical problems, contamination, or other hidden damage.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in Illinois?

  • What is the exact ownership document?
  • Is it a salvage certificate or a junking certificate?
  • Can the document legally be assigned to the intended purchaser?
  • Who will be the licensed rebuilder?
  • If you are not licensed, have you confirmed that a licensed rebuilder will take the project?
  • How old is the vehicle for purposes of Illinois's inspection rules?
  • Does it have flood history?
  • What essential parts need replacement?
  • Have any essential parts already been replaced?
  • Are the required receipts and source-vehicle information available?
  • Are the VIN and other identifying numbers intact?
  • Has the vehicle passed the required IDOT safety test?
  • Does it require a Secretary of State Police salvage inspection?
  • How will the vehicle legally be transported to required inspections?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, rebuilder, inspection, title, tax, registration, and transportation costs remain?
  • Has the quality of the completed repairs been independently evaluated?

For Illinois, the key question is not simply Can this vehicle be repaired?

Does this vehicle have a legal path out of salvage, who will serve as the licensed rebuilder, and can the vehicle and its parts satisfy Illinois's documentation and inspection requirements?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Illinois has a specific rebuilt-vehicle disclosure requirement. State law provides that a person may not knowingly, with intent to defraud or deceive another, sell a vehicle for which a rebuilt title has been issued unless the vehicle is accompanied by a properly signed Disclosure of Rebuilt Vehicle Status form delivered to the buyer.

The Illinois Secretary of State publishes the disclosure form as SOS DOP 115. It identifies the vehicle as carrying a certificate of title showing it to be a REBUILT VEHICLE and includes acknowledgements for the purchaser and seller.

If you later sell an Illinois rebuilt vehicle, do not treat its prior salvage history as something that disappears after the rebuilt title is issued. Verify the current disclosure and transfer requirements for your transaction.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Illinois Secretary of State, Illinois Secretary of State Police, Illinois Department of Transportation, or another appropriate government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, inspection procedures, licensing rules, and other requirements can change and can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate government agency.
