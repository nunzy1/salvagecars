Nevada Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Nevada Salvage Titles, Rebuilt titles, the 65% total-loss threshold, restoration authorization, safety inspections, owner-retained total losses, and what must happen before an eligible salvage vehicle can return to the road.

What you should know

Nevada generally considers a vehicle a total loss when the cost of qualifying repairs is 65% or more of its fair market value immediately before the damage. Certain expenses are excluded from the calculation.

A salvage vehicle receives an orange-colored Salvage Title. That document does not authorize ordinary highway registration or operation.

For salvage vehicles five model years old or newer, the owner must obtain DMV authorization before beginning restoration.

After repairs, an authorized Nevada repair business must certify the vehicle, and the DMV must complete its required inspection.

An eligible vehicle that completes the process receives a title branded Rebuilt. The brand remains on future titles.

A vehicle legally designated Nonrepairable cannot be restored for highway operation in Nevada.

Understand Nevada terminology

Salvage Vehicle

A vehicle declared a total loss, flood-damaged, or nonrepairable, or assigned a salvage or similar designation. Qualifying out-of-state title history is also relevant.

Total Loss Vehicle

Generally, a vehicle whose qualifying repair costs are 65% or more of its pre-damage fair market value. Nevada excludes painting, certain electronic-component replacement costs, and towing from the calculation. Statutory exceptions also apply.

Salvage Title

An orange-colored ownership document for a salvage vehicle. It does not itself authorize ordinary highway use. Nevada uses Application for Salvage Title or Non-Repairable Vehicle Certificate (VP 213).

Rebuilt Vehicle

A vehicle that has completed applicable restoration, inspection, and retitling requirements. The Rebuilt brand remains on future titles.

Nonrepairable Vehicle

A vehicle legally designated for permanent removal from highway use. Examples may include vehicles that have value only as parts or scrap metal, vehicles designated for dismantling, and vehicles that cannot be restored for highway operation. Nevada issues a Non-Repairable Vehicle Certificate rather than a Salvage Title.

Flood-Damaged Vehicle

Nevada generally considers a vehicle flood-damaged when water rises above the door sill, enters a passenger, trunk, or engine compartment, and contacts the electrical system. A vehicle also qualifies as flood-damaged if an insurance company declares it a total loss because of water damage.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, if it is eligible for restoration and is not legally designated nonrepairable.

  • Obtain the proper Salvage Title and confirm eligibility for restoration.
  • Obtain pre-repair authorization if the vehicle is five model years old or newer.
  • Complete repairs and obtain certification from an authorized Nevada repair business.
  • Complete the required DMV inspection.
  • Obtain a title branded Rebuilt and complete registration before ordinary highway use.

A vehicle with a Nevada Non-Repairable Vehicle Certificate cannot be restored for highway operation. Verify the exact ownership document before buying.

How do I get a Nevada salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible salvage vehicle, the general process is:

  1. Obtain the Salvage Title or other acceptable ownership document.
  2. Confirm eligibility for restoration and retitling.
  3. If the vehicle is five model years old or newer, obtain DMV Authorization for Vehicle Restoration (VP 209) before repairs begin.
  4. Repair the vehicle to applicable industry and safety standards.
  5. Retain repair records and identifying information for replacement components.
  6. Have a Nevada-registered garage, licensed body shop, or licensed rebuilder complete the required repair certification on Certificate of Inspection/Affidavit of Construction (VP 064).
  7. Bring the vehicle to a Nevada DMV inspection station for its required inspection and completion of the form.
  8. Submit the title, inspection documents, restoration authorization when applicable, and other required documents.
  9. Pay applicable fees, obtain a title branded Rebuilt, and complete registration before ordinary highway use.

Different forms may apply to motorcycles and trailers. Do not start repairs on a qualifying newer salvage vehicle before obtaining required authorization.

What inspections and verification are required?

Pre-repair authorization

For salvage vehicles five model years old or newer, DMV authorization using VP 209 is required before repairs begin. Vehicles six model years old or older do not need this particular authorization.

Repair certification

After repairs, a Nevada-registered garage, licensed body shop, or licensed rebuilder must complete the applicable certification, generally VP 064 for passenger vehicles. The certification addresses repair standards and required safety equipment.

DMV inspection

The DMV verifies vehicle identity and completes the required inspection and documentation. Replacement component identification may also be involved.

Limits of the inspections

The required inspections and certifications are not a guarantee against hidden defects, poor repairs, or future reliability problems. An independent evaluation remains important.

Does completing Nevada’s rebuilding requirements mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No. A Nevada Rebuilt title indicates that the required process was completed; it does not guarantee the absence of hidden damage or future problems.

Consider an independent evaluation of:

  • Structural repairs
  • Frame or unibody alignment
  • Airbags and restraint systems
  • Brakes, steering, and suspension
  • Electrical systems
  • Water or flood damage
  • Mechanical condition
  • Wheel alignment and tire wear
  • Rust, corrosion, and evidence of poor-quality body repairs
  • Replacement component quality

Ask what was damaged, who performed the repairs, and what documentation is available.

The important question is not simply:

Did the vehicle complete Nevada’s rebuilding inspections?

It is also:

What was damaged, how was it repaired, and what condition is the vehicle in now?

What repair and parts documentation should I keep?

Nevada requires qualifying repair businesses to maintain repair records, including identifying information for parts and equipment. Owners should retain documents relevant to restoration and retitling.

  • Original Salvage Title
  • Authorization for Vehicle Restoration (VP 209), if required
  • Original damage assessments and insurance records
  • Photographs before and during repairs
  • Receipts and donor-vehicle information for replacement parts
  • Repair invoices and safety-equipment records
  • Certificate of Inspection/Affidavit of Construction (VP 064)
  • DMV inspection documentation
  • Final Rebuilt title

Not every item is necessarily a separate submission requirement for every vehicle. Ask for documentation before purchasing a partially repaired vehicle.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company declares it a total loss?

Keeping the vehicle after an insurance total-loss settlement does not eliminate Nevada’s salvage-document requirements. Nevada procedures address insurer preparation and submission of the required salvage application and ownership documents.

Before rebuilding or selling, confirm:

  • Whether the insurer has completed the salvage-title application
  • Whether the existing title must be surrendered
  • Whether a lienholder controls the ownership document
  • Whether the vehicle is salvage or nonrepairable
  • Whether pre-repair authorization and inspections are required
  • Which title and registration steps remain

An owner-retained salvage vehicle cannot be sold or transferred before the required Salvage Title has been issued. Coordinate with the insurer and any lienholder.

Is there an age exemption from Nevada’s salvage process?

Older vehicles and the total-loss definition

For vehicles 10 model years old or older, Nevada provides an exception to its total-loss definition. If the vehicle needs only certain body components replaced, it is not considered a total-loss vehicle under the 65% rule, regardless of the cost of those replacements.

The law identifies specific components and combinations, including the hood, trunk lid, fenders, doors, grille assemblies, bumpers, and lighting assemblies.

This exception does not apply to every older damaged vehicle. A vehicle requiring other repairs may still qualify as a total loss.

Newer vehicles and restoration authorization

Salvage vehicles five model years old or newer require DMV authorization before restoration begins. This particular authorization does not apply to vehicles six model years old or older, but older salvage vehicles may still require a Salvage Title, repair certification, DMV inspection, and Rebuilt title.

Do not assume an older vehicle is exempt from Nevada’s salvage requirements solely because of its age.

What about flood-damaged vehicles?

Nevada generally considers a vehicle flood-damaged when water rises above the door sill, enters a passenger, trunk, or engine compartment, and contacts the electrical system. A vehicle also qualifies as flood-damaged if an insurance company declares it a total loss because of water damage.

Water damage can remain hidden after cleaning and cosmetic repairs. Investigate:

  • Electrical wiring and connectors
  • Electronic control modules
  • Airbags and restraint systems
  • Rust and corrosion
  • Interior materials and carpeting
  • Mechanical components
  • Mold or contamination
  • Evidence of previous water intrusion

Ask about salvage, flood, rebuilt, or other title designations. Vehicle-history reports can help but may not reveal every incident.

What if the vehicle comes from another state?

Nevada may accept properly endorsed out-of-state salvage documents with a complete chain of ownership. However, Nevada distinguishes vehicles eligible for restoration from those with designations prohibiting rebuilding for highway use.

Nevada will not issue a Salvage Title for a vehicle that has or previously had an out-of-state designation indicating that it cannot or should not be repaired for public-road use.

Before buying, verify the exact ownership document, prior title brands, eligibility for restoration, pre-repair authorization requirements, and available repair and inspection records.

Transferring a vehicle between states does not remove its salvage history.

What should I check before buying a Nevada salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Is it an orange Salvage Title, Rebuilt title, or Non-Repairable Vehicle Certificate?
  • Am I legally permitted to buy it in its current status?
  • Why was it declared salvage, and was there an insurance total-loss settlement?
  • Is it eligible for restoration and ordinary road registration?
  • If five model years old or newer, was DMV authorization obtained before repairs?
  • What repairs were completed, by whom, and with what documentation?
  • Were the professional certification and DMV inspection completed?
  • Is there flood damage or out-of-state title history?
  • Has an independent mechanic or body specialist evaluated the vehicle?
  • Are there liens, insurance or financing restrictions, or unresolved costs?

For an unrepaired vehicle, ask:

Can I legally purchase this vehicle, and can it be restored, inspected, titled, and registered for ordinary road use in Nevada?

For a repaired vehicle, ask:

Can I verify its title history, repair quality, and completion of Nevada’s required inspections before buying it?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Nevada requires written disclosure of known or reasonably knowable salvage, rebuilt, or reconstructed status before transfer. An orange Salvage Title does not authorize an unrestricted private sale; sales of unrepaired salvage vehicles are restricted to specified licensed businesses and other permitted recipients.

After restoration, the Rebuilt brand remains on later titles. Before selling:

  • Confirm the correct ownership document and the buyer’s legal eligibility
  • Disclose salvage or rebuilt status in writing
  • Properly assign the title and resolve liens
  • Provide available repair and inspection records
  • Explain whether restoration and retitling are complete

A nonrepairable vehicle cannot be sold as eligible for ordinary highway restoration.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Nevada Department of Motor Vehicles or another government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, and procedures can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate Nevada agency.
