West Virginia Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Learn how West Virginia classifies salvage vehicles, what inspections are required for a Reconstructed title, and what to consider before buying or repairing a salvage vehicle.

What you should know

West Virginia generally considers a vehicle a total loss when it has sustained damage equal to 75% or more of its market value, as determined using a nationally accepted used-car valuation guide.

A vehicle also meets the state’s total-loss definition when it qualifies as flood-damaged under West Virginia law. A flood-damaged vehicle is one submerged in water to the extent that water entered its passenger compartment or trunk.

West Virginia distinguishes among repairable salvage vehicles, vehicles declared cosmetic total losses, and nonrepairable vehicles. These classifications affect the type of ownership document issued and whether the vehicle can return to road use.

A repairable total-loss vehicle generally receives a Salvage Certificate. After repairs, it must pass both an official state safety inspection and a West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV) reconstructed-vehicle examination before receiving a title branded Reconstructed.

West Virginia has a separate procedure for exclusively cosmetic total losses. If no repairs are needed for safe and lawful operation, an eligible vehicle may receive a Cosmetic Total Loss title without the ordinary salvage reconstruction process. Flood- and fire-damaged vehicles do not qualify for this classification.

A nonrepairable vehicle cannot return to ordinary road use through the reconstructed-title process.

Before buying, rebuilding, or transferring a salvage vehicle, verify its title status and requirements with the West Virginia DMV.

Understand West Virginia’s title terminology

Salvage Certificate: An ownership document for a repairable total-loss vehicle; it does not authorize ordinary registration or highway operation.

Reconstructed: A title brand for a previously totaled vehicle rebuilt under applicable requirements. It may also apply to vehicles substantially altered through changes to essential components.

Cosmetic Total Loss: A title brand for a vehicle totaled solely because of cosmetic damage that does not require repair for safe and lawful operation.

Cosmetic Total Loss Salvage Certificate: An ownership document issued when an insurer retains a vehicle classified as a cosmetic total loss.

Nonrepairable Motor Vehicle Certificate: A document for a totaled vehicle that cannot be safely operated and has no resale value other than parts or scrap.

Flood: A designation for qualifying flood damage, including water entering the passenger or trunk compartment.

Fire: A designation for applicable fire-damage history.

A vehicle’s applicable damage-related title brands remain part of its title history even after it has been repaired.

For more information, see West Virginia Code § 17A-4-10.

Can a salvage vehicle return to the road?

A West Virginia Salvage Certificate does not authorize a vehicle to be registered or operated on public roads.

An eligible salvage vehicle must be repaired and pass both the state safety inspection and DMV reconstructed-vehicle examination. The owner must obtain a Reconstructed title and satisfy registration and insurance requirements before returning to ordinary road use.

Nonrepairable vehicles cannot return to highway operation through this process.

A Cosmetic Total Loss title is different from a Salvage Certificate. Under the applicable statutory conditions, a vehicle with exclusively cosmetic damage may remain eligible for road use without the reconstructed-vehicle process.

If a salvage vehicle is not authorized for road use, arrange to have it transported rather than driving it, unless an appropriate permit authorizes the trip.

How do I get an eligible salvage vehicle back on the road?

West Virginia requires a Salvage Certificate before reconstructing a salvage vehicle. The process generally includes the following steps:

  1. Confirm the vehicle’s classification. Verify that it is repairable salvage rather than nonrepairable or subject to another title procedure.
  2. Obtain a West Virginia Salvage Certificate. Submit the existing ownership document and the required salvage application to DMV unless the vehicle already has a West Virginia Salvage Certificate.
  3. Photograph the vehicle before repairs. Take and retain photographs showing the front, rear, and both sides.
  4. Complete the repairs. Restore the vehicle to operating condition. Keep receipts for parts, materials, equipment, and labor, and record the source VIN for major components from another vehicle.
  5. Obtain a state safety inspection. Have the repaired vehicle inspected at an official station licensed by the West Virginia State Police.
  6. Complete the DMV reconstructed-vehicle examination. Present the vehicle, Salvage Certificate, before-repair photographs, and reconstruction records to a DMV inspector.
  7. Prepare the title application. Obtain the Reconstructed Vehicle Examination form and Certificate of Inspection, along with the assigned Salvage Certificate and other documents.
  8. Apply for a Reconstructed title. Submit the documentation to DMV and satisfy applicable title and tax requirements.
  9. Complete registration and insurance requirements. Once properly titled and registered, the vehicle may return to ordinary road use.

West Virginia’s reconstruction instructions require retaining before-repair photographs and reconstruction records for at least three years.

For current instructions, see West Virginia DMV — Titles, under Salvage & Reconstructed Vehicles.

Reconstructed vehicle inspections

West Virginia requires two separate inspections before an eligible salvage vehicle can receive a Reconstructed title.

State safety inspection: After repairs, the vehicle must pass an inspection at an official station licensed by the West Virginia State Police. The station completes the Certificate of Inspection (Form DMV-202-TR).

DMV reconstructed-vehicle examination: After the safety inspection, a DMV reconstructed-vehicle inspector examines the vehicle and reviews its Salvage Certificate, before-repair photographs, and reconstruction records.

The DMV examination helps verify the vehicle’s identity, reconstruction, and supporting ownership documentation. It does not replace the safety inspection.

West Virginia generally requires routine safety inspections every 24 months for registered vehicles. That ongoing requirement is separate from the inspections needed for a Reconstructed title.

What documents and forms may be required?

Depending on the vehicle’s ownership history and repairs, documents may include:

  • DMV-SV-1 — Salvage Certificate Application, when obtaining the Salvage Certificate.
  • Existing title or other acceptable ownership documentation.
  • West Virginia Salvage Certificate, assigned when applicable.
  • Before-repair photographs showing the front, rear, and both sides.
  • Receipts and invoices for replacement parts, equipment, materials, and labor.
  • VIN information for source vehicles supplying major components.
  • DMV-202-TR — Certificate of Inspection from the official safety inspection station.
  • DMV-SV-3 — Reconstructed Vehicle Examination, provided by the DMV inspector.
  • Applicable title application and odometer disclosure documentation.
  • Lien releases or other ownership records, when required.

DMV identifies Form DMV-SV-3 as a multipart form provided by the certified reconstructed-vehicle inspector during the examination. You do not need to download this form.

For current forms and instructions, see West Virginia DMV — Forms and West Virginia DMV — Titles.

Where do I arrange an inspection?

For the safety inspection, take the repaired vehicle to an official inspection station licensed by the West Virginia State Police. The West Virginia DMV Vehicle Inspection page provides information about approved stations.

For the reconstructed-vehicle examination, consult the official examination locations and schedules linked from the West Virginia DMV Titles page. Confirm availability and documentation requirements before bringing the vehicle.

A salvage vehicle may be driven to an inspection location only when an appropriate one-trip permit has been obtained from the West Virginia State Police. Otherwise, it must be towed or hauled.

Because the safety inspection and DMV examination are separate requirements, confirm the order and arrangements for both before transporting the vehicle.

What if the salvage vehicle came from another state?

West Virginia recognizes salvage, reconstructed, flood, fire, cosmetic total loss, and comparable title brands issued by other jurisdictions.

A previously branded vehicle must carry the applicable designation when titled in West Virginia. The brand may use West Virginia terminology, but the relevant damage history is preserved.

If a vehicle previously received a branded title in West Virginia, that brand must be restored when the vehicle is titled in West Virginia again, even if its out-of-state title no longer shows the brand.

Before bringing an out-of-state salvage or rebuilt vehicle into West Virginia, confirm:

  • Whether its existing title is salvage, rebuilt, reconstructed, nonrepairable, or carries another damage designation.
  • Whether West Virginia will issue a Salvage Certificate or an appropriately branded title.
  • Whether the vehicle is eligible for registration and highway operation.
  • Whether West Virginia’s reconstructed-vehicle inspection process is required.
  • Whether ownership and major-component documentation is sufficient.
  • Whether liens or title discrepancies must be resolved.

A rebuilt designation from another state does not automatically satisfy every West Virginia titling or inspection requirement.

For more information, see West Virginia Code § 17A-4-10.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in West Virginia?

A salvage or reconstructed vehicle may cost less than a comparable vehicle with an unbranded title, but its damage history and classification can affect safety, registration, insurance, and resale value.

Before purchasing, consider:

  • Verify the title. Determine whether it has a Salvage Certificate, Reconstructed title, Cosmetic Total Loss title, or Nonrepairable Motor Vehicle Certificate.
  • Confirm eligibility. Make sure the vehicle can be titled and registered for its intended use.
  • Investigate the damage. Review collision, flood, fire, or other damage records.
  • Review photographs and receipts. Ask for before-repair photographs and major-component documentation.
  • Arrange an independent inspection. Consider a mechanic or collision repair specialist, especially after structural or water damage.
  • Check for flood damage. West Virginia’s definition includes water entering the passenger or trunk compartment.
  • Verify inspection requirements. Determine whether both required inspections have been completed.
  • Confirm insurance availability. Check whether the vehicle can be insured for the coverage needed.
  • Estimate the full cost. Include repairs, transportation, inspections, titling, registration, and unexpected expenses.

A Reconstructed title indicates that the vehicle completed the applicable titling process. It does not guarantee the quality of every repair or replace an independent inspection.

What should I know if I later sell the vehicle?

West Virginia requires applicable salvage and damage-related title brands to remain associated with a vehicle when ownership changes.

When a repairable vehicle is declared a total loss, the insurer generally obtains the existing title and arranges for a Salvage Certificate. An owner may retain the vehicle, in which case the certificate may be issued in the owner’s name.

When a vehicle with a Salvage Certificate is sold, the seller must complete the ownership assignment and provide the certificate to the buyer.

A vehicle that completes reconstruction receives a title identifying it as Reconstructed. Its applicable brand remains part of its title history after transfer.

Owners must comply with branding requirements for totaled vehicles even if they do not receive an insurance claim settlement.

Before a sale, the seller should provide the assigned ownership document, disclose known title status, and make available relevant repair and inspection records.

For more information, see West Virginia Code § 17A-4-10.
