Idaho Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Idaho Salvage Certificates, Rebuilt Salvage titles, repair documentation, owner-retained total losses, out-of-state vehicles, and what must happen before an eligible salvage vehicle can return to the road.

What you should know

Idaho requires a Salvage Certificate of Title for vehicles that meet its legal definition of a salvage vehicle.

Unlike states using a fixed percentage threshold, Idaho generally considers whether damage makes a vehicle uneconomical to repair or rebuild, whether it has been declared salvage, or whether an insurer has made a total-loss settlement.

A Salvage Certificate replaces the previous ownership document. It establishes ownership but does not authorize registration or ordinary highway use.

An eligible vehicle may return to the road after repair to meet applicable equipment requirements and completion of the title and registration process.

The new title is branded Rebuilt Salvage, and the brand remains for the life of the vehicle.

Idaho requires a Rebuilt Salvage Vehicle Statement (ITD 3311) describing repairs and who performed them.

Understand Idaho terminology

Salvage Vehicle

Idaho's definition includes a vehicle that:

  • Has a Salvage Certificate, salvage bill of sale, or other document showing it was declared salvage.
  • Has damage that the owner, insurer, or another person acting for the owner determines makes it uneconomical to repair or rebuild, considering repair costs and salvage value.
  • Has been the subject of an insurance payment or other settlement compensating the owner for a total loss.

A vehicle does not have to exceed a certain percentage of its market value to meet this definition.

Review Idaho's salvage definitions

Salvage Certificate of Title

A Salvage Certificate is the ownership document for a salvage vehicle and replaces the previous title or manufacturer’s certificate of origin. It may be assigned to transfer ownership but cannot be used to register the vehicle for ordinary road use. It is surrendered when applying for a branded title after repairs.

Review the Salvage Certificate application

Rebuilt Salvage Vehicle

A vehicle previously declared salvage that has been rebuilt or repaired using like-make-and-model parts so it visually resembles a vehicle originally constructed by its manufacturer. Idaho issues a title branded Rebuilt Salvage, which remains on subsequent Idaho titles.

Total Loss Vehicle

Idaho defines a total-loss vehicle based on whether repair is uneconomical. A qualifying insurance settlement for damage can trigger salvage requirements. Payments solely for medical care, bodily injury, rental vehicles, or other expenses unrelated to vehicle damage are not themselves total-loss compensation under this definition.

Owner-Retained Salvage

A vehicle kept by the titled owner after an insurance total-loss settlement. Keeping it does not eliminate the Salvage Certificate requirement.

Theft Recovery

A vehicle acquired by an insurer through a theft settlement generally receives a Salvage Certificate. If later recovered without damage sufficient to qualify as salvage, Idaho provides a process that can result in a Theft Recovery notation rather than a Rebuilt Salvage brand based on the theft incident.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially. An eligible salvage vehicle can return to highway use after meeting Idaho equipment requirements and completing the branded-title and registration process. A Salvage Certificate alone does not authorize registration.

Before buying, determine:

  • Which ownership document the vehicle has.
  • Whether it is salvage, rebuilt salvage, or designated junk or parts only.
  • Whether it can legally be rebuilt for highway use.
  • What repairs remain and whether ITD 3311 can be completed accurately.
  • Whether an out-of-state VIN inspection is required.
  • Whether Idaho will issue a Rebuilt Salvage title and registration.

Vehicles designated junk, destroyed, dismantled, or parts only may be ineligible for rebuilding and road registration.

How do I get an Idaho salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible vehicle, the general process is:

  1. Obtain the Salvage Certificate or other acceptable salvage ownership document.
  2. Verify rebuilding eligibility.
  3. Repair or rebuild the vehicle to meet applicable federal motor vehicle safety standards and Idaho equipment requirements.
  4. Keep records of work and components repaired or replaced.
  5. Complete Rebuilt Salvage Vehicle Statement (ITD 3311), identifying the salvage event, repairs, and who performed or supervised the work.
  6. Obtain a VIN inspection if required, including for an ownership document issued outside Idaho.
  7. Take the Salvage Certificate, statement, and other required documents to a county assessor’s motor vehicle office.
  8. Apply for a branded Idaho title, pay applicable fees and taxes, and complete registration requirements.
  9. Obtain the new title branded Rebuilt Salvage.

The owner may apply for the branded title and registration during the same transaction. If the vehicle was repaired by another person, or repairs were unnecessary, use the applicable provisions on ITD 3311.

What inspections and verification are required?

Idaho's published guidance emphasizes compliance with vehicle equipment requirements and accurate title documentation.

Vehicle equipment compliance

The applicant certifies on ITD 3311 that the vehicle is in operating condition and complies with applicable federal motor vehicle safety standards and Idaho equipment requirements. The current form also addresses airbag deployment and replacement and prohibits counterfeit or nonfunctional restraint components.

This certification is important, but it should not be treated as a substitute for evaluating whether repairs were actually performed correctly.

Vehicle identification number inspection

A VIN inspection is required when the ownership document was issued by another jurisdiction or when Idaho otherwise requires verification. Idaho uses VIN Inspection Certification (ITD 3403). A VIN inspection verifies identifying information; it is not a comprehensive structural or mechanical assessment.

Repair statement

ITD 3311 requests:

  • Vehicle and VIN information.
  • Salvage declaration date and cause, if known.
  • Components repaired and replaced.
  • Airbag deployment and replacement.
  • Who performed or supervised repairs.
  • Certification of equipment compliance and accuracy of documents.

Do not assume Idaho requires the same special pre-repair and post-repair salvage inspection process used by some other states. Confirm requirements for the particular vehicle with Idaho DMV.

Does completing Idaho's rebuilt salvage requirements mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

A Rebuilt Salvage title establishes that the vehicle completed the applicable titling process. It does not establish that the vehicle is free of defects or that every repair was performed to a high standard.

Before buying, consider independently evaluating:

  • Structural repairs
  • Frame or unibody alignment
  • Airbags and restraint systems
  • Brakes, steering, and suspension
  • Electrical systems
  • Water or flood damage
  • Mechanical condition
  • Wheel alignment and tire wear
  • Rust, corrosion, and evidence of poor-quality body repairs
  • Replacement component quality

The useful question is not simply: Does this vehicle have an Idaho Rebuilt Salvage title?

It is also: What was damaged, how was it repaired, and what condition is the vehicle in now?

What repair and parts documentation should I keep?

Idaho requires ITD 3311 when applying for a Rebuilt Salvage title. It asks the applicant to identify components repaired or replaced and explain who performed the work. Keep enough information to complete it accurately.

Consider retaining:

  • Original damage assessments
  • Insurance settlement documents
  • Photographs taken before and during repairs
  • Parts invoices and receipts
  • Records of major used components
  • Repair invoices
  • Airbag repair or replacement documentation
  • Records identifying the repairer
  • VIN inspection documentation, when applicable
  • Completed Rebuilt Salvage Vehicle Statement

These records can help future buyers. Not every item is a separate statutory submission requirement; confirm what supporting documents Idaho DMV requires.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company declares it a total loss?

Idaho has specific requirements for owner-retained salvage vehicles. If an insurer allows the owner to keep the vehicle after a total-loss settlement:

  • The owner must surrender the existing title to Idaho DMV within 30 days after the claim is satisfied.
  • The insurer must notify Idaho DMV of the total-loss payoff within 30 days.
  • The owner must obtain a Salvage Certificate before selling or otherwise disposing of the vehicle.

The owner’s title-surrender obligation and insurer’s notification obligation are separate. Failure to obtain the required Salvage Certificate or disclose total-loss status when selling may carry penalties. Keeping the vehicle does not authorize ordinary registration under the prior title.

Before rebuilding, determine:

  • Whether the insurer reported the total-loss settlement.
  • Whether the prior title was surrendered.
  • Whether a Salvage Certificate has been issued.
  • Whether the vehicle is eligible for rebuilding.
  • Which repairs and documents are required.
  • Whether a lien affects ownership documents.

If the vehicle has a lien, ask your insurer how the total-loss settlement will be handled and your lienholder what must happen to the outstanding loan. Then contact Idaho DMV or your county motor vehicle office to determine which ownership documents are needed for the Salvage Certificate and, if you rebuild the vehicle, the Rebuilt Salvage title.

Is there an age exemption from Idaho's salvage process?

Idaho's current salvage guidance does not establish a general age-based exemption for ordinary salvage vehicles. ITD 3311 expressly applies when seeking a Rebuilt Salvage title regardless of vehicle age, value, or type.

Historical rules affecting some older or lower-value vehicles should not be assumed to apply today. Before buying or rebuilding, determine:

  • Whether the vehicle meets Idaho’s salvage definition.
  • What ownership document it has.
  • Whether it has been declared salvage.
  • Whether it is eligible for rebuilding.
  • Whether ITD 3311 is required.
  • Whether a special vehicle classification affects title or registration.

An older vehicle is not automatically exempt from Idaho salvage requirements.

What about flood-damaged vehicles?

Flood damage can make a vehicle salvage if repair is uneconomical or a qualifying total-loss settlement occurs. Idaho’s general salvage guidance does not establish a separate flood-damage percentage threshold.

Before buying, investigate:

  • Electrical systems and electronic modules
  • Wiring and connectors
  • Airbags and restraint systems
  • Rust and corrosion
  • Interior materials
  • Mechanical components
  • Mold or contamination
  • Evidence of prior water intrusion

Some problems appear only after repair or extended driving. Review title history and available damage records, and ask Idaho DMV how an existing flood-related or out-of-state title brand will be handled.

What if the vehicle comes from another state?

Idaho recognizes salvage documents from other jurisdictions. An out-of-state Salvage Certificate, salvage bill of sale, or other evidence of total loss may cause the vehicle to be treated as salvage in Idaho.

If a salvage vehicle is acquired in a jurisdiction that does not require surrender of the previous title, Idaho law requires the purchaser to surrender that document and apply for an Idaho Salvage Certificate within 30 days after receiving the title. An out-of-state VIN inspection may be required.

If an Idaho-titled vehicle becomes salvage outside Idaho, the owner or insurer must notify the department and purchaser of the salvage status. If it returns, Idaho may issue a Rebuilt Salvage brand or carry forward a comparable out-of-state brand.

Before buying, determine:

  • The exact ownership document and issuing state.
  • All salvage, rebuilt, junk, or other title brands.
  • Whether Idaho accepts the document.
  • Whether a new Idaho Salvage Certificate is needed.
  • Whether a VIN inspection is required.
  • Whether the vehicle is eligible for road registration.
  • What brand will appear on the Idaho title.

Transferring a vehicle between states does not automatically remove its salvage history.

What should I check before buying an Idaho salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does it have?
  • Is it a Salvage Certificate or Rebuilt Salvage title?
  • Why was it declared salvage?
  • Was there an insurance total-loss settlement?
  • Is it eligible to be rebuilt for highway use?
  • What repairs have been completed?
  • Who performed or supervised repairs?
  • Can the seller provide ITD 3311?
  • Are repair invoices and photographs available?
  • Were airbags deployed and replaced?
  • Does it have an out-of-state title brand?
  • Is a VIN inspection required?
  • Has its condition been independently evaluated?
  • Are there liens or ownership issues?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • If financing is needed, will a lender accept its title history?
  • What repair, transportation, tax, title, registration, and other costs remain?

For an unrepaired vehicle, ask: Can it be repaired to meet Idaho equipment requirements and qualify for a Rebuilt Salvage title?

For an already-repaired vehicle, ask: Can I verify the repairs and obtain the documentation needed for Idaho’s title process?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

An unrepaired salvage vehicle must be transferred using its properly assigned Salvage Certificate. An owner-retained salvage vehicle cannot be sold or otherwise disposed of before the required Salvage Certificate is issued.

Once a vehicle receives a Rebuilt Salvage title, the brand remains on subsequent Idaho titles. Before selling:

  • Confirm the ownership document is accurate.
  • Disclose salvage or rebuilt status.
  • Properly assign the title or Salvage Certificate.
  • Resolve lien-release requirements.
  • Provide available repair records to help the buyer understand its history.

Do not describe a vehicle as having a clean title when its documents show salvage or rebuilt status.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Idaho Transportation Department, Idaho Department of Insurance, your county motor vehicle office, or another government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, and procedures can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate Idaho agency.
