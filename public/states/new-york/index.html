New York Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand New York's rebuilt salvage process, what the DMV salvage examination actually checks, what documentation to keep, and whether a vehicle can legally return to the road.

What you should know

A qualifying salvage vehicle can potentially be repaired, examined by the New York DMV, titled as Rebuilt Salvage, and registered for road use.

New York's salvage vehicle examination is part of the state's Auto Theft Prevention Program. It verifies vehicle identity and helps determine whether the rebuilt vehicle contains stolen parts. It is not the vehicle's safety or emissions inspection.

New York will not examine or issue a title or registration to a rebuilt vehicle with certain restrictive out-of-state ownership documents, including documents identifying the vehicle as parts only, non-rebuildable, or non-repairable.

Understand New York terminology

Salvage vehicle. New York's salvage program applies to vehicles entering salvage status under applicable damage and ownership circumstances. DMV guidance identifies damage exceeding 75% of pre-damage retail value as an important threshold.

New York State Salvage Certificate — MV-907A. This is an ownership document used for a vehicle that has entered salvage status. A rebuilt vehicle using it as proof of ownership must complete the applicable DMV salvage process before normal title and registration.

Rebuilt Salvage. A qualifying vehicle that completes the process receives a New York title displaying the REBUILT SALVAGE brand.

Parts-only, non-rebuildable, and non-repairable. DMV states it will not examine or issue a title or registration to a rebuilt vehicle whose out-of-state ownership document carries these restrictive designations.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Possibly, but check the title or ownership document before evaluating repairs. A qualifying vehicle with a New York Salvage Certificate or another document accepted by DMV can potentially enter the salvage examination process.

An ownership document indicating that the vehicle cannot legally be rebuilt may prevent a New York road title regardless of physical condition.

How do I get a salvage vehicle back on the road?

  1. Establish acceptable proof of ownership.
  2. Repair the vehicle and install necessary replacement parts.
  3. Keep original receipts and supporting records for replacement parts.
  4. Make sure the vehicle is safe for road use.
  5. Complete applicable safety-inspection requirements when required for the process.
  6. Complete Salvage Examination/Title Application (MV-83SAL).
  7. Submit the application, original ownership documents, supporting documents, and applicable fee to DMV.
  8. Wait for DMV to schedule the salvage examination.
  9. Legally transport the completed vehicle to the examination facility.
  10. Present the vehicle and original replacement-part receipts.
  11. If the vehicle passes and the application is complete, wait for the title branded Rebuilt Salvage.
  12. After receiving the title, complete the applicable registration process before normal road use.

What is the New York salvage vehicle examination?

The examination is part of New York's Auto Theft Prevention Program. DMV investigators use it to determine whether the rebuilt vehicle is stolen or contains stolen parts.

It is not a safety inspection, emissions inspection, or insurance inspection. A vehicle can therefore have more than one inspection-related requirement, with each serving a different purpose.

What should I do before the salvage examination?

  • Complete necessary repairs.
  • Install replacement parts.
  • Make sure the vehicle is safe for road use.
  • Make sure the VIN is visible and has not been altered.
  • Organize original receipts for replacement parts.
  • Complete required forms and documentation.

DMV also has specific requirements concerning inflatable restraint systems. If an applicable airbag or restraint system was activated or removed, review current DMV replacement requirements before the examination. DMV states that a used restraint system removed from another vehicle cannot be used as the replacement.

What happens during the salvage examination?

At the examination facility, you or your authorized representative must be prepared to provide access to the vehicle and explain the rebuild. DMV instructs applicants to open the doors, hood, and deck lid; describe repair work; identify replaced parts; and provide original receipts.

DMV investigators examine the vehicle and identifying information as part of the anti-theft examination. A damaged or altered VIN can delay the process and may require another examination.

Why are replacement-part receipts important?

Because the examination is designed partly to identify stolen components, documentation showing where replacement parts came from is particularly important. Keep original receipts as parts are purchased.

If someone else already repaired the vehicle, ask whether the original replacement-part documentation is available before buying it.

How do I apply, and what documents might I need?

New York DMV currently requires the salvage examination application to be submitted by mail. The primary application is the Salvage Examination/Title Application (MV-83SAL).

  • Form MV-83SAL
  • original proof of ownership
  • original bill of sale or dealer reassignment when applicable
  • proof of sales tax payment
  • lien or lien-release documentation when applicable
  • proof of identity
  • applicable examination and title fee

After DMV processes the application, it provides examination appointment information. Use the current form and DMV instructions because forms, fees, sites, and procedures can change.

How do I get the vehicle to the examination?

A salvage vehicle cannot simply be driven to the examination using invalid registration or plates. DMV identifies legal transportation methods that can include towing, flatbed transport, applicable dealer or transporter plates, or a DMV Temporary Transport Permit when eligible.

Additional requirements apply to a Temporary Transport Permit, including applicable insurance and safety-inspection documentation. Determine transportation before the examination date.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

New York's salvage program can include vehicles from other states, but DMV does not accept every out-of-state ownership document as eligible for rebuilding.

DMV specifically states it will not examine or issue a New York title or registration to a rebuilt vehicle whose out-of-state title is labeled parts only, non-rebuildable, or non-repairable. Inspect the actual document before buying or rebuilding.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in New York?

  • What is the exact ownership document?
  • Does it contain a restrictive brand?
  • Is the vehicle eligible for the New York salvage examination?
  • What repairs remain?
  • Are original receipts available for replacement parts?
  • Is the VIN intact and visible?
  • Were airbags or other restraint systems activated or removed?
  • Have applicable safety-inspection requirements been satisfied?
  • How will the vehicle be legally transported to the examination?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What examination, title, registration, tax, transportation, repair, and other costs remain?

A vehicle being physically repairable does not necessarily mean it can legally complete New York's rebuilt salvage process.

What should I know if I later sell the vehicle?

The salvage history does not disappear after rebuilding. A qualifying New York title displays the REBUILT SALVAGE brand.

New York also requires damage disclosure in applicable title transactions, and dealers have additional disclosure responsibilities for branded vehicles. Make sure the title and required disclosures accurately communicate the vehicle's history.
