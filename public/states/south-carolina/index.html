South Carolina Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand South Carolina salvage and rebuilt title brands, rebuilding documentation, and where to find official state guidance.

What you should know

South Carolina generally considers a vehicle salvage when the cost of repairs, including parts and labor, equals or exceeds 75% of its fair market value under the applicable total-loss provision. A vehicle may also receive a salvage brand when an insurance company declares it a total loss or when damage to its body, unibody, or frame makes it unsafe to operate. The state provides exceptions for certain vehicles valued at $2,000 or less and qualifying antique vehicles.

South Carolina distinguishes ordinary salvage damage from flooding and fire damage. Vehicles may receive a Salvage, Salvage Flood, or Salvage Fire title brand. An eligible vehicle that is repaired may receive the corresponding rebuilt designation. Once a salvage brand becomes part of a vehicle’s title history, it cannot be removed.

Vehicles designated Junk are different from repairable salvage vehicles. A junk vehicle cannot be registered for ordinary road use.

Before purchasing or rebuilding a salvage vehicle, confirm its title classification, repair history, and eligibility for registration with the South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles (SCDMV).

Understand South Carolina’s title terminology

Salvage: A vehicle declared a total loss, meeting applicable repair-cost criteria, or having structural damage that makes it unsafe to operate.

Salvage Rebuilt: A previously salvage-branded vehicle repaired and titled under the applicable rebuilt requirements.

Salvage Flood / Salvage Flood Rebuilt: Title brands identifying a vehicle declared a total loss because of qualifying water damage and, where applicable, subsequently rebuilt.

Salvage Fire / Salvage Fire Rebuilt: Title brands identifying a vehicle declared a total loss because of fire damage and, where applicable, subsequently rebuilt.

Junk: A vehicle classified as non-repairable for road use or suitable only for parts or scrap metal.

For more information on these classifications, see SCDMV — Title Brands.

Can a salvage vehicle return to the road?

A salvage title does not, by itself, authorize a vehicle to be operated on public roads. An eligible vehicle must be repaired, appropriately titled, registered, and insured before returning to ordinary road use. Its title will retain the applicable rebuilt brand.

A vehicle designated Junk cannot be registered for ordinary road use in South Carolina. If a vehicle is not authorized for road use, arrange transportation.

How do I get an eligible salvage vehicle back on the road?

South Carolina allows eligible salvage vehicles to receive a regular certificate of title bearing the appropriate rebuilt brand after repairs and completion of required documentation. The general process is:

  1. Confirm title status. Determine whether the vehicle carries Salvage, Salvage Flood, Salvage Fire, or Junk branding and whether it is eligible for rebuilding.
  2. Complete repairs. Retain records of work performed, replacement parts, and associated costs.
  3. Document reconstruction. Prepare the Owner’s/Rebuilder’s Affidavit (Form S-2), when required, identifying the vehicle, repairs, replaced components, and costs.
  4. Gather ownership records. Obtain the assigned title, any lien releases, and supporting documents for replacement parts.
  5. Apply for title. Submit the appropriate SCDMV title application and supporting documents.
  6. Complete any additional verification. SCDMV may request further information or examination depending on the vehicle.
  7. Complete title and registration. After approval, obtain the title with the appropriate rebuilt designation and satisfy registration and insurance requirements before driving.

Rebuilt vehicle inspections

South Carolina’s published salvage rebuilding requirements emphasize documentation of the vehicle’s identity, the source and cost of replacement parts, and the extent of repairs performed.

The Owner’s/Rebuilder’s Affidavit (Form S-2) requires the rebuilder to certify information about the repairs and identify repaired or replaced components and safety equipment.

SCDMV’s general salvage-title guidance does not establish a separate, routinely required post-repair roadworthiness inspection for every rebuilt salvage vehicle. However, additional verification may be required depending on the vehicle’s circumstances and documentation.

Before submitting a rebuilt-title application, confirm with SCDMV whether your vehicle requires any additional examination and where it must be performed.

A rebuilt title is not a guarantee that a vehicle is mechanically sound or safe to operate. Buyers should consider an independent inspection of the vehicle’s mechanical systems, structure, and safety equipment.

What documents and forms may be required?

Documentation depends on whether the application concerns an initial salvage title, an owner-retained total-loss vehicle, or a repaired vehicle. For an initial salvage title, documents may include Form 400-S, the existing title, insurer documentation of the loss and damage type, Form TR-3 when required, and applicable insurer-agent authorization.

For a rebuilt-title application, documents may include the title application, existing salvage-branded title, Owner’s/Rebuilder’s Affidavit (Form S-2) when required, repair and replacement-part records, receipts or bills of sale requested by SCDMV, and applicable lien releases.

Confirm the current requirements and download forms from SCDMV — Forms and Manuals.

What if the salvage vehicle came from another state?

South Carolina requires applicable prior title brands from other states to be preserved when a vehicle is titled in South Carolina. If another state uses different terminology, SCDMV determines the corresponding South Carolina brand. Salvage history cannot be erased by transferring its title through another state.

Before buying or bringing an out-of-state salvage vehicle into South Carolina, confirm that SCDMV will accept its ownership documents, determine whether any brand restricts titling or registration, and ask whether additional reconstruction documentation or verification is required.

General title, insurance, and property-tax requirements also apply. See SCDMV — Moving to South Carolina.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in South Carolina?

A salvage or rebuilt title provides important history, but does not establish the quality of repairs or guarantee safety.

  • Verify the title brand. Check for Salvage, Salvage Rebuilt, Salvage Flood, Salvage Fire, or Junk.
  • Confirm title eligibility. Determine whether the vehicle can be titled and registered for your intended use.
  • Investigate the damage. Review insurance records, photographs, and vehicle history.
  • Check for water damage. Examine electrical systems, interior components, and corrosion.
  • Review repair records. Request receipts, parts information, and structural or mechanical repair documentation.
  • Arrange an independent inspection. Consider a qualified mechanic or collision repair specialist.
  • Check insurance availability. Confirm the coverage you need is available.
  • Estimate total costs. Include repairs, transportation, title and registration requirements, and unexpected expenses.

What should I know if I later sell the vehicle?

Accurately represent the vehicle’s title status and provide the properly assigned title and other required ownership documents. South Carolina requires applicable salvage brands to remain part of the title history through subsequent transfers.

If the vehicle has been declared a total loss but the title has not been updated, address applicable salvage-title requirements before transferring or retitling it.

Buyers of unrepaired salvage vehicles should verify rebuild eligibility and documentation requirements. Buyers of rebuilt vehicles should review the branded title and available repair records.
