Indiana Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Indiana salvage titles, Rebuilt branding, the salvage restoration process, major-component documentation, law-enforcement inspection, and what must happen before a salvage vehicle can return to road use.

What you should know

Indiana requires a salvage title for wrecked or damaged vehicles manufactured within the last seven model years. A salvage title may also be issued for an older vehicle when requested.

A vehicle with a salvage title can potentially return to road use after it is restored and completes Indiana's salvage restoration process.

After successful completion of that process, Indiana issues a certificate of title carrying the Rebuilt brand. The vehicle's salvage history does not disappear.

Indiana's restoration process places significant emphasis on vehicle identity and the ownership and source of major component parts. An Indiana law-enforcement officer physically examines the vehicle, its major component parts, and the ownership documents as part of the restoration process.

A vehicle designated Junk, Non-repairable, Scrap, or a similar designation may not be titled in Indiana.

Understand Indiana terminology

Salvage vehicle

Indiana requires a salvage title for a wrecked or damaged vehicle manufactured within the last seven model years.

For a vehicle more than seven model years old, a salvage title may be issued when requested.

Review Indiana BMV salvage and restoration guidance →

Rebuilt vehicle

A vehicle previously branded as salvage can enter Indiana's salvage restoration process after it has been restored.

The owner applies for a certificate of title carrying the Rebuilt brand. The Rebuilt title does not erase the vehicle's previous salvage history.

Junk, Non-repairable, or Scrap

These designations are not equivalent to an ordinary salvage title.

Indiana's current Affidavit of Restoration states that vehicles designated Junk, Non-repairable, Scrap, or similar may not be titled in Indiana.

This makes the exact ownership document particularly important when evaluating an auction or out-of-state vehicle.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, if the vehicle has a salvage ownership document Indiana accepts for restoration.

The first question should be:

What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?

Then ask:

Will Indiana accept that document for the salvage restoration process?

Do not rely solely on descriptions such as “repairable,” “rebuildable,” or “salvage.”

A vehicle can be physically repairable while its ownership document prevents it from receiving an Indiana road title.

A Junk, Non-repairable, Scrap, or similarly restrictive ownership document should therefore be treated as a major eligibility warning.

How do I get an Indiana salvage vehicle back on the road?

For a vehicle eligible for restoration, the general path is:

  1. Establish ownership with the appropriate salvage ownership document.
  2. Restore the vehicle to operable condition.
  3. Keep proof of ownership and source information for major component parts used during restoration.
  4. Complete the Affidavit of Restoration for a Salvage Motor Vehicle — State Form 44606.
  5. Have the vehicle, its major component parts, and ownership documents physically examined by an Indiana law-enforcement officer.
  6. Submit the salvage title, restoration affidavit, title application, parts documentation, and other required material to the Indiana BMV.
  7. After approval, receive an Indiana certificate of title carrying the Rebuilt brand.

Indiana processes salvage restoration title applications through the BMV Central Office.

What inspection does Indiana require?

Indiana's Affidavit of Restoration includes a physical inspection by an Indiana law-enforcement officer.

The officer certifies that they personally examined:

  • the vehicle,
  • its major component parts, and
  • the ownership documents.

The form also requires an IDACS/NCIC check.

The inspection therefore helps establish vehicle identity, component identity and ownership, and compliance with Indiana's salvage restoration requirements.

It should not be treated as a comprehensive evaluation of the quality of the repairs or as proof that the vehicle is a good purchase.

Review the inspection certification on State Form 44606 →

What documentation should I keep for replacement parts?

Indiana requires proof of ownership and source information for major component parts used during restoration.

State Form 44606 records information about applicable major component parts and their source. Proof of ownership or purchase must be submitted for each applicable major component part.

If restoration uses parts already on hand, Indiana's current restoration packet provides an affidavit procedure for documenting those parts.

Parts documentation should therefore be collected during the rebuild rather than reconstructed after the vehicle is finished.

What documents and forms should I expect?

The Indiana salvage restoration application can require:

  • Application for Certificate of Title for a Vehicle — State Form 205
  • the certificate of salvage title
  • Affidavit of Restoration for a Salvage Motor Vehicle — State Form 44606
  • proof of ownership or purchase for each major component part
  • an odometer disclosure when applicable
  • proof of address
  • applicable tax documentation
  • required payment information.

The exact documents depend on the vehicle and transaction.

Can I drive a vehicle while it still has only a salvage title?

Do not treat a salvage title as authorization for ordinary road use.

Before the vehicle can return to ordinary titled and registered use, it must complete Indiana's applicable restoration and titling requirements.

If the vehicle must be moved for inspection or another restoration step, determine the lawful transportation requirements with the agency performing the inspection rather than assuming the vehicle may be driven.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

Indiana's Affidavit of Restoration specifically states that out-of-state titles are accepted as part of the salvage restoration process.

That does not mean every out-of-state ownership document qualifies.

The same form states that vehicles designated Junk, Non-repairable, Scrap, or similar may not be titled in Indiana.

Therefore, before buying an out-of-state salvage vehicle, ask:

What exact ownership document will Indiana receive?

Then ask:

Does that document qualify for Indiana's salvage restoration process?

Do not assume that another state's Certificate of Destruction, Parts Only, Nonrepairable, Junk, or comparable restrictive document will be treated like an ordinary salvage title.

When an out-of-state certificate of title is used to apply for an Indiana salvage title, Indiana's current salvage-title packet also calls for a completed physical inspection form.

What about Indiana's seven-model-year rule?

Indiana requires a salvage title for wrecked or damaged vehicles manufactured within the last seven model years.

A salvage title may be issued for an older vehicle when requested.

The age rule should not be interpreted to mean that existing title history stops mattering after seven model years.

If an older vehicle already carries a Salvage, Rebuilt, Junk, Non-repairable, or other branded ownership document, evaluate the actual document rather than assuming the vehicle's age eliminates the title issue.

Review the current Indiana BMV seven-model-year guidance →

Does completing the rebuilt process mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

Indiana's salvage restoration process addresses title status, vehicle and component identity, parts ownership, documentation, and compliance with the restoration requirements.

It does not establish that collision repairs were performed to a high standard or that the vehicle is mechanically or structurally sound.

Before buying a rebuilt vehicle, independently investigate structural repairs, steering and suspension, alignment, brakes, airbags and restraint systems, electrical systems, mechanical condition, flood or water damage, corrosion, replacement components, and evidence of incomplete or poorly performed repairs.

Also investigate whether you can obtain the insurance coverage you want and how the Rebuilt title history could affect future resale.

What should I check before buying an Indiana salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Is it Salvage, Rebuilt, Junk, Non-repairable, Scrap, or something else?
  • Will Indiana accept that document for the restoration process?
  • What caused the vehicle to enter the salvage process?
  • Has repair work already started?
  • Are receipts and ownership documents available for major component parts?
  • Can the source of those components be documented?
  • Has the required law-enforcement inspection already occurred?
  • If the vehicle came from another state, what exact designation appears on its ownership document?
  • Has repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, inspection, transportation, title, tax, registration, and other costs remain?

The central Indiana question is:

Does this vehicle have an ownership document Indiana will accept for restoration, and can its repairs and major component parts be documented well enough to obtain a Rebuilt title?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

An Indiana vehicle that successfully completes the salvage restoration process receives a title carrying the Rebuilt brand.

That title history remains relevant when the vehicle is later transferred.

Indiana also has specific written disclosure requirements for licensed dealer transactions involving salvage or rebuilt vehicles. The Indiana Secretary of State provides Salvage or Rebuilt Vehicle Disclosure — State Form 55974 for this purpose.

If you later sell a rebuilt vehicle, accurately represent its branded title and salvage history and comply with the transfer and disclosure requirements that apply to the transaction.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles, Indiana State Police, Indiana Secretary of State, or another government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, inspections, and procedures can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate Indiana agency.
