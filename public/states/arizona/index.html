Arizona Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Arizona Salvage and Restored Salvage titles, nonrepairable vehicles, Level III inspections, replacement-parts documentation, and what must happen before an eligible salvage vehicle can return to the road.

What you should know

Arizona does not define every Salvage vehicle using a single damage-percentage threshold. Instead, its definition includes certain damaged vehicles that the owner, leasing company, financial institution, or insurance company considers uneconomical to repair.

A vehicle with a Salvage title is not eligible for regular registration. An eligible vehicle can be repaired or rebuilt and apply for a Restored Salvage title.

Before a restored salvage vehicle can be regularly registered and driven, it must be repaired to roadworthy condition and complete Arizona's inspection and title requirements.

Arizona also has a separate Nonrepairable vehicle category. A Nonrepairable vehicle does not have the same path back to road use as a Salvage vehicle.

If you are considering buying a Salvage vehicle, the exact title or ownership document matters as much as the apparent condition of the vehicle.

Understand Arizona terminology

Salvage vehicle

Arizona law defines a Salvage vehicle as a vehicle, other than a Nonrepairable vehicle, that has been stolen, wrecked, destroyed, flood or water damaged, or otherwise damaged to the extent that the owner, leasing company, financial institution, or insurance company considers it uneconomical to repair.

This means Arizona's statutory definition is not based on a universal percentage such as 75% or 80% of the vehicle's pre-damage value.

A Salvage Certificate of Title identifies the vehicle's salvage status and remains part of its title history.

Restored Salvage vehicle

A Restored Salvage vehicle is a vehicle that has been restored after a Salvage Certificate of Title or dismantle certificate of title was issued.

Arizona MVD describes a Restored Salvage title as the title issued after a salvage vehicle has been repaired or restored for highway use and completes the applicable state process.

A Restored Salvage title does not erase the vehicle's salvage history.

Nonrepairable vehicle

Arizona treats a Nonrepairable vehicle differently from a Salvage vehicle.

A vehicle can qualify as Nonrepairable when, for example, it has no resale value except as a source of parts or scrap metal and is designated for that purpose. Arizona law also describes certain completely stripped or completely burned vehicles that can qualify.

When Arizona issues a Nonrepairable Vehicle Certificate of Title, the vehicle's registration is cancelled.

The state does not provide the ordinary Restored Salvage path for a vehicle that has been designated Nonrepairable.

Before buying a severely damaged vehicle, determine whether its document says Salvage or Nonrepairable. Those terms are not interchangeable.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, if it has an eligible Salvage title and can complete Arizona's Restored Salvage process.

A Salvage vehicle is not eligible for regular registration while it remains in Salvage status.

Arizona MVD says the vehicle must be repaired to roadworthy condition before applying for the Restored Salvage title and registration.

A vehicle that has received a Nonrepairable Vehicle Certificate of Title does not have the same route back to ordinary title and registration.

Before buying, ask:

What exact title or ownership document does this vehicle have?

Then ask:

Does Arizona provide a path from that document to a Restored Salvage title and registration?

How do I get an Arizona salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible Salvage vehicle, the general process is:

  1. Repair or restore the vehicle to roadworthy condition.
  2. Keep ownership documentation for replacement component parts used during the repair or restoration.
  3. Make an appointment for the required vehicle inspection.
  4. Present the vehicle for the applicable Arizona inspection, normally a Level III inspection for a restored salvage vehicle.
  5. Obtain an emissions compliance certificate, if applicable.
  6. Complete the Title and Registration Application.
  7. Surrender the Salvage title with the application.
  8. Pay the applicable title, inspection, and registration fees.
  9. Complete the registration requirements before ordinary road use.

Arizona MVD currently lists a three-day permit as an option for limited vehicle operation before regular registration. A Salvage title by itself does not authorize normal road use.

What inspection does Arizona require?

Arizona uses its highest vehicle-inspection level, Level III, for restored salvage vehicles.

Level III inspections are conducted by certified ADOT officers at ADOT Enforcement and Compliance Division inspection locations and require an appointment.

Arizona says this inspection is used to verify major component parts and that the vehicle is equipped for highway use.

Arizona law also authorizes the department, when processing a Restored Salvage application, to conduct a Level II or Level III inspection to determine the vehicle's proper identity.

The inspection can include a review of bills of sale, invoices, and other ownership documentation for component parts used during reconstruction.

Does passing the inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

A buyer should not treat a Restored Salvage title or a completed Level III inspection as a substitute for an independent evaluation of the vehicle's condition or repair quality.

Before buying a Restored Salvage vehicle, consider independently evaluating:

  • structural repairs
  • frame or unibody alignment
  • airbags and restraint systems
  • brakes, steering, and suspension
  • electrical repairs
  • water or flood damage
  • mechanical condition
  • wheel alignment and tire wear
  • the quality and origin of replacement components

The useful question is not simply:

Did the vehicle pass Arizona's Level III inspection?

It is also:

What was damaged, how was it repaired, and what condition is the vehicle in now?

What documentation should I keep for replacement parts?

Keep documentation showing lawful ownership of replacement components used in the repair or restoration.

Arizona law allows the inspection process to include review of bills of sale and invoices for component parts.

Acceptable documentation can include invoices, notarized bills of sale, or other proof of ownership acceptable to the Arizona Department of Transportation.

The documentation requirement is important enough that Arizona law requires a seller or seller's agent of a Salvage vehicle to tell the buyer that ownership documentation for certain replacement parts will be required for the restored-salvage inspection.

If satisfactory ownership documentation for a component part is not provided, Arizona law allows the department to seize that component. A stolen component discovered during the inspection can also be seized.

Do not wait until the vehicle has been rebuilt to reconstruct its parts history.

If you are buying replacement components, retain the documentation as you acquire them.

Which component parts receive particular attention?

Arizona law specifically defines component parts for purposes of the Restored Salvage process.

For passenger vehicles, these can include parts such as:

  • cowl or firewall
  • front-end assembly
  • rear clip
  • roof panel
  • frame or portions of the frame
  • doors
  • hood
  • fenders or quarter panels
  • deck lid or hatchback
  • bumpers
  • transmission or transaxle
  • engine or motor

Arizona also defines applicable component parts for trucks, truck-type vehicles, buses, and motorcycles.

You do not need to memorize the statutory list before considering a vehicle. The practical point is to preserve documentation for significant replacement components rather than assuming only the engine or frame will matter.

What documents should I expect?

Depending on the vehicle and transaction, Arizona's Restored Salvage process can involve:

  • the existing Salvage Certificate of Title
  • a Title and Registration Application
  • bills of sale, invoices, or other ownership documentation for replacement components
  • inspection documentation
  • an emissions compliance certificate, if applicable
  • applicable title, inspection, and registration fees

The exact documentation can depend on the vehicle and the parts used during reconstruction.

Use Arizona MVD's current instructions and forms when you are ready to apply.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company declares it a total loss?

Arizona specifically addresses owner-retained total-loss vehicles.

If the owner retains possession of a vehicle that is a Salvage or Nonrepairable vehicle, Arizona law generally requires the owner to comply with the state's salvage or nonrepairable title requirements before receiving the total-loss settlement or otherwise disposing of the vehicle.

This means keeping possession of the vehicle does not make its title consequences disappear.

Determine:

  • whether the vehicle qualifies as Salvage or Nonrepairable
  • what title the vehicle has after the settlement
  • whether any liens must be satisfied
  • whether the vehicle remains eligible for restoration
  • what title and inspection process applies before future registration

If you intend to repair an owner-retained total-loss vehicle, confirm its title status before beginning substantial repairs.

What happens to an older vehicle that is declared a total loss?

Arizona's Salvage definition does not establish a general vehicle-age exemption.

The central question is whether the vehicle falls within Arizona's definition of a Salvage vehicle or Nonrepairable vehicle.

Arizona defines a Salvage vehicle in terms of the vehicle's condition and whether the owner, leasing company, financial institution, or insurer considers it uneconomical to repair.

For an older total-loss vehicle, determine:

  • its value before the loss
  • the nature and extent of the damage
  • whether it has been designated Salvage or Nonrepairable
  • whether an insurance company acquired it after a total-loss settlement
  • whether you retained the vehicle
  • what ownership document exists now

Do not assume that an older vehicle is exempt from Arizona's salvage-title requirements simply because of its age.

What about flood or water-damaged vehicles?

Arizona expressly includes flood or water damage within its Salvage vehicle definition when the damage makes the vehicle uneconomical to repair.

Arizona also has specific statutory provisions concerning flood or water-damaged vehicles and title branding.

For a flood or water-damaged vehicle, investigate more than the title brand.

Consider the possibility of damage involving:

  • electrical systems
  • electronic control modules
  • connectors and wiring
  • airbags and restraint systems
  • corrosion
  • interior materials
  • mechanical components
  • mold or contamination

Water-related problems can appear after a vehicle has been repaired and may not be obvious during a brief inspection.

Before buying, determine what type of water exposure occurred, how high the water reached, how long the vehicle was exposed, and what systems were repaired or replaced.

What if the vehicle comes from another state?

Do not assume that an out-of-state Salvage or Rebuilt title automatically becomes an ordinary Arizona title.

Arizona examines the existing ownership document and title history when the vehicle enters its title system.

Before buying an out-of-state salvage vehicle, determine:

  • the exact title or ownership document
  • every brand shown on that document
  • whether the vehicle is Salvage, Rebuilt, Restored Salvage, Nonrepairable, Junk, Parts Only, or another status
  • whether Arizona recognizes a path from that document to the title and registration you need
  • whether the vehicle has already been repaired
  • whether documentation for replacement components is available
  • whether an Arizona inspection will be required

Verify the vehicle's eligibility before paying for transportation, repairs, inspection, or registration.

What should I check before buying an Arizona salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Is it Salvage, Restored Salvage, or Nonrepairable?
  • Why was it declared Salvage?
  • Has it already been repaired?
  • Has it completed Arizona's Level III inspection?
  • Are bills of sale, invoices, or other ownership records available for replacement components?
  • Are the major replacement components traceable?
  • Was the vehicle flood or water damaged?
  • Has the repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Are there unresolved liens or ownership issues?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • If financing is needed, will a lender accept the vehicle's title history?
  • What repair, transportation, inspection, emissions, title, tax, registration, and other costs remain?

For a vehicle that has not yet been restored, one particularly important question is:

Will I have the parts documentation Arizona may require when this vehicle is presented for inspection?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Arizona requires disclosure of Salvage and Restored Salvage status.

A person who sells a vehicle knowing that a Salvage Certificate of Title has been issued must clearly and conspicuously disclose to the buyer, before the sale is completed, that the vehicle is a Salvage vehicle and has a Salvage Certificate of Title.

Arizona also requires a person selling a vehicle with a Restored Salvage Certificate of Title, with knowledge of that status, to clearly and conspicuously disclose before the sale that the vehicle is a Restored Salvage vehicle and has a Restored Salvage Certificate of Title.

There is another important restriction when a Salvage vehicle has already been rebuilt or restored: the owner cannot transfer that vehicle until a Restored Salvage Certificate of Title has been issued.

These requirements make the title history part of the transaction rather than something a seller should leave for the buyer to discover later.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Arizona Department of Transportation, Motor Vehicle Division, or another government agency.
