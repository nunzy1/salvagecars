Arizona Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Arizona salvage and restored salvage titles, Level III inspections, component-part documentation, and whether a vehicle can legally return to the road.

What you should know

An Arizona salvage title is not a normal road title. A vehicle with a salvage title is not eligible for regular registration and cannot simply be repaired and returned to ordinary road use.

To legally register and drive a qualifying salvage vehicle, it generally must be repaired or restored, complete Arizona's required inspection, satisfy emissions requirements when applicable, surrender the salvage title, and receive a title branded RESTORED SALVAGE.

Arizona's current Motor Vehicle Division guidance directs restored-salvage applicants to a Level III vehicle inspection, the state's highest inspection level.

Arizona does not treat every severely damaged vehicle as rebuildable. A vehicle classified as nonrepairable is a separate legal category and does not have the same path back to road use.

Understand Arizona terminology

Salvage vehicle

Arizona defines a salvage vehicle as a vehicle, other than a nonrepairable vehicle, that has been stolen, wrecked, destroyed, flood or water damaged, or otherwise damaged to the extent that the owner, leasing company, financial institution, or insurance company considers it uneconomical to repair.

A qualifying salvage vehicle can potentially be repaired and returned to highway use through Arizona's restored-salvage process.

Salvage certificate of title

A salvage certificate of title documents the vehicle's salvage status. It does not authorize normal road use.

Restored salvage vehicle

A restored salvage vehicle previously received a title branded salvage, was subsequently repaired or restored, completed the applicable inspection process, and received a title branded RESTORED SALVAGE.

Nonrepairable vehicle

A nonrepairable vehicle is fundamentally different from a salvage vehicle. Arizona law includes vehicles whose remaining value is essentially parts or scrap metal, as well as certain completely stripped or completely burned vehicles meeting the statutory definition.

When Arizona issues a nonrepairable vehicle certificate of title, registration is cancelled and the ownership document is branded NONREPAIRABLE. Do not assume that a vehicle described casually as “salvage” by a seller or auction is legally rebuildable. Inspect the actual ownership document.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Possibly. A vehicle with an Arizona salvage title can potentially be repaired, complete the required inspection and title process, and receive a RESTORED SALVAGE title.

The first question is:

Is this vehicle legally salvage and repairable, rather than nonrepairable?

If the answer is yes, the next question is:

Can the vehicle be repaired for highway use and can you document the vehicle and the component parts used in the restoration?

How do I get a salvage vehicle back on the road?

  1. Confirm that the vehicle has an ownership document that permits restoration.
  2. Repair or restore the vehicle for highway use.
  3. Keep receipts, invoices, bills of sale, and other ownership documentation for repaired or replaced component parts.
  4. Make sure equipment required for highway use is in good working order.
  5. Schedule the required Level III vehicle inspection under current Arizona MVD procedures.
  6. Bring the vehicle and required ownership and parts documentation to the inspection.
  7. Successfully complete the required inspection.
  8. Obtain an emissions compliance certificate if one applies to the vehicle and registration location.
  9. Complete the Title and Registration Application.
  10. Surrender the salvage title.
  11. Complete the applicable title and registration requirements.
  12. If approved, receive an Arizona title branded RESTORED SALVAGE.

What is a Level III inspection?

Arizona uses multiple levels of vehicle inspection. For restored salvage vehicles, current Arizona MVD operational guidance directs applicants to Level III, the state's highest inspection level.

Level III inspections are conducted by certified ADOT officers at designated Enforcement and Compliance Division inspection locations and are by appointment.

Arizona says the inspection is required for restored salvage vehicles to help reduce auto theft. The inspection verifies major component parts such as the front-end assembly, engine, transmission, and, for applicable trucks and truck-type vehicles, the rear-end assembly.

The inspection also determines whether the vehicle is equipped for highway use. Arizona MVD states that equipment required for highway use must be in good working order for the vehicle to pass.

Does the Level III inspection guarantee that the vehicle is a good purchase?

No. Arizona describes the restored-salvage process as including an inspection for roadworthiness, and the Level III inspection verifies major components and required highway equipment.

But passing the state inspection should not be interpreted as a general warranty of the vehicle's condition or the quality and durability of every repair.

A buyer should still independently investigate structural repair quality, mechanical condition, electrical systems, airbag and restraint systems, corrosion or water damage, wheel alignment and suspension condition, insurance availability, and future resale implications.

The state inspection establishes eligibility within Arizona's title and registration process. A buyer still needs to decide whether the particular vehicle is a sensible purchase.

Why are parts receipts important?

Parts documentation is especially important in Arizona's restored-salvage process. Arizona requires proof of ownership for component parts used during repair or restoration.

For inspection, the owner should have receipts, invoices, bills of sale, or other ownership documents for component parts.

Arizona's current guidance says documentation for a replacement component should include identifying information about the source vehicle and seller, such as the source vehicle's VIN, year, make, and model, along with a description of the component and seller information.

This documentation helps establish where major components came from and helps the state detect stolen vehicle parts. If you are buying a partially rebuilt vehicle, ask for this documentation before completing the purchase.

What documents might I need?

Salvage title

The ownership document showing the vehicle's salvage status.

Title and Registration Application

Used to apply for the restored salvage title and applicable registration.

Proof of ownership

Depending on the circumstances, this can include the vehicle title, registration, or bill of sale.

Receipts and invoices for component parts

Used to establish ownership and source information for parts repaired or replaced during restoration.

Valid driver license

Required as part of the inspection process under current MVD guidance.

Emissions compliance certificate, if applicable

Required when Arizona emissions requirements apply to the vehicle and registration situation.

Can I drive a salvage vehicle before it receives a restored salvage title?

Not for ordinary road use. Arizona states that a vehicle with a salvage title is not eligible for regular registration.

Arizona MVD identifies limited temporary-permit options that may apply in particular circumstances. Do not treat a temporary permit as ordinary registration or assume that it automatically applies to your vehicle.

Before driving a salvage vehicle to an inspection or for another limited purpose, verify the current permit, insurance, registration, and vehicle-condition requirements with Arizona MVD.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

The exact ownership document matters more than the terminology used in an auction listing. Arizona law distinguishes between salvage and nonrepairable vehicles, while other states use different terminology for damaged vehicles.

  • Inspect the actual ownership document.
  • Identify every brand or restriction.
  • Determine whether the document represents salvage, nonrepairable, junk, parts-only, or another status.
  • Verify whether Arizona will accept that ownership document for the restored-salvage process.
  • Determine whether additional identification or title requirements apply.

Do not assume that every document called a “salvage title” by a seller provides a path to an Arizona RESTORED SALVAGE title.

What about recovered theft vehicles?

Arizona has specific rules for vehicles declared a total loss because of theft and later recovered.

A recovered theft vehicle may sometimes receive a regular certificate of title rather than a restored salvage title if it was not wrecked or stripped of essential parts and the applicable statutory requirements are satisfied.

Airbag deployment, removal of airbag-system components, structural damage, or removal of other important parts can change that outcome. In some circumstances, a recovered theft vehicle instead follows the restored-salvage process.

If you are considering a recovered-theft vehicle, verify its actual damage, missing components, airbag history, ownership document, and current Arizona MVD requirements.

What about flood or water damage?

Arizona's definition of salvage vehicle expressly includes vehicles that have been flood or water damaged when the damage meets the state's salvage definition.

A flood-damaged vehicle that remains legally repairable can potentially follow the restored-salvage process. But receiving a RESTORED SALVAGE title does not establish that the vehicle is free from hidden flood-related problems.

  • corrosion in electrical connectors and wiring
  • water intrusion into electronic modules
  • contamination inside the passenger compartment
  • mold or odor
  • damage to restraint-system components
  • water exposure to the engine, transmission, or drivetrain
  • long-term electrical reliability

The title process and the mechanical risk assessment answer different questions.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in Arizona?

  • What is the exact ownership document?
  • Is the vehicle salvage or nonrepairable?
  • Does the ownership document permit the vehicle to be restored?
  • Is it an out-of-state vehicle with a different title brand?
  • Why was the vehicle declared salvage?
  • Was it damaged in a crash, flood, fire, or theft?
  • What major components need replacement?
  • Have major components already been replaced?
  • Are receipts and source information available for those parts?
  • Are the VIN and other identifying numbers intact?
  • Is the vehicle capable of meeting Arizona's highway-equipment requirements?
  • Has it already passed the required inspection?
  • Will emissions compliance be required?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, inspection, emissions, title, registration, tax, and transportation costs remain?
  • Has the quality of completed repairs been independently evaluated?

The key question is not simply Can I repair this vehicle?

Is this vehicle legally repairable, can I document the restoration and its component parts, and can it successfully complete Arizona's restored-salvage process?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Arizona has explicit disclosure requirements for salvage vehicles. A person who sells a vehicle for which a salvage certificate of title has been issued and knows that the salvage certificate was issued must clearly and conspicuously disclose that status to the buyer, in writing or electronically, before completion of the sale.

Arizona law also states that if a salvage vehicle has been rebuilt or restored to operation, the owner cannot transfer the vehicle until a restored salvage certificate of title has been issued.

Once a qualifying vehicle completes that process, its restored-salvage history becomes part of its title history. If you later sell the vehicle, accurately represent that branded title history and verify the current disclosure and transfer requirements for the transaction.
