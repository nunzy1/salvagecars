Louisiana Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Louisiana salvage titles, the 75% total-loss threshold, Reconstructed titles, owner-retained total losses, water-damaged vehicles, inspection requirements, and what must happen before a salvage vehicle can return to ordinary road use.

What you should know

Louisiana generally considers a vehicle a total loss when it has sustained damage equal to 75% or more of its market value.

When a vehicle is declared a total loss as the result of an insurance settlement, Louisiana requires a Salvage title. That requirement can apply whether the resulting Salvage title is issued to the insurance company, its authorized agent, or the vehicle owner.

That means an owner who keeps a vehicle after an insurance total-loss settlement does not simply continue with the previous title unchanged.

An eligible Salvage vehicle can be rebuilt. Before it can return to ordinary road use, the vehicle must complete Louisiana's inspection and title process and receive a title marked RECONSTRUCTED.

Louisiana also has a particularly important rule for certain severely water-damaged total-loss vehicles. Those vehicles receive a Certificate of Destruction rather than a Salvage title and cannot later receive a Salvage or Reconstructed title or be registered for road use.

Understand Louisiana terminology

Salvage title

Louisiana defines a Salvage title as the certificate used to document that a vehicle has been declared a total loss through an insurance settlement.

A total loss generally means the vehicle sustained damage equal to 75% or more of its market value.

Louisiana has an exception for qualifying cosmetic hail damage. A vehicle whose damage reaches the threshold solely because of specified cosmetic hail damage is not treated as a total-loss salvage vehicle under that rule, although its title is branded to indicate hail damage.

After receiving a Salvage title, the vehicle may be dismantled, sold, or rebuilt and restored to operation.

Reconstructed

A Salvage vehicle that has been properly rebuilt and restored to operating condition can receive a new Louisiana title marked RECONSTRUCTED.

The Reconstructed brand documents the vehicle's prior salvage history. A Reconstructed title should not be described as a clean title.

Certificate of Destruction

A Certificate of Destruction is substantially different from a repairable Salvage title.

Louisiana requires this document for certain water-damaged total-loss vehicles when flooding resulting from a gubernatorially declared disaster or emergency damages the vehicle's power train, computer, or electrical system. The statutory definition contains exceptions for antique vehicles and vehicles over the specified gross-vehicle-weight threshold.

Once Louisiana issues a Certificate of Destruction, the vehicle cannot later receive a Salvage or Reconstructed title, cannot otherwise be titled or registered for road use, cannot be resold as a retail unit, and must ultimately be dismantled, used for parts, or crushed.

Certificate of Destruction is not another name for a repairable Salvage title.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, if the vehicle has a repairable Salvage title or another ownership document Louisiana accepts for reconstruction and it can successfully complete the required rebuilding, inspection, and title process.

A vehicle carrying a Certificate of Destruction does not have that path.

Before buying, ask:

What exact ownership document does this vehicle have?

Then determine:

Does that document permit the vehicle to be rebuilt and receive a Louisiana Reconstructed title?

Do not rely solely on an auction description such as “repairable,” “rebuildable,” “salvage,” “flood,” or “parts vehicle.”

How do I get a Louisiana salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible Salvage vehicle, the general path is:

  1. Establish ownership with an acceptable Salvage title.
  2. Rebuild or restore the vehicle to operating condition.
  3. Keep receipts and other ownership documentation for major component parts used during reconstruction.
  4. Complete the vehicle inspection required for the Reconstructed-title process.
  5. Provide the required inspection documentation, Salvage title, major-component documentation, and other required records to the Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles.
  6. Complete the applicable title and registration requirements.
  7. Receive a Louisiana title marked RECONSTRUCTED before returning the vehicle to ordinary public-road use.

Louisiana law allows a rebuilder to demonstrate a completed rebuilt vehicle to a prospective purchaser before obtaining the Reconstructed title, but that is a narrow exception. It should not be treated as permission for general road use.

What inspection does Louisiana require?

Louisiana requires inspection before a reconstructed vehicle can be titled and registered.

The inspection process includes verification of the vehicle's identity and review of the major component parts used during reconstruction. Louisiana law requires documentation supporting major components that were replaced.

Depending on the vehicle and circumstances, the inspection can involve verification of identification numbers on the vehicle and its components.

The inspection is therefore an important anti-theft, identity, and documentation step in Louisiana's Reconstructed-title process.

Does passing the inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

The Louisiana inspection is part of the legal process for establishing the vehicle's identity and reconstructed status. It should not be treated as a comprehensive evaluation of every repair.

Before buying a Reconstructed vehicle, independently evaluate issues such as:

  • structural repairs,
  • frame or unibody alignment,
  • airbags and restraint systems,
  • brakes, steering, and suspension,
  • electrical repairs,
  • water intrusion and flood damage,
  • mechanical condition, and
  • the quality of replacement components and repairs.

An independent evaluation is particularly important for a water-damaged vehicle. Water intrusion can cause electrical, mechanical, and corrosion problems even when the vehicle does not meet Louisiana's specific conditions for a Certificate of Destruction.

What documentation should I keep for replacement parts?

Keep receipts and ownership documentation for major components used during reconstruction.

Louisiana's Reconstructed-title process requires documentation for major component parts that were replaced. Depending on the repair, identifying information associated with those components may also be relevant during inspection.

Useful records can include original receipts, invoices, bills of sale, donor-vehicle information, component identification information, photographs of the damage and repair process, and repair records.

Collect the documentation while rebuilding the vehicle rather than attempting to reconstruct the parts history immediately before inspection.

What documents and forms should I expect?

Depending on the vehicle and transaction, Louisiana's process can involve:

  • the Salvage title,
  • an application for the Reconstructed title,
  • the required inspection documentation,
  • receipts for major component parts that were replaced,
  • vehicle and component identification information,
  • proof of ownership,
  • applicable lien documentation,
  • title and registration documentation, and
  • required taxes and fees.

The exact requirements can depend on the vehicle's ownership history, damage, and reconstruction. Use current Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles instructions for the particular transaction.

Can I drive the vehicle while it still has a Salvage title?

Louisiana law states that, except for a limited demonstration to a prospective purchaser by a rebuilder, a rebuilt and restored vehicle cannot be operated on a public street, roadway, or highway until it has been registered with the Office of Motor Vehicles and a Reconstructed title has been issued.

The demonstration exception should not be interpreted as general permission to drive a Salvage-titled vehicle.

What happens to an older vehicle that an insurance company totals?

Louisiana's basic total-loss definition does not establish a general age cutoff.

Instead, it focuses on the relationship between the vehicle's damage and its market value. A vehicle generally meets the definition of a total loss when its damage equals 75% or more of its market value, subject to Louisiana's specific exceptions.

That means an older vehicle is not automatically excluded from Louisiana's salvage-title system merely because of its age.

Because an older vehicle may have a relatively low market value, substantial damage can reach the 75% threshold even when the dollar amount of the repair is lower than it would be for a newer vehicle.

The important questions are the vehicle's market value, the extent of its damage, whether it was declared a total loss as part of an insurance settlement, who retained or acquired it after settlement, and what title was issued.

Do not assume that an older Louisiana vehicle keeps an ordinary title simply because of its age.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company totals it?

Louisiana specifically addresses owner-retained total losses.

When a vehicle is declared a total loss as the result of an insurance settlement, Louisiana requires an application for a Salvage title in the name of the insurance company, its authorized agent, or the vehicle owner, depending on who retains the vehicle.

When the owner is allowed to retain the salvage, the insurance company or its agent must notify the Office of Motor Vehicles of the settlement and the owner-retained salvage value.

The owner-retained vehicle can then follow the applicable Salvage-to-Reconstructed process if it is eligible to be rebuilt.

For buyers, the important point is: Owner retention does not eliminate Louisiana's Salvage-title requirement for an insurance total-loss vehicle.

What about a flood- or water-damaged vehicle?

Water damage deserves separate attention in Louisiana.

There are several materially different possibilities:

Certificate of Destruction

Certain water-damaged total-loss vehicles affected by flooding resulting from a gubernatorially declared disaster or emergency receive a Certificate of Destruction. These vehicles cannot return to titled road use.

Water-damage brand

Louisiana also provides for a water-damage title brand when a vehicle's power train, computer, or electrical system sustained water damage but the vehicle does not meet the criteria for either Salvage or a Certificate of Destruction.

Salvage or Reconstructed

Other vehicles may follow the applicable Salvage and Reconstructed-title process depending on their damage and circumstances.

For a flood-damaged vehicle, never rely solely on the word “flood” in an auction listing. Determine the exact Louisiana ownership document and title brand before deciding whether the vehicle can legally return to the road.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

The exact out-of-state ownership document matters.

Do not assume that every document labeled Salvage, Junk, Nonrepairable, Parts Only, Certificate of Destruction, or a similar term provides the same rebuilding rights in Louisiana.

Before buying an out-of-state vehicle for reconstruction, verify whether Louisiana will accept its existing ownership document for a Reconstructed title. This is especially important for vehicles carrying restrictive documents that may permanently prohibit future road use in the issuing jurisdiction.

Confirm eligibility before investing in transportation or repairs.

What does Louisiana's 75% threshold mean?

Louisiana generally defines a total-loss motor vehicle as one that has sustained damage equal to 75% or more of its market value.

The statute identifies the valuation source used for that determination.

Louisiana also creates a specific exception for qualifying cosmetic hail damage. Even when the cost of specified cosmetic hail repairs reaches the 75% threshold, the vehicle is not treated as a total-loss salvage vehicle under that rule; instead, its title receives a hail-damage brand.

The percentage helps determine title treatment. It does not tell a buyer whether subsequent repairs were performed correctly.

What should I check before buying a Louisiana salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Is it Salvage, Reconstructed, Certificate of Destruction, water-damaged, or another title status?
  • Why was the vehicle declared a total loss?
  • Did the damage reach Louisiana's 75% threshold?
  • Was the damage caused by flooding or another source of water intrusion?
  • If water damaged, does the vehicle have or require a Certificate of Destruction?
  • Did an insurance company acquire the vehicle, or did the owner retain it?
  • If owner-retained, was the required Salvage title obtained?
  • Has the vehicle already been rebuilt?
  • Has the required Louisiana inspection occurred?
  • Are receipts available for major replacement components?
  • Can the source of those components be documented?
  • If it came from another state, will Louisiana accept the exact ownership document for reconstruction?
  • Has the quality of the repairs been independently evaluated?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, inspection, transportation, title, tax, registration, and other costs remain?

The central Louisiana question is:

What ownership document and damage history does this vehicle have, and does that status permit it to complete Louisiana's Salvage-to-Reconstructed process and return to ordinary road use?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Louisiana has an explicit disclosure requirement.

A person who sells, exchanges, donates, or otherwise transfers an interest in a vehicle for which a Salvage title, Certificate of Destruction, Assembled title, or Reconstructed title has been issued must disclose that title status to the recipient.

Louisiana requires the disclosure to be made in a conspicuous written document before the transaction is completed.

Failure to provide the required disclosure can give the recipient the right to seek rescission of the transaction and recovery of the purchase price and certain government fees and taxes. Knowing violations can also carry criminal penalties.

For a future seller, the Reconstructed brand is therefore not merely historical information—it creates a specific disclosure obligation.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles, Louisiana State Police, or another government agency.
