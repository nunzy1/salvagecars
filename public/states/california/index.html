California Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand how California classifies salvage vehicles, whether a vehicle can return to the road, what inspections and documents may be required, and where to find the official state instructions.

What you should know

A salvage vehicle and a non-repairable vehicle are not the same thing in California. A vehicle reported as a total loss salvage may be rebuilt and, after meeting California requirements, registered again as a revived salvage vehicle. A vehicle issued a California Nonrepairable Vehicle Certificate cannot later be titled or re-registered for on-highway use.

That makes the vehicle's current title or certificate status one of the most important things to verify before buying it.

Understand California's terminology

California uses several related terms that describe different vehicle histories and legal statuses. These terms matter because they determine which process applies.

Total loss salvage vehicle

A total loss salvage vehicle is a vehicle that has been wrecked, destroyed, or damaged to the point that the owner or insurer reports it as a total loss. California uses a Salvage Certificate as the ownership document for a total loss salvage vehicle.

Revived salvage vehicle

A revived salvage vehicle is a vehicle previously reported to California DMV as a total loss that has been rebuilt and restored to operational condition. Before it can be registered for on-highway use again, it must meet California's registration and inspection requirements.

Junk and revived junk vehicle

California treats a vehicle previously reported as dismantled or junked differently from a total-loss salvage vehicle. If such a vehicle is later rebuilt and restored to operational condition, California refers to it as a revived junk vehicle.

Non-repairable vehicle

California reserves non-repairable status for vehicles that meet specific criteria, such as certain vehicles intended only for parts or scrap, completely stripped vehicles, or completely burned shells with no usable or repairable parts. Once a Nonrepairable Vehicle Certificate has been issued, the vehicle cannot later be titled or re-registered for on-highway use.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

This is one of the first questions to answer before buying or rebuilding a damaged vehicle. A California salvage vehicle can potentially return to the road after it has been repaired and the applicable registration, verification, inspection, and other requirements have been completed.

A vehicle with a California Nonrepairable Vehicle Certificate cannot.

Before spending money on the vehicle or repairs, examine its ownership documents and determine exactly how California classifies it. If the status is unclear, verify it with California DMV before proceeding.

How do I get a salvage vehicle back on the road?

The exact requirements depend on the vehicle and its history, but a revived salvage vehicle generally requires you to establish ownership, submit the appropriate DMV application, have the vehicle verified or inspected as required, complete the Vehicle Safety Systems Inspection, satisfy any additional requirements that apply, and pay applicable fees.

Do not assume that every vehicle follows exactly the same sequence. California-record vehicles and vehicles coming from another state can have different inspection and documentation requirements.

What inspections and verifications will I need?

California's salvage process can involve more than one type of inspection. They serve different purposes.

DMV or CHP vehicle verification

A vehicle previously salvaged or junked must be verified or inspected before being registered again for on-highway use. Depending on the vehicle and circumstances, this may involve an authorized DMV verification or referral to the California Highway Patrol.

View the official Verification of Vehicle form (REG 31) →

CHP salvage inspection

If DMV refers the vehicle to CHP, the CHP Salvage Vehicle Inspection Program examines the identity of the vehicle and its component parts. CHP may review bills of sale or invoices for the vehicle and replacement components and checks identification numbers and supporting documentation for signs of alteration, improper identification, or theft.

After a successful inspection, CHP issues a CHP 97C Certificate of Inspection for use in completing the DMV registration process.

Read the official CHP Salvage Inspection Program guidance →

Vehicle Safety Systems Inspection

A revived salvage vehicle must pass California's Vehicle Safety Systems Inspection (VSSI). The inspection is performed by a licensed Vehicle Safety Systems Inspection station and covers required safety systems. The station sends the passing electronic certificate to DMV.

Some older California paperwork may still refer to separate brake and light certificates. California BAR states that those certificates are no longer required for this process; the VSSI certificate has replaced them.

If you are considering an already rebuilt vehicle, BAR also provides tools to check prior Vehicle Safety Systems Inspection results and find licensed inspection stations.

Use California BAR's official Safety Systems Inspection resources →

Smog inspection

A Smog Check may also be required depending on the vehicle and circumstances. Verify the requirement for the particular vehicle rather than assuming every revived salvage vehicle follows the same rule.

What documents and forms might I need?

California DMV identifies several core requirements for revived salvage registration. Other forms may apply depending on the vehicle, ownership history, and transaction.

Common core requirements

  • Application for Title or Registration (REG 343)

    Used to apply for title or registration, including re-registering a vehicle after salvage or junk status.

  • Proof of ownership

    Examples may include the applicable salvage/title document and bills of sale needed to establish the chain of ownership.

  • Verification of Vehicle (REG 31) or CHP Certificate of Inspection (CHP 97C), as applicable.
  • Electronic Vehicle Safety Systems Inspection certificate
  • Applicable DMV fees

Additional forms or documents that may apply

  • Application for Salvage Certificate or Nonrepairable Vehicle Certificate (REG 488C)
  • Vehicle/Vessel Transfer and Reassignment (REG 262)
  • Statement of Facts (REG 256)
  • Declaration of Gross Vehicle Weight/Combined Gross Vehicle Weight (REG 4008)
  • Permanent Trailer Identification Application and Certification (REG 4017)
  • Smog certification
  • Weight certificate for certain pickups or trucks
  • Additional ownership or bill-of-sale documentation

Do not treat this as a universal paperwork checklist. Use the current California DMV instructions to determine which forms apply to the specific vehicle.

What will it cost?

California charges fees associated with revived salvage registration and inspection. Other registration fees or penalties can also apply depending on the vehicle and its history.

State fees are only part of the cost of putting a salvage vehicle back on the road. Your total cost may also include repairs, replacement parts, safety inspection work, reinspection, smog testing when required, transportation, taxes, registration costs, and other vehicle-specific expenses.

Check current DMV fee information before making a purchase or registration decision because fees can change.

What if the salvage vehicle came from another state?

A vehicle with an out-of-state salvage history can potentially be registered in California, but the process is not necessarily identical to a California-record salvage vehicle. California has specific procedures for new or nonresident revived salvage vehicles.

Ownership documents and inspection requirements can differ depending on the out-of-state title or salvage certificate. If you are considering buying an out-of-state salvage vehicle for use in California, investigate the California requirements before buying, transporting, or repairing the vehicle.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in California?

  • What does the current title or ownership document say?
  • Is the vehicle salvage, revived salvage, junk, revived junk, non-repairable, or branded another way?
  • Can this particular classification legally return to the road?
  • If the vehicle has already been rebuilt, has it passed California's Vehicle Safety Systems Inspection?
  • Will DMV verification be sufficient, or will the vehicle require CHP inspection?
  • If CHP inspection is required, are there adequate bills of sale or invoices for replacement component parts?
  • Will the vehicle need a Smog Check?
  • If the vehicle came from another state, what additional California requirements apply?
  • Do you have the ownership documents needed to establish the chain of ownership?
  • What repairs, inspections, fees, transportation, taxes, and other costs remain before the vehicle can legally be driven?

Answering these questions before purchase can reveal costs or legal limitations that are difficult to solve after you already own the vehicle.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the California Department of Motor Vehicles, California Highway Patrol, Bureau of Automotive Repair, or another appropriate government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, and procedures can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate state agency.
