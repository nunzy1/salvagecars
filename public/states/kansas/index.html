Kansas Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Kansas salvage titles, the 75% damage threshold for late-model vehicles, rules for older damaged vehicles, Kansas Highway Patrol examinations, Rebuilt Salvage titles, owner-retained total losses, and what must happen before a salvage vehicle can return to ordinary road use.

What you should know

Kansas distinguishes between a Salvage vehicle, a Rebuilt Salvage vehicle, and a Nonrepairable vehicle.

A vehicle with a Kansas Salvage title cannot be registered for ordinary road use.

After an eligible salvage vehicle has been repaired or restored to an operable condition, it must complete the required Kansas motor vehicle examination before a Rebuilt Salvage title can be issued.

The Kansas Highway Patrol performs the rebuilt-salvage examination and affixes a notice to the vehicle identifying it as a Rebuilt Salvage vehicle.

Once a Kansas title has been branded because of salvage history, succeeding Kansas titles continue to reflect that history.

Kansas also treats newer and older damaged vehicles differently when determining whether they meet the definition of a salvage vehicle.

Understand Kansas terminology

Salvage

Kansas has several ways a vehicle can meet its definition of a Salvage vehicle.

For a late-model vehicle, damage generally triggers the salvage definition when the total cost of repair is 75% or more of the vehicle's fair market value immediately before the damage, unless the condition is merely exterior cosmetic damage caused by windstorm or hail.

Kansas defines a late-model vehicle as the model year in which the damage occurred or any of the six preceding model years.

For a vehicle that is not late model, Kansas does not simply apply the 75% threshold. An older vehicle can qualify as salvage when damage or missing or defective required equipment makes it inoperable or unsafe for highway use and substantial repairs would be necessary to restore it to a registrable condition.

A vehicle can also qualify as salvage when an insurer determines that it is a total loss and takes ownership of the vehicle and receives its title as part of the insurance settlement.

Rebuilt Salvage

A Rebuilt Salvage vehicle is a vehicle that previously received a Salvage title and has subsequently been assembled, reconstructed, reconstituted, restored, or otherwise placed in an operable condition.

After the required examination and title process, Kansas issues a Rebuilt Salvage title. The prior salvage history is not erased.

Nonrepairable

A Nonrepairable vehicle is fundamentally different from a repairable Salvage vehicle.

Kansas will not issue a title or registration for an out-of-state vehicle carrying a Nonrepairable, Junk, Certificate of Destruction, or similar restrictive ownership document. If the issuing jurisdiction permits the vehicle to be repaired and returned to roadworthy operation, that jurisdiction must first issue an appropriate Salvage or Rebuilt Salvage title before the owner applies in Kansas.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, if it has an ownership document Kansas accepts for rebuilding and the vehicle can be restored to an operable condition and complete the required examination and title process.

A Salvage title itself does not permit ordinary registration or highway operation.

Before buying, ask:

What exact ownership document does this vehicle have?

Then determine:

Will Kansas accept that document for the Rebuilt Salvage process?

Do not rely solely on an auction description such as “repairable,” “rebuildable,” “salvage,” or “parts vehicle.”

How do I get a Kansas salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible Salvage-titled vehicle, the general path is:

  1. Establish ownership with an acceptable Salvage title.
  2. Repair or restore the vehicle to an operable condition.
  3. Keep ownership and repair documentation needed for the examination and title process.
  4. Obtain the appropriate temporary inspection permit if the vehicle must be driven to the examination location.
  5. Take the vehicle to a Kansas Highway Patrol motor vehicle inspection station.
  6. Complete the required rebuilt-salvage examination and obtain the MVE-1 Motor Vehicle Examination.
  7. Have the required Rebuilt Salvage notice affixed to the vehicle during the examination.
  8. Take the required examination documentation, Salvage title or title application receipt, proof of insurance, and other required documents to the county treasurer's motor vehicle office.
  9. Apply for a Rebuilt Salvage title and registration.

What examination does Kansas require?

A vehicle moving from Salvage to Rebuilt Salvage must complete the Kansas motor vehicle examination process.

Kansas Division of Vehicles directs owners to a Kansas Highway Patrol motor vehicle inspection station to obtain the required MVE-1.

As part of the rebuilt-salvage examination, Kansas Highway Patrol affixes a notice to the left door frame identifying the vehicle by VIN and indicating that it is a Rebuilt Salvage vehicle.

The examination must be completed before a Rebuilt Salvage title can be issued.

Review K.S.A. 8-198 — rebuilt-salvage examination and notice requirements →

Does passing the examination mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

Passing the Kansas examination means the vehicle has completed the state examination required for the Rebuilt Salvage title. It should not be treated as a complete evaluation of the vehicle's repair quality or future reliability.

Before buying a Rebuilt Salvage vehicle, independently evaluate structural repairs, frame or unibody alignment, airbags and restraint systems, brakes, steering and suspension, electrical repairs, water or flood damage, mechanical condition, and the quality of replacement components and repairs.

What documentation should I keep?

Keep the vehicle's ownership documents and records supporting its repair and reconstruction.

Depending on the vehicle, useful records can include the Salvage title, repair invoices, parts receipts, bills of sale, donor-vehicle information, photographs of damage and repairs, insurance documentation, and the MVE-1 issued after examination.

Keep documentation while the vehicle is being repaired rather than trying to reconstruct its history later.

What documents and forms should I expect?

Depending on the vehicle and transaction, the Kansas process can involve:

  • the Kansas Salvage title or acceptable out-of-state ownership document,
  • the Salvage title application receipt when applicable,
  • Form TR-13, Salvage, Non-Highway or Non-Repairable Vehicle Affidavit, in situations such as an owner-retained insurance total loss,
  • a temporary inspection permit, when needed to legally drive the vehicle to its required examination,
  • the MVE-1 Motor Vehicle Examination,
  • proof of insurance,
  • title and registration documentation, and
  • applicable taxes and fees.

The temporary inspection permit is limited transportation authority. It allows the salvage vehicle to be driven for the specified examination trip; it is not permission for ordinary driving.

Can I drive the vehicle while it still has a Salvage title?

Not for ordinary road use.

Kansas provides limited permit authority for a repaired or restored Salvage vehicle that needs to travel for the required examination. The permit authorizes travel over the most direct route from the vehicle's location to the specified location named on the permit and a return to the original location.

Kansas Division of Vehicles specifically instructs an owner retaining an insurance-designated salvage vehicle to obtain an Inspection (One Day) Permit when applying for the Salvage title so the vehicle can be driven to the inspection station. The state's FAQ makes clear that ordinary license plates cannot be used for that trip once the vehicle has been deemed salvage.

Treat the permit as authority for the examination trip, not as a day of general road use.

What happens to an older vehicle that is badly damaged?

Kansas makes an important distinction between late-model and older vehicles.

For a late-model vehicle, the familiar threshold is generally repair costs of 75% or more of fair market value immediately before the damage.

A vehicle outside the late-model definition does not simply become exempt from Kansas's salvage system.

Instead, an older vehicle can qualify as Salvage when it has been wrecked or damaged so that required highway equipment is missing or defective, or the vehicle is inoperable or unsafe for highway use, and substantial repairs would be required to restore it to a condition that permits registration.

An older Kansas vehicle can therefore still require a Salvage title even though the 75% late-model calculation does not apply to it in the same way.

What happens when an insurance company totals the vehicle?

Kansas separately includes within its Salvage definition a vehicle that an insurer determines is a total loss when the insurer takes ownership of the vehicle and receives its title as part of the insurance settlement.

The insurance settlement and ownership outcome therefore matter.

Ask whether the insurer declared the vehicle a total loss, whether the insurer acquired the vehicle and its title, whether the owner instead retained it, and what title was issued after the settlement.

The phrase “insurance total loss” alone does not answer all of those questions.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company totals it?

Kansas Division of Vehicles specifically addresses owner-retained total losses.

If the insurance company designates the vehicle as salvage but the owner keeps it as part of the insurance settlement, the owner must complete the Kansas salvage-title process before moving to Rebuilt Salvage.

Kansas instructs the owner to submit Form TR-13, the current title or applicable electronic-title documentation, and a letter from the insurance company showing that the vehicle was salvaged.

When applying for the Salvage title, the owner can also obtain the Inspection (One Day) Permit needed to drive the repaired vehicle to the Kansas Highway Patrol examination. After the examination, the owner uses the MVE-1 and related documentation to apply for the Rebuilt Salvage title.

For buyers, the important point is: owner retention does not erase the salvage-title requirement when the vehicle has been designated salvage.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

Kansas requires a motor vehicle examination when applying for a Kansas title using an out-of-state title.

For a vehicle with salvage history, the exact ownership document is especially important.

Kansas will not issue a title or registration for a vehicle carrying an out-of-state Nonrepairable, Junk, Certificate of Destruction, or similar restrictive document. If the issuing jurisdiction permits the vehicle to be repaired and returned to roadworthy operation, that jurisdiction must first issue an appropriate Salvage or Rebuilt Salvage title before the owner applies in Kansas.

Do not buy a vehicle with a restrictive out-of-state ownership document assuming Kansas will convert it into a repairable Salvage title.

What does Kansas's 75% threshold mean?

The 75% threshold applies specifically to Kansas's definition of a damaged late-model vehicle.

Kansas defines late model as the vehicle's model year at the time of damage and the six preceding model years.

For those vehicles, the total cost of repair must generally be 75% or more of the vehicle's fair market value immediately before it was wrecked or damaged. Kansas excludes merely exterior cosmetic damage caused by windstorm or hail from that particular rule.

Older vehicles use a different salvage test based on their damaged condition, highway operability, required equipment, and need for substantial repairs.

So the right question is not simply:

Did repairs exceed 75%?

It is first:

Is this vehicle late model under Kansas law, and which salvage definition applies to it?

What should I check before buying a Kansas salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Is it Salvage, Rebuilt Salvage, Nonrepairable, Junk, or another title status?
  • Is the vehicle considered late model under Kansas law?
  • If late model, did repair costs reach the 75% threshold?
  • If older, does its condition independently meet Kansas's Salvage definition?
  • Did an insurance company declare it a total loss?
  • Did the insurer acquire the vehicle and its title, or did the owner retain it?
  • If owner-retained, was the required Salvage title obtained?
  • Has the vehicle been repaired or restored to an operable condition?
  • Has the Kansas Highway Patrol examination occurred?
  • Is the Rebuilt Salvage notice attached to the vehicle?
  • Is the MVE-1 available?
  • If it came from another state, what exact ownership document does it carry?
  • Is that out-of-state document eligible for a Kansas title?
  • Has repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, examination, transportation, title, tax, registration, and other costs remain?

The central Kansas question is:

What title status and salvage definition apply to this particular vehicle, and can it complete the Kansas Highway Patrol examination and qualify for a Rebuilt Salvage title?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Kansas does not remove the vehicle's salvage history after reconstruction.

Kansas Division of Vehicles states that once a title has been branded as salvage, succeeding Kansas titles continue to reflect that history.

A repaired vehicle receives a Rebuilt Salvage title rather than an ordinary unbranded title.

When selling or transferring the vehicle, accurately represent its Rebuilt Salvage status and comply with Kansas's current title-transfer requirements.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Kansas Department of Revenue Division of Vehicles, Kansas Highway Patrol, a Kansas county treasurer, or another government agency.
