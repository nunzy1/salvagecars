Arkansas Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Arkansas Salvage and Rebuilt titles, the state's damage and vehicle-age rules, water-damaged vehicles, reconstruction documentation, Parts Only vehicles, and what must happen before an eligible salvage vehicle can return to the road.

What you should know

For vehicles covered by Arkansas's salvage-title rules, a vehicle generally becomes a Salvage vehicle when it is water-damaged as defined by Arkansas's rules or has other damage equal to or exceeding 70% of its average retail value.

Arkansas also has an important vehicle-age exemption. Certain vehicles that were registered in Arkansas when damaged and are more than seven model years old before the year the damage occurred are excluded from these salvage-title requirements.

That age exemption does not mean an older vehicle can never have a Salvage or Rebuilt title. In particular, Arkansas carries forward salvage and similar brands from other states regardless of the vehicle's age.

An eligible vehicle with a Salvage title can be rebuilt and receive an Arkansas title bearing the notation REBUILT.

A vehicle classified as junk, nonrepairable, Parts Only, or a similar designation does not have the same path back to registration.

Understand Arkansas terminology

Salvage vehicle

Under Arkansas's current salvage-title rules, a covered motor vehicle is a Salvage vehicle if it:

  • is water-damaged as defined by Arkansas's rules, or
  • sustains other damage equal to or exceeding 70% of its average retail value

Arkansas uses an approved pricing guide to determine average retail value for the percentage calculation.

A vehicle subject to the state's salvage-title requirements receives a title bearing the notation SALVAGE.

Rebuilt vehicle

A Rebuilt or reconstructed motor vehicle is a vehicle that previously had a Salvage, damaged, water-damaged, reconstructed, rebuilt, or similar title and has been repaired to the extent that it can be licensed for use on public streets and highways.

When Arkansas issues the new title after the reconstruction requirements have been completed, the title bears the notation REBUILT.

The Rebuilt notation remains part of the vehicle's title history.

Parts Only vehicle

Arkansas can issue a Parts Only title for a vehicle that has no resale value except as a source of parts or scrap.

The title bears the notations PARTS ONLY and Not for Registration.

Vehicles considered junk, nonrepairable, or similarly classified are not eligible for Arkansas registration through the ordinary Rebuilt process.

Before buying a severely damaged vehicle, determine exactly what ownership document it has. A Salvage title and a Parts Only or nonrepairable document do not provide the same options.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, if the vehicle has an eligible Salvage title and can complete Arkansas's reconstruction and title requirements.

After the vehicle has been rebuilt or reconstructed, the repairer or rebuilder must complete an Affidavit of Reconstruction for a Salvage Motor Vehicle. The owner then uses that affidavit and the existing Salvage title when applying for a new title bearing the REBUILT notation.

A vehicle classified as Parts Only, junk, nonrepairable, or subject to a similar restriction cannot simply use the Rebuilt process to return to normal registration.

Before buying, ask:

What exact title or ownership document does this vehicle have?

Then ask:

Does Arkansas provide a path from that document to the title and registration I need?

How do I get an Arkansas salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible vehicle with a Salvage title, the general process is:

  1. Repair or reconstruct the vehicle so that it can be licensed for use on public streets and highways.
  2. Keep the information needed to identify all parts and components repaired or replaced, including the donor vehicle VIN or VIN derivative when Arkansas requires it.
  3. Have the repairer or rebuilder complete and sign the Affidavit of Reconstruction for a Salvage Motor Vehicle.
  4. Have the vehicle owner acknowledge receipt of the affidavit.
  5. Apply for an Arkansas title bearing the REBUILT notation.
  6. Surrender the existing Salvage title.
  7. Submit the completed Affidavit of Reconstruction and the other documentation required for title and registration.
  8. Complete the applicable registration, insurance, tax, and other requirements before ordinary road use.

A VIN derivative is a partial identifying number derived from the vehicle's full VIN; it is not a new VIN.

Arkansas's rule requires the owner to apply for the new Rebuilt title within 10 working days after the vehicle has been rebuilt or reconstructed. This filing period should not be treated as authorization to operate the vehicle on public roads before the applicable title and registration requirements have been completed.

Does Arkansas require a rebuilt-vehicle inspection?

Arkansas's published process for an Arkansas Salvage vehicle focuses on the Affidavit of Reconstruction rather than describing a separate general safety or repair-quality inspection as part of the ordinary Rebuilt-title process.

The repairer or rebuilder must disclose the repairs made and list the parts and components that were repaired or replaced. The current affidavit also requires the repairer or rebuilder to certify that structural repairs comply with manufacturer and/or industry-approved standards.

This documentation requirement should not be confused with an independent evaluation of the vehicle's current condition or repair quality.

Arkansas does separately require VIN verification in certain situations involving vehicles with out-of-state titles carrying Salvage, Prior Salvage, or similar brands.

Does receiving a Rebuilt title mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

A buyer should not treat an Arkansas Rebuilt title or completed Affidavit of Reconstruction as a substitute for an independent evaluation of the vehicle's condition or repair quality.

Before buying a Rebuilt vehicle, consider independently evaluating:

  • structural repairs
  • frame or unibody alignment
  • airbags and restraint systems
  • brakes, steering, and suspension
  • electrical repairs
  • water or flood damage
  • mechanical condition
  • wheel alignment and tire wear
  • the quality and origin of replacement components

The useful question is not simply:

Does the vehicle have an Arkansas Rebuilt title?

It is also:

What was damaged, how was it repaired, and what condition is the vehicle in now?

What documentation should I keep for replacement parts?

Arkansas's current Affidavit of Reconstruction focuses on identifying the repairs and replacement components rather than requiring a general submission of receipts or bills of sale for every major component.

The affidavit requires disclosure of the parts repaired or replaced during reconstruction. For specified replacement components, the VIN of the vehicle from which the part was obtained must be listed. Certain individually numbered parts require a VIN or VIN derivative.

Components identified on the current affidavit include items such as:

  • cowl assembly
  • frame
  • transmission
  • front clip
  • truck cab
  • transaxle
  • front-end assembly
  • truck bed
  • transfer case
  • rear clip
  • engine

The affidavit identifies additional individual parts for which a VIN or VIN derivative must be provided if the part is numbered.

For a water-damaged vehicle, the current affidavit specifically requires all replaced electrical components to be listed.

Do not wait until the rebuild is finished to reconstruct the vehicle's parts history. Keep the information needed for the affidavit as repairs are performed and replacement components are acquired.

What documents should I expect?

Depending on the vehicle and transaction, the Arkansas process can involve:

  • the existing Salvage title
  • an Affidavit of Reconstruction for a Salvage Motor Vehicle
  • an application for registration and certificate of title
  • information identifying repairs and replacement components
  • VIN or VIN-derivative information for applicable replacement components
  • a VIN verification for certain out-of-state branded vehicles
  • proof of insurance and other ordinary registration documents
  • applicable taxes and fees

Use the Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration's current forms and instructions when you are ready to apply.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company declares it a total loss?

Keeping the vehicle does not necessarily prevent Arkansas's salvage-title rules from applying.

If an insurer pays a claim on a vehicle that meets Arkansas's definition of a Salvage vehicle but does not take ownership of the vehicle, the insurer must notify the Arkansas Office of Motor Vehicle using the state's Insurer Notification and Declaration of Damage for a Salvage Motor Vehicle.

The owner can therefore retain the vehicle while its salvage status is still reported to the state.

For an owner-retained vehicle, determine:

  • whether the vehicle meets Arkansas's Salvage definition
  • whether the vehicle qualifies for the vehicle-age exemption
  • whether the insurer has reported the vehicle as Salvage
  • what title the vehicle has after the claim
  • whether any liens must be satisfied
  • whether the vehicle remains eligible for reconstruction
  • what documentation will be required for a Rebuilt title

Confirm the vehicle's title status before beginning substantial repairs.

What happens to an older vehicle that an insurance company declares a total loss?

Arkansas has an important age rule that differs from many states.

For purposes of these salvage-title requirements, Arkansas's rule excludes a motor vehicle that is more than seven model years old before the year in which the damage occurred.

The state explains the calculation by subtracting eight from the current calendar year to determine the latest excluded model year.

This exemption applies to qualifying vehicles that were registered in Arkansas at the time of the damage.

It does not mean that every older vehicle is automatically free of title-branding issues.

An out-of-state Salvage or similarly branded ownership document is treated differently. Arkansas's Affidavit of Reconstruction instructions state that the out-of-state brand is carried forward regardless of vehicle age and that an out-of-state vehicle is not eligible for the age exemption provided to qualifying Arkansas-registered vehicles.

For an older total-loss vehicle, determine:

  • where the vehicle was registered when the damage occurred
  • the model year
  • the year the damage occurred
  • whether the vehicle meets Arkansas's Salvage definition
  • whether an insurer acquired the vehicle or the owner retained it
  • whether another state has already issued a branded ownership document
  • what ownership document exists now

Do not apply Arkansas's age exemption to an out-of-state branded vehicle without first checking the applicable requirements.

What about water-damaged vehicles?

Arkansas has a specific definition of water-damaged.

Under the current rule, a vehicle is water-damaged when it has been submerged or partially submerged in water at a level above the dashboard, regardless of the actual dollar amount of the damage.

That is important because the ordinary 70% damage threshold does not need to be met for a vehicle that satisfies Arkansas's water-damage definition.

The Affidavit of Reconstruction also requires all replaced electrical components to be listed for a water-damaged vehicle.

For a water-damaged vehicle, investigate more than the title brand.

Consider the possibility of damage involving:

  • electrical systems
  • electronic control modules
  • connectors and wiring
  • airbags and restraint systems
  • corrosion
  • interior materials
  • mechanical components
  • mold or contamination

Water-related problems can appear after a vehicle has been repaired and may not be obvious during a brief inspection.

Before buying, determine what type of water exposure occurred, how high the water reached, how long the vehicle was exposed, and what systems were repaired or replaced.

What if the vehicle comes from another state?

Arkansas carries forward Salvage, Rebuilt, Parts Only, and similar designations issued by another state. The designation is printed on subsequent Arkansas titles.

The age exemption available to certain vehicles registered in Arkansas when they were damaged does not erase an out-of-state salvage or similar brand.

When an out-of-state Salvage or similarly branded vehicle has been rebuilt, the owner must surrender the out-of-state ownership document and submit the Affidavit of Reconstruction when applying for the Arkansas Rebuilt title.

Arkansas also requires VIN verification for vehicles with out-of-state titles branded Salvage, Prior Salvage, or with similar designations. The state's registration guidance says the verification may be performed by a certified law enforcement officer of an Arkansas city or county, the Arkansas State Police, or a designee of the Arkansas State Police.

Before buying an out-of-state vehicle, determine:

  • the exact ownership document
  • every brand shown on that document
  • whether the vehicle has already been rebuilt
  • whether Arkansas will carry the brand forward
  • whether the vehicle can qualify for an Arkansas Rebuilt title
  • whether the required reconstruction documentation is available
  • whether VIN verification will be required
  • whether the document indicates Junk, Nonrepairable, Parts Only, or another status that prevents registration

What should I check before buying an Arkansas salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Is the title Salvage, Rebuilt, Parts Only, or another classification?
  • Why was the vehicle declared Salvage?
  • What percentage of its average retail value did the damage represent?
  • Was the vehicle water damaged?
  • How old was the vehicle when the damage occurred?
  • Where was it registered when the damage occurred?
  • Has it already been rebuilt?
  • Is an Affidavit of Reconstruction available?
  • Are the repairs and replacement components documented?
  • Are required VINs or VIN derivatives available for replacement components?
  • Has the repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Are there unresolved liens or ownership issues?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • If financing is needed, will a lender accept the vehicle's title history?
  • What repair, transportation, title, tax, registration, and other costs remain?

For an unrepaired vehicle, one particularly important question is:

Will I have the information Arkansas requires when I apply for the Rebuilt title?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Arkansas has specific buyer-notification requirements for vehicles with Salvage or Rebuilt title brands.

The state's Salvage Title Buyers Notification tells buyers that a seller offering a Salvage vehicle for sale is required to place the buyer notification on a side window of the vehicle.

The seller must also provide information about:

  • the title brand
  • the damage sustained
  • repairs performed
  • parts replaced

The buyer signs an acknowledgement that the vehicle bears the disclosed title brand.

Arkansas's notification form provides for both SALVAGE and REBUILT title brands.

The repair information is based on the sworn reconstruction information maintained by the Arkansas Office of Motor Vehicle.

If you later sell a Salvage or Rebuilt vehicle, do not treat its branded title history as something the buyer is expected to discover independently. Follow Arkansas's current disclosure and buyer-notification requirements.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration, Office of Motor Vehicle, or another government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, and procedures can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate Arkansas agency.
