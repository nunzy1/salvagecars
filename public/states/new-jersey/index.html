New Jersey Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand New Jersey salvage titles, required photographs, replacement-part documentation, MVC salvage inspections, and whether a salvage vehicle can return to road use.

What you should know

New Jersey requires salvage vehicles to receive a Salvage Certificate of Title. A salvage vehicle that is later reconstructed, rebuilt, or repaired must complete a special New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission salvage inspection before it can receive an operable title.

New Jersey's process places particular emphasis on documenting the vehicle before and after repairs and establishing where major replacement components came from.

Before beginning a rebuild, take the required photographs and understand the documentation you will need.

Waiting until the repairs are complete can make the process more difficult.

Understand New Jersey terminology

Salvage vehicle

New Jersey describes a salvage vehicle as a vehicle that has been wrecked, destroyed, or damaged to such an extent that the insurance company considers it uneconomical to repair.

All salvage vehicles must be titled in New Jersey, regardless of whether the owner intends to restore them.

The salvage title identifies the vehicle as salvage and prevents it from being treated as an ordinary road vehicle before the applicable rebuilt process is completed.

Salvage Certificate of Title

The Salvage Certificate of Title establishes the vehicle's salvage ownership status.

A salvage vehicle generally cannot be registered for normal operation on New Jersey public highways while it remains in salvage status.

If the vehicle is subsequently reconstructed, rebuilt, or repaired, the salvage certificate becomes one of the documents required for the MVC salvage inspection and later title process.

Reconstructed, rebuilt, or repaired salvage vehicle

New Jersey uses the terms reconstructed, rebuilt, or repaired for salvage vehicles that have undergone repairs after receiving salvage status.

Before the MVC issues a certificate of ownership for a qualifying rebuilt salvage vehicle, the vehicle must undergo the required salvage inspection.

The resulting certificate of ownership continues to carry a salvage designation, preserving the vehicle's salvage history.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially.

A New Jersey salvage vehicle that is eligible for rebuilding can potentially be repaired, inspected, and converted to an operable title.

However, the salvage certificate itself does not authorize normal highway use.

Does this vehicle have an ownership document that allows it to enter New Jersey's rebuilt-salvage process?

Can I document the vehicle, the repairs, and the replacement components well enough to pass the MVC salvage inspection?

Those questions should be answered before substantial repair work begins.

How do I get a New Jersey salvage vehicle back on the road?

  1. Obtain or confirm the New Jersey Salvage Certificate of Title.
  2. After taking possession of the salvage vehicle, take the required color photographs showing its condition before repairs.
  3. Repair or rebuild the vehicle.
  4. Keep detailed bills of sale for each major component part used in the repairs.
  5. Record the required information about the source of used replacement components, including donor-vehicle VIN information when available.
  6. Take the required photographs showing the vehicle after repairs.
  7. Complete the applicable salvage inspection paperwork.
  8. Submit the required documents to the selected salvage inspection site for review and approval.
  9. After the documents are approved, obtain an appointment for the MVC salvage inspection.
  10. Transport the vehicle to the inspection site using an authorized method.
  11. Present the vehicle for the salvage inspection.
  12. If the vehicle passes and all requirements are satisfied, complete the MVC process for issuance of the applicable certificate of ownership with the salvage designation.

Review the current MVC instructions before beginning the process because forms, fees, inspection locations, and procedures can change.

Why should I take photographs before starting repairs?

Because New Jersey requires them.

The MVC salvage inspection instructions require before-and-after color photographs of the vehicle.

The required before-repair photographs include:

  • the entire front and left side of the vehicle
  • the entire rear and right side of the vehicle

The after-repair photographs must show those same general views after the rebuilding work has been completed.

The photographs must be clear and must not cut off portions of the vehicle.

Take the required photographs before you begin repairs.

If you are buying a vehicle that someone else has already started repairing, ask for the original before-repair photographs before completing the purchase.

What if repairs started before the photographs were taken?

If repairs were started before the required before-repair photographs could be taken, the owner must obtain a damage report from the insurance company.

MVC states that the insurance damage report can substitute for the before photographs. It does not replace the required after-repair photographs.

The current owner is responsible for obtaining the insurance damage report; MVC does not provide it to salvage-inspection customers.

If the original before-repair photographs do not exist, can the required insurance damage report actually be obtained?

Do not assume the missing documentation will be easy to replace after purchasing the vehicle.

Why are parts receipts important?

New Jersey requires a bill of sale for each major component part used to reconstruct, rebuild, or repair the vehicle.

For a new part, the bill of sale must identify the part and part number.

For a used part, documentation generally must identify:

  • the part
  • the seller and buyer
  • the purchase date and price
  • the year, make, and model of the donor vehicle
  • the VIN of the donor vehicle, when available
  • when the part was removed, when applicable
  • who removed or dismantled the part

If the donor vehicle VIN is not available, the owner must obtain a statement from the seller explaining why it is unavailable.

A replacement component without adequate documentation can create a problem during the salvage inspection.

What does New Jersey consider a major component part?

New Jersey's salvage-inspection requirements identify major components that can require supporting documentation.

  • engine
  • transmission or transaxle
  • front and rear bumpers
  • front fenders
  • hood or engine cover
  • doors
  • quarter panels
  • decklid, tailgate, or hatchback
  • roof
  • cowl
  • frame
  • shock tower or apron

New Jersey also identifies assembled sections such as a nose, front clip, rear clip, and short clip.

Because the exact documentation requirement depends on the components used in the particular rebuild, use the current MVC salvage-inspection instructions when preparing the vehicle.

What happens before I can schedule the salvage inspection?

New Jersey requires the salvage-inspection paperwork to be reviewed before the inspection appointment is scheduled.

The current MVC instructions state that all required documents must be sent to the preferred salvage inspection site for review and approval. Only after the paperwork has been reviewed and approved can an inspection appointment be made.

Before submitting the inspection request, make sure you have the applicable:

  • salvage title
  • before-and-after photographs
  • parts receipts and bills of sale
  • donor-vehicle information
  • work order
  • salvage inspection application materials
  • additional documentation required for the particular vehicle

Use the current MVC instructions for the submission method and inspection-site information rather than relying on an older address, email address, or location list.

What does the New Jersey salvage inspection check?

New Jersey's salvage inspection is strongly focused on vehicle identity and the legitimacy of the components used to rebuild the vehicle.

State regulations require inspection of qualifying reconstructed, rebuilt, or repaired salvage vehicles to verify the vehicle identification number and identification numbers associated with major component parts.

The inspection and documentation requirements help New Jersey determine whether the vehicle matches the salvage title, identifying information is valid, major replacement components are properly documented, components came from legitimate sources, and the vehicle was reconstructed using parts associated with stolen vehicles.

The MVC can inspect identifying numbers and, when necessary, remove or access components to verify vehicle identification.

Passing the salvage inspection should not be treated as a general guarantee of the quality, durability, or future reliability of the repairs.

Can I drive a salvage vehicle to the inspection?

A salvage vehicle generally cannot be registered for operation on New Jersey public highways before the applicable rebuilt-title process is completed.

For the salvage inspection, the current MVC instructions state that the vehicle must either be:

  • towed to the inspection site,
  • transported using an authorized temporary registration, or
  • transported using dealer plates when the applicable dealer requirements are satisfied.

New Jersey regulations provide for a limited temporary registration for eligible salvage vehicles in connection with the inspection process.

Treat that as a specific transportation mechanism for the inspection—not as general permission to drive a salvage vehicle.

Verify the current requirements before transporting the vehicle.

What if the vehicle was stolen and later recovered?

New Jersey has separate procedures for recovered stolen vehicles.

The process depends in part on whether the vehicle was damaged, whether it was rendered economically impractical to repair, whether the VIN or VIN plate is missing, altered, or damaged, whether major components were missing, and what documentation law enforcement produced when the vehicle was recovered.

A stolen vehicle recovered in heavily damaged condition can enter the salvage/rebuilt process.

Different procedures can apply when a recovered stolen vehicle has an intact VIN and was not damaged enough to make repair economically impractical.

If you are considering a theft-recovery vehicle, determine the vehicle's actual title status and what MVC documentation or inspection is required.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

An out-of-state salvage vehicle can require New Jersey salvage-title processing before it enters New Jersey's rebuilt process. The exact ownership document matters.

  • inspect the actual title or ownership document
  • identify every brand or restriction
  • determine whether New Jersey will issue a salvage certificate based on that document
  • determine whether the vehicle is eligible for reconstruction
  • determine what documentation MVC will require
  • verify whether the vehicle must complete the New Jersey salvage inspection

Do not rely solely on the terminology used in an auction listing.

Does passing the salvage inspection mean the vehicle is safe to buy?

No.

New Jersey's salvage process is designed in significant part to verify vehicle identity, component identity, ownership documentation, and the legitimacy of the rebuilt vehicle. That is different from an independent assessment of whether the repair work was performed well.

  • structural repair quality
  • suspension and steering
  • wheel alignment
  • airbags and restraint systems
  • electrical systems
  • corrosion
  • flood damage
  • mechanical condition
  • quality of replacement components
  • evidence of incomplete or poor repairs

Also investigate whether you can obtain the insurance coverage you want and how the salvage history could affect future resale.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in New Jersey?

  • What is the exact ownership document?
  • Has a New Jersey Salvage Certificate of Title already been issued?
  • Why was the vehicle declared salvage?
  • What damage did it sustain?
  • Have repairs already begun?
  • Do the required before-repair photographs exist?
  • If not, is an insurance damage report available?
  • Who performed the repairs?
  • What major component parts were replaced?
  • Are bills of sale available for those components?
  • Do receipts contain the information New Jersey requires?
  • Is donor-vehicle VIN information available for used components?
  • Are the vehicle's identifying numbers intact?
  • Are after-repair photographs available?
  • Is there a work order documenting the repairs?
  • Has the inspection paperwork already been reviewed by MVC?
  • Has the vehicle already undergone the salvage inspection?
  • If it came from another state, will New Jersey accept its ownership document into the salvage process?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, inspection, title, tax, and transportation costs remain?
  • Has the quality of completed repairs been independently evaluated?

The central New Jersey question is:

Can I document this vehicle's condition, repairs, and replacement components well enough to complete New Jersey's salvage inspection and title process?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

A New Jersey salvage vehicle that is reconstructed, rebuilt, or repaired and successfully completes the required process receives a certificate of ownership that continues to carry a salvage designation.

The salvage history therefore does not disappear when the vehicle becomes operable.

  • accurately represent its salvage history
  • do not imply that it has an ordinary unbranded title
  • provide the actual title to the buyer
  • comply with the applicable New Jersey ownership-transfer requirements

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission or another appropriate government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, inspection locations, title procedures, and other requirements can change and can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate government agency.
