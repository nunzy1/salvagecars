Maryland Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Maryland salvage certificates, Rebuilt Salvage branding, salvage inspections, safety inspections, parts documentation, and what must happen before a salvage vehicle can return to ordinary road use.

What you should know

Maryland uses a salvage certificate as an ownership document for vehicles that enter the state's salvage system.

Maryland salvage certificates can carry different notations depending on why the vehicle became salvage and whether it remains eligible to return to titled road use.

A repairable vehicle with damage greater than 75% of its pre-damage fair market value can potentially return to road use after repair and completion of Maryland's salvage inspection and titling process.

If the salvage certificate states that the vehicle is not rebuildable, will be used for parts only, and is not to be retitled, Maryland may not issue a certificate of title for the vehicle.

For an eligible repairable vehicle, the resulting Maryland title carries the Rebuilt Salvage notation.

Maryland's salvage inspection and ordinary vehicle safety inspection serve different purposes. Do not treat one as a substitute for the other.

Understand Maryland terminology

Salvage certificate

A Maryland salvage certificate is an ownership document.

Maryland uses salvage certificates in several circumstances, including qualifying insurer claim settlements and certain vehicles acquired by automotive dismantlers and recyclers. The certificate carries a notation describing the vehicle's salvage category.

Review Maryland's Salvage Certificate/Owner Retention Application and definitions →

Rebuilt Salvage

When an eligible repairable vehicle whose salvage certificate reflects damage greater than 75% of its pre-damage fair market value completes the applicable process, Maryland issues a certificate of title conspicuously branded Rebuilt Salvage.

The brand identifies the vehicle's previous salvage history and is carried forward to subsequent Maryland titles.

Flood Damaged

Maryland separately identifies vehicles that sustained flood damage.

When the applicable salvage certificate carries the flood-damage notation and the vehicle later qualifies for a certificate of title, Maryland law requires the resulting title to contain a conspicuous Flood Damaged notation.

X-Salvage

Maryland also uses the X-Salvage title notation in certain circumstances.

Under Maryland law, this includes certain vehicles acquired by an insurance company when the cost to repair the vehicle is 75% or less of its pre-damage fair market value, as well as certain vehicles entering the salvage system through an automotive dismantler and recycler.

X-Salvage should therefore not be treated as another name for every rebuilt salvage vehicle.

Not rebuildable / parts only

This is fundamentally different from a repairable salvage vehicle.

If the salvage certificate states that the vehicle is not rebuildable, will be used for parts only, and is not to be retitled, Maryland law prohibits issuance of a certificate of title for that vehicle.

Before buying a vehicle for rebuilding, verify the exact notation on its ownership document.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, depending on the notation on its salvage certificate or other ownership document.

The first question should be:

What exact ownership document and salvage notation does the vehicle have?

Then ask:

Does that notation permit Maryland to issue a certificate of title after the vehicle is repaired and inspected?

A repairable salvage vehicle can potentially return to titled road use.

A vehicle designated not rebuildable, parts only, or not to be retitled does not have the same path.

Do not rely solely on auction descriptions such as “repairable,” “rebuildable,” “salvage,” or “parts vehicle.” The actual ownership document determines which Maryland process applies.

How do I get a Maryland salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible repairable vehicle, the general path is:

  1. Obtain the appropriate salvage ownership document in your name.
  2. Repair the vehicle until it is fully repaired and roadworthy.
  3. Keep bills of sale and receipts for major repairs and replacement parts.
  4. Schedule the required Maryland salvage inspection.
  5. Bring the vehicle, salvage certificate, identification, proof of ownership, repair receipts, and other required documentation to the inspection.
  6. Pass the salvage inspection.
  7. Apply to the Maryland MVA for the appropriate certificate of title.
  8. Complete Maryland's separate vehicle safety inspection requirements before ordinary registration, when required.

After a successful salvage inspection, the inspector signs and embosses the salvage certificate. The owner can then proceed with the MVA titling process.

What inspection does Maryland require?

Maryland requires a salvage inspection before an eligible salvage vehicle can obtain the applicable Maryland title.

The Maryland State Police Automotive Safety Enforcement Division administers the salvage inspection program.

During the inspection, a certified salvage inspector checks the vehicle's identification numbers and verifies that stolen parts were not used in rebuilding the vehicle.

The owner must bring documentation that can include:

  • valid identification,
  • the salvage certificate,
  • the vehicle,
  • appointment confirmation,
  • proof of ownership, and
  • repair receipts.

If the VIN plate is missing or damaged, Maryland State Police directs the vehicle to a different Auto Theft Unit inspection procedure.

Is the salvage inspection the same as the Maryland safety inspection?

No.

This distinction is important.

The salvage inspection focuses on the vehicle's identity and verifying that stolen parts were not used in the rebuild.

The separate Maryland vehicle safety inspection evaluates whether the vehicle meets Maryland's minimum safety standards, including systems such as brakes, tires, lights, steering, and suspension.

After passing the salvage inspection, the owner can proceed with titling and may obtain a temporary registration to complete the required safety inspection.

Passing the salvage inspection therefore should not be interpreted as proof that the vehicle has passed Maryland's ordinary safety inspection.

What documentation should I keep for replacement parts and repairs?

Keep bills of sale and receipts for major repairs and replacement parts.

Maryland State Police requires repair receipts as part of the salvage inspection documentation, and the MVA's current titling guidance identifies bills of sale or receipts for major repairs and parts among the documents used in the rebuilt-title process.

These documents help establish where the vehicle's components came from and support the anti-theft purpose of the salvage inspection.

Parts and repair documentation should therefore be collected during the rebuild rather than reconstructed after the vehicle is finished.

What documents and forms should I expect?

Depending on the vehicle and transaction, Maryland's salvage and rebuilt process can involve:

  • the vehicle's salvage certificate or other acceptable ownership document,
  • Certificate of Title Application — Form VR-005,
  • Salvage Certificate/Owner Retention Application — Form VR-028,
  • identification,
  • bills of sale or receipts for major repairs and parts,
  • salvage inspection documentation,
  • safety inspection certification when required, and
  • applicable title, tax, and registration documents.

The exact documents depend on the vehicle's salvage category and transaction.

Can I drive a vehicle while it still has only a salvage certificate?

Do not treat a salvage certificate as authorization for ordinary road use.

Maryland State Police requires the vehicle to be fully repaired and roadworthy before its salvage inspection.

After the vehicle passes the salvage inspection, the owner can proceed to the MVA and may obtain a 30-day temporary registration to complete the regular Maryland safety inspection.

Before that point, determine the lawful transportation method that applies rather than assuming the salvage certificate allows the vehicle to be driven.

What if the owner keeps the vehicle after a total-loss settlement?

Maryland specifically addresses owner-retained total-loss vehicles.

For purposes of Maryland's salvage law, a vehicle is not considered to have been acquired by the insurance company when the owner retains possession after settlement of the claim.

The insurance company nevertheless must notify the MVA when the applicable conditions exist, including certain vehicles with repair costs greater than 75% of fair market value, flood damage, or non-repairable/parts-only status.

Maryland's Salvage Certificate/Owner Retention Application — Form VR-028 includes the owner-retention process.

The exact result depends on the vehicle's condition and applicable salvage category, so an owner-retained vehicle should not automatically be treated the same as an insurer-owned salvage vehicle.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

Maryland recognizes salvage history from other states.

Maryland State Police states that a vehicle declared salvage in Maryland or another state generally must pass the applicable inspection before obtaining a Maryland title or registration.

However, a vehicle that has already completed a salvage inspection in another state may not always need another Maryland salvage inspection. Verify the requirement with Maryland State Police before scheduling.

Maryland law also requires the MVA to carry forward an out-of-state title notation when the other state's designation is substantially similar to Maryland's Rebuilt Salvage classification.

Before buying an out-of-state vehicle, ask:

What exact ownership document and title brand does the vehicle have?

Then ask:

How will Maryland classify that document and what inspection will Maryland require?

A restrictive document such as Nonrepairable, Parts Only, Junk, or Certificate of Destruction should not be assumed to provide the same path as an ordinary repairable salvage certificate.

What does Maryland's 75% threshold mean?

Maryland's salvage law uses a 75% repair-cost threshold in important parts of its classification system.

For the applicable repairable category, the cost to repair the vehicle for highway operation is greater than 75% of its fair market value before the damage occurred.

Maryland law excludes towing, storage, vehicle rental, and repair of cosmetic damage from that calculation.

The 75% calculation is important, but it is not the only way a vehicle can enter Maryland's salvage system.

For example, insurer acquisition after a claim settlement, flood damage, theft recovery, and acquisition through certain automotive dismantler and recycler circumstances can lead to different salvage classifications.

Does passing the salvage inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

Maryland's salvage inspection helps establish vehicle identity and verifies that stolen parts were not used in the rebuild.

Maryland's separate safety inspection determines whether the vehicle meets the state's minimum safety standards.

Neither tells you whether the vehicle is a good financial purchase or whether every repair was performed to a high standard.

Before buying a rebuilt vehicle, independently investigate structural repairs, steering and suspension, alignment, brakes, airbags and restraint systems, electrical systems, mechanical condition, flood or water damage, corrosion, replacement components, and evidence of incomplete or poorly performed repairs.

Also investigate whether you can obtain the insurance coverage you want and how the vehicle's title history could affect future resale.

What should I check before buying a Maryland salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • What notation appears on the salvage certificate or title?
  • Is the vehicle repairable, or is it designated not rebuildable or parts only?
  • Why did the vehicle enter the salvage system?
  • Has repair work already started?
  • Are receipts available for major repairs and replacement parts?
  • Can the source of replacement components be established?
  • Has the Maryland salvage inspection already occurred?
  • Has the separate Maryland safety inspection occurred?
  • If the vehicle came from another state, what brands appear on its ownership document?
  • How will Maryland treat those brands?
  • Has repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, inspection, transportation, title, tax, registration, and other costs remain?

The central Maryland question is:

What salvage category and ownership document does this vehicle have, and does that classification permit it to complete Maryland's inspection and titling process and return to ordinary road use?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Maryland uses conspicuous title notations to preserve important information about a vehicle's history.

Depending on the underlying salvage category, the resulting Maryland title can carry a notation such as Rebuilt Salvage, Flood Damaged, or X-Salvage.

Maryland also carries forward certain substantially similar rebuilt-salvage designations from other states.

If you later sell the vehicle, accurately represent its branded title and salvage history and comply with the transfer and disclosure requirements applicable to the transaction.

The title brand should be treated as part of the vehicle's continuing history rather than something eliminated by completing the rebuilding process.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration, Maryland State Police, or another government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, inspections, and procedures can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate Maryland agency.
