North Dakota Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand North Dakota Salvage Certificates of Title, Previously Salvaged titles, the 75% damage threshold, inspections, damage disclosures, and how eligible vehicles can return to the road.

What you should know

North Dakota requires a Salvage Certificate of Title when a vehicle’s damage exceeds 75% of its retail value, using the valuation guide specified by state law. Glass and hail damage are excluded from that calculation.

When the vehicle meets the salvage threshold, the owner must submit the title to the North Dakota Department of Transportation (NDDOT) within 10 days.

Repairing a salvage vehicle does not, by itself, make it eligible for ordinary road use. A qualifying motor vehicle repair business that did not perform the reconstruction must inspect it for compliance with applicable equipment requirements. Vehicle identification must also be verified.

After the requirements are satisfied, NDDOT may issue a regular Certificate of Title branded Previously Salvaged. The brand remains on subsequent titles.

North Dakota will not issue a title based on an out-of-state document marked Junk, Parts Only, Non-Rebuildable, Unrepairable, or a similar restricted designation.

Understand North Dakota terminology

Salvage Vehicle

A vehicle requiring salvage titling has sustained damage exceeding 75% of its retail value under the valuation method specified by law. Glass and hail damage are excluded from the calculation.

Salvage Certificate of Title

This document establishes ownership of a vehicle classified as salvage. It does not establish that the vehicle has completed the reconstruction and inspection process or is eligible for ordinary road registration.

Previously Salvaged Vehicle

A vehicle that was issued a Salvage Certificate of Title and subsequently completed the required reconstruction, inspection, and retitling process. Its regular title carries the Previously Salvaged brand, which remains on later North Dakota titles.

Damaged Vehicle

North Dakota also has a separate damage-disclosure rule for certain title transfers. Qualifying damage generally involves the greater of $10,000 or 25% of the vehicle’s pre-damage retail value. A vehicle may have reportable damage without exceeding the 75% salvage threshold.

Junk, Parts-Only, or Nonrebuildable Vehicle

NDDOT does not accept out-of-state titles marked Certificate of Destruction, Dismantled, Junk, Non-Rebuildable, Parts Only, Unrepairable, or similar restrictions. These documents cannot be used to obtain an ordinary North Dakota road-use title through the salvage rebuilding process.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, if the vehicle has an eligible Salvage Certificate of Title and can complete North Dakota’s reconstruction and retitling requirements.

  • Reconstruct the vehicle and retain relevant repair records
  • Obtain an inspection from a qualifying repair business that did not perform the reconstruction
  • Verify compliance with applicable vehicle equipment requirements
  • Complete required VIN verification
  • Submit the salvage certificate and inspection documents
  • Obtain a Previously Salvaged title and complete registration and insurance requirements

A Salvage Certificate of Title alone does not authorize ordinary road use. Restricted out-of-state documents may prevent the state from issuing a title at all. Confirm the vehicle’s eligibility with NDDOT before buying.

How do I get a North Dakota salvage vehicle back on the road?

  1. Obtain the Salvage Certificate of Title or other acceptable ownership document.
  2. Confirm with NDDOT that the vehicle is eligible for reconstruction and retitling.
  3. Complete necessary repairs and keep repair and parts records.
  4. Arrange inspection by a qualifying motor vehicle repair business that did not perform the reconstruction.
  5. Have the inspector complete Certificate of Motor Vehicle Inspection (SFN 2486).
  6. Complete any required VIN inspection or provide other identity evidence acceptable to NDDOT.
  7. Submit the salvage certificate, inspection documents, title application, and other required materials.
  8. Pay applicable fees, including the statutory $5 fee for the replacement title after reconstruction.
  9. Obtain the Previously Salvaged title and complete registration requirements before ordinary road operation.

The inspecting business must be registered with the North Dakota Secretary of State, be in good standing, and offer motor vehicle repair services to the public. The business that reconstructed the vehicle cannot perform the required inspection.

What inspections and verification are required?

Independent repair-business inspection

A qualifying business that did not perform the reconstruction must inspect the vehicle and certify compliance with applicable vehicle equipment requirements under North Dakota Century Code Chapter 39-21. The business documents the inspection on SFN 2486.

Frame and wheel alignment

The inspection form includes an acknowledgment addressing frame and wheel alignment. The inspecting business may request additional certification if collision damage may have affected the frame, chassis, or alignment.

VIN verification

NDDOT requires verification of vehicle identification before issuing the replacement title unless it accepts other satisfactory proof. The inspection form refers applicants to Certificate of Vehicle Identification Number (VIN) Inspection (SFN 61999) when needed.

What the inspections do not establish

These inspections establish compliance with specified requirements, not a comprehensive guarantee of repair quality, reliability, or freedom from hidden damage. Buyers should still obtain an independent evaluation.

Does passing the required inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No. A Previously Salvaged title indicates that the vehicle completed North Dakota’s reconstruction, inspection, and retitling process. It does not guarantee that all damage was repaired correctly or that future problems will not occur.

Before buying, consider an independent evaluation of:

  • Structural repairs
  • Frame or unibody alignment
  • Airbags and restraint systems
  • Brakes, steering, and suspension
  • Electrical systems
  • Water or flood damage
  • Mechanical condition
  • Wheel alignment and tire wear
  • Rust, corrosion, and evidence of poor-quality body repairs
  • Replacement component quality

Ask the seller what damage occurred, who performed repairs, and what records are available. The question is not simply:

Did the vehicle pass North Dakota’s required inspection?

It is also:

What was damaged, how was it repaired, and what condition is the vehicle in now?

What repair and parts documentation should I keep?

Keep documents that establish ownership, describe repairs, and help the inspector assess the reconstructed vehicle. Useful records include:

  • Original Salvage Certificate of Title
  • Insurance damage assessments and settlement records
  • Photographs before, during, and after repairs
  • Receipts for replacement parts and donor-vehicle information
  • Repair invoices
  • Frame and wheel alignment records
  • Airbag and restraint-system repair or replacement records
  • Certificate of Motor Vehicle Inspection (SFN 2486)
  • VIN inspection documentation, when required
  • Final Previously Salvaged title

Not every document is required in every case, but missing records can make inspection or title processing more difficult. Ask for these records before purchasing a partially reconstructed vehicle.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company declares it a total loss?

An owner may retain a vehicle after a total-loss insurance settlement. The insurer may deduct the vehicle’s salvage value from the settlement. Keeping the vehicle does not eliminate salvage-title requirements when its damage exceeds the statutory threshold.

If damage exceeds 75% of retail value, excluding glass and hail damage as specified by law, the owner must submit the title to NDDOT within 10 days for issuance of a Salvage Certificate of Title.

Before accepting an owner-retained settlement, determine:

  • Whether damage exceeds the salvage threshold
  • Whether the title must be surrendered
  • Whether a lienholder has an interest in the vehicle
  • Whether the vehicle is eligible for reconstruction
  • What inspections and documents will be needed
  • Whether road operation is prohibited until retitling and registration are complete

Keeping possession of a total-loss vehicle does not allow the owner to bypass North Dakota’s salvage requirements.

Is there an age exemption from North Dakota’s salvage process?

North Dakota does not provide a general model-year exemption from its salvage requirements.

A vehicle that meets the statutory salvage threshold must follow the applicable salvage process regardless of its age.

However, North Dakota has a separate age limit for its damage-disclosure form.

Damage disclosure when selling a vehicle

North Dakota generally requires sellers to complete Damage/Salvage Disclosure Statement (SFN 18609) when transferring a vehicle less than nine model years old, including the current model year and the preceding eight model years.

The form informs the buyer about qualifying previous damage and provides information needed by NDDOT to process the title transfer.

For older vehicles, the separate damage-disclosure form generally is not required.

However, a vehicle’s Previously Salvaged title brand remains on subsequent titles, regardless of its age. The seller must transfer the vehicle using its properly branded title and should explain its salvage history to the buyer.

What about flood-damaged vehicles?

North Dakota’s damage-disclosure rules recognize damage caused by submersion in water. Water damage may also contribute to meeting the salvage threshold when the applicable damage calculation exceeds 75% of retail value.

Flood damage can cause hidden problems even when a vehicle has been cleaned and cosmetically repaired. Before buying, investigate:

  • Electrical wiring and connectors
  • Electronic control modules
  • Airbags and restraint systems
  • Rust and corrosion
  • Interior materials and carpeting
  • Mechanical components
  • Mold or contamination
  • Evidence of previous water intrusion

Review title history, damage disclosures, and insurance records. A vehicle-history report may help, but it may not identify every instance of water damage.

Do not assume that a vehicle without a Salvage Certificate of Title has never sustained flood damage.

What if the vehicle comes from another state?

North Dakota generally carries forward existing title brands. It accepts certain out-of-state salvage titles but does not accept documents marked Certificate of Destruction, Dismantled, Junk, Non-Rebuildable, Parts Only, Unrepairable, or similar restrictions.

Before buying, verify:

  • The issuing state and exact type of ownership document
  • All salvage, rebuilt, flood, junk, or other title brands
  • Whether NDDOT accepts the document
  • Whether the vehicle is eligible for reconstruction and registration
  • Whether inspections or other documentation are required
  • Complete ownership history and any outstanding liens

Transferring a vehicle between states does not remove its salvage history.

What should I check before buying a North Dakota salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document and title brands does the vehicle have?
  • Why was the vehicle declared salvage?
  • Did damage exceed 75% of retail value?
  • Was there an insurance total-loss settlement?
  • Is the vehicle eligible for reconstruction and registration?
  • Who performed the repairs?
  • Was the required inspection completed by a different qualified business?
  • Are inspection records, repair invoices, and parts receipts available?
  • Was VIN verification completed?
  • Is there evidence of flood damage?
  • Does the seller have any required damage-disclosure statement?
  • Has the vehicle been independently evaluated?
  • Are there liens, insurance limitations, or financing restrictions?
  • What repair, transport, inspection, tax, title, and registration costs remain?

For an unrepaired vehicle, ask:

Can this vehicle be legally reconstructed, inspected, titled, and registered for ordinary road use in North Dakota?

For an already-repaired vehicle, ask:

Can I verify its title history, repair quality, and completion of North Dakota’s required inspections before buying it?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

North Dakota requires applicable damage disclosures when ownership is transferred. For vehicles within the disclosure period, the seller generally must complete Damage/Salvage Disclosure Statement (SFN 18609). The form addresses qualifying damage and the separate 75% salvage threshold.

A reconstructed vehicle’s Previously Salvaged brand remains on subsequent titles, including after the vehicle ages out of the separate damage-disclosure-form requirement.

Before selling:

  • Use the correctly branded title and complete any required damage-disclosure statement
  • Do not represent a salvage or previously salvaged vehicle as having an unbranded title
  • Properly assign the title and resolve liens
  • Provide available repair and inspection records
  • Explain whether reconstruction and retitling are complete
  • Verify that the buyer can legally receive and register the vehicle in its current status

A restricted ownership document cannot be represented as eligible for ordinary road use when NDDOT prohibits issuing a title for that classification.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the North Dakota Department of Transportation or another government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, and procedures can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate North Dakota agency.
