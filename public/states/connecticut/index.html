Connecticut Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Connecticut Salvage and Rebuilt titles, Salvage Parts Only vehicles, the salvage inspection process, repair and parts documentation, owner-retained total losses, and what must happen before an eligible salvage vehicle can return to the road.

What you should know

When a vehicle is declared a total loss and its Connecticut title is marked Salvage, the vehicle cannot be operated on Connecticut roads. Its registration is cancelled.

An eligible Salvage vehicle can be repaired, but it must pass Connecticut's salvage inspection before it can be retitled and registered. After it passes, Connecticut issues a new title bearing the notation Rebuilt.

Connecticut has a separate Salvage Parts Only classification for severely damaged vehicles. If ten or more major component parts are damaged beyond repair and must be replaced, the vehicle can be designated Salvage Parts Only.

A vehicle branded Salvage Parts Only, Salvage Unrebuildable, or Salvage Unrepairable cannot be registered or retitled in Connecticut.

Connecticut also requires substantial documentation for a salvage inspection, including repair photographs, an insurance appraisal, a Salvage Vehicle Repair Report, and receipts for replaced major component parts.

Understand Connecticut terminology

Salvage

Connecticut uses the Salvage title designation for vehicles declared a total loss under the state's salvage-title process.

A vehicle with a Salvage title cannot be operated on Connecticut roads and cannot be registered or retitled until it has been rebuilt and passes the required salvage inspection.

The Salvage title itself warns that the vehicle cannot be registered or retitled without passing inspection.

Rebuilt

After an eligible Salvage vehicle has been repaired and passes Connecticut's salvage inspection, it can be registered again and Connecticut issues a new title.

The new title no longer carries the Salvage stamp but bears the notation Rebuilt.

The Rebuilt notation tells a future owner that the vehicle was previously declared a total loss and subsequently passed Connecticut's salvage inspection.

Salvage Parts Only

Connecticut uses Salvage Parts Only for certain severely damaged total-loss vehicles.

If the insurer or other responsible party determines that ten or more major component parts are damaged beyond repair and must be replaced, the title is marked Salvage Parts Only rather than Salvage.

A vehicle with a title branded Salvage Parts Only cannot be registered or retitled in Connecticut.

The same restriction applies when the title is branded Salvage Unrebuildable or Salvage Unrepairable.

Before buying a severely damaged vehicle, determine exactly what ownership document and brand it has. A Connecticut Salvage title and a Salvage Parts Only title do not provide the same path forward.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, if it has an eligible Salvage title.

The vehicle must be repaired and then pass Connecticut's salvage inspection. Once it passes, it can be retitled with the Rebuilt notation and registered after the remaining requirements are satisfied.

A Salvage Parts Only, Salvage Unrebuildable, or Salvage Unrepairable vehicle cannot use this process to return to ordinary road use.

Before buying, ask:

What exact title or ownership document does this vehicle have?

Then ask:

Does Connecticut provide a path from that document to a Rebuilt title and registration?

How do I get a Connecticut salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible Salvage vehicle, the general process is:

  1. Obtain and keep the Salvage title and the original insurance appraisal associated with the total-loss determination.
  2. Document the vehicle's condition before repairs.
  3. Repair the vehicle using the applicable repair standards.
  4. Keep receipts for replaced major component parts and the required information identifying the source of used components.
  5. Take the photographs Connecticut requires during the repair process, including photographs before repaired areas are covered by seam sealer, paint, or corrosion protection.
  6. Complete the Salvage Vehicle Repair Report, Form K-186.
  7. Complete an Air Bag Certification Statement, Form B-303, if the vehicle is equipped with an air bag.
  8. Present the vehicle and required documentation for a Connecticut salvage inspection.
  9. After the vehicle passes inspection, complete the applicable title and registration requirements.
  10. Receive a new title bearing the Rebuilt notation.

A private individual cannot drive or tow a Salvage vehicle to the inspection. Connecticut DMV requires it to be transported on a flatbed trailer or car carrier so that no wheel of the vehicle touches the ground.

Connecticut provides a limited exception for a bona fide employee of a licensed dealer or repairer using dealer or repair plates when that dealer or repairer owns the vehicle.

What does Connecticut inspect?

Connecticut requires an inspection before a Salvage vehicle can be retitled.

The inspection process includes the vehicle itself and the documentation supporting its reconstruction. Connecticut classifies Salvage vehicles among the vehicles requiring a safety inspection.

The inspector can review the vehicle's repairs, equipment, identification, replacement components, and supporting documentation. If a repair is questionable, Connecticut DMV warns that undercoating, sealer, or parts may have to be removed so the inspector can examine the repair.

The documentation also helps establish where major replacement components came from.

Connecticut now allows authorized Connecticut licensed dealers and repairers to perform salvage inspections, although they are not required to offer the service. Salvage inspections are also available through the DMV according to its current inspection instructions.

Does passing the salvage inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

Passing Connecticut's salvage inspection means the vehicle satisfied the state's requirements for the salvage inspection and can proceed through the Rebuilt title and registration process.

A buyer should not treat the inspection or Rebuilt title as a substitute for an independent evaluation of the vehicle's present condition or repair quality.

Before buying a Rebuilt vehicle, consider independently evaluating:

  • structural repairs
  • frame or unibody alignment
  • airbags and restraint systems
  • brakes, steering, and suspension
  • electrical repairs
  • water or flood damage
  • mechanical condition
  • wheel alignment and tire wear
  • corrosion protection
  • the quality and origin of replacement components

The useful question is not simply:

Did the vehicle pass Connecticut's salvage inspection?

It is also:

What was damaged, how was it repaired, and what condition is the vehicle in now?

What repair photographs does Connecticut require?

Connecticut requires photographs as part of the salvage inspection documentation.

The photographs must show:

  • the entire vehicle from all four corners
  • the damaged areas before repairs
  • repaired areas with new parts or panels installed before seam sealing, painting, or rust proofing
  • how new parts were attached, including welding
  • areas before painting and corrosion protection when those areas will no longer be visible afterward

These photographs are attached to the Salvage Vehicle Repair Report, Form K-186.

This makes planning important. If you buy a vehicle that has already been partially repaired, determine whether the photographs Connecticut requires were taken before the repairs became concealed.

What documentation do I need for replacement parts?

Connecticut requires receipts for major component parts replaced during the rebuild.

For a used component, the receipt must identify the source of the part and the VIN of the vehicle from which the component was removed.

Connecticut's statutory definition of major component parts includes items such as:

  • engine
  • transmission
  • front fenders
  • hood
  • passenger-compartment doors
  • front or rear bumper
  • rear quarter panels
  • deck lid, tailgate, or hatchback
  • trunk floor pan
  • pickup cargo box
  • frame or equivalent supporting structure on a unitized-body vehicle
  • truck cab
  • passenger-vehicle body

The state can also treat another part as a major component when it is comparable in design or function to the components identified by law.

The current DMV Salvage Inspection Information Sheet requires receipts, invoices, or other applicable documentation for replacement parts and panels. Receipts for major used components should clearly identify the source and donor vehicle VIN.

Do not wait until the vehicle is finished to reconstruct its parts history. Keep the required documentation when components are purchased.

What documents should I expect?

Depending on the vehicle and transaction, the Connecticut salvage process can involve:

  • the Salvage title
  • the original insurance adjuster's appraisal or damage report
  • Salvage Vehicle Repair Report, Form K-186
  • required photographs of the vehicle and repairs
  • receipts or invoices for replacement components
  • donor vehicle VIN information for used major components
  • Air Bag Certification Statement, Form B-303, when applicable
  • salvage inspection documentation
  • Salvage Inspection by Licensed Dealer or Repairer, Form B-270, when an authorized dealer or repairer performs the inspection
  • Application for Registration and Certificate of Title, Form H-13B
  • proof of insurance
  • other documents required for title and registration

Use Connecticut DMV's current forms and instructions when you are ready to rebuild, inspect, title, or register the vehicle.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company declares it a total loss?

Keeping the vehicle does not preserve its ordinary title and registration status.

When an insurer pays a total-loss claim and the insured owner keeps the vehicle, Connecticut law requires the insurer to obtain the vehicle's certificate of title from the insured and submit it to the Connecticut DMV with the required information. DMV then issues the appropriate Salvage title to the vehicle owner.

If the vehicle has a lien, additional title and lien requirements may apply. Confirm with the insurer, lienholder, and Connecticut DMV how the title will be handled as part of the total-loss settlement.

The resulting Salvage vehicle cannot be operated on Connecticut roads. If the owner wants to return it to road use, the vehicle must follow the applicable repair, inspection, retitling, and registration process.

For an owner-retained total-loss vehicle, determine:

  • what designation has been placed on the title
  • whether the vehicle is Salvage or Salvage Parts Only
  • whether it remains eligible to be rebuilt
  • whether the original insurance appraisal is available
  • what repairs are required
  • whether required pre-repair and repair-stage photographs can be obtained
  • whether receipts and donor VIN information will be available for replacement major components
  • whether any liens or ownership issues remain

Keeping the vehicle and being allowed to drive the vehicle are separate questions. A Salvage vehicle cannot be driven on Connecticut roads merely because the original owner retained it.

Is there an exception for a stolen vehicle that is recovered?

Connecticut has a narrow exception for certain vehicles declared a total loss because of theft.

A Connecticut-titled vehicle declared a total loss in settlement of a theft claim does not have to receive the Salvage title marking when it has no damage to a major component part or when its damage remains within the limited thresholds established by Connecticut law.

This exception is specific to qualifying theft claims and should not be treated as a general total-loss exemption.

If a stolen vehicle is recovered after a total-loss settlement, verify the resulting title status and the damage appraisal before assuming the ordinary salvage process applies.

What happens to an older vehicle that an insurance company declares a total loss?

Connecticut generally does not require a certificate of title for vehicles more than 20 model years old.

That ordinary title exemption should not be confused with a general exemption from salvage-related requirements.

Connecticut's current salvage inspection guidance specifically addresses older vehicles and does not state that age alone makes a Salvage vehicle eligible for ordinary road use without completing the applicable salvage process.

For an older total-loss vehicle, determine:

  • the model year
  • whether Connecticut issued or requires a title for the vehicle
  • what ownership document exists now
  • whether the insurer declared the vehicle a total loss
  • whether DMV records show a Salvage or other brand
  • whether the vehicle remains eligible for reconstruction
  • what proof of ownership DMV will require
  • whether the salvage inspection requirements apply

Do not assume that an older vehicle's exemption from Connecticut's ordinary title requirement erases a total-loss or salvage history.

What about flood-damaged vehicles?

Connecticut uses Flood as a title legend.

Connecticut DMV describes the Flood legend as applying when a vehicle has sustained flood damage and has been declared a loss by an insurance company.

A flood-related total loss can therefore create both title-history and repair-condition concerns.

For a flood-damaged vehicle, investigate more than the title brand. Consider the possibility of damage involving:

  • electrical systems
  • electronic control modules
  • connectors and wiring
  • airbags and restraint systems
  • corrosion
  • interior materials
  • mechanical components
  • mold or contamination

Water-related problems can appear after a vehicle has been repaired and may not be obvious during a brief inspection.

Before buying, determine the vehicle's exact title history, the extent of the water exposure, and what systems were repaired or replaced.

What if the vehicle comes from another state?

An out-of-state vehicle can require additional review before Connecticut will issue a title or registration, particularly when its ownership document carries a Salvage or similar brand.

Do not assume that an ownership document accepted by another state will automatically be sufficient in Connecticut.

Connecticut specifically states that a New York State Salvage Certificate, Form MV-907A, is not accepted as a Connecticut title document. Connecticut explains that the vehicle must first complete New York's process and receive a New York title.

This is an important distinction for buyers because New York salvage vehicles are commonly encountered in the Northeast.

Before buying an out-of-state salvage or rebuilt vehicle, determine:

  • the exact ownership document
  • the state that issued it
  • every brand shown on the document
  • whether the issuing state considers it a title or another type of ownership document
  • whether Connecticut accepts that document as proof of ownership
  • whether Connecticut requires a salvage inspection
  • whether the repair and parts documentation Connecticut requires is available
  • whether the vehicle can ultimately receive the Connecticut title and registration you need

For an out-of-state Salvage vehicle, verify the Connecticut requirements before purchasing or beginning repairs.

What should I check before buying a Connecticut salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Is it marked Salvage, Rebuilt, Salvage Parts Only, Unrebuildable, Unrepairable, Flood, or another designation?
  • Why was the vehicle declared a total loss?
  • How many major component parts were damaged beyond repair?
  • Is the original insurance appraisal available?
  • Has repair work already started?
  • Are the required before-repair and repair-stage photographs available?
  • Are receipts available for replaced major components?
  • Do receipts for used major components identify the donor vehicle VIN?
  • Is the Salvage Vehicle Repair Report available or capable of being completed?
  • Does the vehicle have airbags, and can the required certification be obtained?
  • Has the vehicle already passed a Connecticut salvage inspection?
  • Has the repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Are there unresolved liens or ownership issues?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • If financing is needed, will a lender accept the vehicle's title history?
  • What repair, transportation, inspection, title, tax, registration, and other costs remain?

For an unrepaired or partially repaired vehicle, two particularly important questions are:

Will I have all of the documentation Connecticut requires at the salvage inspection?

and

Were the required photographs taken before the repairs became hidden by paint, seam sealer, or corrosion protection?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Connecticut's Salvage title itself carries information intended for subsequent purchasers.

For a total-loss vehicle transferred with the Salvage title, the purchaser's name and address must be recorded as provided on the title, and the applicable damage report accompanies the ownership documentation.

If the owner of a Salvage vehicle needs a replacement title before transferring it, Connecticut DMV says the owner cannot transfer ownership merely by using the Application for Replacement Certificate of Title. The owner must first obtain a duplicate title bearing the Salvage legend and can then assign that title to the purchaser.

After a vehicle has successfully completed the salvage process, the Connecticut title bears the Rebuilt notation, preserving the vehicle's rebuilt history for future owners.

Connecticut also restricts certain sales made by insurers, self-insurers, dealers, and other licensed businesses. In particular, Salvage Parts Only vehicles are subject to stricter transfer restrictions.

Before selling or transferring a Salvage or Rebuilt vehicle, use the title and transfer documents that reflect its actual status and follow Connecticut's current DMV requirements.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Connecticut Department of Motor Vehicles or another government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, and procedures can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.
