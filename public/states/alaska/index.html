Alaska Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Alaska Salvage and Reconstructed titles, Junk-branded vehicles, total-loss claims, VIN inspections, parts documentation, and what must happen before an eligible vehicle can return to the road.

What you should know

Alaska allows certain vehicles with an existing Junk brand to receive a Salvage title as a formal ownership document. This option became available through the DMV’s updated process in 2025.

A Salvage title does not mean that the vehicle is ready or approved for road use. Alaska’s Salvage titles identify the vehicle as incomplete and require the reconstructed-title process to be completed before registration.

An eligible vehicle can return to road use after it has been completely restored to operational, street-legal condition and satisfies Alaska’s reconstructed-vehicle requirements.

Alaska also recognizes non-repairable or Parts Only vehicles. These are materially different from incomplete vehicles that can qualify for reconstruction. A non-repairable or Parts Only title indicates that the vehicle cannot be titled or registered again for road use.

Before buying, determine the exact ownership document, whether the vehicle is eligible for reconstruction, and what evidence of ownership and replacement parts will be required.

Understand Alaska terminology

Salvage title

Alaska’s Salvage title is a formal ownership document for a qualifying Junk-branded vehicle that has not yet been reconstructed.

It may be used to establish ownership when surrendering a vehicle to an insurance company, transferring it to a salvage company, retrieving it from impound, or demonstrating ownership for another purpose.

The title carries the word SALVAGE and identifies the vehicle as incomplete and ineligible for roadway use until the reconstructed-title process has been completed.

An incomplete vehicle is not required to obtain a Salvage title merely to be transferred. Alaska also permits certain transfers using a bill of sale or other contract.

Reconstructed vehicle

Alaska defines a reconstructed vehicle as one materially altered from its original construction through the removal, addition, or substitution of essential parts. Essential parts include the chassis, body, and engine.

Replacing only the engine does not, by itself, make a vehicle reconstructed under this definition.

For an eligible vehicle to receive a reconstructed title and registration, it must be completely restored to operational, street-legal condition and satisfy Alaska’s ownership, documentation, and inspection requirements.

Junk-branded vehicle

A Junk brand can appear in several situations, including when an insurer acquires an unrepairable vehicle after settling a qualifying total-loss claim.

Alaska’s newer Salvage-title procedure allows certain vehicles with a current Junk brand to obtain a formal Salvage ownership document before reconstruction. A Junk history should not be treated as evidence that a vehicle is repairable or eligible for registration.

Non-repairable or Parts Only vehicle

Alaska describes a non-repairable or Parts Only title as indicating that the vehicle cannot be titled or registered again because its remaining value is as parts or scrap metal. This restriction is materially different from a Salvage title issued to an incomplete vehicle that may qualify for reconstruction.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, if the vehicle is eligible for Alaska’s reconstructed-title process.

A vehicle with an Alaska Salvage title cannot be registered until reconstruction is complete. The Salvage title is proof of ownership, not permission to drive.

An eligible reconstructed vehicle must be restored to operational, street-legal condition and satisfy the applicable documentation and inspection requirements.

A vehicle designated non-repairable or Parts Only does not have that path back to ordinary title and registration.

Before buying, ask: What exact ownership document does the vehicle have, and does Alaska recognize a path from that document to a Reconstructed title?

How do I get an Alaska salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible vehicle, the general process is:

  1. Establish ownership using the existing title, an appropriate Salvage title, or other evidence accepted by Alaska DMV.
  2. Reconstruct the vehicle and retain documentation for the major components used.
  3. Restore the vehicle to operational, street-legal condition.
  4. Obtain the required vehicle identification inspection from an authorized law enforcement officer or DMV representative.
  5. Obtain lien releases for any liens recorded on the vehicle’s DMV record, if needed.
  6. Complete Form 829, Reconstructed Vehicle Affidavit.
  7. Submit the affidavit, inspection documentation, required titles, bills of sale and invoices, and the applicable title and registration application to Alaska DMV.

The DMV may also require additional information to establish the identity of the vehicle or the lawful acquisition of its components.

What inspection does Alaska require?

Alaska requires a vehicle identification inspection as part of its reconstructed-vehicle process.

A law enforcement officer or authorized DMV representative can perform the inspection. Alaska DMV states that inspection at a state DMV office is free.

The inspection verifies the vehicle identification number and the identity of the vehicle. The applicable regulation also permits an inspection for stolen parts.

If the vehicle must be taken to a DMV office for inspection, ask the DMV about its special one-way trip permit rather than assuming an incomplete or unregistered vehicle can be driven there.

Does passing the inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

A buyer should not treat the existence of a Reconstructed title or a completed VIN inspection as a substitute for an independent evaluation of the vehicle’s condition or repair quality.

Before buying, consider independently evaluating:

  • structural repairs and frame or unibody alignment
  • airbags and restraint systems
  • brakes, steering, and suspension
  • electrical repairs
  • water or flood damage
  • mechanical condition
  • the origin and quality of replacement components
  • the vehicle’s overall suitability for the conditions in which it will be used

The useful question is not simply:

Did the vehicle complete Alaska’s reconstructed-title process?

It is also:

What was damaged, how was it repaired, and what condition is the vehicle in now?

What documentation should I keep for replacement parts?

Alaska requires proof of ownership and purchase records for major components used during reconstruction.

The applicable regulation specifically identifies receipts for the engine, transmission, drivetrain, frame, and body.

Alaska DMV also directs applicants to submit the titles of vehicles from which the chassis or frame VIN was obtained, unless those titles were previously surrendered.

Keep bills of sale, invoices, donor-vehicle identification information, and other ownership records as parts are acquired.

Missing documentation can prevent the DMV from establishing the identity of the reconstructed vehicle or the lawful acquisition of its components.

What documents and forms should I expect?

Depending on the vehicle and its existing ownership status, the process may involve:

  • Form 857 — Application for Salvage Vehicle Title, if a Salvage ownership document is needed
  • satisfactory evidence of ownership, such as an existing title
  • Form 829 — Reconstructed Vehicle Affidavit
  • Form 811 — Verification of Vehicle Inspection
  • the applicable Vehicle Transaction Application
  • original titles from vehicles used in reconstruction, when required
  • bills of sale and invoices for major parts
  • lien releases, when applicable
  • the applicable title and registration fees

Alaska DMV’s current forms page is the appropriate place to obtain the forms before starting an application.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company declares it a total loss?

First determine whether the insurer acquired ownership of the vehicle as part of the settlement or whether you retained it.

Alaska’s insurance-company rules specifically address an insurer obtaining title to an unrepairable vehicle after settling an actual or constructive total-loss claim.

When those rules apply, the insurer must mark the title Junk and surrender the required documents to the DMV.

An owner who retains the vehicle should verify its existing title status and whether the applicable insurance and DMV procedures require a change to its ownership documents.

Do not assume that retaining possession automatically means the title remains unchanged or that the vehicle is eligible for registration.

If the vehicle is incomplete and currently Junk-branded, Alaska’s Salvage-title process may provide an appropriate formal ownership document.

What happens to an older vehicle that is declared a total loss?

Alaska’s published Salvage-title guidance does not establish a general vehicle-age exemption.

Its insurance-company regulation distinguishes between actual and constructive total losses. A constructive total loss involves damage for which the cost of repair exceeds the vehicle’s worth or insured value.

For an older vehicle, determine:

  • the vehicle’s value before the loss
  • the nature and extent of the damage
  • whether the insurer acquired ownership
  • whether the vehicle was designated unrepairable
  • whether a Junk brand was recorded
  • what ownership document exists now

An older vehicle’s low value can affect whether repairing it is economically practical, but age alone does not establish that the vehicle can be registered or reconstructed.

What if the vehicle has a Junk history?

This deserves particular attention because of Alaska’s updated Salvage-title procedure.

Alaska DMV can now issue a Salvage title to certain currently Junk-branded vehicles that have not yet been reconstructed.

The Salvage title supplies a formal ownership document. It does not establish that the vehicle has been repaired, inspected, or approved for road use.

If reconstruction is permitted, the owner must still complete the reconstructed-title process before registration.

A buyer should verify the precise status of the vehicle and not assume that every vehicle described as Junk can return to the road.

What if the vehicle comes from another state?

The exact out-of-state ownership document matters.

Alaska’s reconstructed-vehicle procedure requires appropriate ownership records and supporting documentation for major components. Its Salvage-title process also recognizes qualifying evidence of ownership from other jurisdictions.

Before buying an out-of-state salvage vehicle, verify:

  • its exact title or ownership document
  • all existing brands
  • whether it is incomplete, reconstructed, or designated non-repairable
  • whether Alaska will accept the document for the intended title process
  • whether original donor-vehicle titles and component records are available
  • whether an inspection will be required

Do not assume that an out-of-state Rebuilt title, Salvage title, or bill of sale automatically qualifies the vehicle for Alaska registration.

What should I check before buying an Alaska salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Is the vehicle Salvage, Junk, Reconstructed, non-repairable, or Parts Only?
  • If Junk-branded, is it eligible for Alaska’s reconstructed-title process?
  • Did an insurance company acquire the vehicle after a total-loss claim?
  • Has the vehicle already been reconstructed?
  • Are the original title and relevant donor-vehicle titles available?
  • Are the required bills of sale and component receipts available?
  • Can the vehicle complete Alaska’s VIN inspection?
  • Does it have outstanding liens?
  • Has its repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • If financing is needed, will a lender accept the vehicle’s title history?
  • What transportation, repair, inspection, title, registration, and other costs remain?

The central question is:

Does this vehicle have a documented, legally available path from its current ownership status to the title and registration I need?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

A reconstructed vehicle’s title and registration carry its Reconstructed designation.

When selling, make the vehicle’s existing ownership document and title history available to the buyer. Do not describe a Salvage title as evidence that a vehicle has already completed reconstruction.

Alaska’s Reconstructed Vehicle Affidavit also instructs the legal owner responsible for the reconstruction to obtain title in their name before reselling the vehicle.

If a vehicle is being dismantled, scrapped, or destroyed, separate surrender and documentation requirements may apply.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Alaska Division of Motor Vehicles or another government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, inspections, and procedures can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate Alaska agency.
