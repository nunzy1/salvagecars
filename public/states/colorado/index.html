Colorado Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Colorado salvage, rebuilt-from-salvage, and nonrepairable titles, roadworthiness requirements, certified VIN inspections, and whether a salvage vehicle can return to the road.

What you should know

Colorado distinguishes between salvage, rebuilt from salvage, and nonrepairable vehicles.

A salvage vehicle cannot be driven on public roads while it remains in salvage status. If it can be repaired to meet Colorado's roadworthy requirements, it can potentially complete a certified VIN inspection and receive a title branded REBUILT FROM SALVAGE.

A vehicle with a nonrepairable title is intended only as a source of parts or scrap and cannot be registered as a complete vehicle.

Understand Colorado terminology

Salvage vehicle

Colorado generally defines a salvage vehicle as a vehicle damaged by collision, fire, flood, accident, or another qualifying event when the cost of repairing it for safe road use exceeds its retail fair market value immediately before the damage.

Colorado's statutory definition contains exclusions and special rules, so not every damaged or insurance-totaled vehicle necessarily falls into the salvage category.

Rebuilt from salvage

A salvage vehicle repaired to roadworthy condition can potentially receive a Colorado title branded REBUILT FROM SALVAGE. Colorado also requires those words to be physically stamped into the vehicle before the certified inspection.

Nonrepairable vehicle

A nonrepairable title is for a vehicle designated as a source of parts or scrap. It cannot be registered as a complete vehicle.

Junk vehicle

Colorado separately defines junk as a vehicle incapable of operating on roads and no longer a vehicle because it has been destroyed, dismantled, or changed. Colorado does not issue a certificate of title for a junk vehicle.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially—if it has a salvage title and can be restored to roadworthy condition.

A nonrepairable or junk vehicle does not have the same path.

How do I get a Colorado salvage vehicle back on the road?

  1. Confirm that the vehicle has a salvage title and is eligible for rebuilding.
  2. Repair it to a roadworthy condition.
  3. Keep invoices, bills of sale, and receipts for parts.
  4. Complete the owner portion of DR 2424, Rebuilt from Salvage Title Statement of Facts.
  5. Sign the statement on the salvage title certifying roadworthiness.
  6. Permanently stamp REBUILT FROM SALVAGE into the vehicle in the specified location.
  7. Have the vehicle examined by an authorized certified inspector.
  8. Provide repair documentation and receipts.
  9. Have the inspector complete the applicable portions of DR 2424 and the DR 2704 Colorado Certified VIN Inspection.
  10. If it passes, apply at the county motor vehicle office for the Rebuilt from Salvage title.

What does roadworthy mean in Colorado's rebuilt process?

The owner must certify that the repaired vehicle is roadworthy under applicable state vehicle-equipment requirements, and the certified inspector evaluates roadworthiness.

The current DR 2415 checklist identifies examples including:

  • lights
  • tires and wheels
  • mirrors
  • windshield and windshield wipers
  • sufficient power to operate with normal traffic
  • permanently attached vehicle parts and seats
  • absence of torn or jagged metal
  • the required REBUILT FROM SALVAGE marking

What is the Colorado Certified VIN Inspection?

A rebuilt salvage vehicle must undergo a Colorado Certified VIN Inspection, documented on DR 2704, by an authorized certified inspector.

The inspector examines the vehicle and identifying information, checks applicable rebuilt-from-salvage requirements, and reviews invoices, bills of sale, or receipts associated with parts used to make it roadworthy.

The current DR 2415 checklist states that the inspection used for the title application cannot be more than one year old.

What is Form DR 2424?

DR 2424 documents how the salvage vehicle was made roadworthy, including the vehicle, purchase information, parts used, repairs performed, and who performed the work.

The owner certifies that invoices and receipts for parts and labor are available for inspection. A certified inspector completes the verification portion.

Why do I have to stamp “REBUILT FROM SALVAGE” on the vehicle?

Colorado requires more than a title brand. Before the certified inspection, the applicant must stamp REBUILT FROM SALVAGE into the vehicle using letters at least one-quarter inch high.

For most motor vehicles, the marking goes on the driver's-side B-pillar. Colorado specifies other locations for motorcycles, Class A motor homes, and trailers.

Why are parts receipts important?

DR 2424 requires the owner to list parts used and repairs made, and the current DR 2415 checklist directs the applicant to provide invoices, bills of sale, or receipts to the certified inspector.

The county motor vehicle office can also request receipts for major parts. If you are buying a partially rebuilt vehicle, obtain this documentation before completing the purchase.

What if the inspector determines that the vehicle needs an assigned VIN?

If the certified inspector determines that an assigned VIN is required, Colorado directs the applicant to the separate Assigned Vehicle Identification Number process.

Investigate missing, damaged, or altered VIN information and major structural replacement involving VIN-bearing components before purchasing the vehicle.

Can I drive a salvage vehicle to the inspection?

A vehicle with a salvage title cannot be driven on Colorado roads until it has been fully repaired and a Rebuilt from Salvage title has been issued.

Colorado guidance allows temporary credentials in applicable situations for vehicles needing an inspection, but availability and requirements should be verified with the appropriate county motor vehicle office. Trailer or tow transport may be appropriate.

Do not assume an inspection appointment itself gives permission to drive an unregistered salvage vehicle.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

Colorado recognizes title brands placed by other jurisdictions. Inspect the actual ownership document and identify every brand or restriction.

Determine whether Colorado treats it as salvage, rebuilt from salvage, nonrepairable, junk, or another status, whether a certified VIN inspection is required, and whether it can enter Colorado's rebuilt-from-salvage process.

An ordinary VIN verification for an out-of-state transfer is not necessarily the certified VIN inspection required for a rebuilt-from-salvage vehicle.

What about flood-damaged vehicles?

Colorado recognizes flood damaged as a title brand. Flood damage generally involves submersion deep enough for water to enter the passenger compartment and damage electrical, computer, or mechanical components.

Investigate corrosion, electronics, drivetrain contamination, airbags and restraints, interior contamination, mold, and long-term electrical reliability. Title eligibility and physical condition are separate questions.

Does passing the certified inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No. Colorado's inspection is meaningful because roadworthiness is part of the process, but it is not a general warranty of repair quality, durability, or future reliability.

Consider independently evaluating structural repairs, steering and suspension, alignment, airbags, electrical systems, mechanical condition, corrosion or flood damage, replacement components, and incomplete repairs.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in Colorado?

  • What is the exact ownership document?
  • Is it salvage, nonrepairable, junk, or already rebuilt from salvage?
  • Why did it receive its title brand?
  • What damage and repairs occurred?
  • What major components were replaced?
  • Are invoices, bills of sale, and receipts available?
  • Are VINs intact, or could an assigned VIN be required?
  • Has REBUILT FROM SALVAGE been stamped into the vehicle?
  • Has DR 2424 been completed?
  • Has it undergone a Colorado Certified VIN Inspection?
  • How will Colorado treat an out-of-state brand?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What costs remain?
  • Have completed repairs been independently evaluated?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

A Colorado vehicle that completes the process receives a title branded REBUILT FROM SALVAGE, and the vehicle itself carries the physical marking.

Colorado uses branded-title disclosure requirements when applicable. Its branded-title law was amended in 2025, so use current title and disclosure documents.

  • accurately represent the rebuilt-from-salvage history
  • do not imply the title is unbranded
  • provide the applicable branded-title disclosure
  • complete normal ownership-transfer requirements
