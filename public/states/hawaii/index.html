Hawaii Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Hawaii Salvage and Rebuilt titles, the state's damage and vehicle-age rules, water-damaged vehicles, reconstruction documentation, Parts Only vehicles, and what must happen before an eligible salvage vehicle can return to the road.

What you should know

Hawaii requires a Salvage Certificate when a vehicle enters the salvage process following a qualifying salvage sale or total-loss insurance settlement.

A Salvage Certificate allows possession, transportation, and transfer of ownership. It does not authorize driving the vehicle on public highways.

An eligible salvage vehicle may return to road use after rebuilding and completing the applicable certification, inspection, titling, and registration process.

Hawaii's vehicle registration system is administered by county governments. The county where the vehicle will be registered is therefore an important part of the process.

After a salvage vehicle completes Hawaii's rebuilding process, its new ownership and registration certificates must display Rebuilt Vehicle.

Understand Hawaii terminology

Salvage Certificate

A Salvage Certificate is an ownership document issued through the appropriate county vehicle-registration authority. It authorizes possession, transportation, and transfer of ownership, but not driving on public highways. The certificate displays Salvage.

Review Hawaii's Salvage Certificate requirements

Rebuilt Vehicle

Hawaii defines a Rebuilt Vehicle as a vehicle declared a total loss by an insurer and subsequently rebuilt or repaired for operation on public highways. For this definition, the vehicle has material damage, including possible flood damage, to its electronics, frame, unitized structure, or suspension system, and projected repair costs exceed its market value when the damage occurred.

After completion of the required rebuilding process, the new ownership and registration certificates display Rebuilt Vehicle.

Reconstructed Vehicle

Hawaii also recognizes Reconstructed Vehicle as a separate classification, generally involving vehicles assembled from parts or materially modified through changes to essential components.

A Reconstructed Vehicle is not automatically the same as a Rebuilt Vehicle with a salvage history. Documentation and inspection procedures may differ.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially.

Hawaii provides a process for rebuilding an eligible salvage vehicle and obtaining new ownership and registration certificates.

Before buying, determine:

  • What ownership document the vehicle has.
  • Whether it is eligible to be rebuilt.
  • Whether a qualified repair dealer can perform and certify the repairs.
  • What documentation the county requires.
  • Which inspections must be completed.
  • Whether the vehicle can ultimately receive Rebuilt Vehicle ownership and registration certificates.

A salvage vehicle cannot be driven on public highways simply because its repairs are complete.

How do I get a Hawaii salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible salvage vehicle, the general process is:

  1. Obtain the Salvage Certificate and required ownership documentation.
  2. Confirm the applicable requirements with the county vehicle-registration authority.
  3. Have the vehicle repaired according to the original manufacturer's recognized repair procedures, specifications, and allowable tolerances.
  4. Obtain the required certification from the qualifying registered or certified motor vehicle repair dealer who rebuilt the vehicle.
  5. Complete the application for registration of the rebuilt vehicle.
  6. Submit the Salvage Certificate, prescribed bill of sale, repair certification, application, and additional required documents and fees to the county.
  7. Complete the applicable vehicle safety inspection and any additional county requirements.
  8. Obtain new ownership and registration certificates displaying Rebuilt Vehicle.

Hawaii law requires certification signed by the qualifying repair dealer who rebuilt the vehicle and meets the applicable bonding requirements.

If you are considering rebuilding the vehicle yourself, do not assume personal repair work alone will satisfy the statutory certification requirement.

What inspections and repair certifications are required?

Hawaii requires a certification signed by the qualifying motor vehicle repair dealer who rebuilt the salvage vehicle.

The certification attests that the repairs followed the original manufacturer's recognized repair procedures or specifications and allowable tolerances for the vehicle's model and year.

The county may require additional documents and fees. The vehicle must also meet applicable Hawaii vehicle safety inspection requirements before returning to road use.

Because Hawaii distinguishes Rebuilt Vehicles from Reconstructed Vehicles, verify which procedures apply.

Does passing the required inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

Hawaii's repair certification and inspection requirements are part of the process for returning an eligible vehicle to road use. They are not a substitute for an independent evaluation of the vehicle's present condition and repair quality.

Before buying, consider independently evaluating:

  • Structural repairs
  • Frame or unibody alignment
  • Airbags and restraint systems
  • Brakes, steering, and suspension
  • Electrical systems
  • Water or flood damage
  • Mechanical condition
  • Wheel alignment and tire wear
  • Corrosion protection
  • Replacement component quality

The useful question is not simply: Did the vehicle complete Hawaii's rebuilding requirements?

It is also: What was damaged, how was it repaired, and what condition is the vehicle in now?

What repair and parts documentation should I keep?

Hawaii's salvage statute requires specific documents for registering a rebuilt vehicle, including:

  • Salvage Certificate
  • Prescribed bill of sale
  • Application for registration
  • Required repair dealer inspection certification
  • Any additional documents required by the county

Before beginning repairs, confirm what documentation the county and qualifying repair dealer will require.

Useful records to maintain include:

  • Original damage assessments
  • Insurance loss documentation
  • Photographs taken before and during repairs
  • Replacement parts invoices
  • Used-parts purchase records
  • Donor vehicle information
  • Repair invoices
  • Repair dealer certification

Not every item in this second list is expressly required by Hawaii's salvage statute. A complete repair history can help document the work and inform future buyers.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company declares it a total loss?

Hawaii provides specific rules for owner-retained total-loss vehicles.

When an insurance settlement results in the owner or claimant keeping the salvage vehicle, the insurer or its authorized agent must, within 10 days of settlement:

  • Notify the appropriate county director of finance that the owner or claimant retained the vehicle.
  • Notify the owner or claimant in writing about Hawaii's salvage recertification requirements.

The notification must use the form prescribed by the county director of finance. Keeping the vehicle does not authorize continued driving on public highways.

Before repairing an owner-retained total-loss vehicle, determine:

  • What ownership document will be issued or retained.
  • What notification the insurer must provide.
  • Whether a lien affects the ownership documents.
  • Whether the vehicle is eligible for rebuilding.
  • Which repair dealer certification is required.
  • What inspections and registration requirements apply.

If the vehicle has a lien, ask your insurer how the total-loss settlement will be handled and your lienholder what must happen to the outstanding loan. Then contact your county vehicle-registration office to determine which ownership documents you need to obtain a Salvage Certificate and, if you rebuild the vehicle, complete the registration process.

Is there an age exemption from Hawaii's salvage process?

Hawaii's general salvage-certificate requirements do not provide a vehicle-age exemption.

Separate rules may apply to certain older, antique, or specially classified vehicles, but age alone does not remove requirements associated with a vehicle entering the salvage process.

For an older total-loss vehicle, determine:

  • What ownership document exists.
  • Whether a Salvage Certificate has been issued.
  • Whether the vehicle is eligible for rebuilding.
  • Which repair certification requirements apply.
  • What the county requires for registration.
  • Whether separate antique or special-interest provisions apply.

Do not assume an older vehicle is exempt simply because of its age.

What about flood-damaged vehicles?

Hawaii's statutory definition of a Rebuilt Vehicle expressly recognizes flood damage as a possible form of material damage.

The definition concerns damage to specified vehicle systems or structures, together with projected repair costs exceeding market value.

Before buying a flood-damaged vehicle, investigate:

  • Electrical systems and electronic modules
  • Wiring and connectors
  • Airbags and restraint systems
  • Corrosion
  • Interior materials
  • Mechanical components
  • Mold or contamination

Water-related problems can appear after repairs and may not be obvious during a brief inspection. Determine the extent of water exposure, which systems were affected, and what repairs were performed.

What if the vehicle comes from another state?

An out-of-state salvage or rebuilt vehicle requires additional attention because Hawaii's counties administer vehicle registration.

Before buying, determine:

  • The exact ownership document.
  • The state that issued it.
  • Every title brand or notation shown on the document.
  • Whether the document is a title or another form of ownership evidence.
  • Whether the destination county accepts the document.
  • Whether Hawaii's salvage rebuilding requirements apply.
  • Whether a qualifying repair dealer must certify the vehicle.
  • Which inspections are required.
  • What additional documentation the county requires.

If the vehicle will be shipped to Hawaii, confirm shipping documentation and initial registration requirements before arranging transportation.

Do not assume an out-of-state rebuilt title automatically qualifies the vehicle for ordinary Hawaii registration.

What should I check before buying a Hawaii salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Has a Salvage Certificate been issued?
  • Why was the vehicle declared a total loss?
  • What damage occurred to its structure, suspension, or electronics?
  • Has repair work already begun?
  • Who performed the repairs?
  • Can a qualifying repair dealer provide the required certification?
  • Are damage photographs and repair records available?
  • What additional documents does the county require?
  • Has the vehicle completed applicable inspections?
  • Has repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Are there unresolved liens or ownership issues?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • If financing is needed, will a lender accept the vehicle's title history?
  • What repair, shipping, inspection, title, registration, and other costs remain?

For an unrepaired salvage vehicle, ask: Can this vehicle be repaired and certified by a qualifying Hawaii motor vehicle repair dealer in accordance with the state's requirements?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Hawaii establishes specific requirements for selling a vehicle that still has a Salvage Certificate.

The seller must transfer the Salvage Certificate and provide the purchaser with the prescribed bill of sale. The seller must also notify the purchaser in writing about Hawaii's salvage recertification requirements.

Hawaii restricts the sale of salvage vehicles to certain licensed businesses or purchasers who complete the required affidavit. The affidavit addresses whether the vehicle will be used to construct a Rebuilt Vehicle and acknowledges the applicable registration requirement if it will be rebuilt.

After a salvage vehicle successfully completes the rebuilding process, its new ownership and registration certificates must display Rebuilt Vehicle.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the appropriate Hawaii county vehicle-registration authority, Hawaii Department of Transportation, or another government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, and procedures can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate Hawaii agency.
