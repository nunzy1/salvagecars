Nebraska Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Nebraska Salvage Certificates of Title, Previously Salvaged titles, the 75% damage threshold, vehicle inspections, owner-retained total losses, flood-damaged vehicles, and what must happen before an eligible salvage vehicle can return to the road.

What you should know

Nebraska generally classifies a late-model vehicle as salvage when estimated repairs to restore it to its previous condition and legal road use meet or exceed 75% of its retail value at the time of damage.

The late-model definition considers age or retail value. A vehicle seven model years old or less can qualify, and an older vehicle can also qualify if its retail value exceeds the applicable statutory threshold.

An owner may voluntarily obtain a Salvage Certificate of Title regardless of the vehicle’s age, value, or extent of damage.

An eligible salvage vehicle must be repaired and pass the required identification inspection before the owner applies for a title branded Previously Salvaged. The brand continues on later titles and registrations.

Nebraska’s identification inspection verifies identity and ownership information. It is not a comprehensive assessment of repair quality or roadworthiness.

Understand Nebraska terminology

Salvage Vehicle

A late-model vehicle whose estimated repair cost meets or exceeds 75% of its retail value, as defined in Nebraska law. Repair cost includes parts and reasonable labor. An owner may also voluntarily designate a vehicle as salvage.

Late-Model Vehicle

A vehicle seven model years old or less, or one exceeding the statutory retail-value threshold. The DMV states $12,500 through 2029 and $13,000 beginning January 1, 2030. Older vehicles may still qualify based on value.

Salvage Certificate of Title

An ownership document for a vehicle designated salvage; it does not by itself authorize ordinary road registration.

Previously Salvaged Title

A title issued after an eligible salvage vehicle is repaired, inspected, and retitled. The Previously Salvaged brand is carried forward.

Vehicle Identification Inspection

An inspection generally performed by a county sheriff or authorized inspector to verify VIN, odometer, and relevant identity and ownership information.

Flood-Damaged Vehicle

Nebraska has a separate flood-damage definition and Flood Damaged title brand. Generally, water must enter the passenger compartment above the floorboard and damage electrical, computerized, or mechanical components. Statutory exceptions apply.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially. An eligible vehicle with a Nebraska Salvage Certificate of Title may be repaired, inspected, and retitled for ordinary road use. The owner must obtain a Previously Salvaged title and complete registration requirements before ordinary highway operation.

A vehicle documented as nonrepairable, parts only, junk, or non-highway use may not be eligible for a title or registration that permits ordinary road use. Before buying, confirm with Nebraska DMV whether the particular ownership document allows the vehicle to be retitled and registered.

How do I get a Nebraska salvage vehicle back on the road?

  1. Obtain the Salvage Certificate of Title or other acceptable ownership document.
  2. Confirm that the vehicle is eligible for repair and retitling.
  3. Complete the necessary repairs and retain repair records, parts receipts, and photographs.
  4. Contact the county treasurer or sheriff’s office about the required identification inspection.
  5. Bring the vehicle and required ownership documents to the inspection in Nebraska.
  6. Obtain the completed Vehicle Inspection Certificate.
  7. Submit the inspection certificate, Salvage Certificate of Title, and title application to the county treasurer.
  8. Pay applicable fees and taxes.
  9. Obtain a title branded Previously Salvaged and complete registration requirements before ordinary road use.

The inspection certificate generally expires 90 days after inspection. Confirm current fees, scheduling, and documents with the appropriate office.

What inspections and verification are required?

Identification inspection

Nebraska requires an identification inspection to convert a Salvage Certificate of Title to a Previously Salvaged title. The inspection generally checks the VIN against ownership records, records the odometer reading, and includes required vehicle identity checks. Major component parts must be properly attached in their correct locations.

Ownership documents

The owner must present the required ownership documents. If a lienholder has the title, coordinate with the county treasurer and lienholder before scheduling.

In-state inspection

For conversion from Salvage Certificate of Title to Previously Salvaged, the inspection must occur in Nebraska. An out-of-state inspection does not substitute for this requirement.

What the inspection does not establish

An identification inspection is not a comprehensive structural, mechanical, or electrical safety evaluation. Passing it does not guarantee that the vehicle was repaired correctly.

Does passing Nebraska’s inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No. Passing the identification inspection does not establish that a vehicle is safe, mechanically reliable, or properly repaired. Consider an independent inspection before buying.

  • Structural repairs and frame or unibody alignment
  • Airbags and restraint systems
  • Brakes, steering, and suspension
  • Electrical systems and evidence of water damage
  • Mechanical condition and tire wear
  • Rust, corrosion, and evidence of poor-quality body repairs
  • Replacement component quality

Ask what was damaged, who performed the repairs, what records are available, and what condition the vehicle is in now.

What repair and parts documentation should I keep?

Nebraska requires ownership documents for the identification inspection and retitling. The standard identification inspection should not be described as a comprehensive parts-receipt audit. Additional documentation may be required for assembled or reconstructed vehicles.

  • Original Salvage Certificate of Title and ownership-transfer documents
  • Insurance settlement and original damage assessments
  • Before-and-during repair photographs
  • Receipts for replacement components and donor vehicle information
  • Repair invoices and airbag records
  • Completed Vehicle Inspection Certificate
  • New Previously Salvaged title

Not every item above is necessarily required for a standard salvage title application. Keep them to help verify the repair history and answer buyer questions.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company declares it a total loss?

When an insurer pays a total-loss settlement on a vehicle meeting Nebraska’s salvage definition and the owner keeps the vehicle, the insurer must notify Nebraska DMV and advise the owner of the salvage title responsibility.

The owner must forward the properly endorsed ownership document to the appropriate county treasurer within 30 days after settlement of the loss. A Salvage Certificate of Title is generally issued unless the vehicle has already been repaired and inspected as required for a Previously Salvaged title.

  • Confirm the insurer reported the loss and whether a salvage brand was recorded.
  • Ask whether the existing title must be surrendered and which title is appropriate.
  • Determine whether repairs and an identification inspection are required.
  • Coordinate with the insurer and lienholder about any outstanding loan and title documents.

Is there an age exemption from Nebraska’s salvage process?

Nebraska’s late-model definition includes a vehicle seven model years old or less, or one exceeding the applicable retail-value threshold. The age condition is not a blanket exemption for older vehicles.

An older vehicle may still qualify because of its value. An owner may also voluntarily obtain a Salvage Certificate of Title regardless of age, value, or damage.

Before buying, verify the model year, retail value at the time of damage, existing title brands, insurance history, and applicable inspection requirements.

What about flood-damaged vehicles?

Nebraska separately defines and recognizes flood-damaged vehicles. Generally, the definition applies when a vehicle has been submerged in water above the floorboard and the water has damaged electrical, computerized, or mechanical components. The law includes certain exceptions.

A Flood Damaged brand is distinct from a Salvage brand, although a vehicle can have more than one relevant designation. Water damage may remain hidden after cleaning or repairs.

  • Electrical wiring, connectors, and electronic modules
  • Airbags and restraint systems
  • Rust, corrosion, and interior materials
  • Mechanical components
  • Mold, contamination, and evidence of prolonged moisture exposure

What if the vehicle comes from another state?

Nebraska generally requires an identification inspection for vehicles arriving with out-of-state ownership documents. An out-of-state salvage vehicle may need to complete the Nebraska salvage retitling process before ordinary road registration.

A vehicle with an out-of-state salvage ownership document generally cannot obtain a Previously Salvaged Nebraska title until it has been repaired and inspected.

The inspection to convert a salvage title to Previously Salvaged must occur in Nebraska. Nebraska also carries forward qualifying damage-related title brands and may identify the state that issued the prior branded title.

  • Confirm the exact ownership document, issuing state, and every title brand.
  • Ask whether Nebraska accepts the document and permits retitling and registration.
  • Verify whether an in-state inspection is required.
  • Check for liens, damage history, and additional documentation.

Transferring a vehicle between states does not remove its salvage history.

What should I check before buying a Nebraska salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Is it a Salvage Certificate of Title or Previously Salvaged title?
  • Why was the vehicle designated salvage, and was there a total-loss settlement?
  • Did estimated repair costs meet or exceed Nebraska’s 75% threshold?
  • Can the vehicle legally be repaired, inspected, titled, and registered?
  • What repairs were performed, by whom, and with what documentation?
  • Are parts receipts, repair invoices, and photographs available?
  • Has the required Nebraska identification inspection been completed?
  • Are there flood or out-of-state title brands?
  • Has an independent mechanic or body specialist inspected the vehicle?
  • Are there liens, insurance restrictions, or financing issues?
  • What are the remaining repair, transportation, inspection, title, tax, and registration costs?

For an unrepaired vehicle, ask:

Can this vehicle be repaired, inspected, titled, and registered for ordinary road use in Nebraska?

For a repaired vehicle, ask:

Can I verify the repairs, ownership history, and inspection documentation before buying it?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Use the appropriate ownership document and disclose the vehicle’s salvage or Previously Salvaged status. A Salvage Certificate of Title alone does not authorize ordinary highway registration.

The Previously Salvaged brand continues on subsequent titles and registrations. Properly assign the title or Salvage Certificate, resolve liens, and provide available repair and inspection records.

Vehicles with junk, parts-only, nonrepairable, or non-highway documents may face restrictions; verify their transfer and retitling eligibility before selling.

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Nebraska Department of Motor Vehicles, your county treasurer’s motor vehicle office, or another government agency.
