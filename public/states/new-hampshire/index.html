New Hampshire Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand how New Hampshire treats salvage and rebuilt vehicles, what happens during the salvage inspection, what documentation you may need, and where to find the official state requirements.

What you should know

A vehicle with a New Hampshire salvage title cannot simply be repaired and registered for normal road use. After the vehicle has been restored, it must undergo the required salvage inspection and complete the state's title and registration process.

The key transition is from salvage to rebuilt. After the requirements are satisfied, New Hampshire can issue a title and registration identifying the vehicle as a rebuilt vehicle. The rebuilt history does not disappear when the vehicle returns to the road.

Understand New Hampshire's terminology

Salvage vehicle

New Hampshire treats a vehicle as salvage when it has been declared a total loss under the state's salvage-title rules. The law includes vehicles considered physically or economically impractical to repair as part of an insurance claim settlement and, for certain newer vehicles, applies a repair-cost threshold based on the vehicle's pre-damage fair market value.

A salvage certificate of title serves as proof of ownership and identifies the vehicle as having been declared a total loss.

Rebuilt vehicle

A rebuilt vehicle is a vehicle that was once declared salvage and has been restored so that it can return to legal road use. When New Hampshire issues a qualifying rebuilt vehicle a new title and registration, they contain the legend “rebuilt vehicle.”

Junk vehicle

New Hampshire separately addresses vehicles classified as junk. A junk vehicle should not automatically be treated as equivalent to a salvage vehicle that can follow the normal salvage-to-rebuilt process.

Can a salvage vehicle return to the road?

Yes. A salvage vehicle can potentially return to legal road use in New Hampshire after it has been properly restored and completed the required salvage inspection and title process.

If New Hampshire has issued a salvage certificate of title after a vehicle was declared a total loss, the owner cannot obtain a new title or registration for the vehicle until the required inspection has been completed.

How do I get a salvage vehicle back on the road?

The general path is:

  1. Obtain and retain the vehicle's salvage ownership documents.
  2. Repair or restore the vehicle to the condition required for the salvage inspection.
  3. Keep documentation for the repairs and major component parts used in rebuilding the vehicle.
  4. Complete the Salvaged Motor Vehicle Affidavit Application.
  5. Schedule and pass a New Hampshire salvage inspection.
  6. Complete the applicable title and registration process.
  7. Receive a title and registration identifying the vehicle as rebuilt.

The exact paperwork can depend on the vehicle and its history, so use the current New Hampshire DMV requirements for the particular vehicle rather than treating this as a universal checklist.

What happens during the salvage inspection?

New Hampshire's salvage inspection is more than a check of the vehicle identification number. The authorized inspector verifies the VIN and reviews bills of sale, titles, or original repair receipts for major component parts used to rebuild the vehicle.

You should also be prepared to document the damage that caused the vehicle to be declared a total loss. New Hampshire's rules address the insurance adjuster's collision report and alternatives when that report cannot be obtained.

What does the inspection look at?

Current New Hampshire salvage guidance addresses areas including:

  • doors, hood, and bumpers
  • windshield and other vehicle glass
  • seat belts and airbags
  • seats
  • mirrors and sun visors
  • windshield wipers, washers, defroster, and heating
  • steering and suspension
  • the ABS system
  • body panels
  • the frame and undercarriage

For example, factory-installed airbags must be functioning, and the state's guidance calls for electronic verification. The frame and undercarriage must also meet the state's inspection expectations.

What should I bring to the salvage inspection?

New Hampshire DMV instructs applicants to bring documentation including:

  • proof that you own the vehicle, such as the applicable title
  • the insurance adjuster's report or appraisal identifying the damage
  • proof of repair of salvage items
  • bills of sale or title certificates for major component parts used to rebuild the vehicle
  • a completed Salvaged Motor Vehicle Affidavit Application (DSMV 545A)

The DMV warns that an incomplete DSMV 545A can result in the applicant being turned away and having to reschedule the inspection. Keep receipts and ownership documentation for major replacement parts as you rebuild rather than trying to reconstruct that paperwork when the vehicle is finished.

How do I schedule the salvage inspection?

New Hampshire DMV conducts salvage inspections at designated locations and requires an appointment. The state's current inspection-location document provides the locations, schedules, appointment instructions, items to bring, and current inspection fee.

Because locations, schedules, fees, and appointment procedures can change, use the current DMV information when you are ready to schedule rather than relying on a copied list.

What documents and forms might I need?

Important documents in the salvage-to-rebuilt process

Salvaged Motor Vehicle Affidavit Application (DSMV 545A)

Used to certify information about the restored vehicle and its identification numbers as part of the salvage-inspection process.

Application for Certificate of Title (TDMV 23 or TDMV 23B)

The applicable title application is prepared as required by New Hampshire's title process.

Salvage certificate of title

The properly assigned salvage title establishes the vehicle's salvage ownership history.

Vehicle Identification report (DSMV 547)

An authorized agent completes this document as part of the required inspection and verification process.

Additional documents can be required depending on the vehicle and circumstances. Use the current New Hampshire DMV requirements to determine exactly what applies.

What if the salvage vehicle came from another state?

New Hampshire also addresses vehicles that another state has declared salvage or junk. A vehicle with an out-of-state salvage or junk history must be submitted for inspection when an owner seeks a New Hampshire title or registration.

For a repaired or rebuilt vehicle, New Hampshire's rules provide for documentation including the applicable title application, the properly assigned out-of-state salvage certificate or other required ownership documentation, and evidence of the required vehicle inspection.

In some circumstances, New Hampshire can accept an out-of-state salvage inspection form verifying that a salvage inspection has already been completed. Do not assume that an out-of-state rebuilt or salvage title automatically eliminates New Hampshire's requirements.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in New Hampshire?

  • What does the current title or ownership document say?
  • Was the vehicle declared salvage in New Hampshire or another state?
  • Has the vehicle already been rebuilt and inspected?
  • If it has not been inspected, what repairs remain before it can pass New Hampshire's salvage inspection?
  • Can you obtain documentation showing the damage that caused the total-loss declaration?
  • Are there bills of sale, titles, or receipts for major component parts already used in the rebuild?
  • Are the airbags, seat belts, structural components, and other inspected systems complete and functioning?
  • If the vehicle came from another state, what additional New Hampshire title or inspection requirements apply?
  • What repairs, inspection costs, title and registration fees, transportation costs, and other expenses remain?

A low purchase price does not necessarily mean a low total cost. Understanding the inspection and documentation requirements before buying can reveal problems that may be expensive or difficult to correct later.

What should I know if I later sell the vehicle?

New Hampshire requires the salvage or rebuilt history to remain visible to future buyers.

Before selling a used vehicle whose title is required to be marked salvage, or whose title and registration are required to be marked rebuilt, the seller must disclose in writing that the vehicle is a salvage vehicle and explain the reason for that designation.

New Hampshire also places a “salvage vehicle” decal on a rebuilt vehicle as part of the process. State law prohibits removing or causing the removal of that decal.

Rebuilding the vehicle therefore does not erase its salvage history. That history remains relevant when you eventually sell it.
