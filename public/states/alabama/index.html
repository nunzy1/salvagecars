Alabama Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Alabama Salvage and Rebuilt titles, the 75% total-loss threshold, who is allowed to rebuild a salvage vehicle, the rebuilt inspection process, restrictive title brands, and what must happen before a salvage vehicle can return to ordinary road use.

What you should know

Alabama generally treats a damaged vehicle as a Salvage vehicle when an insurance company or another person makes a monetary settlement and the damage is 75% or more of the vehicle's fair retail value before the damage.

A vehicle with a Salvage title cannot be registered or operated on Alabama highways or other public places until a Rebuilt title has been issued.

Alabama allows an eligible Salvage vehicle to return to titled road use after it has been completely restored in Alabama, passes the required Alabama Department of Revenue inspection, and receives a Rebuilt title.

Who owned the vehicle before it became salvage matters. A prior owner has a path to apply without becoming a licensed rebuilder. Someone who did not own the vehicle before salvage generally needs a master dealer regulatory license to apply for the rebuilt inspection.

Vehicles branded Parts Only, Parts Car, Non-Rebuildable, Certificate of Destruction, or Junk cannot be rebuilt through this process.

Understand Alabama terminology

Salvage vehicle

Alabama describes a Salvage vehicle as one for which an insurance company or another person makes a monetary settlement when damage is at least 75% of the vehicle's fair retail value before the damage.

Review Alabama's Salvage vehicle guidance →

Rebuilt vehicle

A Rebuilt vehicle is restored in Alabama to the operating condition it had before the event that caused the Salvage title. After approval and inspection, Alabama attaches an inspection decal and issues a title carrying the legend REBUILT.

Review Alabama's Rebuilt vehicle requirements →

Restrictive ownership documents

Parts Only, Parts Car, Non-Rebuildable, Certificate of Destruction, and Junk documents do not provide a path through Alabama's rebuilt process.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, if it has an eligible Salvage title and can complete Alabama's rebuilt process. A vehicle that remains Salvage cannot be registered or operated on Alabama highways or other public places.

Before buying, ask: What exact ownership document does this vehicle have? Then ask: Am I legally eligible to have this particular vehicle inspected and retitled as Rebuilt in Alabama?

Who is allowed to rebuild a salvage vehicle in Alabama?

If you owned the vehicle before it became salvage

Alabama allows the prior owner to apply for the rebuilt inspection using documentation showing ownership before the Salvage title was issued instead of obtaining a rebuilder's license. The current process uses the Department of Revenue's online portal.

Review Alabama's prior-owner rebuilt requirements →

If you did not own the vehicle before it became salvage

Alabama requires a master dealer regulatory license to apply for a rebuilt inspection. An ordinary consumer should not assume that buying an Alabama Salvage-title vehicle at auction provides a personal path to rebuild it and apply for inspection.

How do I get an Alabama salvage vehicle back on the road?

  1. Confirm that the ownership document permits rebuilding.
  2. Confirm that you are eligible to apply.
  3. Completely restore the vehicle in Alabama.
  4. Keep bills of sale and identifying information for replacement components.
  5. Submit the rebuilt application and supporting documentation.
  6. Wait for review and approval.
  7. A Motor Vehicle Inspector will contact you to coordinate inspection.
  8. Present the completely restored vehicle.
  9. After approval and successful inspection, Alabama attaches the inspection decal and issues a REBUILT title.
  10. Complete applicable registration requirements before ordinary road use.

What inspection does Alabama require?

The Alabama Department of Revenue's Motor Vehicle Division performs the rebuilt inspection after the application has been processed. The inspection examines vehicle and component identification numbers and looks for indications that the vehicle or parts are stolen.

Review Alabama's rebuilt inspection requirements →

Does passing the inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

A buyer should not treat the existence of a Rebuilt title as a substitute for an independent evaluation of the vehicle's condition or repair quality.

Before buying, consider independently evaluating structural repairs, frame or unibody alignment, airbags and restraint systems, brakes, steering and suspension, electrical repairs, water or flood damage, mechanical condition, wheel alignment and tire wear, and the quality and origin of replacement components.

The useful question is not simply: Did Alabama issue a Rebuilt title?

It is also: What was damaged, how was it repaired, and what condition is the vehicle in now?

What documentation should I keep for replacement parts?

Alabama requires bills of sale for replaced components. For major component parts, bills of sale must include identifying information for the donor vehicle; current DOR guidance for prior owners states that major-component bills of sale must be notarized.

Keep the documentation as each component is purchased rather than trying to reconstruct the paper trail after the vehicle is finished.

What documents should I expect?

Depending on the situation, the process can involve the Salvage Certificate of Title, rebuilt inspection application, prior-ownership evidence, component bills of sale, donor-vehicle VIN information, fees, inspection documentation, and the Rebuilt-title application.

Alabama currently directs rebuilt applications through electronic systems. Use current DOR instructions rather than older paper submission procedures.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company declares it a total loss?

Alabama provides a rebuilt path for someone who owned the vehicle before it became salvage. If the insurer obtains the Salvage title and the prior owner subsequently acquires an Alabama Salvage Certificate of Title to the same vehicle, the owner can apply without obtaining a rebuilder's license, provided prior ownership can be documented.

Review Alabama's prior-owner application requirements →

What happens to an older vehicle that is declared a total loss?

Alabama's published Salvage definition centers on the 75% damage-to-value threshold and does not present a general age-based exemption. Do not assume an older vehicle is outside Alabama's salvage process simply because of its age.

Review Alabama's Salvage vehicle definition →

What if the vehicle was rebuilt outside Alabama?

Alabama's rebuilt process applies to a vehicle restored in Alabama. DOR guidance states that the state's rebuilt inspection process does not provide for inspection of a vehicle rebuilt in another state. Verify treatment of the existing title and rebuilt history before buying.

What if the vehicle has an out-of-state restrictive title?

Alabama specifically says a vehicle carrying a Parts Only brand from another state cannot be rebuilt through Alabama's process. Parts Car, Non-Rebuildable, Certificate of Destruction, and Junk documents are also restrictive. Verify the exact document and eligibility before paying for the vehicle, transportation, or repairs.

What should I check before buying an Alabama salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Does the damage meet Alabama's 75% Salvage threshold?
  • Did I own this vehicle before it became salvage?
  • If not, who is legally eligible to apply for its rebuilt inspection?
  • Would a master dealer regulatory license be required?
  • Does it carry a restrictive brand?
  • Was it already repaired, and were those repairs performed in Alabama?
  • Are parts records available and major components traceable?
  • Has repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Can you obtain the insurance and financing you want?
  • What repair, transportation, licensing, inspection, title, tax, registration, and other costs remain?

Even if I can buy this Salvage vehicle, am I legally eligible to complete Alabama's process for putting it back on the road?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Alabama requires disclosure when a Salvage or Rebuilt vehicle is sold. The seller must provide a disclosure, in type no smaller than 10-point, stating: “This vehicle's title contains the designation salvage or rebuilt.”

The ability to buy or own a Salvage vehicle is not the same as eligibility to apply for Alabama's rebuilt inspection.
