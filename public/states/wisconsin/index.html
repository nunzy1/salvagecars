Wisconsin Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Wisconsin salvage titles, Rebuilt Salvage branding, the 70% damage threshold, salvage inspections, major-parts documentation, and what must happen before a salvage vehicle can return to ordinary road use.

What you should know

Wisconsin generally defines a salvage vehicle as a vehicle less than seven model years old that is not considered junk and has been damaged by collision or another occurrence, other than hail damage, when the cost of repair exceeds 70% of the vehicle's fair market value.

Wisconsin also treats a vehicle of any model year as salvage when it was last titled in another jurisdiction with a salvage brand.

A vehicle with a salvage title can potentially return to road use after it is repaired and passes a Wisconsin salvage inspection performed by a certified state salvage inspector.

After successfully passing the inspection, the vehicle's title brand changes to Rebuilt Salvage.

A salvage vehicle cannot receive Wisconsin registration or license plates until it successfully completes the salvage inspection. Wisconsin generally prohibits operation of a salvage vehicle except for travel to or from an inspection site.

A junk vehicle is fundamentally different. Wisconsin does not issue titles for junk vehicles, and a vehicle designated junk cannot return to ordinary titled and registered road use.

Understand Wisconsin terminology

Salvage vehicle

Wisconsin generally considers a vehicle salvage when it is less than seven model years old, is not considered junk, and has collision or other qualifying damage, other than hail damage, for which the repair cost exceeds 70% of its pre-damage fair market value.

A vehicle of any model year that was last titled in another jurisdiction with a salvage brand is also treated as salvage.

Review Wisconsin DMV title-brand definitions →

Rebuilt Salvage

A Wisconsin salvage vehicle that is repaired and successfully passes the required salvage inspection receives the Rebuilt Salvage title brand.

Once the vehicle is branded Rebuilt Salvage, it becomes eligible for registration and license plates and can legally return to ordinary road use. The Rebuilt Salvage brand remains part of the vehicle's title history.

Junk vehicle

Wisconsin does not issue a title for a junk vehicle.

A vehicle designated junk, junked, or the equivalent in another state or country is not eligible for Wisconsin title or registration.

This makes the distinction between Salvage and Junk especially important before buying a vehicle for rebuilding.

Reconstructed vehicle

A Reconstructed vehicle is not simply another name for a rebuilt salvage vehicle.

Wisconsin uses Reconstructed for a motor vehicle that has been substantially altered or modified from the original manufacturer's specifications to such an extent that it no longer resembles the originally manufactured vehicle.

A conventional salvage vehicle that has been repaired and passed its salvage inspection receives the Rebuilt Salvage brand rather than the Reconstructed brand.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, if the vehicle is Salvage rather than Junk and can successfully complete Wisconsin's repair and inspection process.

The first question should be:

What exact ownership document and title brand does the vehicle have?

Then ask:

Will Wisconsin treat that document as repairable Salvage or as a non-titleable Junk vehicle?

A Wisconsin salvage title is eligible for title only while the vehicle remains salvage. It is not eligible for ordinary registration.

After the repaired vehicle successfully passes the required Wisconsin salvage inspection, it can receive the Rebuilt Salvage brand and become eligible for registration and license plates.

Do not rely solely on auction descriptions such as “repairable,” “rebuildable,” “salvage,” or “parts vehicle.” The actual ownership document and vehicle record matter.

How do I get a Wisconsin salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible salvage vehicle, the general path is:

  1. Establish ownership with an acceptable salvage title.
  2. Repair the vehicle.
  3. Keep documentation for major parts used during the repair.
  4. Complete the Major Parts Statement — Form MV2673.
  5. Obtain bills of sale for applicable replacement major parts.
  6. Schedule an inspection with a certified Wisconsin salvage title inspector.
  7. Present the repaired vehicle and required documentation for inspection.
  8. Pass the Wisconsin salvage inspection.
  9. Complete the applicable Wisconsin title and registration process.
  10. Receive a title carrying the Rebuilt Salvage brand.

Once the vehicle is branded Rebuilt Salvage, it becomes eligible for registration and ordinary road use.

What inspection does Wisconsin require?

A repaired salvage vehicle must be inspected by a certified Wisconsin salvage title inspector.

The inspector verifies that the vehicle and its individual parts are not stolen, required equipment is present, and the vehicle is in safe operating condition.

This makes Wisconsin different from states where the salvage inspection is primarily an identity or anti-theft examination.

Only authorized certified inspectors may perform the inspection, and inspections require an appointment.

Does passing the salvage inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

Wisconsin's inspection is more safety-oriented than many salvage inspections because it verifies required vehicle equipment and safe operating condition in addition to checking for stolen vehicles and parts.

But passing the inspection should not be interpreted as a comprehensive evaluation of repair quality or as proof that the vehicle is a good financial purchase.

Before buying a rebuilt vehicle, independently investigate structural repairs, steering and suspension, alignment, brakes, airbags and restraint systems, electrical systems, mechanical condition, flood or water damage, corrosion, replacement components, and evidence of incomplete or poorly performed repairs.

Also investigate whether you can obtain the insurance coverage you want and how the Rebuilt Salvage title history could affect future resale.

What documentation should I keep for replacement parts?

Wisconsin uses the Major Parts Statement — Form MV2673 for repaired salvage vehicles.

The form identifies major parts such as the engine, transmission, doors, hood, grille, bumpers, front fenders, deck lid, tailgate or hatchback, and rear quarter panels.

If a major part has been changed, a bill of sale must be attached for that part.

Parts documentation should therefore be collected as the vehicle is repaired rather than reconstructed immediately before inspection.

What documents and forms should I expect?

Depending on the vehicle and transaction, Wisconsin's process can involve:

  • the salvage certificate of title,
  • Title and License Plate Application — Form MV1,
  • Major Parts Statement — Form MV2673,
  • bills of sale for applicable replacement major parts,
  • salvage inspection documentation,
  • proof of ownership, and
  • applicable title, tax, registration, and fee information.

The exact documents depend on the vehicle and transaction.

Can I drive a vehicle while it still has a salvage title?

Ordinary road use is not allowed while the vehicle remains titled Salvage.

Wisconsin DMV states that operation of a salvage vehicle is illegal except to or from an inspection site.

A salvage vehicle is eligible for title only and cannot receive ordinary registration or license plates until it passes the salvage inspection.

After the vehicle successfully passes inspection and receives the Rebuilt Salvage brand, it becomes eligible for registration and can legally operate on Wisconsin roads.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

Wisconsin treats a vehicle of any model year as salvage when it was last titled in another jurisdiction with a salvage brand.

That means Wisconsin's less-than-seven-model-years rule does not erase an existing out-of-state salvage history.

However, a vehicle designated junk, junked, or the equivalent in another state or country is not eligible for Wisconsin title or registration.

Before buying an out-of-state vehicle, ask:

What exact ownership document and title brand does the vehicle have?

Then ask:

Will Wisconsin treat that document as Salvage or as a non-titleable Junk designation?

Do not assume that another state's Junk, Nonrepairable, Parts Only, Certificate of Destruction, or similar restrictive document will be treated like an ordinary salvage title.

What does Wisconsin's 70% threshold mean?

For the applicable Wisconsin salvage definition, the vehicle must be less than seven model years old and the estimated or actual repair cost, whichever is greater, must exceed 70% of the vehicle's fair market value.

Wisconsin defines fair market value using the condition of the vehicle immediately before the damage and reliable valuation sources generally accepted by the automotive and insurance industries.

Hail damage is excluded from this particular salvage definition.

The 70% threshold should not be used by itself to evaluate an out-of-state vehicle that already carries a salvage or restrictive title brand. Wisconsin separately recognizes existing out-of-state salvage history.

What happens to an older vehicle that is declared a total loss?

An insurance company's decision to declare a vehicle a total loss does not, by itself, determine the Wisconsin title brand.

Wisconsin's ordinary Salvage brand based on collision or other qualifying damage applies to vehicles less than seven model years old when repair costs exceed 70% of fair market value. The Claim Paid brand is also limited to vehicles less than seven model years old and applies in certain insurer-transfer situations involving damage above 30% but not more than 70% of fair market value.

As a result, an older Wisconsin-titled vehicle can be declared a total loss by an insurer without automatically receiving either a Salvage or Claim Paid brand. If no other Wisconsin branding rule applies, the insurance total-loss determination alone does not put the vehicle through Wisconsin's Salvage → inspection → Rebuilt Salvage process.

This does not mean every older total-loss vehicle remains titleable. Wisconsin's Junk rules are separate. A vehicle can be considered junk if it is incapable of highway operation and has no resale value except as parts or scrap, or if an insurance company takes possession of or title to the vehicle and the estimated repair cost exceeds its fair market value. A junked vehicle cannot later receive a Wisconsin title.

Other brands can also apply independently. For example, Wisconsin's Flood Damaged brand is not limited to vehicles less than seven model years old.

For buyers, the practical consequence is important: an older Wisconsin vehicle can have significant prior damage or an insurance total-loss history without showing a Salvage or Rebuilt Salvage brand on its Wisconsin title. An unbranded title should therefore not be treated as proof that an older vehicle has never been totaled or substantially damaged.

What should I check before buying a Wisconsin salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Is it Salvage, Rebuilt Salvage, Junk, or something else?
  • Will Wisconsin issue the title you ultimately need?
  • Why did the vehicle enter the salvage system?
  • Does the 70% threshold apply to this vehicle?
  • For an older vehicle, has it ever been declared a total loss or sustained major damage even if the Wisconsin title does not show a Salvage or Rebuilt Salvage brand?
  • Has repair work already started?
  • Are bills of sale available for replacement major parts?
  • Can the source of those components be documented?
  • Has the Wisconsin salvage inspection already occurred?
  • If the vehicle came from another state, what exact brand appears on its ownership document?
  • Has repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, inspection, transportation, title, tax, registration, and other costs remain?

The central Wisconsin question is:

Does this vehicle have an ownership document Wisconsin will accept as repairable Salvage, and can its repairs and major replacement parts be documented well enough to pass the Wisconsin salvage inspection and obtain a Rebuilt Salvage title?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Wisconsin title brands are permanent notations that provide future buyers with information about a vehicle's history.

A salvage vehicle that has been repaired and successfully inspected receives the Rebuilt Salvage brand.

Wisconsin requires sellers to disclose applicable title-brand information.

If you later sell the vehicle, accurately represent its Rebuilt Salvage history and comply with the title-transfer and disclosure requirements that apply at the time of sale.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Wisconsin Department of Transportation, Wisconsin State Patrol, or another government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, inspections, and procedures can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate Wisconsin agency.
