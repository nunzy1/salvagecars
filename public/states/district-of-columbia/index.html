District of Columbia Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand DC Salvage Titles, Rebuilt Salvage Titles, non-repairable vehicles, inspection requirements, insurance total losses, and what must happen before an eligible salvage vehicle can return to the road.

What you should know

The District of Columbia requires a Salvage Title for vehicles meeting its legal definition of a salvage vehicle.

A vehicle generally meets that definition when the estimated or actual cost of repairing damage exceeds 75% of its pre-damage retail value. The owner can also voluntarily designate a qualifying vehicle as salvage.

The owner must apply for a Salvage Title within 30 calendar days of the damage and before repairing the vehicle.

A vehicle with a Salvage Title cannot be registered for ordinary road use.

An eligible salvage vehicle can return to the road after completing DC DMV's required safety, emissions, and anti-theft inspections and obtaining a Rebuilt Salvage Title.

DC also recognizes junk or non-repairable vehicles. These vehicles cannot complete the ordinary salvage-to-rebuilt registration process.

Understand DC terminology

Salvage Vehicle

Under DC law, a Salvage Vehicle is a motor vehicle, other than a qualifying historic motor vehicle, that meets either of these conditions:

  • The estimated or actual cost of parts and labor needed to restore the vehicle to its pre-damage condition and legal road use exceeds 75% of its pre-damage retail value.
  • The owner voluntarily designates the vehicle as salvage under DC law.

The damage may result from a collision, fire, flooding, or another event. The threshold is based on pre-damage retail value using an approved vehicle-valuation source.

The threshold is more than 75%, not 75% or more.

Salvage Title

A Salvage Title is issued by DC DMV to identify a vehicle as salvage. It cannot be registered for ordinary road use, and ownership must be transferred using its Salvage Title.

Rebuilt Salvage Title

A Rebuilt Salvage Title identifies a vehicle previously issued a Salvage Title that subsequently passed the required inspections. DC DMV issues the title after completion of the applicable process. Ownership must be transferred using its Rebuilt Salvage Title, and the designation remains part of the title history.

Junk Vehicle Certificate and Non-Repairable designation

DC law also recognizes Junk Vehicles: vehicles DMV determines cannot operate on public roads and have no resale value except as parts or scrap, or vehicles voluntarily designated junk by their owners.

A vehicle issued a Junk Vehicle Certificate cannot be titled or registered by DC DMV. DC DMV's public guidance also uses the term Non-Repairable.

Before purchasing a vehicle with either designation, verify its exact ownership document and restrictions. Do not assume it can be repaired and registered as a Rebuilt Salvage Vehicle.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially.

An eligible vehicle with a DC Salvage Title can return to road use after completing the required inspections and obtaining a Rebuilt Salvage Title.

DC DMV requires salvage vehicles to pass:

  • DC safety inspection
  • Applicable emissions inspection
  • Metropolitan Police Department (MPD) anti-theft inspection

The vehicle must also satisfy ordinary DC registration requirements, including valid insurance. A vehicle with a Junk Vehicle Certificate cannot be titled or registered by DC DMV.

Before buying, determine:

  • What exact ownership document the vehicle has.
  • Whether it is salvage or non-repairable.
  • Whether it can be repaired to pass the required inspections.
  • Whether it can receive a Rebuilt Salvage Title.
  • Whether it has already completed any required inspections.

How do I get a DC salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible salvage vehicle, the general process is:

  1. Obtain the Salvage Title and verify that the ownership document is properly assigned.
  2. Confirm that the vehicle is eligible for the Rebuilt Salvage process.
  3. Repair the vehicle so it can meet applicable safety and emissions requirements.
  4. Take the vehicle through DC DMV's safety and emissions inspection process, telling the inspector that a salvage inspection is required.
  5. Obtain the passed salvage inspection report.
  6. Complete the MPD Anti-Theft Inspection Form and DC DMV Salvage Vehicle Procedure Acknowledgment Form.
  7. Submit the required documents through DC DMV at 95 M Street SW, Washington, DC 20024.
  8. Complete the MPD anti-theft review and any additional physical inspection required by MPD.
  9. After approval, complete the remaining DC DMV titling and registration requirements.
  10. Obtain the Rebuilt Salvage Title and registration.

DC DMV's procedure requires the safety and emissions inspection before the MPD anti-theft review.

DC DMV states that salvage vehicle transactions are issued only one 45-day temporary tag. The MPD Anti-Theft Inspection Form must be submitted through DC DMV; MPD does not accept it in person.

What inspections and verification are required?

DC uses two distinct inspection processes for salvage vehicles.

DC DMV safety and emissions inspection

The vehicle must pass applicable DMV inspection requirements. Tell the inspector that a salvage inspection is required. If the vehicle cannot pass, DC DMV's procedure states it can be processed only for a Salvage Title, not registration as Rebuilt Salvage.

MPD anti-theft inspection

After the DMV inspection, the owner submits the required paperwork to DC DMV for the Metropolitan Police Department's anti-theft review. MPD examines the vehicle's title and history to verify its identity and investigate possible theft-related concerns. MPD may require a physical inspection.

DC DMV's procedure identifies these documents:

  • MPD Anti-Theft Inspection Form
  • Original vehicle title
  • Current DC insurance
  • Current DC driver license or identification card
  • Passed salvage inspection report
  • DC DMV Salvage Vehicle Procedure Acknowledgment Form

The exact documents can depend on whether the vehicle is being processed for a Salvage Title only or for Rebuilt Salvage registration.

Does passing the required inspections mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

DC's safety, emissions, and anti-theft inspections are part of the legal process for registering an eligible salvage vehicle. They are not a substitute for an independent evaluation of the vehicle's condition and repair quality.

Before buying a rebuilt salvage vehicle, consider independently evaluating:

  • Structural repairs
  • Frame or unibody alignment
  • Airbags and restraint systems
  • Brakes, steering, and suspension
  • Electrical systems
  • Water or flood damage
  • Mechanical condition
  • Wheel alignment and tire wear
  • Rust, corrosion, and evidence of poor-quality body repairs
  • Replacement component quality

The useful question is not simply: Did the vehicle pass DC's required inspections?

It is also: What was damaged, how was it repaired, and what condition is the vehicle in now?

What repair and parts documentation should I keep?

DC's published salvage registration procedure identifies specific documents required for inspection and anti-theft review. Unlike some states, the reviewed procedure does not establish a detailed statewide list of required repair receipts or donor vehicle VIN records.

However, keeping a complete repair history is valuable. Consider retaining:

  • Original damage assessments
  • Insurance settlement documentation
  • Photographs taken before and during repairs
  • Replacement parts invoices
  • Used-parts receipts
  • Donor vehicle information for major used components
  • Repair invoices
  • Safety and emissions inspection reports
  • MPD anti-theft approval documentation

Before repairs, confirm whether DC DMV requires additional documents for your vehicle. Do not assume another state's receipts or photograph requirements apply in DC.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company declares it a total loss?

DC provides specific rules for owner-retained salvage vehicles.

When an insurer settles a claim for a salvage or junk vehicle but does not take ownership, it must notify both the owner or lessor about the obligation to obtain the appropriate ownership document and DC DMV under the prescribed procedure.

The insurer must provide these notifications within 30 calendar days of settlement. The owner must apply for a Salvage Title within 30 days of damage and before repairs.

These are separate deadlines; the insurer's notification deadline does not replace the owner's obligation. If the insurer acquires ownership, DC law generally gives it 30 days after delivery of the title to apply for the appropriate document.

Keeping a totaled vehicle does not permit ordinary road use under a Salvage Title.

Before rebuilding an owner-retained vehicle, determine:

  • Whether the vehicle meets DC's salvage or junk definition.
  • Whether the required Salvage Title application was submitted.
  • Whether the insurer completed its notifications.
  • Whether the vehicle is eligible for rebuilding.
  • Which inspections apply.
  • Whether a lien affects title or settlement.

If the vehicle has a lien, ask the insurer how the total-loss settlement will be handled and the lienholder what must happen to the outstanding loan. Then ask DC DMV what ownership documents are needed for the Salvage Title and subsequent rebuilding process.

Is there an age exemption from DC's salvage process?

DC law excludes qualifying historic motor vehicles from its statutory definition of a Salvage Vehicle. This is not a general exemption for every older vehicle.

A vehicle must meet DC's historic motor vehicle definition. An older vehicle that does not qualify can still be subject to salvage requirements, including the repair-cost threshold.

Before buying or rebuilding an older damaged vehicle, determine:

  • Whether it qualifies as a historic motor vehicle under DC regulations.
  • What ownership document it has.
  • Whether it has already been designated salvage or junk.
  • Whether DC DMV will issue the ownership document and registration needed for its intended use.
  • Which inspections apply.

Vehicle age alone does not establish that the historic-vehicle exclusion applies.

What about flood-damaged vehicles?

DC defines a Flood Vehicle as one submerged to the point that water entered its passenger compartment or trunk.

An owner or lessor transferring a Flood Vehicle must give the buyer written notice of flood status before transfer and notify DC DMV. A lessee whose vehicle becomes a Flood Vehicle must notify the lessor within 30 days of damage.

DC DMV must indicate flood status on the title when notified or when it determines through inspection that the definition applies. Flood damage can also contribute to the separate Salvage Vehicle definition.

Before buying, investigate:

  • Electrical systems and electronic modules
  • Wiring and connectors
  • Airbags and restraint systems
  • Corrosion
  • Interior materials
  • Mechanical components
  • Mold or contamination

Water-related problems can appear after repairs and may not be obvious during a brief inspection.

What if the vehicle comes from another state?

DC DMV specifically addresses out-of-state salvage vehicles. An out-of-state title branded Salvage can be titled in DC after the required emissions, safety, and anti-theft inspections.

DC DMV states that the new title retains the previous state's salvage brand, such as MD Salvage, VA Salvage, or CA Salvage. The prior title history does not disappear.

Before buying, determine:

  • The exact ownership document and issuing state.
  • Every title brand or notation.
  • Whether the document is transferable and accepted by DC DMV.
  • Whether the vehicle can complete DC's inspections and MPD anti-theft review.
  • What title brand DC will issue.
  • Whether it can be registered for its intended use.

DC law also authorizes DMV to carry forward another jurisdiction's information about damage or restrictions on use.

What should I check before buying a DC salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Is it a Salvage Title, Rebuilt Salvage Title, or Junk Vehicle Certificate?
  • Why was it declared salvage?
  • Did repair costs exceed 75% of pre-damage retail value?
  • Has the required Salvage Title been issued?
  • Has repair work already begun?
  • Can it pass DC safety and emissions inspections?
  • Can it complete MPD anti-theft review?
  • Are damage photos and repair records available?
  • Has repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Is there evidence of flood damage?
  • Are there unresolved liens or ownership issues?
  • Can you obtain the insurance you want?
  • If financing is needed, will a lender accept its title history?
  • What repair, transportation, inspection, title, tax, registration, and other costs remain?

For an unrepaired salvage vehicle, ask: Can this vehicle be repaired to meet DC's inspection requirements and qualify for a Rebuilt Salvage Title?

For a repaired vehicle, ask: Has it completed the DMV and MPD requirements, or will I need to finish that process after purchasing it?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

DC requires ownership of a Salvage Vehicle to be transferred using a Salvage Title. Once rebuilt and issued a Rebuilt Salvage Title, ownership must be transferred using that title.

A vehicle with a Junk Vehicle Certificate has stricter restrictions: ownership may be transferred only once, and DC DMV cannot title or register it.

For a Flood Vehicle, the owner or lessor must notify the buyer in writing of flood status before transfer and notify DC DMV.

Before selling, ensure the ownership document accurately reflects the vehicle's status. Do not represent a salvage or junk vehicle as rebuilt salvage unless it has completed the required process.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the District of Columbia Department of Motor Vehicles, Metropolitan Police Department, or another government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, and procedures can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate District of Columbia agency.
