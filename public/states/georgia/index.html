Georgia Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Georgia salvage and rebuilt titles, rebuilder licensing, the rebuilt-vehicle inspection, parts documentation, and whether a vehicle can legally return to the road.

What you should know

A salvage vehicle cannot legally be operated on Georgia public roads while it remains in salvage status.

For a qualifying vehicle to return to road use, it must complete Georgia's rebuilt-vehicle process and receive a title branded REBUILT.

Three requirements deserve particular attention before buying:

  • A person purchasing a salvage or wrecked vehicle for the purpose of restoring or rebuilding it must be a licensed rebuilder.
  • Photographs must document the vehicle in its salvage or wrecked condition before repairs are made.
  • The rebuilt vehicle must be inspected after restoration but before it is painted.

Some vehicles cannot return to the road at all. A vehicle declared nonrebuildable under another state's laws cannot receive a Georgia title and may be used only for parts. Georgia can also determine during inspection that damage is too extensive to return a vehicle to a safe, operable condition.

Understand Georgia terminology

Salvage vehicle

Georgia identifies several circumstances in which a vehicle is considered salvage. These include certain vehicles damaged to the extent that restoration requires replacement of two or more component parts, vehicles for which an insurance company has paid a total-loss claim and that have not yet been repaired, and vehicles coming from another state with certain brands such as Total Loss, Salvage, Fire, Flood, or Water.

Salvage title

A salvage title documents ownership of a vehicle that is in salvage status. It does not mean the vehicle is approved for normal road use.

Rebuilt vehicle

After an eligible salvage vehicle has been properly rebuilt and successfully completed the required inspection and title process, Georgia issues a certificate of title carrying the legend REBUILT.

Nonrebuildable or parts-only vehicle

Not every damaged vehicle can enter Georgia's rebuilt process. Georgia law states that if another state has declared a vehicle nonrebuildable, Georgia will not issue a certificate of title for it. The vehicle may be used only for parts.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Possibly. A qualifying salvage-titled vehicle can potentially be rebuilt, inspected, and issued a Georgia REBUILT title.

However, there are two separate questions to answer before spending money on the vehicle:

Is the vehicle legally eligible to be rebuilt?

Are you legally eligible to rebuild it?

Georgia Department of Revenue guidance states that anyone who purchases a salvage or wrecked vehicle for the purpose of restoring or rebuilding it must be licensed as a rebuilder.

Do I need to be a licensed rebuilder?

If you purchase a salvage or wrecked vehicle for the purpose of restoring or rebuilding it, Georgia says you must be a licensed rebuilder. The rebuilt-title application also requires a copy of the rebuilder's license.

If you are not a licensed rebuilder, do not assume that you can buy a salvage vehicle, rebuild it yourself, and then simply present it for inspection. Verify the current licensing requirements before purchasing the vehicle.

How do I get a salvage vehicle back on the road?

  1. Make sure the vehicle's ownership document allows it to be rebuilt.
  2. Make sure the person purchasing and rebuilding the salvage vehicle satisfies Georgia's rebuilder licensing requirements.
  3. Photograph the vehicle in its salvage or wrecked condition before repairs begin.
  4. Repair or restore the vehicle.
  5. Keep detailed receipts and documentation for the parts used.
  6. Complete Form T-129 — Labor and Parts Certification.
  7. Prepare Form T-22R — Request for Inspection of a Rebuilt Motor Vehicle as required for the inspection route being used.
  8. Have the restored vehicle inspected by an authorized Georgia salvage vehicle inspector before the vehicle is painted.
  9. Provide the required vehicle, ownership, parts, repair, photograph, and licensing documentation.
  10. Correct any deficiencies identified during inspection.
  11. Submit the required title application and inspection documentation.
  12. If approved, receive a Georgia title branded REBUILT.

Why must I photograph the vehicle before repairs?

Georgia requires one or more photographs showing the vehicle in its salvage condition before any repairs are made. The photographs are used during the state's inspection and title process.

Georgia's rules require the photographs to be sufficient in size and quality to clearly show damage to the vehicle's major component parts.

If you purchase a salvage vehicle that someone else has already begun repairing, ask whether compliant pre-repair photographs exist. Missing photographs can create a title-processing problem later.

When should the vehicle be inspected?

This timing requirement is especially important:

The vehicle must be restored, but it must be inspected before it is painted.

Georgia law requires a person rebuilding or repairing a salvage motor vehicle to obtain the required inspection before painting the vehicle. Do not finish the vehicle cosmetically and then assume it is ready for inspection.

What does the Georgia rebuilt inspection establish?

Georgia's rebuilt inspection is more than a visual confirmation that the vehicle exists. The inspection includes verification of the vehicle identification number, the salvage title, the Labor and Parts Certification, repaired or replaced parts, and the required pre-repair photographs.

Georgia law also requires the inspection to verify that a vehicle represented as having been rebuilt in Georgia was actually rebuilt in Georgia. The state's rebuilt-title process requires documentation identifying the rebuilder, the work performed, and the parts used in the rebuild.

Who can perform the rebuilt inspection?

Georgia allows the required rebuilt-vehicle inspection to be performed by a state inspector or an approved private inspector.

A private inspector must be registered with the Georgia Department of Revenue and meet the state's qualifications for salvage-vehicle inspectors. The inspector cannot be the builder, rebuilder, or owner of the vehicle being inspected.

During a rebuilt-vehicle inspection, the registered inspector verifies the VIN, reviews the Labor and Parts Certification and repaired or replaced parts, verifies the required pre-repair photographs, and completes the state's Salvage Motor Vehicle Inspection Report.

The application and submission process differs somewhat depending on whether you use a state inspector or an approved private inspector. Check the current Georgia Department of Revenue instructions before scheduling the inspection.

Does passing the inspection guarantee the vehicle is a good purchase?

No. The Georgia inspection is an important legal and regulatory step, but a REBUILT title should not be treated as a general warranty of the vehicle's future reliability or the quality of every repair.

A prospective buyer should still investigate structural repair quality, mechanical condition, electrical systems, restraint and airbag systems, corrosion or water damage, wheel alignment and suspension condition, insurance availability, and future resale implications.

A legal road title answers a different question from whether the particular vehicle is a sensible purchase.

Why are parts receipts important?

Georgia requires detailed documentation for parts used to restore or rebuild a salvage vehicle.

Parts receipts should identify the parts and stock numbers and show the vehicle owner or rebuilder as the purchaser. For used parts, Georgia requires additional information, including the name of the part and the VIN of the vehicle from which the part was removed.

The parts vehicle must have a title on file with the Georgia Department of Revenue or its title must be submitted with the application. If the parts came from another state, additional certification from that state may be required.

Georgia uses Form T-129 — Labor and Parts Certification to document the rebuilding work and parts.

Can I drive the vehicle to the inspection?

For a vehicle still in salvage status, plan on transporting rather than driving it. Georgia states that salvage vehicles cannot legally be operated on public roads.

For the Georgia-salvage rebuilt process using a state inspector, the Department of Revenue instructs that the vehicle must be towed, not driven, to the inspection site.

There is a distinction for certain vehicles that already have a valid out-of-state rebuilt title. Georgia's current instructions state that such a vehicle may be driven to the inspection site if it has valid out-of-state registration; otherwise, it must be towed.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

Georgia's treatment depends heavily on the exact out-of-state ownership document. Georgia requires the rebuilt process for vehicles carrying certain out-of-state brands, including Salvage, Flood, Water, Fire, and Total Loss.

A vehicle that already has a valid out-of-state title branded rebuilt follows a somewhat different Georgia process from a vehicle that is still titled as salvage.

Most importantly, Georgia law states that if another state has declared a vehicle nonrebuildable, Georgia will not issue a title for that vehicle. It may be used only for parts.

Do not rely solely on an auction's description of the title.

What if Georgia determines the vehicle cannot safely be rebuilt?

Passing through the rebuild process is not guaranteed merely because the vehicle has a salvage title.

Georgia law gives the state authority to determine during inspection that damage is so extensive that returning the vehicle to a safe, operable condition is impossible. If that determination is made, the salvage certificate is revoked and the vehicle may be used only for scrap or parts.

Georgia law states that such a vehicle cannot later receive a title, including through a surety bond.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in Georgia?

  • What is the exact ownership document?
  • Is the vehicle legally eligible for Georgia's rebuilt process?
  • Has another state classified it as nonrebuildable?
  • Has it previously been scrapped, dismantled, demolished, or designated for parts?
  • Are you or the person rebuilding it properly licensed?
  • Were clear photographs taken before repairs began?
  • Has someone already started repairing or painting the vehicle?
  • What major components need replacement?
  • Are compliant receipts available for parts already installed?
  • Can the source of used parts be documented?
  • Are the VIN and other identifying information intact?
  • Which authorized inspector will inspect the vehicle?
  • Can the vehicle be inspected before painting?
  • How will it be transported to inspection?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, inspection, title, licensing, transportation, tax, and registration costs remain?

For Georgia, the key question is not simply Can I rebuild this vehicle?

Is this vehicle legally rebuildable, am I permitted to rebuild it, and can I document and inspect the rebuild the way Georgia requires?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Georgia's rebuilt history does not disappear after the vehicle completes the process. The Georgia title carries the REBUILT legend, informing subsequent owners of the vehicle's history.

Georgia law requires a person, business, or corporation that rebuilds or repairs a vehicle whose current title is marked salvage to apply for and obtain the appropriate certificate of title before selling or transferring the vehicle.

Georgia's administrative rules further state that no vehicle subject to this rebuilt process may be sold or transferred until the Georgia certificate of title has been issued and received by the owner. After inspection, the rebuilder or individual must also provide the purchaser with copies of the applicable documents at the time of sale.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Georgia Department of Revenue, Motor Vehicle Division, the agency responsible for rebuilder licensing, or another appropriate government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, inspection procedures, licensing rules, and other requirements can change and can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate government agency.
