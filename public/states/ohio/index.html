Ohio Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Ohio salvage and rebuilt salvage titles, the Ohio State Highway Patrol inspection, parts-documentation requirements, and whether a vehicle can legally return to the road.

What you should know

An Ohio salvage certificate of title is not a normal road title.

If a vehicle with a salvage certificate of title is restored for highway operation, Ohio requires an inspection by the Ohio State Highway Patrol before a rebuilt title can be issued.

The inspection includes verifying ownership, checking vehicle identification information, reviewing documentation for materials and component parts used in the restoration, and checking applicable vehicle equipment requirements.

After the process is successfully completed, the new Ohio title is branded REBUILT SALVAGE. That brand remains on subsequent Ohio titles.

A vehicle whose ownership document is marked FOR DESTRUCTION cannot use the normal rebuilt process. Ohio law restricts such a vehicle to parts and scrap metal.

Understand Ohio terminology

Salvage certificate of title

A salvage certificate of title identifies a vehicle as salvage rather than as an ordinary road vehicle. A vehicle that remains titled with a salvage certificate generally cannot be operated on Ohio highways. Ohio law provides a limited exception for delivering the vehicle pursuant to an appointment for the required inspection.

Rebuilt salvage

If a salvage-titled vehicle is restored for highway operation and successfully completes Ohio's required process, the Clerk of Courts can issue a regular-form certificate of title branded REBUILT SALVAGE.

The brand does not disappear after another transfer. Subsequent Ohio titles, memorandum titles, and duplicate titles for that vehicle must continue to carry the REBUILT SALVAGE notation.

FOR DESTRUCTION

Ohio law also recognizes salvage certificates marked FOR DESTRUCTION in certain circumstances. A vehicle with this restriction is not a candidate for the normal rebuilt-salvage process and may be used only for parts and scrap metal.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Possibly. A vehicle with an appropriate salvage certificate of title can potentially be restored, inspected, and issued an Ohio REBUILT SALVAGE title.

But do not assume that every damaged or salvage vehicle has that path. Before buying, identify the exact ownership document and any restrictions attached to it.

In particular, a vehicle whose title has been marked FOR DESTRUCTION cannot be restored through the normal rebuilt-salvage process for highway use.

How do I get a salvage vehicle back on the road?

  1. Establish acceptable ownership of the salvage vehicle.
  2. Repair or rebuild the vehicle for highway operation.
  3. Keep receipts, bills of sale, titles, and other ownership documentation for major component parts used during the rebuild.
  4. Obtain the required Ohio motor vehicle Receipt for Inspection.
  5. Schedule the required inspection with the Ohio State Highway Patrol.
  6. Take the completed vehicle and required documentation to the inspection.
  7. Have the State Highway Patrol inspect the vehicle, its identification information, component parts, documentation, and applicable equipment.
  8. Receive the inspection documentation after successfully completing the process.
  9. Apply through a Clerk of Courts title office for the new certificate of title and surrender the salvage certificate as required.
  10. Receive an Ohio title branded REBUILT SALVAGE.

What does the Ohio salvage inspection establish?

Ohio's inspection has several purposes. State law requires the Ohio State Highway Patrol to establish proof of ownership and inspect the vehicle's motor number and vehicle identification number. The owner must also present documentation or receipts for materials used in the restoration.

Ohio's administrative rules also require identification of component parts used to rebuild or restore the vehicle. The inspection can include the vehicle identification number, other manufacturer-applied identifying numbers, major body components, the frame, drivetrain components, and other component parts used during reconstruction.

Ohio's rules also require salvage-titled vehicles to be inspected for compliance with applicable state equipment requirements. This means the inspection is not simply a paperwork check.

Does the inspection certify that the vehicle meets every safety standard?

No.

Ohio requires salvage-titled vehicles to be inspected for compliance with specified state equipment requirements. However, Ohio's rules also require the Ohio State Highway Patrol inspector to document that the vehicle has not been inspected to meet federal motor vehicle safety standards.

In other words, passing Ohio's rebuilt-salvage inspection should not be interpreted as a certification that the vehicle meets all federal motor vehicle safety standards or as a general guarantee of its condition, repair quality, or crashworthiness.

A buyer should still independently evaluate the quality and condition of the rebuilt vehicle.

Why are receipts and parts documentation important?

Ohio specifically requires proof of ownership for major component parts used in rebuilding a salvage vehicle.

For major components purchased through a casual sale, Ohio's administrative rules call for a notarized bill of sale. For components purchased through a business, an official business receipt can be used. A certificate of title can also establish ownership of an applicable component.

Major component parts include, but are not limited to, major body parts, the frame, and drivetrain components. Keep this documentation as you rebuild the vehicle rather than trying to recreate the paper trail immediately before inspection.

How do I get the inspection receipt?

Ohio's administrative rules require an applicant for a rebuilt-salvage inspection to obtain a motor vehicle Receipt for Inspection through a deputy registrar location.

The original inspection receipt must be presented to the inspecting officer. Ohio's rules state that the inspection will not be performed without the original receipt.

Because payment methods, scheduling systems, and operational procedures can change, use current Ohio BMV and Ohio State Highway Patrol information when you are ready to schedule the inspection.

Can I drive a salvage-titled vehicle to the inspection?

Ohio generally prohibits operating a vehicle on the highway while its title is evidenced by a salvage certificate. However, Ohio law provides an exception for delivering the vehicle pursuant to an appointment for the required inspection.

Do not interpret that exception as general permission to drive a salvage-titled vehicle before it has completed the rebuilt process. Before driving the vehicle to an inspection, verify the current transportation, registration, insurance, and appointment requirements that apply to your situation.

What documents might I need?

Salvage certificate of title

Establishes the vehicle's salvage ownership status and is surrendered as part of the rebuilt-title process.

Motor vehicle Receipt for Inspection

Required for the Ohio State Highway Patrol inspection.

Receipts and invoices

Used to document applicable component parts purchased from businesses.

Notarized bills of sale

Used under Ohio's rules to document major components obtained through casual sales.

Certificates of title for component parts

Can be used when applicable to establish ownership of components.

Ohio State Highway Patrol inspection documentation

Evidence from the required rebuilt-salvage inspection.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

Treat the exact ownership document as a major pre-purchase question. Ohio's title system recognizes salvage history from other states, but out-of-state terminology is not uniform.

A document described by an auction or seller simply as a “salvage title” may contain a brand or restriction that materially affects what can be done with the vehicle.

  • Inspect the actual ownership document.
  • Identify every brand or restriction on it.
  • Determine whether Ohio will issue the ownership document needed for the rebuilt process.
  • Confirm applicable inspection and titling requirements with the Ohio BMV or Clerk of Courts title office.

Do not rely solely on an auction's description of the title.

What about a vehicle marked FOR DESTRUCTION?

This is one of the most important Ohio-specific checks.

Ohio law allows certain salvage certificates to be marked FOR DESTRUCTION. A motor vehicle whose title or assignment has been marked FOR DESTRUCTION and surrendered as provided by Ohio law may be used only for parts and scrap metal.

It cannot simply be repaired, inspected, and converted to a REBUILT SALVAGE road title through the normal process. Before buying a damaged vehicle with the intention of rebuilding it, verify that its ownership document does not carry this restriction.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in Ohio?

  • What is the exact ownership document?
  • Is it an Ohio salvage certificate, an out-of-state salvage document, or an already rebuilt title?
  • Does the ownership document contain a restriction such as FOR DESTRUCTION?
  • Can Ohio issue the title needed to enter the rebuilt-salvage process?
  • What repairs remain?
  • What major components have already been replaced?
  • Are receipts, invoices, notarized bills of sale, or titles available for those components?
  • Are the VIN and other identifying numbers intact and consistent?
  • Has the vehicle already undergone an Ohio State Highway Patrol inspection?
  • If not, is it ready to meet the applicable equipment requirements?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, inspection, title, tax, registration, and transportation costs remain?
  • Has the quality of the completed repairs been independently evaluated?

The key question is not simply Can I fix this vehicle?

Can I document the vehicle and its parts, pass Ohio's required inspection, obtain a REBUILT SALVAGE title, and end up with a vehicle that makes sense for me to own?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Ohio's REBUILT SALVAGE brand is persistent. Once an Ohio rebuilt-salvage title is issued, subsequent Ohio certificates of title, memorandum titles, and duplicate titles must continue to carry the REBUILT SALVAGE notation.

Ohio also has consumer-disclosure requirements that can matter when dealers sell rebuilt-salvage vehicles. Ohio law provides a remedy in specified dealer transactions when a vehicle's title indicates rebuilt salvage and that status was not disclosed to the retail purchaser in writing before the purchase agreement was executed.

If you later sell or trade a rebuilt-salvage vehicle, accurately represent its branded title history and verify the transfer and disclosure requirements that apply to your transaction. If you are buying from a dealer, make sure the vehicle's rebuilt-salvage history is disclosed in the transaction documents.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Ohio Bureau of Motor Vehicles, Ohio State Highway Patrol, Clerk of Courts title office, or another appropriate government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, inspection procedures, and other rules can change, and the requirements that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate government agency.
