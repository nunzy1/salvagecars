Delaware Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Delaware Salvage Certificates, reconstructed vehicles, State Police Auto Theft Unit inspections, repair and parts documentation, owner-retained total losses, and what must happen before an eligible salvage vehicle can return to the road.

What you should know

When a Delaware-titled vehicle becomes salvage as the result of a total-loss insurance settlement, Delaware issues a Salvage Certificate as the vehicle's proof of ownership.

A vehicle with a Salvage Certificate cannot be driven until it has been reconstructed, presented to the Delaware State Police Auto Theft Unit for inspection, passed the applicable Delaware DMV safety inspection, and been retitled.

Delaware requires substantial documentation during the reconstruction process. Before repairs begin, the rebuilder must take color photographs showing the vehicle's damage. Repairs must be documented, receipts must be kept for parts and repairs, and receipts for used parts must identify the VIN of the vehicle from which the parts came.

There is another particularly important restriction: Delaware State Police will not inspect a salvage vehicle if its repairs were performed by a previous salvage owner.

In practical terms, the person or Delaware dealer who owns the salvage vehicle while it is being repaired should be the one who takes it through the State Police inspection process. A vehicle repaired while owned by one salvage owner and then sold to another owner before inspection can create a problem because State Police will not inspect it for the new owner.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially.

A vehicle with an eligible Delaware Salvage Certificate can be reconstructed and returned to road use after completing the required inspections and retitling process.

Before buying, ask: What exact ownership document does this vehicle have?

Then ask: Who owned the vehicle while the repairs were performed?

That second question matters in Delaware because State Police will not inspect a salvage vehicle if its repairs were performed by a previous salvage owner.

Does passing the State Police inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

The Delaware State Police Auto Theft Unit inspection serves the state's salvage and vehicle-identification process. The vehicle also goes through the applicable DMV vehicle inspection requirements before retitling.

Neither process should be treated as a substitute for an independent pre-purchase evaluation of a rebuilt vehicle's present condition or repair quality.

What photographs does Delaware require?

Take the photographs before repairs begin.

Delaware requires color photographs showing the entire vehicle before it is repaired. DMV's owner-retained guidance says two photographs are normally sufficient when they show the rear and entire left side, and the front and entire right side.

Do not begin repairs before documenting the vehicle.

This requirement also makes a salvage vehicle that has already been repaired risky to purchase if the previous owner did not take the required photographs—and, separately, State Police will not inspect a salvage vehicle whose repairs were performed by a previous salvage owner.

Can I buy a repaired or partially repaired Delaware salvage vehicle and finish the process myself?

This can create a serious problem.

Delaware State Police states that the Auto Theft Unit will not inspect a salvage vehicle if the repairs were performed by a previous salvage owner.

In practical terms, the Delaware resident or Delaware dealer who owns the salvage vehicle while it is being repaired should take it through the State Police inspection before transferring it to another salvage owner.

The inspection documentation must also be in the same dealer's or individual's name, and the required photographs must have been taken before repairs were performed.

Do not assume you can purchase someone else's repaired or unfinished salvage project and then present it for Delaware inspection.

Is there an age exemption from Delaware's salvage process?

Delaware does not provide a general vehicle-age exemption from its total-loss salvage process.

Separate Delaware rules apply to some antique, older, or specially classified vehicles, but a vehicle's age alone does not remove the salvage requirements that result from a total-loss insurance settlement.

Do not assume that an older vehicle is exempt from Delaware's salvage requirements simply because of its age.

What about flood-damaged vehicles?

Delaware recognizes Flood Damaged as a title brand.

A flood-damaged vehicle can present risks that extend beyond the title designation. Determine the exact title brand, the extent of the water exposure, and what systems were repaired or replaced.

Delaware's dealer-disclosure requirements specifically include vehicles whose titles are branded flood damaged.

What if the vehicle comes from another state?

Vehicles purchased outside Delaware generally must complete Delaware's titling and inspection requirements before a Delaware title is issued.

For a salvage, rebuilt, reconstructed, flood-damaged, or similarly branded vehicle, the exact out-of-state ownership document and title brands are particularly important.

Some states identify title brands with abbreviations or codes rather than words such as Salvage, Rebuilt, or Flood. Delaware DMV recommends checking the meaning of any unfamiliar notation on an out-of-state title.

Understand Delaware terminology

Salvage Certificate

When a Delaware-titled vehicle becomes salvage as the result of a total-loss insurance settlement, Delaware requires the ordinary title to be replaced by a Salvage Certificate.

If the insurance company takes ownership of the totaled vehicle, it submits the vehicle's title to Delaware DMV as part of that process. If the owner keeps the totaled vehicle, Delaware provides a separate owner-retained salvage procedure for obtaining the Salvage Certificate.

Delaware recognizes the Salvage Certificate as proof of ownership.

The Salvage Certificate does not authorize ordinary road use.

Reconstructed vehicle

Delaware defines a reconstructed vehicle as a vehicle assembled or constructed largely using essential parts from other vehicles, or an originally constructed vehicle that has been materially altered through the removal, addition, or substitution of essential parts.

In Delaware's salvage process, the damaged vehicle must be reconstructed before it can complete the State Police and DMV requirements needed for retitling and registration.

How do I get a Delaware salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible vehicle with a Salvage Certificate, the general process is:

  1. Make sure the Salvage Certificate is properly assigned to the Delaware resident or Delaware dealer who will rebuild and present the vehicle for inspection.
  2. Obtain the bill of sale or, for an owner-retained vehicle, the applicable total-loss insurance settlement letter.
  3. Take the required color photographs of the entire vehicle before performing repairs.
  4. Repair or reconstruct the vehicle.
  5. Document all repairs.
  6. Keep the required receipts for parts and repairs.
  7. For used replacement parts, make sure the receipt identifies the VIN of the vehicle from which the part was taken.
  8. Present the vehicle and required documentation to the Delaware State Police Auto Theft Unit.
  9. Pass the Auto Theft Unit inspection.
  10. Complete the applicable Delaware DMV safety and emissions inspections.
  11. Complete DMV retitling and registration requirements.

When the vehicle successfully passes the Auto Theft Unit inspection, the inspectors provide an Application for Title, Form MV214.

What documentation do I need for replacement parts and repairs?

Delaware requires all repairs to be documented and receipts to be retained for parts and repairs.

For parts, the current State Police instructions require the item purchased, purchase price, purchaser's name and address, and purchase date. If a used part came from another vehicle, the donor vehicle VIN must appear on the receipt.

Delaware's current State Police instructions state that handwritten receipts are not accepted.

The Salvage Certificate, bill of sale or applicable total-loss settlement letter, and receipts for parts and repairs must all be in the same dealer's or individual's name.

Do not wait until the rebuild is complete to reconstruct the parts history.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company declares it a total loss?

Delaware expressly provides an owner-retained salvage process.

If the owner receives a total-loss insurance settlement and chooses to keep the vehicle, the insurer can submit the certificate of title to DMV as part of the salvage process, or require the owner to obtain a Salvage Certificate from DMV before the insurer pays the total-loss settlement.

Under the current DMV procedure, the owner provides the Delaware title and a letter from the insurance company stating that the vehicle is an owner-retained salvage vehicle resulting from a total-loss insurance settlement.

If the vehicle's registration is still current, DMV places the license plate in retention when the Salvage Certificate is issued.

The vehicle cannot simply continue to be driven because the owner retained it. It must be reconstructed, complete the required inspections, and be retitled before returning to ordinary road use.

What should I check before buying a Delaware salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Why was the vehicle declared a total loss?
  • Has anyone already started repairing it?
  • Who owned the vehicle when those repairs were performed?
  • Were the required color photographs taken before repairs?
  • Are compliant receipts available for all parts and repairs?
  • Do receipts for used parts identify the donor vehicle VIN?
  • Are the Salvage Certificate, bill of sale or settlement letter, and receipts all in the required name?
  • Will the Delaware State Police Auto Theft Unit accept the vehicle for inspection?
  • Has the repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Are there unresolved liens or ownership issues?

For an unrepaired vehicle, ask: Can I document this rebuild from the beginning in the way Delaware requires?

For a repaired or partially repaired salvage vehicle, ask: Were any of these repairs performed while the Salvage Certificate was in someone else's name?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Delaware law specifically requires a dealer or dealer's agent to disclose to a buyer when a vehicle's title is branded reconstructed, flood damaged, salvage or rebuilt salvage, or previous taxi.

The buyer must acknowledge the disclosure on a DMV-approved disclosure statement.

For private sales, use the ownership document that accurately reflects the vehicle's title history and follow Delaware's current title-transfer requirements.
