South Dakota Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand South Dakota salvage and rebuilt title requirements, vehicle inspections, and where to find official state guidance.

What you should know

South Dakota generally requires a salvage title when an insurer or self-insurer determines that a qualifying vehicle is a total loss because of theft, fire, vandalism, collision, weather, submersion in water, or flooding. Unlike states that use a specific repair-cost percentage, South Dakota’s salvage-title definition focuses on the insurer’s total-loss determination.

The insurance-related salvage-title definition excludes vehicles more than ten model years old and vehicles with a gross vehicle weight rating greater than 16,000 pounds. These exclusions do not necessarily eliminate other title, damage-disclosure, or inspection requirements.

South Dakota also has a separate title procedure for certain stolen vehicles recovered after an insurance total-loss determination. Depending on the vehicle’s condition and the applicable requirements, it may receive a title identifying it as Recovered Theft rather than a salvage title. A theft-related total loss does not always produce the same title classification as a vehicle totaled because of physical damage.

When an insurer declares a qualifying vehicle a total loss but the owner retains it, the owner must obtain a salvage title before selling or transferring the vehicle. An eligible vehicle may subsequently receive a Rebuilt title after meeting documentation and inspection requirements.

Vehicles designated nonrebuildable or parts only are treated differently from repairable salvage vehicles and may receive a junking certificate. Verify the vehicle’s title status and eligibility with the South Dakota Department of Revenue before buying or rebuilding.

Understand South Dakota’s title terminology

Salvage: A designation for qualifying vehicles determined to be a total loss by an insurer or self-insurer, or carrying an applicable out-of-state salvage brand.

Rebuilt: A designation for a vehicle restored to operation under applicable requirements, or carrying a recognized out-of-state rebuilt brand.

Recovered Theft: A designation available to certain stolen vehicles recovered after a theft-related total loss under statutory conditions.

Junking Certificate: A document for vehicles designated for dismantling, parts, or other non-road uses.

Nonrebuildable / Parts Only: Restrictive classifications that do not permit return to ordinary road use through the rebuilt-title process.

For more information, see South Dakota Codified Laws — Chapter 32-3

Can a salvage vehicle return to the road?

A salvage title does not, by itself, authorize operation on public roads. An eligible vehicle must be restored to operating condition, meet the applicable inspection and title requirements, and be registered before ordinary highway use.

Vehicles designated nonrebuildable or parts only are not eligible for ordinary road use through the rebuilt-title process. Confirm title and registration status before driving; otherwise, arrange transportation.

How do I get an eligible salvage vehicle back on the road?

South Dakota requires documentation and an inspection for eligible rebuilt vehicles. The general process is:

  1. Confirm the title status and eligibility for rebuilding.
  2. Complete necessary repairs and preserve documentation for replacement parts and labor.
  3. Gather existing titles, ownership records, bills of sale, invoices, and receipts for the vehicle and major components.
  4. Complete the Application for Motor Vehicle Title & Registration and the Application for Inspection of Rebuilt Motor Vehicle.
  5. Submit the applicable application and documentation through the county treasurer’s office.
  6. Schedule an inspection at a designated South Dakota location and present the vehicle and supporting documents.
  7. After inspection, submit the inspection certificate and remaining title documents and complete applicable tax and registration requirements.
  8. Receive a title bearing the Rebuilt designation after the Department of Revenue approves the application.

For current instructions, see South Dakota Department of Revenue — Rebuilt Vehicles

Rebuilt vehicle inspections

South Dakota requires a rebuilt-vehicle inspection. Under state law, an employee of the Department of Revenue or a highway patrol officer may perform the examination.

The process helps establish vehicle identity and verify ownership and documentation for the components used in rebuilding. Additional examination may apply to vehicles with components from different vehicles or incomplete ownership documentation.

Keep original titles or manufacturer documents, bills of sale, invoices, and receipts for replacement parts and labor. The rebuilt-vehicle inspection should not be treated as a comprehensive mechanical or safety certification. Consider an independent inspection to evaluate repair quality and roadworthiness.

What documents and forms may be required?

Depending on the vehicle, documentation may include:

  • Application for Motor Vehicle Title & Registration (Form 1001).
  • Application for Inspection of Rebuilt Motor Vehicle and accompanying affidavit (Form 1016).
  • Existing salvage title or other acceptable ownership document.
  • Original titles or manufacturers’ statements or certificates of origin for applicable components.
  • Bills of sale, invoices, and receipts identifying replacement parts and labor.
  • Documentation of the source of major components.
  • Power of Attorney (Form 1023), if an agent signs for the owner.
  • Certificate of inspection after the required examination.
  • Applicable tax and registration documents.

The rebuilt-vehicle inspection application includes a notarized affidavit. Required documents vary by vehicle history and construction.

For current forms and instructions, see South Dakota Department of Revenue — Rebuilt Vehicles

Where to schedule an inspection

South Dakota conducts rebuilt-vehicle inspections at three designated locations: Sioux Falls, Pierre, and Rapid City. Applicants are responsible for scheduling their inspections.

Before scheduling, confirm that the vehicle is fully assembled and in running condition and that required applications and ownership documents are prepared.

For current inspection schedules, locations, and scheduling instructions, see South Dakota Department of Revenue — Rebuilt Vehicles

What if the salvage vehicle came from another state?

South Dakota distinguishes out-of-state salvage titles from out-of-state rebuilt titles. A vehicle carrying an out-of-state salvage brand generally receives a South Dakota salvage title or, at the owner’s option, a junking certificate. A vehicle with an out-of-state rebuilt designation approved for highway use generally receives a South Dakota rebuilt title.

A vehicle’s out-of-state salvage or rebuilt title history remains on its South Dakota title, even if the designation changes to South Dakota’s terminology.

Before purchasing, confirm the existing title classification, whether South Dakota will issue a salvage title, rebuilt title, or junking certificate, and whether additional inspection or ownership documentation is required.

For statutory requirements, see South Dakota Codified Law § 32-3-51.5

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in South Dakota?

A salvage or rebuilt vehicle may cost less than a comparable unbranded vehicle, but its condition, history, and reconstruction requirements can affect safety, value, and registration eligibility.

  • Verify whether the title is salvage, rebuilt, recovered theft, or subject to a junking certificate.
  • Confirm the vehicle can be rebuilt, titled, and registered for the intended use.
  • Investigate collision, theft, fire, weather, and flood damage.
  • Request repair photographs, parts receipts, invoices, and information about the work performed.
  • Consider an independent mechanical and structural inspection.
  • Verify ownership documentation for major replacement components.
  • Confirm insurance availability and estimate the full cost, including transportation, repairs, inspection, and registration.

A rebuilt title indicates compliance with applicable title requirements, not a guarantee of mechanical condition or repair quality.

What should I know if I later sell the vehicle?

Buyers and sellers must complete the applicable title-transfer documents and preserve required title-brand information. When an insurer declares a qualifying vehicle a total loss but the owner retains it, the owner must obtain a salvage title before selling or transferring it. The insurer must notify the owner of this requirement in writing.

Provide a properly assigned title and required ownership documentation. If the vehicle has already been rebuilt, its title continues to identify it as rebuilt. Purchasers generally must apply for an updated title within 45 days of purchase.

For current transfer guidance, see South Dakota Department of Revenue — All Vehicles: Title, Fees & Registration

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the South Dakota Department of Revenue or another appropriate government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, inspection procedures, and other rules can change, and the requirements that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate state agency.
