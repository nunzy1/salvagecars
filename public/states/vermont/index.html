Vermont Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Learn how Vermont classifies salvage vehicles, what inspections are required for a Rebuilt/Restored title, and what to consider before buying or repairing a salvage vehicle.

What you should know

Vermont generally considers a vehicle salvaged when it has been acquired as salvage, scrapped, dismantled, destroyed, or declared a total loss by an insurance company. The state’s statutory definition does not establish a single repair-cost percentage that applies to every salvage vehicle.

Vermont requires a salvage certificate of title for qualifying vehicles. The application generally must be submitted within 15 days after the vehicle is acquired as salvage, dismantled, destroyed, or declared a total loss, subject to statutory exemptions and special insurer procedures.

Vermont has a separate exception for certain stolen vehicles. A vehicle that is stolen and recovered without damage does not require a salvage certificate, provided its original vehicle identification number (VIN) plate remains intact, has not been altered, and matches the original title. A recovered vehicle with damage may be subject to different requirements.

A salvage vehicle that is eligible for rebuilding may return to highway use after being repaired and completing Vermont’s rebuilt-vehicle inspection and title requirements. The examination includes verification of the VIN and ownership records for major components, along with applicable safety documentation. The resulting title carries a Rebuilt brand.

Vermont distinguishes salvage and rebuilt vehicles from those designated Dismantled (Parts Only) or Junk. These restrictive designations affect whether a vehicle may be driven, titled, or registered.

Vermont also requires sellers to disclose salvage, rebuilt, or totaled status both orally and in writing before a sale or transfer, including when the vehicle has already been repaired.

Before buying, rebuilding, or transferring a salvage vehicle, verify its title status and requirements with the Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles.

Understand Vermont’s title terminology

Salvage: A vehicle acquired as salvage, scrapped, dismantled, destroyed, or declared a total loss by an insurer.

Salvage certificate of title: A certificate of ownership identifying a salvaged motor vehicle; it does not establish eligibility for ordinary highway operation.

Totaled: A vehicle declared a total loss by an insurance company.

Rebuilt: A vehicle previously subject to a salvage or comparable restrictive ownership document that has been restored for highway operation and has satisfied applicable examination requirements.

Salvage Retention: A title designation indicating the owner retained a vehicle classified as salvage.

Dismantled (Parts Only): A vehicle that may be sold for parts but cannot legally be driven.

Junk: A vehicle classified as ineligible for titling or registration and valued only for parts or scrap.

Reconstructed: A vehicle permanently altered from its original construction by removing, adding, or substituting major components; distinct from an ordinary repaired salvage vehicle.

Vermont also maintains brands for flood damage, saltwater damage, fire damage, collision, recovered theft, and other conditions. A vehicle may carry more than one applicable brand.

For more information, see Vermont DMV — Title Brands.

Can a salvage vehicle return to the road?

A salvage certificate of title does not, by itself, authorize a vehicle to be operated on public roads.

An eligible salvage vehicle must be rebuilt and restored for highway operation, complete the required rebuilt-vehicle examination, and satisfy Vermont’s title and registration requirements before returning to ordinary road use.

A vehicle designated Dismantled (Parts Only) cannot legally be driven. Vermont’s Junk designation identifies a vehicle that cannot be titled or registered.

After the rebuilt process, the vehicle receives a title branded Rebuilt. That designation identifies its previous salvage history.

Before driving a repaired vehicle, verify that its title, registration, insurance, and inspection status permit operation. If the vehicle is not authorized for road use, arrange to have it transported rather than driving it.

How do I get an eligible salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible salvage vehicle, the process generally includes:

  1. Confirm the vehicle’s title status. Review the existing title or salvage documentation and verify eligibility for rebuilding.
  2. Obtain the salvage certificate of title. If an insurer has not already obtained and transferred it, determine whether you must apply for one.
  3. Complete the repairs. Restore the vehicle to operating condition and keep records of major components and replacement parts.
  4. Gather the required records. Prepare ownership documents, bills of sale, component records, a vehicle history report, and other documents requested by DMV.
  5. Complete the required forms. Prepare the Rebuilt/Salvage Title Application (VT-017), VIN verification (VT-010), and other applicable forms.
  6. Obtain safety documentation. Arrange the after-repair vehicle safety inspection report and any other required inspection.
  7. Complete the rebuilt-vehicle examination. Present the vehicle and records to DMV or an authorized representative.
  8. Apply for the rebuilt title and registration. Submit the application, inspection documentation, ownership records, and registration materials.
  9. Complete titling and registration. Once approved, the title identifies the vehicle as Rebuilt. Confirm registration and insurance requirements before driving.

The precise documents and inspection arrangements depend on the vehicle’s history. For current instructions, see Vermont DMV — Salvage or Rebuilt Title.

Rebuilt vehicle inspections

Vermont requires a rebuilt-vehicle examination before a rebuilt or totaled vehicle covered by its rebuilt-vehicle law can receive a new title or registration.

The examination is conducted by the Commissioner of Motor Vehicles or an authorized representative. It includes VIN verification and review of bills of sale or titles for major component parts used to rebuild the vehicle.

Vermont DMV also requires documentation showing that the repaired vehicle meets applicable safety standards. Its guidance identifies an after-repair vehicle safety inspection report among the documents needed for the rebuilt process.

Rebuilt-vehicle examination: Reviews vehicle identity, major-component ownership records, and eligibility for a rebuilt title.

Safety inspection: Evaluates the vehicle against applicable Vermont safety standards.

VIN verification: Confirms identification information and may be required for vehicles with salvage documentation.

When necessary, Vermont may assign a new VIN.

Vermont also requires periodic inspections for registered vehicles at approved stations. That ongoing requirement is separate from the rebuilt-vehicle examination.

What documents and forms may be required?

Depending on the vehicle and transaction, documents may include:

  • VT-017 — Rebuilt/Salvage Title Application.
  • VT-010 — Verification of VIN/HIN.
  • VD-119 — Registration/Tax/Title Application.
  • Existing title, salvage certificate, and bill of sale.
  • Bills of sale, receipts, or titles for major components used during reconstruction.
  • After-repair vehicle safety inspection report.
  • Vehicle history report, such as CARFAX or a comparable report.
  • Identification and residency documentation, when required.
  • Proof of insurance for registration.
  • Applicable lien-release and ownership documentation.

Form VT-017 requests information about the vehicle’s damage and damaged components. If the original VIN is missing or cannot be used, Vermont may require an assigned VIN application (VT-003).

For current forms and instructions, see Vermont DMV — Salvage or Rebuilt Title.

Where do I arrange an inspection?

Vermont requires a rebuilt-vehicle examination by the Department of Motor Vehicles or an authorized representative. This examination is separate from the safety inspection performed at state-approved vehicle inspection stations.

To arrange the rebuilt-vehicle examination, contact Vermont DMV to confirm where the vehicle must be presented, how to schedule the examination, and which documents to bring.

For the required safety inspection, use Vermont’s official inspection station locator to find a state-approved station.

If a separate VIN verification is required, confirm with DMV who is authorized to perform it and whether it can be completed during another examination.

Before transporting the vehicle, verify which inspections are required and whether the vehicle is authorized to be driven.

What if the salvage vehicle came from another state?

Vermont recognizes title brands issued by other jurisdictions. When an out-of-state title is transferred to Vermont, the existing brands are carried forward.

A vehicle being restored for highway use after receiving a salvage certificate, parts-only certificate, or comparable restrictive ownership document from another jurisdiction may be subject to Vermont’s rebuilt-vehicle examination requirements.

Before buying or bringing an out-of-state salvage or rebuilt vehicle into Vermont, confirm:

  • Whether the existing title or ownership document is acceptable.
  • Which damage and salvage brands Vermont will carry forward.
  • Whether the vehicle is eligible for a Vermont title and registration.
  • Whether Vermont’s rebuilt-vehicle examination is required.
  • Whether VIN verification or a safety inspection is necessary.
  • Whether ownership and major-component documentation is complete.

Do not assume an out-of-state rebuilt designation eliminates every Vermont inspection or documentation requirement. See Vermont DMV — Title Brands.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in Vermont?

A salvage or rebuilt vehicle may cost less than an unbranded vehicle, but its damage history, repair quality, and title classification can affect safety, insurance, registration, and resale value. Before purchasing:

  • Verify the title brand. Check for Salvage, Rebuilt, Totaled, Dismantled (Parts Only), Junk, and other relevant designations.
  • Confirm eligibility. Make sure the vehicle can be titled and registered for its intended use.
  • Investigate damage. Review collision, flood, fire, theft, or other damage records.
  • Review repairs. Ask for photographs, invoices, and receipts for major components.
  • Arrange an independent inspection. Have a qualified mechanic or collision repair specialist assess the vehicle.
  • Verify the VIN. Match the identification number to the ownership documents.
  • Confirm insurance. Check whether the desired coverage is available.
  • Estimate the full cost. Include repairs, transportation, inspections, titling, and registration.
  • Review disclosures. Vermont requires applicable salvage, rebuilt, and totaled status to be disclosed before the transaction.

A Rebuilt title confirms completion of applicable titling requirements; it does not guarantee the quality of every repair.

What should I know if I later sell the vehicle?

Vermont has specific disclosure requirements for anyone selling, trading, or offering to sell or trade a salvaged, salvaged and rebuilt, or totaled vehicle.

The seller must disclose the vehicle’s status both orally and in writing before the sale or transfer.

Written disclosure must be conspicuously included on the bill of sale, purchase agreement, transfer document, or other applicable agreement. The vehicle’s title must also carry the required designation.

Under Vermont law, a seller who fails to provide the required disclosures may be required, at the buyer’s option, to refund the purchase price, including taxes, license fees, and similar governmental charges.

A seller should provide the properly assigned title and other required ownership documents. Before completing the sale, verify that the title accurately identifies the vehicle’s history and that disclosures are documented.

For more information, see 23 V.S.A. § 2093 — Salvaged, Totaled, and Rebuilt Vehicles.
