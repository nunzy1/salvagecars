Maine Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Maine salvage certificates, Repaired, Rebuilt, and Rebuilt Salvage title brands, owner-retained insurance total losses, older vehicles that may not require a title, parts documentation, inspections, and what must happen before a salvage vehicle can return to ordinary road use.

What you should know

Maine uses a Certificate of Salvage to document a vehicle that has been declared salvage.

When an insurer declares an insured vehicle a total loss, the insurer must apply for a Certificate of Salvage. This applies even when the owner wants to keep the vehicle. In an owner-retained total loss, the insurer obtains the salvage certificate and then transfers it back to the owner.

A salvage vehicle can potentially be repaired or rebuilt for road use, but Maine requires the vehicle and its component parts to be inspected and verified before it can be titled or registered for operation.

The resulting Maine title brand depends on how the vehicle was repaired. Maine distinguishes among Repaired, Rebuilt, and Rebuilt Salvage vehicles.

Maine also has an unusual age-based title rule. In 2026, vehicles with a model year of 2001 or newer generally require a title. Vehicles more than 25 years old are generally outside the normal title requirement.

That title rule is separate from the question of whether a vehicle has been declared salvage or has prior salvage history.

Understand Maine terminology

Certificate of Salvage

Maine issues a Certificate of Salvage when a vehicle has been declared a salvage vehicle.

When an insurer declares a Maine-titled vehicle salvage, the insurer or its designee must surrender the title and apply for a Certificate of Salvage within 30 days after settlement of the insurance claim.

An owner who independently declares a titled vehicle salvage generally must surrender the title and obtain a Certificate of Salvage before transferring the vehicle, unless it is transferred to a licensed recycler as permitted by Maine law.

Repaired

A Maine title can be branded Repaired when a salvage vehicle is repaired without replacing component parts.

Rebuilt

A Maine title is generally branded Rebuilt when a salvage vehicle has at least one but fewer than five component parts replaced.

For purposes of this particular title-brand calculation, Maine law does not count airbags as component parts.

Rebuilt Salvage

A Maine title must be branded Rebuilt Salvage in circumstances that include five or more component parts being replaced, two or more vehicles with different frames being joined, a total-loss vehicle being repaired using a front or rear clip, or an existing Rebuilt Salvage title from Maine or another jurisdiction accompanying the application.

The number and type of components used during reconstruction therefore affect the resulting title brand.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially.

Maine law allows an eligible salvage vehicle to be repaired or rebuilt for operation on public ways.

Before the repaired or rebuilt vehicle can be titled or registered for operation—or offered for sale in Maine—the vehicle identification number and identification numbers of its component parts must be inspected and verified. A new VIN may be assigned when necessary.

Before buying, ask:

What exact ownership document and title history does this vehicle have?

Then determine:

Can the vehicle complete Maine's repaired-or-rebuilt salvage process, and what title brand will result?

Do not assume that every repaired salvage vehicle receives the same title brand.

How do I get a Maine salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible salvage vehicle, the general path is:

  1. Establish ownership using the appropriate Certificate of Salvage or other acceptable ownership documentation.
  2. Repair or rebuild the vehicle for operation.
  3. Keep documentation for the vehicle and any component parts used during the repair.
  4. Have the vehicle and applicable component identification numbers inspected and verified.
  5. Complete Form MVT-103, Affidavit of Rebuilt or Repaired Salvage Vehicle.
  6. Provide the required title or salvage documents, repair and parts information, and other required documentation to Maine BMV.
  7. Apply for the appropriate Maine title and registration.
  8. Receive a title carrying the applicable Repaired, Rebuilt, or Rebuilt Salvage brand.

The resulting title brand depends on the nature and extent of the reconstruction rather than simply on the fact that the vehicle was once salvage.

What inspection does Maine require?

Maine law requires the identification number of a repaired or rebuilt salvage vehicle and its component parts to be inspected and verified before the vehicle can be titled or registered for operation or offered for sale.

If necessary, Maine can assign a new vehicle identification number.

The inspection and documentation requirements help establish the identity of the vehicle, the identity and source of replacement components, and whether the vehicle's reconstruction is properly documented.

The process should not be treated as a comprehensive evaluation of repair quality.

Does passing the inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

Maine's salvage inspection and documentation process addresses vehicle identity, component identity, and the legal requirements associated with rebuilding a salvage vehicle.

It does not replace an independent evaluation of issues such as:

  • structural repairs,
  • frame or unibody alignment,
  • airbags and restraint systems,
  • brakes, steering, and suspension,
  • electrical repairs,
  • water or flood damage,
  • corrosion,
  • mechanical condition, and
  • the quality of replacement components and repairs.

A vehicle can satisfy Maine's title requirements and still have repair-quality or mechanical issues that matter to a buyer.

What documentation should I keep for replacement parts?

Documentation is particularly important in Maine because the parts used during reconstruction can determine the resulting title brand.

Maine uses Form MVT-103, Affidavit of Rebuilt or Repaired Salvage Vehicle, to document the repairs and replacement parts.

For replacement parts, the repairer identifies whether the part was new, used, or repaired.

For new components, MVT-103 requires bills of sale to accompany the form. For used parts, the repairer provides donor-vehicle VIN and title information.

Maine law also allows the Secretary of State or a future transferee to require the repairer or rebuilder to produce receipts for the vehicle or component parts used in reconstruction.

Keep this documentation while the vehicle is being repaired rather than trying to reconstruct the parts history later.

What documents and forms should I expect?

Depending on the vehicle and transaction, Maine's process can involve:

  • the Certificate of Salvage or other acceptable ownership document,
  • the title application,
  • Form MVT-103, Affidavit of Rebuilt or Repaired Salvage Vehicle,
  • receipts or invoices for replacement components,
  • donor-vehicle VIN and title information for used components,
  • inspection and identification documentation,
  • lien documentation when applicable,
  • registration documentation, and
  • applicable taxes and fees.

The exact documentation depends on how the vehicle was repaired and which title brand applies.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company totals it?

Maine specifically addresses this situation.

If the insurance company declares the vehicle a total loss, Maine law requires the insurer to apply for a Certificate of Salvage.

According to Maine's Bureau of Insurance, that remains true when the owner wants to keep and repair the vehicle. The owner turns the title over to the insurer, the insurer obtains the salvage certificate, and the insurer then transfers that salvage certificate back to the owner.

After the vehicle is repaired, the owner applies to Maine BMV for a new title.

The resulting title is branded Repaired, Rebuilt, or Rebuilt Salvage depending on the nature and extent of the repairs.

For buyers, the important point is: Keeping a totaled vehicle does not allow the owner to bypass Maine's salvage-certificate process.

What happens with an older totaled vehicle?

Maine requires us to separate two different questions:

Does the vehicle require a Maine title because of its age?

and

Does the vehicle have salvage or total-loss history?

They are not the same question.

Maine requires titles for vehicles with a model year within the last 25 years.

As of January 1, 2026:

  • model year 2001 or newer requires a Maine title under the age-based rule, and
  • a model year 2000 or older vehicle falls outside that normal age-based title requirement.

The cutoff moves forward as vehicles age. Maine BMV states that beginning January 1, 2027, model year 2002 or newer vehicles require titles.

This means an older Maine vehicle may legitimately be transferred or registered without the type of current certificate of title a buyer would expect for a newer vehicle, subject to the documentation required for the particular transaction.

That does not mean the vehicle has no ownership history or that prior salvage history can be ignored.

For an older vehicle, determine what ownership document the seller actually has, whether the vehicle was previously titled, whether it was ever declared a total loss or salvage, whether a Certificate of Salvage or branded title was previously issued, whether it came from another jurisdiction with a title or salvage document, and what Maine BMV requires for the particular transaction.

“No Maine title required because of age” does not mean “no salvage history.”

How does Maine determine whether a vehicle is salvage?

Maine does not rely on the simple 70% or 75% repair-cost threshold used by some states for its general salvage definition.

Maine BMV describes a salvage vehicle as one damaged by collision, fire, flood, accident, trespass, or other occurrence to the extent that its fair salvage value plus the cost of repairing the vehicle for legal operation on roads or highways exceeds its fair market value before the damage.

That means a buyer should not assume that a familiar percentage threshold from another state applies in Maine.

For an insurance total loss, the insurer's declaration also triggers Maine's salvage-certificate process.

The practical questions are therefore: What was the vehicle worth before the damage? What is its salvage value? What would it cost to repair it for legal road operation? Was it declared a total loss by an insurer or owner? What ownership document was issued afterward?

What if the vehicle came from another state?

This deserves particular attention in Maine.

Maine law states that the Secretary of State may refuse to title a vehicle declared salvage in another jurisdiction.

Maine can also carry forward certain prior title legends from another jurisdiction. A vehicle previously branded Rebuilt or Rebuilt Salvage can therefore retain that history when titled in Maine.

Do not assume that possession of an out-of-state Salvage title guarantees that Maine will issue a road-use title after the vehicle is repaired.

Before buying an out-of-state salvage vehicle for a Maine reconstruction project:

Verify the exact ownership document with Maine BMV and confirm that Maine will accept the vehicle for the intended title process.

That verification is particularly important before paying transportation costs or investing in repairs.

What should I check before buying a Maine salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Does the vehicle's age mean a Maine title is required?
  • If no current Maine title is required, what documents establish ownership and title history?
  • Was the vehicle declared a total loss by an insurer or owner?
  • Has a Certificate of Salvage been issued?
  • If the owner retained an insurance total loss, was the salvage-certificate process completed?
  • Has the vehicle already been repaired or rebuilt?
  • How many component parts were replaced?
  • Will the resulting title be Repaired, Rebuilt, or Rebuilt Salvage?
  • Are receipts available for replacement components?
  • Can used parts be traced to their donor vehicles?
  • Have the vehicle and component identification numbers been inspected and verified?
  • If the vehicle came from another state, will Maine accept its existing ownership document?
  • Has the quality of the repairs been independently evaluated?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, inspection, transportation, title, tax, registration, and other costs remain?

The central Maine question is:

What ownership and salvage history does this vehicle have, and what Maine title or ownership document will apply after the vehicle is repaired?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Maine has an important written-disclosure requirement.

A repairer or rebuilder transferring a repaired or rebuilt salvage vehicle must disclose in writing that the vehicle was a salvage vehicle.

The repairer or rebuilder must also disclose what repairs were made to the vehicle.

Maine law additionally allows a transferee to demand receipts for the vehicle or component parts used in the repair or rebuilding process.

The vehicle's branded history also matters. Maine titles can carry Repaired, Rebuilt, or Rebuilt Salvage legends, and Maine law preserves specified prior brands when a previously branded Maine vehicle leaves the state and is later retitled in Maine.

For a future seller, retain the reconstruction records rather than assuming the title brand alone tells the complete story.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles, Maine Bureau of Insurance, or another government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, inspections, and procedures can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate Maine agency.
