Montana Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Montana Salvage Certificates, Rebuilt Salvage titles, Stage III VIN inspections, repair documentation, owner-retained total losses, and what must happen before an eligible salvage vehicle can return to the road.

What you should know

Montana generally defines a salvage vehicle as one damaged so extensively that the cost of parts and labor makes it uneconomical to repair. There is no fixed percentage-of-value threshold.

A Salvage Certificate establishes ownership but does not authorize ordinary highway use.

An eligible salvage vehicle can return to the road after the owner provides the required ownership documents and receipts for replacement components, the vehicle passes the Stage III VIN inspection, and the owner obtains a new title and registration.

The new title is branded Rebuilt Salvage. The inspection verifies identity and components; it does not certify safety or roadworthiness.

Understand Montana terminology

Salvage Vehicle

Montana defines a salvage vehicle as a motor vehicle damaged by collision, fire, flood, accident, trespass (wrongful interference with someone else’s property), or another occurrence to the extent that the owner, insurer, or someone acting on the owner’s behalf determines it is uneconomical to repair. A vehicle does not have to exceed a certain percentage of its market value to meet this definition.

Salvage Certificate

An ownership document for a salvage vehicle, not permission for ordinary highway operation. Montana uses Salvage Certificate of a Vehicle Application (MV206).

Rebuilt Salvage Title

A new title issued after the required inspection and retitling process, marked Rebuilt Salvage.

Stage III VIN Inspection

An identification and component-documentation inspection, recorded on Vehicle Inspection Report MV208; not a safety certification.

Junk Vehicle Certificate

A separate document used in certain wrecking-facility situations. Confirm whether a particular junk or parts-only document can support retitling before buying.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially. Eligible salvage vehicles must complete the inspection and retitling process before ordinary road use. A limited temporary permit may be available for travel to and from an inspection site.

Check the exact ownership document, title brands, eligibility, receipts for components, and applicable equipment and registration requirements.

How do I get a Montana salvage vehicle back on the road?

  1. Obtain the Salvage Certificate or other acceptable ownership document.
  2. Confirm the vehicle is eligible for rebuilding and retitling.
  3. Repair the vehicle to comply with applicable highway equipment requirements.
  4. Keep traceable receipts or bills of sale for replacement components.
  5. Schedule a Stage III VIN inspection with an authorized Montana MVD regional contact.
  6. Bring the vehicle and required ownership and component records.
  7. Obtain the completed Vehicle Inspection Report (MV208).
  8. Apply for a new title with the required documents and inspection report.
  9. Pay applicable title and registration fees.
  10. Obtain the Rebuilt Salvage title and complete registration before ordinary road use.

What inspections and verification are required?

Stage III VIN inspection

Montana requires a Stage III VIN inspection for vehicles with Montana or out-of-state salvage certificates. Inspectors verify identity and review receipts or bills of sale for replacement components, including signs of stolen vehicles or parts.

Replacement component verification

The owner must provide traceable receipts or bills of sale showing the source of components used in rebuilding. Missing records can delay retitling.

What the inspection does not establish

Montana law expressly states that the VIN inspection does not attest to roadworthiness or the safety condition of the vehicle.

Does passing the required inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No. A VIN inspection is not an independent mechanical or structural assessment.

Consider an independent inspection of structural and frame repairs, airbags, brakes, steering, suspension, electrical systems, flood damage, mechanical condition, tires, rust, corrosion, and evidence of poor-quality body repairs.

Ask what was damaged, how it was repaired, and what condition the vehicle is in now.

What repair and parts documentation should I keep?

Keep the Salvage Certificate, ownership bills of sale, traceable component receipts, repair invoices, damage assessments, insurance documents, photographs, donor-vehicle information, airbag records, and the completed inspection report. Montana provides Supplemental Form MV207 for component and bill-of-sale explanations.

Not every record listed is a separate statutory submission requirement; verify what applies to the vehicle.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company declares it a total loss?

Montana provides an owner-retained salvage process. When an insurer settles a total loss and the owner keeps the vehicle, the insurer uses Notice of Total Loss Payoff — Owner Retained Salvage Vehicle (MV205) to notify MVD.

Ask MVD whether the existing title must be surrendered, whether a Salvage Certificate will be issued, and what inspections and title brands will apply. Resolve lien and settlement questions with the insurer and lienholder.

Is there an age exemption from Montana’s salvage process?

Montana Code § 61-3-211 uses a less-than-15-years rule for certain insurer-acquired salvage vehicles and particular auction situations. This is not a general exemption for every older damaged vehicle.

Verify model year, insurer acquisition versus owner retention, existing title brands, and inspection and retitling requirements before relying on an age-based rule.

What about flood-damaged vehicles?

Flood is expressly included among causes of damage in Montana’s salvage definition when the vehicle is uneconomical to repair.

Investigate wiring, modules, airbags, corrosion, interior materials, mechanical components, and mold. Water-related problems may emerge after purchase.

What if the vehicle comes from another state?

Montana requires Stage III VIN inspections for vehicles with out-of-state salvage certificates. Bring the ownership document, bills of sale, and traceable component receipts.

Confirm whether Montana accepts any junk or parts-only document and whether the vehicle is eligible for retitling.

Transferring a vehicle between states does not remove its salvage history.

What should I check before buying a Montana salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document and title brands does it have?
  • Why was it declared salvage and was there an insurance settlement?
  • Can it legally be rebuilt, inspected, titled, and registered?
  • What damage and repairs are documented?
  • Who performed the repairs?
  • Are traceable replacement-component receipts available?
  • Has it completed the Stage III inspection?
  • Is there flood damage or an unresolved lien?
  • Can it be insured or financed?
  • What are the remaining repair, transport, inspection, tax, title, and registration costs?

For an unrepaired vehicle, confirm rebuilding eligibility and documentation. For an already repaired vehicle, verify repair quality and whether the required inspection and title process has been completed.

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Transfer the vehicle with the appropriate ownership document and disclose its salvage or rebuilt status. Resolve liens and provide available repair and component records.

A Salvage Certificate does not authorize highway use; a Rebuilt Salvage title identifies the vehicle’s history. Do not describe a branded vehicle as having a clean title.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Montana Department of Justice Motor Vehicle Division, your county treasurer’s motor vehicle office, or another government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, and procedures can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate Montana agency.
