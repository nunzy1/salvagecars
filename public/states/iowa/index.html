Iowa Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Iowa salvage titles, the 70% damage threshold, salvage theft examinations, Rebuilt titles, owner-retained total losses, and what must happen before a salvage vehicle can return to ordinary road use.

What you should know

Iowa generally considers a damaged vehicle wrecked or salvage when repair costs exceed 70% of its fair market value before the damage and the vehicle had a pre-damage fair market value of at least $500.

A Salvage-titled vehicle cannot be registered for ordinary road use. After complete repair, it must pass Iowa's salvage theft examination before it can receive a Rebuilt title and become eligible for registration.

The examination is primarily an anti-theft and vehicle-identity examination, not a comprehensive mechanical or safety inspection.

If an insurer acquires a vehicle through settlement of a damage claim or unrecovered theft, the insurer must obtain an Iowa Salvage title. An owner-retained total loss can follow a different path.

Understand Iowa terminology

Salvage

A damaged vehicle generally qualifies as wrecked or salvage when repair costs exceed 70% of its pre-damage fair market value and its pre-damage fair market value was at least $500.

Rebuilt

After an eligible salvage vehicle is completely repaired and passes Iowa's salvage theft examination, the owner can apply to change the title from Salvage to Rebuilt. Iowa DOT states that the vehicle does not receive a clean title after inspection.

Junking certificate

A junking certificate is different from a Salvage title. Iowa DOT describes it as the vehicle's “death certificate.” Except for narrow reinstatement provisions, a vehicle issued a junking certificate cannot be titled again.

Potentially, if the vehicle has an ownership document Iowa accepts for the salvage-rebuilding process and can successfully complete the required repair and examination.

How do I get an Iowa salvage vehicle back on the road?

  1. Establish ownership with an acceptable Salvage title.
  2. Completely repair the vehicle.
  3. Keep receipts, invoices, and documentation for repair parts.
  4. Complete Iowa's online Affidavit of Salvage Vehicle Repairs.
  5. Pay the examination fee and schedule the examination.
  6. Bring the required vehicle and documentation to the examination.
  7. Pass the salvage theft examination.
  8. Take the examination documentation to the appropriate county treasurer's office.
  9. Apply for a Rebuilt title and registration plates.

What inspection does Iowa require?

Iowa requires a repaired salvage vehicle to be physically examined by a peace officer certified by the Iowa Law Enforcement Academy before a Rebuilt title can be issued.

The officer verifies that no stolen parts were used and authenticates the vehicle as having been rebuilt. The vehicle generally must be completely repaired before examination, except for limited minor body items.

Does passing the salvage examination mean the vehicle is safe?

No. Iowa's salvage examination is primarily an anti-theft and vehicle-identity examination. It should not be interpreted as a comprehensive evaluation of structural repair quality, crashworthiness, brakes, steering, airbags, mechanical condition, or overall repair quality.

What documentation should I keep for replacement parts?

Keep receipts, invoices, bills of sale, and other ownership documentation for parts used to rebuild the vehicle. Iowa requires documentation for component parts replaced.

Additional inspection or identification requirements may apply if a major component such as the cab or frame has been replaced, or if a vehicle identification number is missing, damaged, altered, or otherwise cannot be properly verified. In some circumstances, Iowa DOT may assign a replacement VIN.

What documents and forms should I expect?

  • the Salvage title or certified copy,
  • the online Affidavit of Salvage Vehicle Repairs,
  • bills of sale for replaced component parts,
  • receipts and invoices for repair parts,
  • proof of examination payment,
  • inspection documentation after a successful examination, and
  • title, tax, registration, and fee documentation.

Can I drive a vehicle while it still has a Salvage title?

Not for ordinary road use. Iowa provides a limited permit through the salvage-repair affidavit process allowing the vehicle to be driven directly to and from the inspection location. Iowa DOT states that stops are not permitted along the route.

What happens to an older vehicle that an insurance company totals?

Iowa does not generally exempt an otherwise qualifying vehicle from the Salvage definition simply because it is older. The ordinary definition looks at repair cost and pre-damage fair market value.

When an insurance company acquires a motor vehicle through settlement arising from damage or unrecovered theft, Iowa requires the insurer to obtain an Iowa Salvage title. An older vehicle acquired by an insurer after a total-loss settlement can therefore still enter Iowa's Salvage system.

What if I keep my vehicle after the insurance company totals it?

If the insurer declares the vehicle a total loss but allows the owner to retain the vehicle and title, Iowa DOT says the owner does not need to go through the salvage theft examination process required for a Salvage-branded vehicle.

However, damage must be disclosed in writing when the vehicle is later sold. For buyers, this distinction matters: an insurance total-loss determination and an Iowa Salvage title are not always the same thing.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

An out-of-state Salvage title can be used in Iowa's salvage examination process. Iowa DOT says an Iowa owner does not necessarily have to convert it to an Iowa Salvage title before inspection if repairs and inspection are completed within 30 days of purchase. If that period is missed, an Iowa Salvage title in the owner's name is required before proceeding.

What does Iowa's 70% threshold mean?

For Iowa's ordinary wrecked-or-salvage definition, repair costs must exceed 70% of the vehicle's fair market value before it became damaged, and the vehicle must have had a pre-damage fair market value of at least $500.

Are there restrictions on buying and selling a Salvage-titled vehicle?

Yes. Iowa DOT states that a Salvage title may only be assigned or reassigned to an individual buyer by an authorized recycler licensed under Iowa law or a licensed new motor vehicle dealer. Iowa also limits whom an individual may sell a Salvage-titled vehicle to.

What should I check before buying an Iowa salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Is it Salvage, Rebuilt, Junk, or another branded title?
  • Was it acquired by an insurer or retained by the owner after a total loss?
  • Are damage disclosure and repair records available?
  • Has the salvage theft examination occurred?
  • Are receipts and bills of sale available for replacement components?
  • Were major components such as the cab or frame replaced, or is there a VIN identification issue requiring additional Iowa DOT review?
  • If it came from another state, what exact title or ownership document does it carry?
  • Is the seller legally permitted to transfer the Salvage-titled vehicle to you?
  • Has repair quality been independently evaluated?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

A vehicle that completes Iowa's Salvage process does not return to a clean title. Iowa DOT states that it will continue to carry a Rebuilt brand.

Damage-disclosure requirements can also apply, particularly to owner-retained total-loss vehicles that did not go through the Salvage-to-Rebuilt process.
