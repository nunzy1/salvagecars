Missouri Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Missouri salvage titles, Prior Salvage branding, vehicle examinations, parts documentation, junking certificates, and what must happen before a salvage vehicle can return to ordinary road use.

What you should know

Missouri uses several related but distinct terms for damaged and rebuilt vehicles.

A vehicle can qualify as a salvage vehicle because of the extent of its damage, because its owner or another party has declared it salvage, because an insurance company declared it salvage after settling a claim, or because its ownership is already evidenced by a salvage title.

For the damage-based part of Missouri's definition, a vehicle damaged during a year that is no more than six years after its model year is considered salvage when the cost of rebuilding or reconstructing it for legal road operation exceeds 80% of its pre-damage fair market value. Missouri excludes certain costs from that calculation.

A vehicle with a salvage title can potentially return to ordinary titled use after repair and completion of the applicable Missouri examination and title process.

When Missouri issues an ordinary title after a vehicle previously had a salvage title, the title is generally branded Prior Salvage. A junking certificate is different and should not be assumed to provide a path back to ordinary road use.

Understand Missouri terminology

Salvage Vehicle

Missouri's statutory definition is broader than a simple damage percentage. A vehicle can be considered salvage if it meets the applicable damage-and-value threshold, has been declared salvage by an owner or other interested party, has been declared salvage by an insurance company as part of a claim settlement, already has a salvage title, or falls within certain salvage or abandoned-property circumstances.

For the damage-based definition, the threshold applies to a vehicle damaged during a year no more than six years after its model year when qualifying repair costs exceed 80% of its pre-damage fair market value. Missouri excludes certain costs from that statutory repair-cost calculation.

Prior Salvage

Prior Salvage is an important Missouri brand for a vehicle that previously had salvage or comparable damage history but has returned to an ordinary certificate of title.

Missouri's current titling guidance uses Prior Salvage when converting qualifying Missouri or out-of-state salvage documents after the required examination and when carrying forward various out-of-state damage or rebuilt brands. The designation remains on subsequent Missouri titles.

Reconstructed Motor Vehicle

Missouri uses Reconstructed Motor Vehicle as a separate defined category. It generally means a vehicle altered from its original construction by the addition or substitution of two or more new or used major component parts.

This means “reconstructed” should not simply be used as another word for every repaired salvage vehicle in Missouri.

Junking certificate

A junking certificate is not the same thing as a salvage title. Treat it as a warning that the vehicle may not have a legal path back to ordinary road use.

Before buying a vehicle represented as junk, scrap, or parts-only, verify the actual ownership document and determine whether Missouri will issue the type of title you need.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, if the vehicle has a salvage title that can be converted through Missouri's title process.

The first question should be:

What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?

Then ask:

Will Missouri allow that document to support issuance of an ordinary Missouri title with the appropriate Prior Salvage brand?

Do not rely solely on descriptions such as “salvage,” “repairable,” “rebuilt,” or “reconstructed.” Those words can have different meanings, and Missouri uses some of them as specific legal title classifications.

How do I get a Missouri salvage vehicle back on the road?

For a vehicle that currently has a salvage title and is eligible to return to titled road use, the general process is:

  1. Establish ownership with the outstanding salvage title.
  2. Repair the vehicle.
  3. Keep receipts, bills of sale, titles, and other documentation establishing ownership and the source of major component parts used in the rebuild.
  4. Obtain the required Missouri vehicle examination.
  5. Submit the title application, outstanding salvage title, examination documentation, and applicable fees to the Missouri Department of Revenue.
  6. If the requirements are satisfied, obtain the Missouri certificate of title carrying the appropriate Prior Salvage designation.

The exact requirements can depend on the vehicle's history and whether it also falls into another Missouri category such as Reconstructed Motor Vehicle.

What examination does a former salvage vehicle need?

When applying for an original Missouri title for a vehicle previously titled as salvage, Missouri requires a Vehicle Examination Certificate (Form 551).

The examination is performed by an authorized Missouri State Highway Patrol inspector or an authorized St. Louis City or County Auto Theft Unit.

The examination addresses matters including vehicle identity and major component parts. Form 551 requires information about applicable major components that were replaced, including their source.

The completed examination becomes part of the documentation used by the Department of Revenue to determine the appropriate Missouri title and brand.

Why are parts receipts important?

Missouri's Form 551 specifically requires documentation for major component parts used in the vehicle.

The form states that bills of sale for listed parts must be in the applicant's name and accompany the form when it is submitted for title. For each applicable major component part, the form requires identifying information about the part and its source.

Missouri's statutory definition of major component parts includes components such as the cowl, rear clip, frame, body, cab, and front clip or front-end assembly. For a motorcycle, the frame is a major component part.

If you are buying a partially rebuilt salvage vehicle, ask for the parts documentation before buying it. A vehicle can be physically repaired but still have documentation problems that interfere with the title process.

Additional documents can apply depending on the vehicle's title history and circumstances. Use current Missouri Department of Revenue instructions for the particular transaction.

Is the salvage examination a complete safety evaluation?

No. The examination used in Missouri's salvage-title process should not be treated as a comprehensive evaluation of repair quality or mechanical condition.

Its title-process functions include establishing vehicle identity and documenting applicable major components and their sources.

A buyer should independently evaluate the quality and safety of the repairs. Missouri's ordinary safety-inspection and registration requirements are separate questions and can depend on the vehicle and transaction.

What if I kept my vehicle after an insurance company declared it a total loss?

Missouri has an important distinction for an owner who retains a vehicle after a total-loss settlement.

Current Missouri Department of Revenue Form 5043, Notice of Vehicle Titling Requirements, distinguishes between a retained vehicle that is not operable and one that is operable.

If the retained vehicle is not operable

The owner must apply for a Salvage title using the required title documents.

If the retained vehicle is operable

Current Form 5043 says the owner may apply for a Prior Salvage title, regardless of whether repairs have already been made.

The owner must provide the required inspection documentation. If the vehicle will be registered, the form calls for a current safety inspection meeting the stated age requirement; if it will not yet be registered, an identification or odometer inspection can be used.

Once the Prior Salvage title has been issued and the vehicle has been safety inspected, current Form 5043 says the vehicle's license plates may be affixed and it may be driven on the roadways.

This makes the vehicle's operability after the total-loss settlement an important part of Missouri's owner-retained process.

Can I drive a vehicle while it still has only a salvage title?

Do not treat a salvage title as authorization for ordinary road use.

A salvage title is an ownership document for the salvage vehicle. Returning the vehicle to ordinary titled and registered road use requires completing the applicable Missouri process.

If the vehicle needs to be moved before that process is complete, determine what lawful transportation method applies rather than assuming the salvage title allows it to be driven.

For an owner-retained total-loss vehicle that remains operable, follow the separate Department of Revenue procedure described in Form 5043.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

Missouri pays close attention to both the condition of an incoming vehicle and the brands already appearing on another state's title.

Out-of-state salvage title

A vehicle being titled in Missouri from an out-of-state salvage certificate can generally enter Missouri's examination and title process. Current Missouri titling guidance says a Missouri title issued from a qualifying Missouri or out-of-state salvage certificate, together with the required Vehicle Examination Certificate, is branded Prior Salvage.

Out-of-state rebuilt or damaged title

Missouri carries forward prior title history. Current Department guidance identifies out-of-state designations including Prior Salvage, Damaged, Rebuilt, Rebuilt Salvage, Flood Damaged, Previously Damaged, Fire Damaged, Previously Damaged by Collision, and similar designations as relevant to Missouri's Prior Salvage branding.

Vehicle brought into Missouri already wrecked or damaged

Missouri law specifically addresses vehicles brought into the state in a wrecked or damaged condition. Those vehicles are subject to the applicable Missouri State Highway Patrol examination procedure. If the examination shows the vehicle is in salvage or junk condition, that condition affects the resulting ownership document.

What about an out-of-state junk or nonrepairable document?

Treat it as a major eligibility question.

Do not assume an out-of-state Junk, Nonrepairable, Parts Only, Certificate of Destruction, or similar restrictive document is equivalent to an ordinary salvage title that Missouri will convert to Prior Salvage.

Before buying such a vehicle for rebuilding, have the Missouri Department of Revenue determine whether the ownership document can support the title you ultimately need.

What is a Reconstructed Motor Vehicle, and does that change the process?

Potentially.

Missouri defines a Reconstructed Motor Vehicle based on changes to the vehicle's original construction involving two or more major component parts.

Section 301.190 establishes a separate title procedure for vehicles in this category. That procedure requires documentation including proof of ownership and the source of major components, the applicable statutory fee, and an inspection certificate that includes a stolen-vehicle check.

The resulting title is designated Reconstructed Motor Vehicle.

For a vehicle that has extensive component replacement, determine whether Missouri considers it a Reconstructed Motor Vehicle rather than assuming the ordinary former-salvage procedure is the only process involved.

Does a Prior Salvage title mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No. Prior Salvage tells you important information about the vehicle's title history. It does not tell you whether the repairs were performed well.

Before buying, independently investigate structural repairs, frame or unibody condition, steering and suspension, alignment, brakes, airbags and restraint systems, electrical systems, mechanical condition, flood damage, corrosion, replacement components, and evidence of incomplete or poorly performed repairs.

Also investigate whether you can obtain the insurance coverage you want and how the Prior Salvage history could affect future resale.

What should I check before buying a Missouri salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Is it currently Salvage, Prior Salvage, Reconstructed Motor Vehicle, Junk, or something else?
  • What caused the vehicle to enter the salvage process?
  • Is it legally eligible to return to ordinary titled use?
  • Has repair work already started?
  • Are receipts and bills of sale available for applicable major component parts?
  • Are those documents in the applicant's name where required?
  • Has a Form 551 examination already been completed?
  • If the vehicle came from another state, what brands appear on its title?
  • Will Missouri carry those brands forward?
  • Does the amount of component replacement cause the vehicle to fall within Missouri's Reconstructed Motor Vehicle category?
  • Has repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, examination, title, tax, transportation, registration, and other costs remain?

The central Missouri question is:

What ownership document and title category apply to this vehicle, and can its identity, repairs, and major component parts be documented well enough to obtain the appropriate Missouri title?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

Missouri's title brands remain relevant when the vehicle is later transferred.

A vehicle returned from salvage status generally carries the Prior Salvage designation on its Missouri title, and Missouri's titling guidance indicates that title branding carries forward on subsequent Missouri titles.

Out-of-state damage and rebuilt brands can also be carried into Missouri title history.

If you later sell the vehicle, accurately represent its title and salvage history and comply with the transfer and disclosure requirements applicable to the transaction.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Missouri Department of Revenue, Missouri State Highway Patrol, or another government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, examinations, and title classifications can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate Missouri agency.
