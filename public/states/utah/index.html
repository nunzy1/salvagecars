Utah Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Learn how Utah classifies salvage vehicles, what inspections are required for a Rebuilt/Restored title, and what to consider before buying or repairing a salvage vehicle.

What you should know

Utah generally considers a vehicle salvage when the cost of repairing damage from a collision, flood, or another occurrence for safe operation exceeds its fair market value. A vehicle may also be classified as salvage based on an applicable insurer or out-of-state salvage determination.

Utah uses Salvage and Rebuilt/Restored title brands to distinguish vehicles that have not been restored from those repaired and returned to operating condition. When a vehicle’s title changes to Salvage, its existing registration is canceled.

A salvage vehicle that has been rebuilt and is applying for a Rebuilt/Restored title must pass a safety inspection. Utah may also require a vehicle identification number (VIN) inspection, particularly when a vehicle is being titled in the state for the first time. Emissions inspections may apply depending on the county, vehicle, and model year.

Utah also recognizes nonrepairable vehicles intended for parts, scrap, or destruction rather than restoration. Once a salvage or rebuilt/restored brand has been applied, that title history remains associated with the vehicle, including after transfer from another state.

Understand Utah’s title terminology

Salvage: A vehicle meeting Utah’s salvage definition that has not been restored to operation, or one carrying an applicable salvage designation from an insurer or another jurisdiction.

Salvage certificate: A certificate of ownership for a salvage vehicle that does not authorize registration.

Rebuilt/Restored: A title brand identifying a salvage vehicle repaired and restored to operating condition.

Flood damage: A designation or recorded history involving flooding, which may remain part of a vehicle’s title history after restoration.

Nonrepairable vehicle: A vehicle meeting Utah’s nonrepairable criteria, including certain vehicles designated for parts, scrap, or destruction.

Nonrepairable certificate: A certificate of ownership for a nonrepairable vehicle, rather than a title permitting ordinary road use.

Reconstructed vehicle: A vehicle substantially altered from its original construction by adding, removing, or replacing essential components, such as the body or frame. For example, installing the body of one vehicle onto another vehicle’s frame may result in a reconstructed classification. This is different from repairing a salvage vehicle and obtaining a Rebuilt/Restored title.

For more information on these classifications, see Utah DMV — Salvage Vehicles and Branded Titles.

Can a salvage vehicle return to the road?

A salvage certificate does not authorize registration or ordinary operation on public roads. When Utah DMV changes a vehicle’s title brand to Salvage, any active registration is canceled.

An eligible salvage vehicle must be repaired, meet the applicable inspection and title requirements, receive a Rebuilt/Restored designation, and be properly registered before returning to ordinary road use. Emissions testing may also apply.

A nonrepairable vehicle cannot return to ordinary road use through the salvage rebuilding process. Before driving a repaired vehicle, confirm that its title, registration, and insurance permit operation. Otherwise, arrange transportation.

How do I get an eligible salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible salvage vehicle, the process generally includes:

  1. Confirm the title status. Verify the vehicle has a Salvage designation and is eligible for restoration rather than classified as nonrepairable.
  2. Complete repairs. Restore the vehicle to operating condition and retain available repair and replacement-part records.
  3. Obtain a safety inspection. A rebuilt salvage vehicle applying for a Rebuilt/Restored title must pass a safety inspection.
  4. Determine whether a VIN inspection applies. Vehicles first titled in Utah generally require VIN verification, subject to exceptions.
  5. Prepare title documents. Gather the existing title and complete Form TC-656, Application for Utah Title, when required.
  6. Apply for the Rebuilt/Restored designation. Submit the required documents and inspection information to Utah DMV.
  7. Complete registration. Establish new registration and satisfy any applicable emissions and insurance requirements before operating the vehicle.

The vehicle’s title continues to disclose its prior salvage history through the Rebuilt/Restored brand. See Utah DMV — Title Corrections for current instructions.

Rebuilt vehicle inspections

Utah distinguishes three types of inspections, each serving a different purpose.

Safety inspection. A rebuilt salvage vehicle applying for a Rebuilt/Restored title must pass a safety inspection. This is an exception to Utah’s general rule that most ordinary passenger vehicles do not need routine safety inspections for registration.

VIN inspection. A VIN inspection verifies vehicle identification information. Utah generally requires one when a vehicle is first titled in the state, subject to exceptions. It does not replace the rebuilt-vehicle safety inspection.

Emissions inspection. Depending on the vehicle, model year, and county of registration, emissions testing may apply. Utah has county-specific programs in Cache, Davis, Salt Lake, Utah, and Weber counties.

Confirm which inspections apply before applying for title and registration.

What documents and forms may be required?

Depending on the vehicle and whether it is already titled in Utah, documents may include:

  • Existing Utah salvage title or acceptable replacement-title documentation
  • Form TC-656, Application for Utah Title, when required
  • Required safety inspection documentation
  • VIN inspection or other vehicle-identification documentation, when applicable
  • Ownership and lien-release documents, when applicable
  • Emissions inspection documentation for registration, when required
  • Available receipts and records of replacement parts and repairs

Reconstructed vehicles follow a separate process. Vehicles substantially altered through changes to essential components, such as the body or frame, may qualify as reconstructed vehicles under Utah law. That process can require additional ownership documentation, photographs, and an assigned vehicle identification number. If your repairs involve major changes to the vehicle’s original construction, confirm with Utah DMV which title process applies.

See Utah DMV — Title Corrections and Reconstructed Vehicles.

Where to schedule an inspection

Utah requires a rebuilt salvage vehicle applying for a Rebuilt/Restored title to obtain a safety inspection at an authorized inspection station. Confirm that the station performs the inspection needed for rebuilt salvage vehicles before arranging an appointment.

If a VIN inspection is also required, confirm which authorized inspector can perform it and whether the vehicle must be presented at a DMV office or another approved location. Emissions testing, when applicable, follows the relevant county program.

See Utah DMV — Vehicle Inspections for current requirements.

What if the salvage vehicle came from another state?

Utah recognizes salvage and rebuilt title brands issued by other jurisdictions. An out-of-state salvage or rebuilt title history remains associated with the vehicle when it is titled in Utah, even if Utah uses different terminology for the designation.

Vehicles first titled in Utah generally require a VIN inspection, subject to exceptions. Before purchasing or bringing a vehicle into the state, confirm:

  • Whether Utah accepts the existing ownership documents
  • Whether it is classified as Salvage, Rebuilt/Restored, Nonrepairable, or another designation
  • Whether Utah’s salvage-to-rebuilt/restored process is required
  • Whether a safety or VIN inspection applies
  • Whether emissions testing is required in the registration county
  • Whether the vehicle is eligible for registration and ordinary road use

A rebuilt title issued elsewhere does not erase the vehicle’s prior salvage history. See Utah DMV — Salvage Vehicles and Branded Titles.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in Utah?

A salvage or Rebuilt/Restored title identifies significant history but does not guarantee the quality of repairs or the vehicle’s safety. Before buying:

  • Verify the title brand. Determine whether the vehicle is Salvage, Rebuilt/Restored, or Nonrepairable.
  • Confirm eligibility. Verify it can be titled and registered for your intended use.
  • Investigate damage. Review collision, flood, theft, or other loss records.
  • Review repairs. Request receipts, photographs, and structural or mechanical repair records.
  • Get an independent inspection. Consider a qualified mechanic or collision-repair specialist.
  • Check for flood damage. Look for electrical issues, corrosion, and evidence of water intrusion.
  • Verify inspections. Identify safety, VIN, and emissions requirements.
  • Confirm insurance. Check the availability of the coverage you need.
  • Estimate the full cost. Include repairs, transportation, inspections, title and registration requirements, and contingencies.

What should I know if I later sell the vehicle?

Utah requires written disclosure when selling vehicles with applicable salvage or Rebuilt/Restored histories. Subject to statutory exceptions, sellers generally must notify prospective buyers in writing if a salvage certificate or branded title was issued or an insurer declared the vehicle a total loss.

Utah DMV identifies Form TC-814 for these disclosures. Requirements apply to private sellers and dealers; dealers also have display requirements for vehicles offered for sale. Advertisements must disclose applicable salvage or branded-title history in accordance with Utah law.

Provide a properly assigned title and any other required ownership documents. The title brand remains part of the vehicle’s history after transfer.

See Utah Motor Vehicle Enforcement Division — Salvage and Rebuilt/Restored Vehicles.
