Massachusetts Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Massachusetts salvage titles, owner-retained vehicles, salvage inspections, reconstructed titles, and what must happen before a salvage vehicle can return to registered use.

What you should know

Massachusetts generally treats a vehicle as salvage when it has been declared a total loss because of collision, fire, theft, flood, vandalism, or another qualifying cause.

A vehicle issued a Salvage Repairable title cannot be registered for road use until it has been repaired, passed the required Massachusetts salvage inspection, and completed the applicable title process.

The salvage inspection is not a safety inspection. Its primary purpose is to verify the vehicle's identity and help determine whether the vehicle or major component parts used in its reconstruction are stolen.

Massachusetts also has a distinct Owner Retained process for certain owners who keep their vehicle after an insurance total-loss settlement. A qualifying owner-retained vehicle can remain registered and driven during and after that title process.

A Massachusetts salvage classification remains part of the vehicle's history. A vehicle that has gone through the salvage process does not later receive a clear title.

Understand Massachusetts terminology

Total loss salvage motor vehicle

Massachusetts generally considers a vehicle a total loss salvage motor vehicle when it has been stolen and not recovered, or when collision, fire, water, or another occurrence damages it to the point that the owner or insurer considers repair uneconomical.

Massachusetts law contains exemptions from the salvage-title requirement, so the vehicle's particular circumstances and title history matter.

Salvage Repairable

A Salvage Repairable title identifies a salvage vehicle that can potentially be repaired and returned to titled and registered use.

After repair, the vehicle must complete the Massachusetts salvage inspection before it can proceed through the applicable reconstructed-title process.

Reconstructed

After a salvage vehicle has been restored, passes the required salvage inspection, and satisfies the applicable title requirements, Massachusetts issues a new certificate of title carrying the notation Reconstructed.

The vehicle does not return to a clear, unbranded title.

Owner Retained

An Owner Retained title applies in certain situations when an insurer declares a vehicle a total loss but the registered owner keeps the vehicle.

Current RMV guidance says an owner-retained vehicle must have an active registration and must have been capable of being legally and safely operated on a public way at the time of the loss. A qualifying owner-retained vehicle does not require a salvage inspection and can remain registered and driven during and after the title process.

Parts-Only

A Parts-Only brand means the vehicle cannot be registered in Massachusetts.

A vehicle intended only for parts or scrap should not be purchased with the expectation that ordinary repairs and a salvage inspection will make it eligible for Massachusetts registration.

Secondary salvage brands

Massachusetts uses secondary brands to identify the event that caused the total loss. Current RMV guidance lists Collision, Fire, Flood, Flood/Salt, Theft, Vandalism, and Other.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, if the vehicle is eligible for reconstruction.

A vehicle with a Salvage Repairable title can potentially return to registered use after repair, salvage inspection, and completion of the reconstructed-title process. A Parts-Only vehicle cannot.

Before buying, first ask:

What exact title or ownership document does this vehicle have?

Then ask:

Does that document allow the vehicle to complete Massachusetts's reconstruction process?

Do not rely only on a seller's description of a vehicle as “salvage,” “repairable,” or “rebuildable.”

How do I get a Massachusetts salvage vehicle back on the road?

For a vehicle following the Salvage Repairable path, the general process is:

  1. Obtain the appropriate Salvage Repairable title in the owner's name.
  2. Repair or restore the vehicle.
  3. Keep bills of sale and other required documentation for major component parts used in the repair.
  4. Complete the Application for Inspection of a Salvaged Motor Vehicle and required owner's affidavit.
  5. Schedule the Massachusetts salvage inspection.
  6. Bring the vehicle and required documentation to the inspection.
  7. Pass the salvage inspection.
  8. Apply through the RMV for a title carrying the appropriate reconstructed designation.

Registration and Massachusetts safety/emissions inspection requirements are separate from the salvage inspection.

What happens at the Massachusetts salvage inspection?

Massachusetts salvage inspections are performed by the Massachusetts State Police.

The inspector reviews the vehicle's identity and documentation, including the salvage ownership documentation and bills of sale for applicable major component parts.

The purpose is primarily to combat fraud and prevent stolen vehicles or stolen components from being returned to circulation.

The inspection is not performed to determine the vehicle's roadworthiness or general safety condition.

Passing the salvage inspection therefore should not be interpreted as a state determination that the repairs were performed well or that the vehicle is mechanically safe.

Why are parts receipts important?

Massachusetts law requires documentation showing how major component parts used to restore the vehicle were acquired.

For applicable parts, the bill of sale must identify the vehicle from which the component was removed when the part contains or should contain a manufacturer's vehicle identification number.

This documentation helps the inspector establish the lawful origin of components used in the rebuild.

If you are considering buying a partially rebuilt salvage vehicle, ask to see the parts documentation before completing the purchase.

Is the salvage inspection also a Massachusetts safety inspection?

No.

The salvage inspection examines vehicle identity and major-component documentation and helps detect stolen vehicles or parts.

It does not determine whether the vehicle is roadworthy or satisfies Massachusetts's ordinary motor vehicle safety requirements.

A successful salvage inspection should therefore not substitute for an independent evaluation of repair quality when deciding whether to buy a rebuilt vehicle.

Can I drive a Salvage Repairable vehicle to the salvage inspection?

No. Massachusetts says state law prohibits operation of a vehicle with a Salvage Repairable title until it has passed the required salvage inspection.

That means you should plan to transport the vehicle to the inspection site rather than drive it there.

This restriction should not be confused with Massachusetts's separate Owner Retained process. A qualifying Owner Retained vehicle can remain registered and driven during and after that title process.

What if I kept my own vehicle after the insurance company totaled it?

Massachusetts has a specific Owner Retained process for this situation.

Current RMV guidance says the vehicle must have an active registration and must have been capable of being legally and safely operated on a public way at the time of the loss.

A qualifying Owner Retained vehicle does not require a salvage inspection. The RMV says it remains registered and can be driven throughout the owner-retained title process and after the Owner Retained title is issued.

The RMV also recommends contacting the insurance company because the insurer may choose not to continue physical-damage coverage such as comprehensive and collision.

If you are buying a vehicle from someone who says it was “owner retained,” inspect the actual title and determine its current status rather than assuming the original owner's process automatically carries over to you.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

Massachusetts applies its salvage process to out-of-state salvage vehicles.

Current RMV guidance says all out-of-state salvage vehicles, regardless of model year, must pass a Massachusetts salvage inspection before they can be registered or titled for ordinary use in Massachusetts.

Inspections by other states generally are not honored unless Massachusetts has a prior agreement with that state.

If an individual purchases a vehicle with an out-of-state salvage title, the purchaser must obtain the appropriate Massachusetts Salvage Title in their own name before the Massachusetts salvage inspection.

Massachusetts specifically addresses New York MV-907A Salvage Certificates and Connecticut Salvage Certificates: a Massachusetts resident or licensed dealer acquiring one must obtain a Massachusetts Salvage Title before selling the vehicle or presenting it for salvage inspection.

Pay particular attention to nonrepairable or parts-only documents

If the out-of-state ownership document identifies the vehicle as Parts Only, Nonrepairable, Junk, Certificate of Destruction, or uses another designation indicating that the vehicle cannot return to road use, do not assume Massachusetts will allow it to enter the reconstructed-title process.

Massachusetts's own Parts-Only classification cannot be registered for road use. Before buying an out-of-state vehicle with a comparable restrictive document or brand, have the Massachusetts RMV determine whether the ownership document is eligible for a Massachusetts Salvage Repairable title.

Before buying an out-of-state salvage vehicle, inspect the actual ownership document, identify every brand or restriction, confirm that Massachusetts will accept the document, and determine what Massachusetts title must be obtained before inspection.

What about older vehicles?

Massachusetts law contains an exemption from the requirement to obtain a salvage title for certain older passenger vehicles.

However, this should not be confused with a vehicle that already has a salvage title.

Current RMV guidance says that once a Salvage Title has been issued in Massachusetts or another state, the Massachusetts salvage-title and salvage-inspection process must be completed regardless of the vehicle's age.

Do not assume an older vehicle can avoid the salvage process merely because of its age if it already carries salvage documentation.

What if the vehicle is Parts-Only or has been junked?

Treat this as a major eligibility warning.

A Massachusetts Parts-Only vehicle cannot be registered in Massachusetts.

Massachusetts also says a vehicle whose ownership document has been surrendered for cancellation because the vehicle is being junked or scrapped can never again be titled or registered in the Commonwealth.

Before spending money repairing a severely damaged vehicle, establish that its ownership document actually permits reconstruction.

Does passing the salvage inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No.

The Massachusetts salvage inspection is primarily an identity and anti-theft inspection, not a comprehensive evaluation of repair quality or mechanical condition.

Before buying a reconstructed vehicle, independently investigate structural repairs, steering and suspension, alignment, brakes, airbags and restraint systems, electrical systems, mechanical condition, flood or saltwater damage, corrosion, replacement components, and incomplete or poorly performed repairs.

Also investigate whether you can obtain the insurance coverage you want and how the reconstructed title history could affect future resale.

What should I check before buying a Massachusetts salvage vehicle?

  • What is the exact title or ownership document?
  • Is the vehicle Salvage Repairable, Owner Retained, Parts-Only, Reconstructed, or something else?
  • What event caused the total loss?
  • What secondary salvage brands appear on the title?
  • Is the vehicle legally eligible to return to registered use?
  • Who currently owns the vehicle?
  • Has repair work already started?
  • Are bills of sale available for applicable major component parts?
  • Can donor-vehicle information be documented where required?
  • Has the Massachusetts salvage inspection already been completed?
  • If it came from another state, has the proper Massachusetts salvage title been obtained?
  • Has repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, inspection, title, tax, transportation, registration, and other costs remain?

The central Massachusetts question is:

Does this vehicle's actual ownership document allow it to complete the Massachusetts salvage process, and can its repairs and major components be properly documented?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

A salvage or reconstructed vehicle's history remains relevant when the vehicle is transferred.

Massachusetts law requires an owner transferring a vehicle for which a salvage title has been issued to properly assign and deliver that salvage title to the buyer.

After a vehicle successfully completes the reconstruction process, its title carries the Reconstructed notation rather than becoming a clear title.

If you later sell the vehicle, accurately represent its title and salvage history and comply with the title-transfer requirements applicable at that time.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles, Massachusetts State Police, or another government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, inspection procedures, and title classifications can change, and the rules that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate Massachusetts agency.
