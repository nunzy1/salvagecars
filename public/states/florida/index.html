Florida Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Florida salvage rebuildable, rebuilt, and Certificate of Destruction rules, rebuilt inspections, documentation, and official state requirements.

What you should know

A Florida vehicle with a salvage rebuildable title can potentially be repaired, inspected, and titled as Rebuilt.

A vehicle with a Certificate of Destruction is different. Florida law prevents another certificate of title from being issued after the applicable Certificate of Destruction has been issued.

Determine the exact title or ownership document before buying the vehicle or spending money repairing it.

Understand Florida terminology

Salvage rebuildable. A qualifying salvage vehicle remains eligible for rebuilding. Florida also recognizes Rebuildable Flood in applicable cases.

Rebuilt vehicle. An eligible salvage vehicle repaired into road-operable condition can complete Florida's rebuilt process and receive a title branded Rebuilt.

Certificate of Destruction. This status is fundamentally different from salvage rebuildable. Florida law provides that another certificate of title may not be issued after the applicable Certificate of Destruction has been issued.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

A qualifying salvage rebuildable vehicle can potentially return to the road after repair, rebuilt inspection, and completion of the Florida rebuilt-title process.

A vehicle subject to a Certificate of Destruction does not have that path. Restrictive out-of-state ownership documents can also affect eligibility.

Before evaluating repair costs, determine whether the ownership document legally allows the vehicle to be rebuilt and titled for road use in Florida.

How do I get a salvage vehicle back on the road?

For a qualifying salvage rebuildable vehicle, the general Florida path is:

  1. Establish acceptable ownership.
  2. Repair or rebuild the vehicle into road-operable condition.
  3. Keep documentation for repairs and major component parts.
  4. Complete required title and rebuilt documentation.
  5. Submit the rebuilt application through an appropriate FLHSMV Bureau of Dealer Services Regional Office or authorized Private Rebuilt Vehicle Inspection Program facility.
  6. Present the vehicle and documentation for rebuilt inspection.
  7. Resolve deficiencies if necessary.
  8. After successful inspection, receive the rebuilt decal associated with the vehicle.
  9. Complete the applicable Florida rebuilt-title process.

Review FLHSMV procedure TL-37 →

What happens during the rebuilt inspection?

The rebuilt inspection verifies the vehicle, its ownership documentation, major component parts, receipts and supporting records, and the completed rebuild. The vehicle must be in rebuilt and road-operable condition before applying for a rebuilt title.

A rebuilt decal is not assigned until the vehicle successfully completes the rebuilt inspection.

Where do I get a rebuilt inspection?

Florida rebuilt applications can be handled through an FLHSMV Bureau of Dealer Services Regional Office or an authorized Private Rebuilt Vehicle Inspection Program (PRVIP) facility.

Because participating facilities and operational details can change, use FLHSMV's current procedure and provider information rather than an old copied list.

What documents should I keep while rebuilding the vehicle?

Keep records as repairs occur. Depending on the vehicle, documentation can include the salvage rebuildable title or other ownership evidence, required photographs, original receipts for major component parts, information identifying donor vehicles for used components, title forms, the Statement of Builder, VIN or odometer verification when applicable, and other vehicle-specific records.

If someone else has already repaired the vehicle, ask what documentation is available before buying it.

What is the Statement of Builder?

Florida's Statement of Builder (Form HSMV 84490) documents the vehicle and applicable major component parts used during building or repair. Keep component documentation as parts are acquired and installed.

What forms might I need?

Important Florida documents can include the title application, Statement of Builder (HSMV 84490), Application for Salvage Title/Certificate of Destruction (HSMV 82363), and VIN or odometer verification when applicable. Requirements vary with the vehicle and transaction.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

Florida examines the ownership document issued by the previous jurisdiction. A vehicle already carrying a rebuilt, reconstructed, or similar completed designation can follow a different path from one still in salvage status.

An out-of-state document indicating that the vehicle is nonrepairable, junked, for parts, or dismantling only can prevent a normal Florida title. Review the actual document rather than relying on an auction description.

Review FLHSMV TL-37 for out-of-state rebuilt and salvage vehicles →

What about flood vehicles?

Florida specifically recognizes Rebuildable Flood in its salvage process. Legal eligibility to rebuild does not establish the vehicle's mechanical, electrical, or structural condition.

  • Verify the exact title brand.
  • Investigate water exposure when possible.
  • Inspect electrical and electronic systems carefully.
  • Consider delayed corrosion.
  • Verify repairs and component documentation.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in Florida?

  • What is the exact title or ownership document?
  • Is it salvage rebuildable, Rebuildable Flood, rebuilt, or subject to a Certificate of Destruction?
  • Does the document allow road use after rebuilding?
  • How will Florida recognize an out-of-state document?
  • Has the vehicle completed a Florida rebuilt inspection?
  • What repairs remain?
  • Are pre-repair photographs available when relevant?
  • Are original receipts and component records available?
  • Can the Statement of Builder be completed accurately?
  • Are there signs of flood damage?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What title, inspection, repair, transportation, tax, and registration costs remain?

Investigate legal status before repair economics. A low purchase price is not useful if the vehicle cannot obtain the title status you need.

What should I know if I later sell the vehicle?

Florida's rebuilt status remains important when the vehicle is sold. Florida law includes disclosure requirements for rebuilt vehicles, including requirements connected with sale and advertising.

The Florida title remains branded Rebuilt. Verify current disclosure requirements before advertising or transferring the vehicle.

Review Florida Statutes section 319.14 →

What if I rebuild or sell vehicles as a business?

Florida imposes additional requirements on dealers and businesses involved with salvage and rebuilt vehicles. Dealer licensing, rebuilt-vehicle sales, advertising, and disclosure rules can differ from the individual-owner process.

If you intend to buy, rebuild, or sell salvage vehicles regularly, review current FLHSMV dealer requirements before proceeding.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the appropriate Florida government agency. Requirements, forms, fees, inspection procedures, and other rules can change, and the requirements that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate state or local agency.
