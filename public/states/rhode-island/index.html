Rhode Island Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Rhode Island salvage classifications, licensed rebuilder requirements, inspections, and where to find official state guidance.

What you should know

Rhode Island distinguishes between salvage vehicles that are repairable and those designated for parts only. Classification A identifies vehicles with extensive damage that are suitable for parts only. Classification B identifies vehicles with considerable damage that are considered repairable. Insurance companies generally assign these classifications, subject to review by the Rhode Island Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV).

Rhode Island requires salvage vehicles to be rebuilt by a Rhode Island-licensed salvage rebuilder. Owners cannot simply repair a salvage vehicle themselves and obtain a reconstructed salvage title through the standard process. A vehicle rebuilt without the required license is subject to a FOR PARTS ONLY title designation.

After an eligible vehicle is repaired, Rhode Island requires a salvage inspection before issuing a title branded re-constructed salvage. The DMV requires documentation of the original damage, repairs, replacement parts, and ownership. The vehicle must also have a new Rhode Island state inspection sticker before the salvage inspection.

For certain damaged vehicles not declared total losses by an insurer, a separate salvage-title rule applies when repair costs exceed 75% of pre-damage fair market value and the vehicle meets the statutory age condition. Verify the classification and title eligibility with Rhode Island DMV before buying or rebuilding.

Understand Rhode Island’s title terminology

Salvage certificate of title

An ownership document issued for a vehicle subject to Rhode Island salvage requirements.

Classification A — Parts only

A salvage vehicle with extensive damage considered suitable for parts only, not restoration for ordinary road use.

Classification B — Repairable

A salvage vehicle with considerable damage that is considered eligible for repair.

Re-constructed salvage

The title designation applied after an eligible salvage vehicle has been restored and satisfied applicable DMV inspection and title requirements.

FOR PARTS ONLY

A designation preventing the ordinary rebuilt-title process, including when repairs were performed without the required salvage rebuilder license.

The classification matters because not every salvage vehicle is eligible to return to ordinary road use.

Can a salvage vehicle return to the road?

A salvage certificate of title does not itself authorize ordinary road use. An eligible Classification B vehicle may qualify after repairs by a licensed salvage rebuilder, the required inspections, and issuance of the appropriate reconstructed salvage title and registration.

Classification A vehicles are designated for parts only. Arrange lawful transportation for vehicles that are not currently authorized to operate on public roads.

How do I get an eligible salvage vehicle back on the road?

For a repairable vehicle that qualifies for reconstruction, the general process is:

  1. Confirm that the vehicle is Classification B and eligible for reconstruction.
  2. Obtain a properly assigned salvage certificate of title and required ownership documents.
  3. Have the repairs performed by a Rhode Island-licensed salvage rebuilder.
  4. Retain original-damage photographs, the insurance appraisal, parts and labor receipts, and the required used-parts list.
  5. Have the licensed rebuilder complete the Certificate of Salvage Repair.
  6. Obtain a new Rhode Island state vehicle inspection sticker before the salvage inspection.
  7. Submit the Salvage Inspection Application and required supporting documents to the DMV Enforcement Office.
  8. Present the fully restored vehicle, identification, properly assigned salvage title, and original supporting records for the salvage inspection.
  9. Complete the applicable reconstructed salvage title and registration process after satisfying DMV requirements.

Salvage inspections and state vehicle inspections

Rhode Island requires a salvage inspection after an eligible salvage vehicle has been restored. This is separate from the ordinary Rhode Island vehicle safety and emissions inspection. The vehicle must have a new state inspection sticker before it is presented for its salvage inspection.

The DMV salvage inspection includes ownership verification and review of documentation about the vehicle’s damage and repairs. The DMV requests original-damage photographs, an insurance appraisal, parts and labor receipts, a used-parts list, and the Certificate of Salvage Repair.

Replacement-part documentation must include source-vehicle VIN information where required by DMV. The vehicle must be fully restored, and original supporting documents and photographs must be presented at the appointment.

Passing the ordinary state inspection does not eliminate the separate salvage inspection requirement.

What documents and forms may be required?

For an owner-retained total-loss vehicle, the salvage-title application generally requires:

  • A completed title application.
  • The existing Rhode Island title in the owner’s name.
  • An insurer letter confirming the total loss, the owner’s retention of the vehicle, and the Classification A or B designation.
  • A written insurance damage estimate or appraisal.

For the salvage inspection following reconstruction, DMV generally requires:

  • A completed Salvage Inspection Application.
  • A copy of the salvage title.
  • The Certificate of Salvage Repair completed by the licensed rebuilder.
  • Parts and labor receipts, with source-vehicle VIN information where required.
  • Photographs of the original damage.
  • The insurance appraisal and used-parts list.

At the inspection, bring identification, the properly assigned salvage title, the restored vehicle with its new state inspection sticker, and the original supporting documents and photographs. Consult DMV’s current instructions for submission and payment requirements.

Where do I schedule the salvage inspection?

Rhode Island DMV’s Enforcement Office administers the salvage inspection process. Submit the completed Salvage Inspection Application and supporting documentation according to DMV’s instructions; DMV then handles the appointment process.

Before submitting the application, confirm that the licensed rebuilder has completed the repairs, the vehicle has a new Rhode Island state inspection sticker, and all required records are ready. Bring the original documents to the appointment; missing documents may require another appointment.

What if the salvage vehicle came from another state?

Rhode Island generally requires used vehicles brought from another state to undergo a VIN inspection by a local municipal police department, regardless of model year, under requirements effective January 1, 2024.

An out-of-state salvage or rebuilt vehicle may also be subject to Rhode Island’s salvage classification, inspection, and title requirements. Before purchasing, confirm whether DMV will accept the existing title, whether the vehicle is eligible for registration, and whether additional salvage inspections or licensed-rebuilder documentation are required.

Do not assume that a rebuilt title issued by another state automatically qualifies a vehicle for registration in Rhode Island. Verify the vehicle’s specific history and title eligibility with DMV.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in Rhode Island?

  • Confirm whether the vehicle is Classification A (parts only) or Classification B (repairable).
  • Review the title for salvage, reconstructed salvage, parts-only, or other relevant designations.
  • Obtain the damage appraisal and photographs showing the vehicle before repairs.
  • Confirm that required repairs were or will be performed by a Rhode Island-licensed salvage rebuilder.
  • Ask for the Certificate of Salvage Repair, receipts, and replacement-part records.
  • Consider an independent mechanical and structural inspection.
  • Verify the title path with DMV, particularly for an out-of-state or unusually branded vehicle.
  • Budget for repairs, transportation, inspections, titling, registration, insurance, and unexpected expenses.

A vehicle that appears fully repaired may still have title or documentation problems that prevent registration.

What should I know if I later sell the vehicle?

The documents required to sell or transfer a salvage vehicle depend on its classification and whether it has already been reconstructed and titled. Provide the properly assigned title and other required ownership documentation, and accurately represent the vehicle’s title status.

For an unrepaired salvage vehicle, buyers should confirm whether it is Classification A or B and whether it may legally be reconstructed. A reconstructed salvage brand remains relevant to future buyers, who should review the title and available repair documentation.

When a damaged vehicle meets Rhode Island’s non-insurer salvage requirements, the owner must follow the applicable salvage-title process before transferring it. Verify current transfer requirements with DMV.
