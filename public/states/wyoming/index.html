Wyoming Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Learn how Wyoming classifies salvage vehicles, how to obtain a rebuilt title and decal, and what to consider before buying or repairing a salvage vehicle.

What you should know

Wyoming generally requires a Salvage title when an insurance company declares a vehicle a total loss. When an insurance company is not involved in the settlement, a vehicle qualifies as salvage if the cost of restoring it to its pre-accident condition exceeds 75% of its actual retail cash value.

Wyoming’s salvage-title requirements generally do not apply to vehicles with more than eight years of service or to certain commercial vehicles. Existing salvage, rebuilt, flood, or comparable title brands must still be carried forward on Wyoming titles, regardless of the vehicle’s age.

Wyoming also has a separate Flood title brand for vehicles that meet its flood-damage definition but do not otherwise qualify as salvage. A vehicle with more extensive flood damage may instead receive a Salvage brand, so a Salvage title alone does not identify the cause of the damage.

An eligible salvage vehicle may return to ordinary road use after completing Wyoming’s rebuilt-title requirements. The owner obtains a Rebuilt Salvage Vehicle Decal, attaches it to the vehicle, and has a Wyoming law enforcement officer verify the vehicle identification number (VIN) and decal placement before obtaining a title branded Rebuilt.

Certain vehicles branded salvage because of cosmetic-only hail or theft damage, and vehicles already titled rebuilt or reconstructed in another state, may qualify for registration without Wyoming’s ordinary rebuilt-title process. Confirm eligibility with the Wyoming Department of Transportation (WYDOT) and the appropriate county clerk.

Understand Wyoming’s title terminology

Salvage: A title brand for a qualifying vehicle declared a total loss by an insurer or meeting Wyoming’s repair-cost threshold when no insurer is involved.

Certificate of title branded Salvage: The ownership document issued by a Wyoming county clerk for a salvage vehicle. It does not, by itself, authorize ordinary registration.

Rebuilt: The title brand issued to an eligible previously salvaged vehicle after completing Wyoming’s rebuilt-title requirements.

Rebuilt Salvage Vehicle Decal: A secure decal issued by WYDOT and attached to a rebuilt salvage vehicle.

Flood: A title brand for a vehicle submerged in water above the door sill, with water entering the passenger or trunk compartment, when the damage does not otherwise meet the salvage definition.

Nonrepairable: A restrictive designation indicating that the vehicle is not eligible for ordinary road use.

Junk: A title brand issued under a separate Wyoming procedure for qualifying junk vehicles; it is not an ordinary registration title.

Reconstructed: A title designation that may appear on vehicles titled in another state; Wyoming preserves applicable out-of-state brands.

A vehicle’s applicable title brands remain part of its title history even after repairs or transfer from another state. See Wyoming Statutes, Title 31.

Can a salvage vehicle return to the road?

A Wyoming Salvage title does not, by itself, authorize ordinary registration or operation on public roads. An eligible vehicle must complete the applicable rebuilt-title process before it receives a Rebuilt title and returns to ordinary road use.

Certain cosmetic-only hail or theft vehicles and vehicles already titled Rebuilt or Reconstructed in another state may qualify for an exception, provided the applicable Wyoming branded title and supporting requirements are satisfied.

Do not assume a nonrepairable or otherwise restricted vehicle can be rebuilt and registered. If a vehicle is not authorized for road use, transport it rather than driving it.

How do I get an eligible salvage vehicle back on the road?

Wyoming’s typical salvage-to-rebuilt process involves the county clerk and WYDOT Motor Vehicle Services:

  1. Confirm eligibility. Verify the vehicle’s title status and whether an exception applies.
  2. Obtain a Wyoming Salvage title. Apply through the county clerk’s office in the owner’s name unless the required title has already been issued.
  3. Complete repairs. Restore the vehicle and retain available repair and replacement-part documentation.
  4. Prepare Form MV-600. Identify the damage before repairs and provide the name and address of the person or business that repaired the vehicle.
  5. Apply to WYDOT. Submit Form MV-600, a copy of the Wyoming Salvage title, and photographs of the repaired vehicle.
  6. Attach the decal. Place the issued Rebuilt Salvage Vehicle Decal on the driver’s-side door jamb; for a motorcycle, use the specified fork-crown location without obscuring the VIN.
  7. Obtain the law enforcement inspection. A Wyoming law enforcement officer verifies the VIN and decal placement and completes the certification. The decal must be attached before inspection.
  8. Apply for a Rebuilt title. Bring the Salvage title, signed inspection certification, and other required documents to the county clerk.
  9. Complete registration. Satisfy applicable registration and insurance requirements before road use.

See WYDOT’s salvage vehicle instructions.

Rebuilt vehicle inspections

Wyoming’s rebuilt-title inspection verifies the VIN and confirms that the required Rebuilt Salvage Vehicle Decal is properly attached. A Wyoming law enforcement officer completes the inspection certification.

The decal must be attached before the inspection. This inspection should not be mistaken for a comprehensive mechanical or structural safety evaluation. A buyer should consider an independent inspection of the vehicle’s repairs and condition.

What documents and forms may be required?

For a typical Wyoming salvage-to-rebuilt application, documents and information may include:

  • Wyoming certificate of title branded Salvage.
  • Form MV-600 — Application for Rebuilt Salvage Vehicle Decal.
  • A written description of the vehicle’s damage before repairs.
  • Name and address of the person or business that repaired or rebuilt the vehicle.
  • Photographs of the repaired vehicle. WYDOT does not expressly require before-repair photographs on Form MV-600.
  • Owner’s declaration that the application is accurate and that, to the owner’s knowledge, no stolen parts were used.
  • Rebuilt Salvage Vehicle Decal and Wyoming law enforcement VIN/decal certification.
  • Applicable ownership, lien-release, titling, and registration documents.

The applicant’s name on Form MV-600 must match the name on the Wyoming Salvage title. See the official MV-600 application.

Where do I arrange an inspection?

Contact a Wyoming law enforcement agency to ask where a VIN and rebuilt-decal inspection can be completed, whether an appointment is needed, and what documentation to bring.

First obtain and attach the decal in accordance with WYDOT’s instructions. If the vehicle is not authorized for road use, arrange transportation to the inspection location.

What if the salvage vehicle came from another state?

Wyoming preserves applicable salvage, rebuilt, reconstructed, flood, nonrepairable, and comparable brands from other jurisdictions. WYDOT may also apply a brand based on the National Motor Vehicle Title Information System (NMVTIS), even if the current out-of-state title appears unbranded.

A vehicle arriving with an out-of-state Salvage title generally must obtain a Wyoming Salvage title and complete the applicable rebuilt process before ordinary registration.

Wyoming provides an exception for vehicles already titled Rebuilt or Reconstructed by another state. When the existing brand is carried forward on a Wyoming title, the vehicle may qualify for registration without completing Wyoming’s ordinary rebuilt-title process.

An exception also applies to certain vehicles branded salvage solely because of cosmetic-only hail or theft damage. Confirm eligibility and any required supporting statements with WYDOT and the county clerk.

Before purchasing or transferring an out-of-state vehicle, verify its title history, registration eligibility, decal and inspection requirements, and ownership documentation. See WYDOT’s salvage guidance.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in Wyoming?

  • Verify the title brand and whether the vehicle is eligible for registration.
  • Check title history, including possible brands recorded through NMVTIS.
  • Investigate damage from collisions, hail, flooding, theft, or other causes. A Salvage brand alone does not establish whether flood damage occurred.
  • Review repair records, including invoices and available photographs.
  • Arrange an independent inspection by a qualified mechanic or collision repair specialist.
  • Confirm the decal and titling requirements and whether any exception applies.
  • Check insurance availability and estimate repair, transport, titling, and registration costs.

A Rebuilt title confirms completion of the applicable title process; it does not guarantee the quality of repairs or the vehicle’s mechanical condition.

What should I know if I later sell the vehicle?

Wyoming requires disclosure of applicable branded-title information before a vehicle is sold or traded. Dealers and private sellers who knowingly offer a branded vehicle must use the prescribed Branded Title Disclosure Statement (Form MV-602) and obtain the buyer’s acknowledgment.

Failure to obtain the acknowledgment may make a sale voidable at the buyer’s election; additional statutory provisions address intentional nondisclosure. Complete the ownership assignment and provide the required title and disclosure documents to the buyer.

If an owner retains a vehicle after an insurance settlement and the damage requires a Salvage or Nonrepairable brand, the owner must obtain the applicable branded title before commercial repairs or transfer. See WYDOT’s branded-title disclosure guidance.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.
