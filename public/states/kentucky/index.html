Kentucky Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Kentucky salvage titles, rebuilt vehicles, certified inspections, parts documentation, insurance total losses, and what must happen before a salvage vehicle can return to ordinary road use.

What you should know

Kentucky distinguishes between a vehicle with a Salvage title and a vehicle that has completed the rebuilding process and receives a title branded Rebuilt Vehicle.

A vehicle generally meets Kentucky's salvage definition when the estimated or actual cost of parts and labor needed to rebuild it to its pre-accident condition and legal road operation exceeds 75% of its retail value, subject to the details in Kentucky law.

A vehicle with a Kentucky Salvage title cannot be registered for highway use. After an eligible vehicle has been rebuilt, Kentucky requires the applicable rebuilt-title documentation and a certified vehicle inspection before a Rebuilt Vehicle title can be issued.

Kentucky's salvage rules are not simply an age-based system. Older vehicles can still enter the salvage-title process when the statutory conditions are met.

Understand Kentucky terminology

Salvage title

Kentucky generally requires a Salvage title when a vehicle has been wrecked, destroyed, or damaged to the extent that the estimated or actual cost of parts and labor to rebuild or reconstruct it to its pre-accident condition and legal road operation exceeds 75% of its retail value. Kentucky law excludes the cost of reinstalling a deployed airbag system from that particular 75% salvage calculation.

A Salvage title is proof of ownership, but the vehicle cannot be registered for highway use while that title is in force.

Review KRS 186A.520 — Kentucky Salvage Titles →

Rebuilt Vehicle

After an eligible salvage vehicle has been rebuilt and successfully completes Kentucky's applicable title and inspection requirements, the resulting title is branded Rebuilt Vehicle.

The brand is carried forward on later Kentucky titles. A rebuilt title should not be described as a clean title.

Junk or unrebuildable documents

Kentucky has unusual provisions for some vehicles carrying an out-of-state junk, unrebuildable, or similar ownership document. Under specified conditions, Kentucky can issue a specially identified Salvage title and, after the required process, a specially identified Rebuilt Vehicle title.

That does not mean every restrictive out-of-state document is automatically acceptable. The exact document, issuing state, vehicle condition, photographs, damage estimates, and other statutory requirements matter.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Potentially, if the vehicle has an ownership document Kentucky accepts for the rebuilt-title process and it can successfully complete the required reconstruction, documentation, inspection, and titling steps.

A Salvage title itself does not permit ordinary highway registration.

Before buying, ask:

What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?

Then determine:

Will Kentucky accept that document for the rebuilt-title process?

This is particularly important for vehicles from another state because terms such as Salvage, Junk, Unrebuildable, Nonrepairable, or Certificate of Destruction can have different legal effects.

How do I get a Kentucky salvage vehicle back on the road?

For an eligible salvage-titled vehicle, the general path is:

  1. Establish ownership with the original Salvage title or another ownership document Kentucky accepts.
  2. Rebuild the vehicle and keep original receipts for purchased parts.
  3. Complete TC 96-215, Affidavit of Motor Vehicle Assembled from Wrecked or Salvaged Motor Vehicles.
  4. Complete TC 96-182, Application for Kentucky Certificate of Title or Registration.
  5. Document the repairs as required by Kentucky's current rebuilt-title process.
  6. Complete the certified vehicle inspection required for the rebuilt title.
  7. Submit the rebuilt-title documentation through the applicable Kentucky process.
  8. After approval, obtain the Kentucky title branded Rebuilt Vehicle.

Kentucky requires original documents for several parts of the rebuilt application. Do not wait until the vehicle is finished to begin collecting documentation.

What inspection does Kentucky require?

Kentucky law requires proof of passing the inspection required by KRS 186A.115 before a qualifying rebuilt vehicle can receive a Rebuilt Vehicle title.

The inspection is performed by a Kentucky certified motor vehicle inspector or other authorized special inspector under current law. Certified inspectors are designated through the county sheriff's office, and Kentucky law now also permits certain specially appointed inspectors.

The inspection includes verification of vehicle identity and applicable roadworthiness and equipment requirements.

Review KRS 186A.115 — Kentucky vehicle inspections →

Does passing the inspection mean the vehicle is a good purchase?

No. Kentucky's inspection is an important legal step and includes vehicle-identity and applicable equipment or roadworthiness checks, but buyers should not treat it as a complete evaluation of the quality of every repair.

An independent pre-purchase inspection can help evaluate structural repair quality, alignment, airbags and restraint systems, electrical work, mechanical condition, water intrusion, and other issues that may matter to ownership and safety.

What documentation should I keep for replacement parts?

Kentucky requires original receipts for purchased parts in the rebuilt-title application.

If parts were purchased from an individual rather than a business, Kentucky's current guidance says the seller information and the parts acquired must be documented, along with the VIN of the vehicle from which the parts were taken. If the seller cannot provide that VIN, Kentucky requires a notarized statement explaining why it cannot be provided.

Keep receipts, invoices, bills of sale, donor-vehicle information, VIN information, repair records, and photographs as the rebuild progresses.

Review Kentucky's current parts-documentation requirements →

What documents and forms should I expect?

Depending on the vehicle and transaction, Kentucky's rebuilt process can require:

  • the original Salvage title or acceptable out-of-state ownership document,
  • TC 96-215, Affidavit of Motor Vehicle Assembled from Wrecked or Salvaged Motor Vehicles,
  • TC 96-182, Application for Kentucky Certificate of Title or Registration,
  • original receipts for purchased parts,
  • donor-vehicle VIN information or required explanations when a VIN cannot be provided,
  • TC 96-353 documenting repairs,
  • lien-release documentation when applicable,
  • inspection certification, and
  • other documents required for the particular vehicle.

Requirements can vary with the vehicle's history, age, damage, and source of parts.

What happens to an older vehicle that an insurance company totals?

Kentucky's core salvage definition is based primarily on the extent and value of the damage rather than a general rule excluding older vehicles.

The key threshold is whether the estimated or actual cost of qualifying parts and labor needed to rebuild or reconstruct the vehicle exceeds 75% of its retail value.

That means an older vehicle can still qualify for a Kentucky Salvage title. In fact, an older vehicle's lower retail value can make it easier for repair costs to cross the percentage threshold.

An insurer's use of the term “total loss” should not substitute for checking the actual Kentucky title status. Determine the damage calculation, who retained or acquired the vehicle after settlement, and what title was issued.

What if the owner keeps the vehicle after an insurance total loss?

Kentucky specifically addresses claim settlements in which the vehicle is not transferred to the insurance company.

When the damage meets or exceeds 75% of the vehicle's value, the insurer generally cannot make payment until it has proof that the owner surrendered the title or applied for a Salvage title. Kentucky law requires the owner to apply for the Salvage title within the period specified by the statute. A separate hail-damage rule can apply.

So owner retention does not mean a qualifying Kentucky total-loss vehicle simply keeps an unbranded title.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

Kentucky can accept a similar salvage title from another jurisdiction for the rebuilt process when that title does not disqualify the vehicle from highway titling in the issuing state.

Kentucky also has a special statutory process that can apply to some out-of-state junk, unrebuildable, or similarly restrictive documents. That path has additional requirements and produces specially identified Kentucky title branding.

Do not assume a restrictive document is acceptable merely because Kentucky has this exception. Verify the exact document and eligibility before buying or beginning repairs.

Review Kentucky's out-of-state salvage and restrictive-document rules →

What does Kentucky's 75% threshold mean?

Kentucky generally compares the qualifying estimated or actual cost of parts and labor needed to rebuild or reconstruct the vehicle with its retail value.

If that cost exceeds 75% of the retail value, the vehicle meets Kentucky's core salvage definition. The statute contains details about how parts and labor are valued and excludes the cost of reinstalling a deployed airbag system from this particular salvage-threshold calculation.

The percentage determines title treatment; it does not tell a buyer whether the eventual repairs were performed correctly.

What should I check before buying a Kentucky salvage vehicle?

  • What exact ownership document does the vehicle have?
  • Does it currently have a Salvage, Rebuilt Vehicle, or another title status?
  • Why was it declared a total loss or placed into the salvage system?
  • Did the qualifying repair cost exceed Kentucky's 75% threshold?
  • Who acquired or retained the vehicle after the insurance settlement?
  • If the owner retained it, was a Salvage title obtained as required?
  • Has the vehicle already been rebuilt?
  • Has the required certified inspection occurred?
  • Are original receipts available for significant replacement components?
  • Can donor vehicles and used components be identified?
  • If it came from another state, will Kentucky accept the exact ownership document?
  • Has repair quality been independently evaluated?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, inspection, title, tax, registration, and other costs remain?

The central Kentucky question is:

Does this vehicle have an ownership document Kentucky will accept for rebuilding, and can its repairs and replacement components be documented well enough to complete the inspection and obtain a Rebuilt Vehicle title?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

A Kentucky vehicle that completes the salvage-rebuilding process does not return to an ordinary unbranded title. The Rebuilt Vehicle brand is carried forward on later titles.

Kentucky also requires disclosure of applicable title brands to prospective buyers or transferees. Dealer and private-owner disclosure requirements differ, so verify the current rule that applies to the transaction.

Do not describe a rebuilt vehicle as having a “clean title” merely because it is eligible for registration.

About this guidance

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet, a Kentucky county clerk, a certified vehicle inspector, or another government agency.
