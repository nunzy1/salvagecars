Virginia Salvage Vehicle Guidance

Understand Virginia salvage, nonrepairable, and rebuilt vehicle titles, the required safety inspection, DMV rebuilt-vehicle examination, and whether a damaged vehicle can legally return to the road.

What you should know

Virginia distinguishes between salvage, nonrepairable, and rebuilt vehicles.

A vehicle with a Virginia salvage certificate can potentially be repaired and returned to road use. Before Virginia will issue a rebuilt title, however, the repaired vehicle must pass a Virginia state safety inspection and then undergo a separate examination by the Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles.

A vehicle for which a Virginia nonrepairable certificate has been issued cannot later be titled, registered, or legally driven in Virginia.

A vehicle that successfully completes Virginia's rebuilt process receives a title permanently branded REBUILT.

A salvage vehicle can potentially return to the road. A nonrepairable vehicle cannot.

Understand Virginia terminology

Salvage vehicle

A salvage vehicle is a vehicle that has been declared salvage and for which Virginia issues a salvage certificate.

Virginia also requires certain late-model damaged vehicles to enter the salvage process. For an uninsured or self-insured late-model vehicle titled in Virginia, the owner must generally apply for a salvage certificate when the estimated repair cost exceeds 75% of the vehicle's actual cash value.

A vehicle with an active salvage certificate cannot be registered or operated on Virginia highways.

Late-model vehicle

Virginia defines a late-model vehicle as the current-year model and the five preceding model years, or any vehicle whose actual cash value before damage was at least $10,000. This definition is important because several Virginia salvage requirements specifically apply to late-model vehicles.

Nonrepairable vehicle

Virginia defines a nonrepairable vehicle as one that its insurer or owner has determined has no value except for parts and scrap metal, or a vehicle for which a nonrepairable certificate has been issued or applied for.

Once a Virginia nonrepairable certificate has been issued, the vehicle cannot later be titled, registered, or legally driven in Virginia. It may be used only for parts.

Rebuilt vehicle

Virginia generally defines a rebuilt vehicle as a salvage vehicle repaired for use on public highways, or a late-model vehicle that has been repaired when its estimated repair cost exceeded 75% of its actual cash value, subject to the statutory calculation.

A rebuilt vehicle that completes Virginia's requirements receives a title permanently branded REBUILT. The brand carries forward on subsequent Virginia titles for the life of the vehicle.

Can this vehicle return to the road?

Possibly—if its ownership status allows it.

A Virginia salvage vehicle can potentially be repaired, inspected, examined, retitled, registered, and returned to highway use. A vehicle for which Virginia has issued a nonrepairable certificate cannot.

The first question should therefore be:

What exact ownership document does this vehicle have?

If it has a salvage certificate, the next question is:

Can the repaired vehicle pass both Virginia's state safety inspection and DMV's rebuilt-vehicle examination?

Answer those questions before investing substantially in the project.

How do I get a Virginia salvage vehicle back on the road?

  1. Confirm that the vehicle's ownership document permits rebuilding.
  2. Repair or rebuild the vehicle.
  3. Keep receipts and other documentation for parts used in the repairs.
  4. Keep photographs showing the vehicle before repairs.
  5. Retain old component parts displaying a VIN when applicable.
  6. Have the completed vehicle pass a Virginia state safety inspection.
  7. Complete the Request for Examination of Rebuilt Salvage Vehicle (LES 022A).
  8. Submit the required title or salvage documents, application materials, taxes, and fees to Virginia DMV.
  9. Wait for a DMV Special Agent to contact you to schedule the rebuilt-vehicle examination.
  10. Present the vehicle and required documentation for examination.
  11. Correct any documentation irregularities or vehicle defects identified by DMV.
  12. If the vehicle passes, DMV authorizes release of the title with the appropriate REBUILT brand.

Use the current Virginia DMV instructions and forms when completing the process because forms, fees, taxes, and procedures can change.

Why does the vehicle need a Virginia state safety inspection first?

Virginia requires each salvage vehicle rebuilt for highway use to pass a state safety inspection before DMV conducts its rebuilt-vehicle examination.

The safety inspection must be performed by an inspector who is unaffiliated with the person requesting the inspection.

This step matters because Virginia separates two questions:

Does the rebuilt vehicle satisfy the applicable state safety-inspection requirements?

Does the vehicle and its documentation satisfy DMV's rebuilt-vehicle examination requirements?

Passing one does not eliminate the need for the other.

What happens during the DMV rebuilt-vehicle examination?

After the vehicle has passed the state safety inspection, Virginia DMV examines the rebuilt vehicle before issuing a title.

The DMV examination can include review of photographs or video showing the vehicle before it was rebuilt, documentation for parts and labor used in the repair, and verification of the vehicle identification number, confidential identifying numbers, odometer information, engine, transmission, and electronic modules when applicable.

Virginia DMV's current instructions tell applicants to bring proof that the vehicle passed a Virginia state safety inspection, a copy of the LES 022A examination request, receipts for original parts, a photograph of the vehicle before repairs, and old component parts displaying the VIN when applicable.

If DMV identifies irregularities in the documentation or obvious vehicle defects, the owner must correct them before the examination can be completed.

What is Form LES 022A?

Virginia's Request for Examination of Rebuilt Salvage Vehicle (LES 022A) is the application used to request the DMV rebuilt-vehicle examination.

The form records information about the owner or applicant, the vehicle, who repaired the vehicle, repaired and replaced parts, applicable rebuilder information, and the completed Virginia state safety inspection.

Virginia's current form states that the vehicle must be rebuilt before the examination request is submitted. Painting is optional.

The form also identifies documentation that should be available for the examination, including original parts receipts, a pre-repair photograph, and old VIN-bearing components when applicable.

Why are pre-repair photographs important?

Virginia DMV's current instructions tell applicants to bring a photograph showing the vehicle before repairs to the rebuilt-vehicle examination. Virginia law also allows the DMV examination to include review of photographs or video showing the vehicle before it was rebuilt.

If you are considering buying a partially or completely rebuilt salvage vehicle from someone else, ask whether pre-repair photographs exist before completing the purchase.

Why are parts receipts important?

Virginia DMV examines documentation for parts used to repair a salvage vehicle.

The current examination instructions tell applicants to bring receipts for original parts and old components displaying a VIN when applicable.

These records help DMV verify the identity and ownership history of components used in the rebuilt vehicle.

If you are buying a partially rebuilt project, determine whether the seller can provide the parts documentation before you take ownership. A missing documentation trail can complicate your later DMV examination.

Can I drive the rebuilt vehicle to its safety inspection?

A vehicle with an active salvage certificate cannot be used for ordinary highway operation.

Virginia law nevertheless provides limited mechanisms for transporting a rebuilt salvage vehicle for the required state safety inspection.

A licensed salvage dealer or rebuilder who is also licensed as a motor vehicle dealer can use dealer plates under specified conditions to transport an eligible vehicle to and from the safety inspection station. For other rebuilt salvage vehicles, Virginia DMV can issue a temporary trip permit when applicable for that purpose.

These provisions should not be treated as general permission to drive a salvage vehicle. Verify the current permit, insurance, vehicle-condition, route, and documentation requirements before attempting to drive the vehicle.

What if the vehicle came from another state?

Virginia can accept out-of-state salvage titles or other appropriate documents showing that another state declared the vehicle salvage. Virginia generally carries forward applicable brands or indicators.

If an out-of-state salvage vehicle has not already completed an acceptable rebuilt process, Virginia can issue a salvage certificate and require the vehicle to complete Virginia's rebuilt requirements.

The situation becomes significantly different when an out-of-state vehicle has been branded junk, for destruction, parts only, not to be repaired, or with a similar restrictive designation.

Virginia law contains a special provision for certain vehicles that received such a designation but were subsequently rebuilt, titled, and registered by another state before coming to Virginia. In qualifying circumstances, Virginia can issue a nonnegotiable title rather than a normal negotiable title.

Before buying an out-of-state vehicle, inspect the actual ownership document, identify every brand or restriction, determine whether the vehicle has already been rebuilt and titled elsewhere, and verify what type of Virginia title and examination requirements would apply.

What is a Virginia nonnegotiable title?

Virginia has an unusual provision for certain vehicles that previously received an out-of-state designation such as junk, for destruction, parts only, or not to be repaired but were later rebuilt, titled, and registered by another state.

When the statutory conditions are satisfied, Virginia can issue a nonnegotiable title. This does not erase the vehicle's restrictive history or turn it into an ordinary rebuilt vehicle.

Virginia law places significant limitations on vehicles with these titles, including restrictions on subsequent transfer.

If you are considering an out-of-state vehicle with a restrictive historical brand, verify its eligibility directly with Virginia DMV before purchasing it.

Are there limits on how many salvage vehicles I can rebuild?

Yes.

Virginia defines a rebuilder as a person who acquires and repairs for highway use two or more salvage vehicles within a 12-month period. Virginia DMV requires rebuilders to be licensed.

The current LES 022A instructions likewise warn that a person must be licensed as a rebuilder to have two or more salvage vehicles examined within a consecutive 12-month period.

Someone rebuilding a single personal project should therefore avoid assuming that the same rules apply if they begin repeatedly acquiring and rebuilding salvage vehicles.

What should I check before buying a salvage vehicle in Virginia?

  • What is the exact ownership document?
  • Is the vehicle salvage or nonrepairable?
  • Does it have an out-of-state junk, parts-only, destruction, or similar brand?
  • Is it considered a late-model vehicle under Virginia law?
  • Why was it declared salvage?
  • What damage occurred?
  • Has rebuilding already begun?
  • Who performed the repairs?
  • Are pre-repair photographs available?
  • Are receipts available for replacement parts?
  • Are old VIN-bearing components available when applicable?
  • Are the vehicle's identifying numbers intact?
  • Has the vehicle already passed a Virginia state safety inspection?
  • Has it already undergone a DMV rebuilt-vehicle examination?
  • If it came from another state, what title will Virginia issue?
  • Can you obtain the insurance coverage you want?
  • What repair, inspection, examination, title, registration, tax, and transportation costs remain?
  • Has the quality and condition of completed repairs been independently evaluated?

The central Virginia question is:

Does this vehicle's ownership history allow it to receive a road title, and can it successfully complete both Virginia's safety-inspection and DMV rebuilt-examination requirements?

What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?

A Virginia salvage vehicle that successfully completes the rebuilt process receives a title permanently branded REBUILT. The brand remains on subsequent Virginia titles for the life of the vehicle.

Virginia DMV also requires a person selling a rebuilt vehicle to provide the purchaser with the assigned title certificate and a Rebuilt Vehicle Disclosure Statement.

If you later sell or transfer the vehicle, accurately represent its REBUILT history, provide the required rebuilt-vehicle disclosure, and complete the applicable Virginia title-transfer requirements.

If the vehicle has a Virginia nonnegotiable title rather than an ordinary rebuilt title, different transfer restrictions apply. Verify those requirements directly with DMV before attempting a sale or transfer.
