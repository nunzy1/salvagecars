State Page Template

Reusable structure for SalvageCars.com state guidance pages. This is a living document, initially validated against California and New Hampshire and intended to evolve as additional states are implemented.

Purpose

A state page helps visitors understand how a state classifies salvage and rebuilt vehicles, whether a vehicle can return to legal road use, what process and documentation may apply, what to verify before buying, which official resources govern the process, and what to do next.

The template provides consistency without forcing every state into the same content shape. California demonstrated a complex multi-agency process; New Hampshire demonstrated a lighter process with a consolidated inspection path and state-specific resale requirements.

Core principle

A State Guidance page is not a copy of state DMV instructions. It provides a decision layer:

What is this vehicle? → Can it return to the road? → What do I need to do? → What should I verify before I act? → Where is the official source?

Official state sources remain the authority. SalvageCars.com explains, organizes, and connects those sources without replacing them.

Required page elements

1. Breadcrumb

Orient the visitor, reinforce the State Guidance hierarchy, and provide a route back to /states/.

2. H1 and concise lead

Use [State] Salvage Vehicle Guidance. Summarize the classification, road eligibility, process or inspections, and official state requirements. Avoid generic introductions and SEO filler.

3. What you should know

A prominent direct answer identifying the state's most important decision or distinction.

4. State terminology

Explain only terms that materially affect the process, such as salvage, rebuilt, revived salvage, junk, non-repairable, flood, or other state-specific classifications. Depth varies by state.

5. Can this vehicle return to the road?

Directly explain whether relevant classifications can return to on-highway use. Surface classifications that permanently prevent road use.

6. How to get it back on the road

Give a plain-language overview of the state process rather than reproducing every agency instruction. Typical steps may include ownership, repair, documentation, inspection, forms, fees, and rebuilt-title issuance.

7. Before you buy

Turn the state's actual requirements into practical pre-purchase questions. Do not substitute a generic salvage checklist.

8. About this guidance

Include the standard informational disclaimer, customized with state agency names where useful.

SalvageCars.com provides general educational information to help you understand salvage vehicles, identify questions you may need to answer, and find the government agencies and official resources responsible for the applicable requirements.

This information is not legal advice and is not a substitute for guidance from the appropriate state agency or another government authority. Requirements, forms, fees, inspection procedures, and other rules can change, and the requirements that apply can depend on the particular vehicle and transaction.

Before buying, rebuilding, selling, titling, or registering a salvage vehicle, verify the current requirements with the appropriate state agency.

9. Official sources

List the authoritative sources used to create the page. Include the agency, source title, direct official link, and a short description of what it covers.

10. What should I do next?

Provide clear next actions. Use SalvageCars.com guides for explanation and official sources where action must occur.

11. Last reviewed

Display the month and year the guidance was last substantively reviewed. The review date is part of the trust model.

Recommended sections

Inspections and verification

Use when formal inspections or verifications are material. Explain what is required, who performs it, what it verifies, required documentation, and whether different inspections serve different purposes.

Documents and forms

Surface forms visitors are reasonably likely to need. Identify the official name and number, link to the official source when practical, explain the purpose, and distinguish core from conditional documents.

Out-of-state vehicles

Include when the state has meaningful nonresident or out-of-state inspection, title, documentation, recognition, or exception rules.

Fees and costs

Include fees when authoritative, useful, and reasonably stable. For volatile fees, explain the category and link to current official information.

Conditional sections

Scheduling an inspection

Use when appointments, designated locations, or state-managed scheduling apply. Link to current scheduling information rather than copying volatile schedules.

What to bring to the inspection

Use separately when documentation requirements merit emphasis; otherwise incorporate them into the inspection section.

Smog, emissions, safety, or equipment requirements

Use when additional emissions, safety systems, VIN, stolen-parts, structural, or similar inspections apply.

Special vehicle classes

Use only when requirements differ materially for motorcycles, trucks, trailers, commercial vehicles, reconstructed or specially constructed vehicles, older vehicles, or other relevant classes.

State-specific sections

Add sections when state law creates an important visitor question that does not fit naturally into the standard structure. State pages should not imitate one another merely for visual symmetry.

Selling or transferring the vehicle

Research resale and transfer requirements for every state. Use a dedicated section when disclosure obligations, title-brand transfer rules, dealer obligations, decals, labels, or resale limitations materially affect the visitor.

Other state-specific requirements

Add state-specific exceptions, prohibitions, unusual classifications, insurance requirements, or other decision points only when authoritative rules make them relevant.

Contextual official links

Link to official sources at the point where visitors may need to verify a requirement, open a form, schedule an inspection, locate a service, review a rule, or begin a state process.

Explain the requirement on SalvageCars.com → link to the exact official source where the visitor can verify or act.

Forms linking rule

  • Use the official state form or forms page.
  • Use the official form name and number.
  • Explain what the form is used for.
  • Avoid third-party form mirrors.
  • Prefer current agency pages over stale PDFs when both exist.
  • Use the official forms page when a direct form URL is not durable.

Source currency rule

Official does not automatically mean current. When official sources conflict:

  1. Prefer the agency responsible for the current program.
  2. Check current procedural guidance.
  3. Check statutes or administrative rules when needed.
  4. Identify legacy language.
  5. Do not publish a definitive instruction until the discrepancy is resolved.

Stable section IDs

Major sections receive stable, human-readable IDs. State-specific IDs are allowed. Once published, treat them as stable URLs and do not rename them casually.

Visual pattern

  • Shared site header and footer
  • Breadcrumb above title
  • H1 with concise lead
  • Reading column around max-w-3xl
  • 18px body text and approximately 1.65 line height
  • Generous vertical spacing
  • Restrained bordered “What you should know” and disclaimer surfaces
  • Contextual official links
  • Quiet bordered rows for forms, sources, and next actions where useful
  • No decorative cards or heavy shadows without a real information need

Research and page-production workflow

  1. Research the state using the State Page Content Model.
  2. Identify authoritative agencies, statutes, rules, forms, and procedures.
  3. Determine the state's important visitor decision points.
  4. Draft the visitor-facing content.
  5. Include only template sections justified by the state's rules.
  6. Add state-specific sections where necessary.
  7. Verify substantive process statements against authoritative sources.
  8. Add contextual official links and direct form links where useful.
  9. Add stable section IDs.
  10. Build the HTML using the Design System.
  11. Review for usefulness, not merely completeness.
  12. Record the review date.
  13. Add the page to the State Guidance index.

Before large-scale production of the remaining states, validate the template against at least one additional state with a materially different regulatory model. Modify the template when that test, or later implementations, reveals a genuinely reusable pattern.

The template should evolve from demonstrated content needs rather than hypothetical completeness.
