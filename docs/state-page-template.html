State Page Template

Production template validated against California, New Hampshire, Texas, Florida, and New York implementations. The template remains a living document and should evolve when additional state implementations reveal a reusable content need.

Purpose

Use this template to turn authoritative state salvage-vehicle requirements into a clear visitor decision path. Official state sources provide the authority; SalvageCars.com provides the explanation, context, and next-step guidance.

What is this vehicle? → Can it return to the road? → What do I need to do? → What should I verify before I act? → Where is the official source?

Template principles

  • Research the state before deciding which sections the finished page needs.
  • Do not force every state into identical visible sections.
  • Include state-specific sections when they answer a meaningful visitor question.
  • Do not add empty sections merely to match the template.
  • Prefer current guidance from the agency responsible for the process.
  • Verify substantive process claims against authoritative sources.
  • Record unresolved conflicts or outdated official wording during research rather than guessing.
  • Preserve published section IDs as stable URLs.
  • The template should evolve from demonstrated content needs rather than hypothetical completeness.

Required page structure

1. Breadcrumb

Home → State Guidance → [State]

2. H1 and concise lead

Use “[State] Salvage Vehicle Guidance” as the standard H1. The lead should summarize the state's most important salvage/rebuilt distinction and what the page helps the visitor understand.

3. What you should know

Provide a short direct-answer summary of the state's most consequential rules or distinctions. This should help a visitor understand the situation before reading procedural detail.

Stable ID: #what-you-should-know

4. State terminology

Explain the title brands, certificates, classifications, and state-specific terms necessary to understand road eligibility and the process. Depth should vary by state.

Stable ID: #state-terminology

5. Can this vehicle return to the road?

Clearly distinguish vehicles that may have a legal path back to road use from classifications or ownership documents that prevent rebuilding, retitling, registration, or normal highway use.

Stable ID: #can-this-vehicle-return-to-the-road

6. How to get it back on the road

Describe the general state-specific process in a logical sequence. Keep it understandable and link to current official procedures where the visitor must verify details or act.

Stable ID: #how-to-get-it-back-on-the-road

7. Documents and forms

Identify important ownership documents, applications, builder statements, component-part records, receipts, inspection documents, and other forms when applicable. Explain what each important form is for rather than merely listing it.

Stable ID: #documents-and-forms

8. Before you buy

Convert the state's rules into practical questions a prospective buyer should answer before purchasing or spending money on repairs.

Stable ID: #before-you-buy

9. About this guidance

Include the standard SalvageCars.com educational-information disclaimer and identify the appropriate state agency or agencies.

10. Official sources

Consolidate the primary authoritative state sources used for the page. Prefer the responsible agency, current procedures, official forms, statutes, and administrative rules.

Stable ID: #official-sources

11. What should I do next?

Give visitors a small number of situation-based next actions, such as buying, already owning, dealing with an out-of-state vehicle, or preparing for an inspection.

Stable ID: #what-should-i-do-next

12. Last reviewed

Record the month and year when the state's substantive requirements and official links were last reviewed.

Recommended sections when applicable

Inspections and verification

Explain not only what inspection, examination, or verification is required, but also its purpose, who performs it, what it establishes, and what it does not establish when that distinction matters.

Stable ID: #inspections-and-verification

Out-of-state vehicles

Include when the state has meaningful rules for recognizing out-of-state salvage, rebuilt, junk, nonrepairable, parts-only, or similar ownership documents.

Stable ID: #out-of-state-vehicles

Selling or transferring the vehicle

Research this topic for every state. Include a dedicated section when title branding, written disclosure, advertising, decals, transfer requirements, or other obligations would materially affect a future seller or buyer.

Preferred visible heading: “What should I know if I later sell or transfer the vehicle?”
Preserve the existing stable ID: #selling-a-rebuilt-vehicle

Fees and costs

Include when authoritative fee information materially helps the visitor. Avoid hard-coding volatile fees when linking to the current agency source is more durable.

Conditional and state-specific sections

Add sections when the state's actual process creates a meaningful visitor question. Examples discovered during implementation include:

  • inspection scheduling or what to bring
  • component-part documentation
  • transporting a vehicle to an examination
  • flood vehicles
  • airbags or restraint systems
  • Certificate of Destruction or other nonrepairable classifications
  • dealer or business licensing
  • special vehicle classes
  • emissions, safety, equipment, or verification requirements

These examples are not required template sections. Add them only when the state demonstrates the need.

Contextual official-source rule

Explain the requirement in plain language, then link to the most specific current official source where the visitor can verify the requirement or take the next action.

Avoid replacing explanation with a directory of government links. Do not duplicate volatile agency-maintained information when a current official source can be linked instead.

  • Put important official links at the point of need.
  • Use the Official Sources section to consolidate the principal references at the bottom.
  • Link important official forms where they help the visitor act.
  • Prefer current agency web pages or current form pages over stale PDFs when possible.
  • If a direct form URL is not durable, link to the agency's official forms page.
  • Do not copy agency-maintained inspection-location lists or similar volatile operational information unless there is a compelling reason.
  • Official does not automatically mean current. Reconcile outdated or conflicting official instructions before publishing a definitive statement.

Production workflow

  1. Research the state using the State Page Content Model.
  2. Identify authoritative agencies, statutes, rules, forms, and procedures.
  3. Determine the important visitor decision points.
  4. Draft visitor-facing content.
  5. Include only justified template sections.
  6. Add state-specific sections when necessary.
  7. Verify substantive process claims against authoritative sources.
  8. Add contextual official links and forms.
  9. Add and preserve stable section IDs.
  10. Build the HTML using the SalvageCars.com Design System.
  11. Review for usefulness, not merely completeness.
  12. Record the review date.
  13. Add the completed page to the State Guidance index.

Visual pattern

  • Use the shared site header and footer.
  • Use breadcrumb → H1 → concise lead.
  • Use a reading width around max-w-3xl.
  • Use approximately 18px body text with line-height around 1.65.
  • Use generous vertical spacing.
  • Use restrained bordered treatments for “What you should know” and the informational disclaimer.
  • Use quiet bordered rows for forms, official sources, and next actions when useful.
  • Avoid decorative cards, heavy shadows, and visual containers that do not improve comprehension.
