State Page Content Model

Research, verification, and maintenance standards for SalvageCars.com State Guidance covering all 50 states and the District of Columbia.

Purpose

The State Page Content Model defines what we research, verify, and maintain before publishing or updating State Guidance. It is separate from the State Page Template, which defines how verified information is presented to visitors.

Official state sources establish requirements; SalvageCars.com explains their meaning, conditions, and practical implications in plain language.

Research principles

  • Use authoritative government sources as the factual foundation: motor vehicle agencies, statutes, administrative rules, law-enforcement programs, and other responsible public agencies.
  • Prefer current guidance from the agency directly responsible for the requirement.
  • Do not use commercial auction, salvage, title-information, law-firm, blog, forum, or aggregator sites to establish state requirements.
  • Translate official requirements into plain-language answers and link to the relevant official source at the point where the visitor needs it.
  • Distinguish general rules from exceptions, exemptions, and conditional requirements; never infer that a rule applies universally.
  • Identify unresolved conflicts, outdated instructions, and uncertainty rather than guessing.
  • Explain the purpose of inspections and distinguish identity/theft checks from safety, emissions, and roadworthiness requirements where applicable.
  • Do not reproduce volatile fee amounts, mailing addresses, or office phone numbers. Link to current official information instead.
  • Record the month and year of substantive review and keep official-source links current.

Core visitor questions

Research should help a visitor understand the vehicle’s legal status, whether it can return to the road, what steps apply, and where to verify requirements before acting.

  • Terminology: Which title brands, certificates, and classifications does the state use?
  • Eligibility: Can the vehicle be rebuilt, titled, registered, and returned to road use? Which classifications prevent that?
  • Process: What steps, documents, and conditional requirements apply?
  • Inspections: Who performs each inspection, what does it establish, and what documents result?
  • Buying: What should a buyer verify before purchase or repair?
  • Out-of-state vehicles: How does the state treat vehicles and ownership documents from other jurisdictions?
  • Selling or transferring: What title-brand disclosures, documentation, and transfer requirements may apply?
  • Official resources: Which agencies, forms, statutes, and instructions support each important claim?

Reusable question and source framework

For every relevant research topic, establish the rule, its applicability and exceptions, the responsible agency, the supporting official URL, and the visitor-facing consequence. Do not add an inapplicable section merely to fill a checklist.

Source reconciliation

When official sources appear inconsistent, do not silently select an answer or repeat outdated wording. Compare the responsible agency’s current public guidance with current procedural materials, statutes, and rules as appropriate. Record the discrepancy, resolve it before publishing a definitive instruction, or state the uncertainty clearly if it remains unresolved.

Official does not automatically mean current. A changed program, form, or inspection requirement may leave older official pages online.

Information to research for each State Guidance page

Use the approved State Page Template for presentation and stable section IDs. Research the following information where applicable:

  1. What you should know: consequential state-specific distinctions, summarized in concise prose paragraphs.
  2. State terminology: title brands, certificates, and classifications.
  3. Can this vehicle return to the road? eligibility and restrictions.
  4. How to get it back on the road: understandable process and official next steps.
  5. Inspections and verification: applicable examinations and their purposes.
  6. Documents and forms: core and conditional paperwork with official links.
  7. Before you buy: state-specific pre-purchase checks.
  8. Out-of-state vehicles: special recognition or processing rules.
  9. Selling or transferring: relevant obligations and disclosures.
  10. About this guidance: the standard educational-information explanation and verification reminder.
  11. Official sources: principal government references with useful descriptions.
  12. What should I do next? situation-based next actions and relevant internal journeys.
  13. Last reviewed: month and year of substantive verification.

State-specific subjects such as flood classifications, component documentation, or special vehicle classes should be included when supported by the state’s actual requirements.

Reusable research checklist

  • ☐ Identify responsible agencies and current official sources.
  • ☐ Verify brands, definitions, and classifications.
  • ☐ Verify road eligibility, restrictions, and exceptions.
  • ☐ Verify titling, rebuilding, registration, and transfer procedures.
  • ☐ Distinguish each inspection’s purpose, responsible authority, and documentation.
  • ☐ Verify forms and separate general from conditional requirements.
  • ☐ Research out-of-state treatment and pre-purchase considerations.
  • ☐ Research selling, transfer, and disclosure requirements.
  • ☐ Resolve conflicting or outdated official instructions; record remaining uncertainty.
  • ☐ Add contextual official links where readers need to verify or act.
  • ☐ Avoid fee amounts, mailing addresses, and office phone numbers.
  • ☐ Check consistency with the State Page Template and relevant question journeys.
  • ☐ Preserve published section IDs and verify all affected internal destinations.
  • ☐ Record the substantive review month and year.

Ongoing maintenance

Revisit official sources and substantive requirements on the schedule established in the site maintenance process. Prioritize changes to title terminology, eligibility, inspections, forms, procedures, and transfer obligations.

For every update, check contextual links and the Official Sources section, preserve existing section IDs, and validate links from the seven question journeys. Run the repository’s applicable navigation and link checks before publishing. Update the page’s Last Reviewed date only after substantive verification, not for cosmetic edits alone.

Record unresolved discrepancies for follow-up rather than presenting an unsupported conclusion. The maintenance process will define the review cadence and tracking responsibilities.
