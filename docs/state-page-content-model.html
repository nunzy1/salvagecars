State Page Content Model

A research and information model for SalvageCars.com State Guidance pages. California is the first reference case used to discover the questions, source types, conditions, and decision points that a useful state page must handle.

Purpose

The State Page Content Model defines what we need to research and understand before writing a public State Guidance page. It is not the State Page Template. The eventual template will define how validated information is consistently presented after the model has been tested with real states.

Working sequence

Content model → California page → contrasting state test (New Hampshire) → finalized State Page Template.

Research principles

  • Use authoritative sources as the factual foundation: state motor vehicle agencies, legislatures and statutes, administrative agencies and rules, law-enforcement programs, and other responsible government bodies.
  • Prefer the agency directly responsible for a requirement over secondary summaries.
  • Do not use commercial salvage, auction, title-information, law-firm, blog, forum, or aggregator sites to establish state requirements.
  • Translate official requirements into plain-language visitor answers, while preserving a direct path to the authoritative source.
  • Do not infer that a rule is universal when the official source describes it as conditional.
  • Record uncertainty, conflicts, outdated wording, and unresolved questions instead of forcing a definitive answer.
  • Record a review date because agency pages, forms, fees, inspections, and procedures can change.

Core visitor jobs and questions

The model should help a visitor move from “I have or am considering this vehicle” to “I understand what the state calls it, whether it can return to the road, what I must do, and where the official instructions are.”

Identify the vehicle’s legal status

What does the state call this vehicle? What does its brand or certificate mean? Is it salvage, rebuilt or revived, junk, non-repairable, flood, or another state-defined category?

Determine road eligibility

Can this vehicle legally return to on-highway use, and are there classifications that permanently prevent that?

Understand the process

What must happen, in what general sequence, before the vehicle can be titled and registered?

Prepare for inspections and documentation

Who inspects the vehicle, what is being inspected, what documents and forms are required, and which requirements depend on the vehicle?

Avoid a bad purchase or process surprise

What should a buyer verify before purchasing, especially for out-of-state vehicles or classifications with restricted road eligibility?

Source reconciliation rule

When official sources appear inconsistent, do not silently choose wording or reproduce an older instruction. Check the most current page from the agency responsible for the program, check current procedural material, record the discrepancy, and resolve it before publishing a definitive instruction.

California example: current BAR and DMV VSSI material establishes the electronic Vehicle Safety Systems Inspection process. DMV states that one passing VSSI certificate replaces the former brake and light certificates. Older official material may still contain legacy wording.

Proposed State Page information model

This is a working content model, not yet the State Page Template. It may change after California is written and the model is tested against New Hampshire.

  1. 1. What you should know — essential state-specific answer and decision points.
  2. 2. State terminology — definitions and distinctions that determine which process applies.
  3. 3. Can the vehicle return to the road? — eligibility and stop conditions.
  4. 4. How to get it back on the road — plain-language process overview.
  5. 5. Inspections and verification — who performs them, what they establish, and conditional inspections.
  6. 6. Documents and forms — core versus conditional documentation, with official links.
  7. 7. Fees and costs — authoritative state fees where useful, without implying a universal total.
  8. 8. Out-of-state and special situations — conditional journeys that materially change the process.
  9. 9. Before you buy — state-specific checks that can prevent an avoidable purchase problem.
  10. 10. Official sources — direct authoritative sources used for the page.
  11. 11. What should I do next? — next state action or relevant SalvageCars.com guide.

Reusable research checklist

  • ☐ Identify the responsible state agencies.
  • ☐ Identify official terminology and title/certificate brands.
  • ☐ Determine which classifications can and cannot return to on-highway use.
  • ☐ Identify the general rebuild, re-title, and re-registration process.
  • ☐ Identify vehicle verification, safety, emissions, VIN, theft/component, or other inspections.
  • ☐ Identify who performs each inspection and what documentation it produces.
  • ☐ Identify required forms and distinguish core from conditional forms.
  • ☐ Identify state fees when clearly stated by an authoritative source.
  • ☐ Research out-of-state vehicle treatment.
  • ☐ Identify buyer-before-purchase checks derived from state requirements.
  • ☐ Reconcile conflicting or potentially outdated official instructions.
  • ☐ Record official URLs and review date.
  • ☐ Record unresolved questions instead of guessing.

California findings that shape the model

  • A state page needs an early road-eligibility decision because California distinguishes salvage vehicles that may be revived from vehicles issued a non-repairable certificate that cannot later be titled or re-registered for revived on-highway use.
  • “Inspection” cannot be treated as one generic event. California may involve DMV/CHP vehicle verification, BAR VSSI, and conditional smog requirements with different purposes.
  • Out-of-state vehicles deserve a conditional path because California has specific nonresident revived-salvage procedures.
  • Forms should be explained in context rather than presented as an undifferentiated list.
  • Current-program verification matters: California’s VSSI transition shows that official documentation can retain legacy terminology.

Open questions for the California writing pass

  • What is the clearest visitor-facing distinction among salvage, revived salvage, junk, revived junk, and non-repairable without oversimplifying California definitions?
  • What is the clearest general sequence of DMV, verification/CHP, VSSI, smog when applicable, fees, and final registration—and which parts cannot safely be presented as one fixed sequence?
  • Which forms are common enough to surface on the public page, and which should remain conditional details reached through DMV?
  • Which fees are stable and useful enough to state directly, and which should instead link to DMV’s current fee information?
  • What California-specific pre-purchase warnings are directly supported by official requirements?
  • Are additional vehicle classes or special cases important enough for V1, or should they remain linked exceptions?
