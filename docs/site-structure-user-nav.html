Site Structure & User Navigation

1. Purpose

This document defines how visitors experience SalvageCars.com.

It describes how information is organized, how visitors navigate the platform, how related content connects together, and how the website guides users toward informed decisions.

Rather than organizing the platform around articles or features, SalvageCars.com is organized around the questions visitors are trying to answer. This document serves as the blueprint for navigation, content organization, homepage design, templates, and future expansion.

2. User Experience Philosophy

Visitors rarely arrive knowing where information is located. They arrive with a problem they are trying to solve.

  • Should I buy this salvage vehicle?
  • What does a branded title mean?
  • Can I register this vehicle in my state?
  • How much will repairs cost?
  • What happens after my insurance company totals my vehicle?

Our responsibility is to help visitors answer those questions quickly, accurately, and confidently.

Every page should reduce uncertainty, build trust, and naturally lead visitors toward the next question they are likely to ask.

3. User Jobs (Jobs To Be Done)

Rather than designing for demographics, SalvageCars.com is designed around the jobs visitors are trying to accomplish.

Job 1 — Evaluate a Salvage Vehicle

  • Is this a good purchase?
  • What should I pay?
  • What damage should concern me?
  • What hidden costs should I expect?
  • Can I insure it?
  • Can I finance it?

Job 2 — Understand My State's Requirements

  • What does my state call a salvage vehicle?
  • What inspections are required?
  • Can I rebuild the vehicle?
  • Can I register it?
  • Where are the official DMV resources?

Job 3 — Rebuild a Vehicle

  • What documentation do I need?
  • What inspections are required?
  • What should I photograph during repairs?
  • What mistakes should I avoid?

Job 4 — Sell a Branded Vehicle

  • How much is it worth?
  • What disclosures are required?
  • How does a branded title affect value?

Job 5 — Learn About Salvage Vehicles

  • What is a salvage title?
  • What is a rebuilt title?
  • What is a flood vehicle?
  • What is a non-repairable vehicle?

4. Question Map

The Question Map connects common visitor questions to the information needed to answer them. Seven question journeys are available today. Each helps visitors select a jurisdiction and navigate to relevant sections of its State Guidance page.

  • Can my salvage vehicle be rebuilt? — Rebuilding eligibility
  • What should I know before buying? — Before buying
  • How do I get my vehicle back on the road? — Returning to the road
  • What if I buy a vehicle from another state? — Out-of-state vehicles
  • How do I sell or transfer my vehicle? — Selling or transferring
  • What do title brands mean? — Title brands
  • When does my vehicle need an inspection? — Vehicle inspections

These journeys complement the broader questions addressed in educational guides. New guides and future tools should continue to answer identifiable visitor questions.

5. User Journeys

Visitors may arrive through the homepage, a search result, a guide, a question journey, or a jurisdiction page. Navigation should work from any of these entry points.

Question-first journey

A visitor chooses a question on the homepage, selects their state or the District of Columbia, and follows a link to the relevant section of that jurisdiction's guidance. Each destination uses a state-specific page anchor.

State-first journey

A visitor uses the State Guidance directory on the homepage to open a jurisdiction page, then reviews its terminology, procedures, official sources, and related next steps.

Guide-first journey

A visitor opens an educational guide, learns the general concepts, and follows relevant links to jurisdiction-specific requirements or other guidance.

The goal is not to force visitors through a fixed sequence. Each page should help them complete their current task and discover the next useful resource.

6. Site Structure

Home

The homepage introduces the site, presents “What do you need help with?”, provides links to seven question journeys, and includes the State Salvage Vehicle Guidance directory.

Questions

Seven task-oriented journeys at /questions/ connect common questions to relevant sections of the 51 jurisdiction pages. Individual journeys have their own URLs.

State Guidance

Individual pages at /states/<jurisdiction>/ cover all 50 states and the District of Columbia. The directory is on the homepage at /#state-guidance; there is no separate state index page.

Guides

The guide library currently contains four educational guides, with room to expand into additional buying, rebuilding, selling, and auction topics.

Site and policy documents

Public pages include the Vision, Architecture, Privacy Policy, and Terms of Use. These describe the site’s purpose, structure, and policies.

Future navigation considerations

Potential future sections include calculators, other decision-support tools, vehicle categories, search, and marketplace-related discovery features.

7. Navigation Standards

Navigation should remain simple, predictable, and task-oriented. The current homepage provides primary links to the guide library and State Guidance, while the seven question links offer direct task-based entry points.

  • Use clear link labels that describe the destination or task.
  • Keep the homepage State Guidance directory as the single state index.
  • Preserve stable jurisdiction URLs and section anchors used by question journeys.
  • Provide a clear path back to the homepage and relevant related information.
  • Add navigation for new features only when the destinations exist.

8. Homepage Philosophy

The homepage should help visitors identify their next step without attempting to explain every aspect of salvage vehicles.

The current homepage begins with “Making Sense of Salvage Vehicles,” followed by the question-led section “What do you need help with?”. It links to seven question journeys.

The homepage also contains “State Salvage Vehicle Guidance,” a directory of all 50 states and the District of Columbia, followed by educational guides and an explanation of why SalvageCars.com exists.

The question journeys and state directory provide complementary paths: visitors can begin with a task or with a jurisdiction.

9. Internal Linking Strategy

Every page should function as a useful entry point, including for visitors arriving directly from search engines.

  • Link to relevant guides, jurisdiction pages, or question journeys when they help answer the reader's next question.
  • Use official government sources for state-specific regulatory information.
  • Link to specific jurisdiction sections when an appropriate anchor exists.
  • Do not require every page to contain every kind of link; choose links that suit the page's purpose.
  • Avoid links to planned pages or tools until those destinations exist.

10. URL Philosophy

URLs should be short, descriptive, stable, and human-readable.

/guides/how-to-buy-a-salvage-car/
/questions/can-my-vehicle-be-rebuilt/
/states/new-hampshire/
/#state-guidance

11. Navigation Promise

Every page on SalvageCars.com should help visitors answer their current question while naturally guiding them toward the next question they are likely to have.

12. Definition of Success

  • Visitors quickly find the information they need.
  • Visitors naturally discover related resources.
  • Visitors leave with greater confidence than when they arrived.
  • The platform remains easy to expand.
  • Every guide, state page, and tool fits naturally into the overall structure.

If a first-time visitor can arrive from a search engine, solve their immediate problem, discover additional relevant resources, and leave with greater confidence, the navigation has achieved its purpose.
