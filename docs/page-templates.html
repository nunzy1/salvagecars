Page Templates

Reusable page structures for SalvageCars.com. These templates define information hierarchy, required and optional sections, and visual patterns. They are internal standards, not public pages.

Guide Page Template

The Guide Page template is based on the production how-to-buy-a-salvage-car guide. Its job is to answer a question, establish context, explain the subject, connect relevant state information when appropriate, and help the visitor understand what to do next.

Core principle

A Guide Page should not behave like a generic blog post. It should function as a decision-support page: question → answer → explanation → context → next decision.

Required page anatomy

1. Breadcrumb

Orient the visitor within the site hierarchy. Use: Home → Guides → Current Guide.

2. H1 and lead

State the topic or user question plainly. Follow with a concise lead that explains what the reader will understand, evaluate, or decide. The lead may use one or two short paragraphs.

3. “What you should know”

Give the reader the essential answer near the top of the page. Do not require the visitor to read the entire guide before learning the main conclusion or decision factors.

4. Main explanatory sections

Organize the body around the actual decisions, risks, concepts, or steps the visitor needs to understand. Section count and headings vary by guide.

5. “What should I do next?”

End the main decision path with concrete next actions or questions. Link to another SalvageCars.com page when a useful destination exists. Do not create placeholder links merely to satisfy the template.

6. Related guides

Provide a small number of relevant next-reading options, normally two or three. Prefer usefulness over volume.

Conditional sections

State Guidance connection

Include when state law, title terminology, inspections, rebuilding, registration, or other state-specific requirements materially affect the answer.

Pattern: “Your state matters”

Explain why the visitor must verify state-specific requirements, then link to /states/.

Official sources

Include when authoritative sources materially support the guide. Prefer government agencies, statutes, administrative rules, and official programs.

When a useful external service may charge visitors, explain what the service is, state that a fee may apply, disclose lack of affiliation when appropriate, and link to an authoritative landing page rather than selecting a commercial provider for the visitor.

Visual pattern

Guide pages should feel like a natural extension of Homepage V2: spacious, restrained, readable, and content-first.
